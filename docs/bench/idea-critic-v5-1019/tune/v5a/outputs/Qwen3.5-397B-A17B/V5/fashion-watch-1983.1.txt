First call: GO. The pivot from "quality timekeeper" to "fashion accessory" neutralizes the reputation risk by changing the evaluation criteria from durability to style, while the low cost enables the volume required to hit the target.

**Steelman:** Leveraging existing movement technology to enter the high-volume fashion segment creates a new revenue stream that is immune to traditional quality comparisons, making the 1M unit target achievable through breadth of SKUs rather than single-model dominance.

**Options:**
1.  **Launch (Proposed):** High upside, high volume, distinct brand separation. Reversible via limited initial production runs.
2.  **Do Nothing:** Safe reputation, but cedes the emerging low-cost fashion market to competitors; stagnant growth.
3.  **Premium Only:** Maintains reputation strictly but ignores the mass market shift; high margin but low volume ceiling.
The proposal beats "do nothing" because the market is shifting toward disposability and style; staying static is the greater risk.

**Fit:** Assumes the strategic goal is market share expansion and utilizing excess movement capacity. If the north star is solely "heritage prestige," this fails; if it is "growth and volume," it fits perfectly.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the company markets these as "cheap watches" instead of "fun fashion," causing brand cannibalization. It is a two-way door; unsold inventory can be liquidated with minimal loss due to low unit cost. The cost of delay is losing the first-mover advantage in the plastic fashion segment to agile competitors.

**Evidence:** The proposal relies on the assumption that "fashion" buyers do not expect the same longevity as "tool" watch buyers. This is a valid segment distinction. The 1M target is aggressive but mathematically possible if each of the 12 models sells ~83k units globally, a small fraction of the total addressable market for inexpensive accessories.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (reputation, taste, competition) are managed by the positioning strategy (fashion vs. quality) and the low cost basis (allowing price flexibility).
**Risks:** Brand dilution if messaging fails; inventory obsolescence if trends shift mid-year. These are mitigated by treating the launch as a capped pilot per SKU.

**Kill Criterion:** If the first 3 models fail to achieve 10% sell-through in the first quarter despite marketing support, halt the remaining 9 models and liquidate stock.

DECISION: GO
