First call: GO. The proposal leverages existing assets (compact platform, converted factory) to address a clear market gap (garage-friendly family van) created by the company's recent stabilization via government backing.

**Steelman:** A capital-efficient launch of a novel "car-based van" category using proven components to capture families needing space without commercial stigma, executed within existing financial constraints.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Category Risk:** The claim "no such category exists" is the opportunity, not a blocker. Families currently compromise with wagons (too small) or full-size vans (too large/unwieldy). Creating the category is the strategy.
2.  **Financial Constraint:** Little cash prohibits all-new development. Reusing the compact platform and converting a factory are the *only* viable paths; building a new platform or factory is impossible. This constraint validates the approach.
3.  **Brand Perception:** Risk of being seen as a commercial van is mitigated by marketing it as a "garage-fit" family vehicle with car-like driving dynamics, distinct from work trucks.
4.  **Competition:** Rivals copying is a future problem; being first establishes the brand definition. Waiting for perfect conditions guarantees irrelevance.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the vehicle drives like a truck despite the FWD claim, or if marketing fails to distinguish it from commercial workhorses. However, the compact car platform inherently provides car-like handling, addressing the core mechanical risk.

**Blockers vs. Risks:**
*   **Blockers:** NONE. The financial constraint forces the proposed solution; no alternative reaches the same upside with available capital.
*   **Risks:** Market education (new category), copycats, residual commercial perception. These are managed by speed to market and clear positioning, not by stopping the project.

**Kill Criteria:** If pilot testing shows >60% of buyers perceive it strictly as a commercial vehicle despite marketing, pivot messaging or halt expansion. Leading metric: Ratio of family vs. commercial buyers in first quarter.

The proposal turns constraints (cash, factory) into strategic advantages (low cost, speed). The absence of a category is the value proposition.

DECISION: GO
