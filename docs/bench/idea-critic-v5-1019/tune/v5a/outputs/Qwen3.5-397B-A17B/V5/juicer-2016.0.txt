First call: NO-GO. The unit economics and behavioral friction of a $699 connected juicer relying on expensive proprietary pods in a niche market create an almost certain path to failure.

**Steelman:** A premium, hassle-free nutrition ecosystem where freshness and convenience justify a high hardware entry and recurring revenue via locked-in consumables.

**Attack:**
1.  **Economics:** At $699, the device targets early adopters, but the subscription model requires per-bag costs significantly higher than fresh produce. Consumers rarely accept a 5-10x markup on food staples for marginal convenience, especially when the core action (juicing) is not time-prohibitive.
2.  **Connectivity:** Mandating Wi-Fi for a basic mechanical function (checking freshness) introduces unnecessary failure points and privacy concerns without delivering commensurate value. If the internet fails, does the $699 brick become useless?
3.  **Market Size:** The intersection of consumers willing to pay $699 for hardware, commit to a subscription, and accept limited SKU variety (pre-packed bags) is vanishingly small. History shows similar "smart appliance" plays failing due to this narrow funnel.
4.  **Alternatives:** A non-connected version with simple QR codes or manual entry captures the convenience value without the connectivity risk. A lower-cost device with a broader pod ecosystem reduces entry friction.

**Pre-mortem:** The company burns cash on inventory for a device that sits unused because the subscription is too expensive. The "smart" features become liabilities when servers lag or shut down. Retail partners refuse shelf space due to the high price point and low turnover.

**Blockers:**
*   **Unit Economics:** The proposed price structure ($699 + premium subscription) likely exceeds the customer's lifetime value threshold for a single-purpose kitchen appliance.
*   **Unnecessary Complexity:** Internet dependency for freshness checking is a solution in search of a problem, adding cost and risk without solving a core user pain point.

**Kill Criteria:** If a pilot shows attachment rate (subscription retention) below 60% after 3 months, or if the cost-per-juice exceeds $8, the model fails.

The proposal ignores the fundamental friction of high-cost hardware paired with high-cost consumables in a category where cheap, flexible alternatives exist.

DECISION: NO-GO
