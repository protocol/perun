First call: GO. The company leverages its dominant hardware position to solve the industry's existential crisis (piracy) with a legal, convenient alternative, despite thin margins.

**Steelman:** A tightly integrated ecosystem where proprietary hardware is the exclusive gateway to the only legal, affordable, and user-friendly music library, creating a defensible moat against both piracy and subscription rivals.

**Attack & Analysis:**
*   **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" cedes the market to piracy, eroding hardware value. Subscription models (the rival path) suffer from poor 2003 connectivity and consumer resistance to renting vs. owning. Per-song sales match current consumer behavior.
*   **Fit:** Directly protects the core hardware business; the store is a loss leader for device sales.
*   **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if labels withdraw licenses or the proprietary format limits adoption too severely. However, labels are desperate for revenue; the "walled garden" is a feature, not a bug, ensuring quality control.
*   **Evidence:** The proposal relies on the verified fact that piracy is rampant (demand exists) and labels are vulnerable (supply is negotiable). The 99-cent price point is a psychological anchor, not a profit center.
*   **Blockers:** None. The risks (margin, licensing, platform limits) are known variables that the integrated model specifically mitigates by prioritizing hardware volume over software profit. The "rival" subscription model is prematurely complex for the current infrastructure.
*   **Kill Criteria:** If major labels refuse licensing within 6 months, or if device sales do not increase by 15% post-launch, the strategy fails.

**Verdict:** The proposal correctly identifies that the value is in the device, not the song margin. It turns a commodity (music) into a differentiator for the hardware. The restrictions (own computers only) drive hardware adoption. The risks are manageable compared to the certainty of hardware commoditization without a content ecosystem.

DECISION: GO
