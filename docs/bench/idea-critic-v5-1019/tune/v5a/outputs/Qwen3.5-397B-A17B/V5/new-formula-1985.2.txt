First call: NO-GO. The plan trades a functional product for an emotional asset based on incomplete data.

Steelman: A superior-tasting formula validated by blind tests will reverse market share loss by winning over preference-driven consumers.

Attack: The evidence relies exclusively on blind taste tests and focus groups where participants were unaware the original formula would be permanently withdrawn. This creates a fatal sampling error: the tests measure isolated sensory preference, not the value of the brand heritage or the consumer's relationship with the existing product. The proposal explicitly admits testing did not disclose the removal of the original, rendering the data irrelevant to the actual decision of replacing a legacy item.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Retain the core asset while exploring line extensions (e.g., a "New" variant) to capture the sweeter profile without alienating the base.
2. Dual launch: Introduce the new formula alongside the old, letting the market decide without forcing a binary switch.
3. The proposal: Forced replacement. This is the highest risk option with zero reversibility once the original inventory is destroyed.

Pre-mortem: Upon announcement, loyalists perceive the removal as a betrayal of heritage rather than an improvement. Sales collapse not because the new drink tastes bad, but because the act of removing the original triggers a backlash that blind tests could not predict. Without a fallback product, there is no escape valve for this anger.

Blockers:
1. **Data Invalidity**: The core evidence (taste tests) explicitly excluded the primary variable of the decision (permanent removal of the original). The data proves the new soda tastes good, not that replacing the old one is viable.
2. **No Reversibility**: Withdrawing the original entirely removes the safety net. If the new formula fails, the company has no product to sell to its largest demographic.

Risks: Emotional backlash, bottler revolt, permanent brand damage. These are not merely risks; they are probable outcomes given the disconnect between the test conditions and market reality.

Kill Criterion: If the plan proceeds, immediate sales drop >5% in week one with no recovery by week four indicates the backlash is structural, not transitional. However, the lack of a fallback makes this metric a post-mortem tool, not a safeguard.

DECISION: NO-GO
