First call: NO-GO. The proposal mandates a paid subscription and broadband requirement in a 2002 market where broadband penetration is low, competitors offer free online play, and the company lacks any operational history in running hosting or support services.

Steelman: A unified, premium online service with shared identity and voice chat creates a superior, sticky ecosystem that differentiates the console through quality and convenience, justifying a subscription fee despite higher barriers.

Options:
1. **Proposed Plan:** High cost (subscription + hardware), high risk (adoption, operations), low reversibility (brand damage if failed).
2. **Do Nothing/Free Tier:** Match rivals by offering free basic matchmaking per game; zero subscription friction, lower operational complexity.
3. **Hybrid/Staged:** Launch free basic services to build user base, introduce premium voice/friends features later once broadband adoption grows and operations are proven.

Fit: The strategy assumes users will pay for convenience before the infrastructure (broadband) is ubiquitous. This contradicts the stated market reality of few broadband homes and expectations of free play.

Pre-mortem: The service launches to a tiny addressable market (broadband owners willing to pay). The company burns capital building server infrastructure it cannot staff or maintain, leading to outages. Gamers reject the "double dip" (console + subscription) when rivals are free. The brand becomes synonymous with "expensive and broken."

Evidence: The proposal itself admits "few homes have broadband," "players expect online play to be free," and the company has "never run" these operations. These are not risks; they are stated facts that directly contradict the core revenue and adoption model.

Blockers:
1. **Market Mismatch:** The core requirement (broadband) excludes the majority of the 2002 install base, while the pricing model (paid) fights established consumer expectations of free play.
2. **Operational Void:** The company explicitly lacks the capability to run the required hosting and support, creating a high probability of service failure.
3. **Competitive Disadvantage:** Rivals offer similar core utility (online play) for free, making the paid wall a non-starter for mass adoption.

Risks: High churn, brand reputation damage, sunk costs in unused infrastructure.

Kill Criterion: If broadband adoption does not exceed 40% of the target demographic within 12 months, the subscription model fails.

DECISION: NO-GO
