First call: GO. The shift in buyer demographics toward American-brand customers seeking smaller sedans represents a clear market opportunity that the current lineup ignores. Waiting cedes this segment to competitors who can offer compact luxury options.

Steelman: Leveraging an existing mass-market platform allows the luxury brand to enter the compact segment rapidly and cost-effectively, offering premium features at a competitive price point to capture a new demographic without the R&D lead time of a ground-up design.

Options analysis shows "do nothing" risks losing the emerging demographic entirely. Building a unique platform is too costly and slow for a test of this specific buyer behavior. The shared platform approach is the only viable path to speed and margin, provided the "luxury" perception holds.

Fit: This aligns with the north star of expanding the buyer base, assuming the brand equity is strong enough to withstand a lower-tier association.

Pre-mortem: The plan fails if the "badge engineering" is too obvious, causing existing luxury buyers to flee while new buyers still reject the price. However, this is a risk, not a proven fact. The cost of delay is high; if these buyers are already looking, they will settle for a rival's compact offering within a quarter.

Evidence: The proposal cites a verified trend: buyers are shifting from European to American brands and seeking smaller sizes. No internal data contradicts this.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (platform visibility, price justification, salesperson behavior, prestige dilution) are execution challenges and market uncertainties, not factual contradictions of the core opportunity. They become kill criteria: if initial surveys show existing owners rejecting the brand due to the new model, or if sales data shows zero uptake from the target demographic after six months, the project stops.

Risks: Brand dilution and channel resistance. These are managed by distinct marketing, separate sales channels, and strict quality control on the "added trim and features" to ensure the premium price feels justified.

Kill criterion: If pre-orders from the target demographic (American-brand switchers) do not reach 15% of total compact sales within the first year, or if net promoter score from existing luxury owners drops by more than 10 points, the model is discontinued.

DECISION: GO
