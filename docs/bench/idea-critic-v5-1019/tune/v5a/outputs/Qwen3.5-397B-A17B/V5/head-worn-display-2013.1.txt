First call: NO-GO. The stated facts describe a product with unresolved fundamental blockers regarding social viability and utility, not merely execution risks.

Steelman: An invitation-only launch at $1,500 allows a controlled rollout to validate privacy norms and use cases before a general consumer release.

Attack: The proposal positions the device as an "everyday consumer product" while admitting there is "no clear everyday use for most people." This is a contradiction. A high price ($1,500 in 2013) is justifiable only for indispensable utility or status; without a defined use case, the value proposition collapses. Furthermore, "privacy objections" and "social acceptance" are not risks that can be patched post-launch; they are adoption barriers. If the public rejects the camera on sight, the product fails regardless of technical specs. The "invitation-only" tactic does not solve the core problem that the device lacks a reason to exist for the mass market it targets.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids reputational damage from a failed consumer launch.
2. Enterprise pivot: Target specific industrial use cases (logistics, medical) where the camera adds verified value and privacy is managed by policy, not social norm.
3. Delay: Wait until battery life improves and a killer app is identified.

Pre-mortem: The product launches, early adopters wear it once for novelty, then abandon it due to short battery and social awkwardness. Media amplifies privacy fears, branding the company as intrusive. Sales flatline, and the general release is quietly cancelled.

Blockers:
1. The proposal explicitly states "no clear everyday use for most people" while targeting the "general public" as an "everyday consumer product." You cannot sell an everyday solution without a problem.
2. Unresolved "social acceptance" and "privacy objections" constitute a market rejection risk that an invitation-only program cannot mitigate if the fundamental design (visible camera) is the trigger.

Risks: High price, short battery life, execution difficulty.

Kill Criterion: If pilot users do not identify a daily recurring use case within 30 days of receipt, the general release must be cancelled.

The plan fails the basic test of product-market fit as described. It attempts to solve social and utility gaps with a pricing and distribution strategy, which is insufficient.

DECISION: NO-GO
