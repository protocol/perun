First call: GO. The proposal leverages a structural cost advantage to solve a verified user pain point (storage exhaustion) with a differentiated core capability (search), funded by an existing, high-margin model (text ads).

**Steelman:** Offer 1,000x the standard storage to eliminate user deletion behavior, using superior search as the primary interface, monetized via contextual ads, while managing initial scale through invitations.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Cost:** Critics cite storage expense. However, hardware costs follow a steep decline curve; buying early at scale locks in a moat before competitors can match the offering without burning cash. The "do nothing" alternative cedes the high-value user segment to whoever breaks the storage barrier first.
2.  **Privacy:** Scanning mail for ads raises valid concerns. Yet, users already trade privacy for free services; the value exchange (unlimited storage vs. targeted text ads) is favorable compared to the friction of managing megabytes. The risk is reputational, not existential, provided the scanning is automated and strictly limited to ad-matching.
3.  **Market Maturity:** Incumbents are entrenched but complacent, optimizing for low-cost storage rather than utility. They are trapped by legacy infrastructure. A "cheaper" launch with standard storage fails because it offers no reason to switch. Only a step-change in utility (1GB) drives migration.
4.  **Revenue:** No direct revenue at launch is a feature, not a bug. The invitation-only constraint creates scarcity, drives viral demand, and caps infrastructure load while the ad engine trains on real data.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if ad relevance is too low to fund operations or if privacy backlash triggers regulatory blocking. However, the text-ad model is proven in search; applying it to email is a logical extension, not a new invention.

**Blockers vs. Risks:**
*   **Blockers:** NONE. The math holds if hardware costs drop as projected (a safe bet in 2004). The ad model is viable.
*   **Risks:** Privacy perception, slower-than-expected ad yield. These are managed by the invitation-only rollout (kill criterion: pause invites if cost-per-user exceeds ad-yield by 20% within 6 months).

The proposal turns a commodity (email) into a premium product via storage abundance, funded by high-intent data. It beats "do nothing" and standard launches.

DECISION: GO
