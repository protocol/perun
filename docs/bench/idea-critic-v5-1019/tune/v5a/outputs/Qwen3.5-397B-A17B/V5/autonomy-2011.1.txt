First call: NO-GO. The stated facts already contain a blocker: the target's financial reporting has been publicly questioned since 2009, yet the buyer proposes an 11x revenue premium with only a few weeks for due diligence.

**Steelman:** The acquisition instantly pivots a hardware legacy into high-margin software analytics, justifying a control premium to secure market leadership before competitors react.

**Attack:**
1.  **Financial Integrity:** A buyer lacking software experience cannot verify the quality of earnings in "a few weeks" when red flags have existed for two years. The finance chief's internal reservation confirms this incapacity. Buying at 64% above market price while suspecting the numbers are flawed is not strategy; it is speculation with shareholder capital.
2.  **Integration & Capability:** The buyer admits zero software experience. Integrating a complex analytics firm at this price point requires deep domain expertise to retain talent and merge roadmaps. The risk of value destruction post-close is extreme given the cultural and technical chasm.
3.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" or pursue smaller, tuck-in acquisitions allows the company to build software competency organically without betting the farm on a potentially toxic asset. The proposed deal does not beat "do nothing" because the downside (buying fraud or failure) outweighs the upside given the verification gap.

**Blocker:** The combination of known accounting suspicions (since 2009), internal finance leadership doubts, and an insufficient due diligence window creates a factual basis that the core asset value cannot be verified. Proceeding as proposed ignores a stated negative fact that removes the route to upside (validating the price).

**Risk:** Even if accounts are clean, the 11x multiple leaves no room for integration error by an inexperienced team.

**Kill Criterion:** If independent forensic accounting cannot clear the 2009–2011 revenue recognition practices within 14 days, the deal must terminate. The current proposal lacks this hard stop, committing to full scale before verification.

The plan fails the operator test: you do not write a check for $11.7 billion when your own CFO doubts the numbers and the clock is too short to prove them right.

DECISION: NO-GO
