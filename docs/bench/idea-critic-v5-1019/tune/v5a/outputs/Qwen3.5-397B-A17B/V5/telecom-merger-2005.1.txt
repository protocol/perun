First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits $36 billion to acquire a network explicitly described as incompatible and limited, requiring the buyer to run legacy equipment while absorbing massive goodwill risk without a stated plan to resolve the technical clash.

**Steelman:** The acquisition is justified solely by the target's high-value business customers, assuming their retention outweighs the cost and complexity of integrating two fundamentally incompatible networks.

**Options:**
1.  **Merge as proposed:** Highest cost ($36B), highest risk (integration failure, legacy drag), low reversibility.
2.  **Do nothing:** Zero integration cost, zero goodwill risk, but forfeits the specific business customer segment.
3.  **Asset-only purchase:** Acquire the customer contracts and migrate them to the buyer's existing network, leaving the incompatible legacy infrastructure behind. This captures the upside (customers) while eliminating the primary blocker (incompatible network integration).

**Fit:** Running incompatible legacy technology contradicts the operational goal of a unified, scalable national network. The proposal forces a step backward in technical coherence to gain market share.

**Pre-mortem:** The integration stalls because the technologies cannot interoperate efficiently. Costs balloon as the company maintains two parallel networks. The "limited for data" legacy network becomes a financial anchor, eroding the value of the acquired customer base through poor service or high migration churn. The goodwill impairment wipes out equity value.

**Evidence:** The proposal itself admits the technologies are "different, incompatible" and the target network is "limited for data." It acknowledges "large goodwill" and "integration cost" as risks but offers no mechanism to mitigate them.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Technical Incompatibility:** The core asset (the network) cannot be integrated with the buyer's standard, forcing permanent legacy operations or a costly, risky rip-and-replace not detailed in the plan.
2.  **Superior Alternative:** Option 3 (buy customers, not network) achieves the strategic goal (acquiring business users) without the fatal flaw of inheriting an incompatible, limited network.

**Risks:** Customer churn during migration, goodwill write-down, inflated integration costs.

**Kill Criterion:** If a customer-only acquisition structure is not explored and rejected based on cost before signing, the deal fails the "cheaper alternative" test.

DECISION: NO-GO
