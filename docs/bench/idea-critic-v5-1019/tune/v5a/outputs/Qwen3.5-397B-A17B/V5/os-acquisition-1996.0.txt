First call: GO. The company faces existential decline with no viable internal path; acquiring a modern core is the only mechanism to reset the trajectory, despite high friction.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the external modern OS provides an immediate, proven technical foundation that internal teams failed to deliver, while repatriating the founder leverages unique vision to unify strategy and execution, outweighing integration risks.

**Options:**
1.  **Acquire:** High upfront cost and integration risk, but offers full control and a modern architecture. Reversible only via divestiture.
2.  **License:** Lower initial cost, but cedes strategic control, margins, and roadmap to a competitor. High long-term dependency risk.
3.  **Do Nothing:** Continued market share erosion leading to insolvency. Highest risk, zero upside.

**Fit:** Directly addresses the "dated OS" blocker and the failure of internal replacement. Assumes the target's architecture is compatible enough to migrate.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the founder's return triggers internal mutiny or if the technical debt of migration exceeds the runway. The "difficult history" is a known variable, not a surprise. The cost is high relative to revenue, but value is based on survival, not current earnings.

**Evidence & Calculation:** The proposal states internal replacement failed *twice*. This validates the "build" option as exhausted. The target has "few units sold," implying low commercial distraction but unproven scale; however, the acquirer provides the scale. The price ("few hundred million") is a cash burn risk but likely below the cost of total company collapse.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (migration difficulty, founder personality, price) are execution challenges, not factual contradictions. No alternative reaches the same upside (full ownership of a modern core) cheaper. Licensing sacrifices the strategic autonomy required to save the hardware business.

**Kill Criteria:** If the founder refuses the advisor role or the technical audit reveals the OS cannot run critical legacy apps within 12 months, the thesis fails.

**Decision:** The "do nothing" and "license" paths lead to irrelevance. The acquisition buys a future.

DECISION: GO
