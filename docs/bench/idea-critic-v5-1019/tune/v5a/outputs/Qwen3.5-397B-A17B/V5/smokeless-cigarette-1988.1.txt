First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks to launch and widely promote a product where the core value proposition (taste, ease of use) is explicitly flagged as likely failed in testing, and the primary marketing angle (health/cleaner) faces immediate regulatory prohibition.

Steelman: Launching now captures first-mover advantage in reduced-risk smoking before competitors, leveraging sunk R&D costs to establish a new category.

Attack:
1.  **Product-Market Fit:** The proposal admits smokers may dislike the taste and lighting mechanism. If the user experience is worse than the incumbent (ordinary cigarettes) without a guaranteed health claim, adoption will stall. A "complicated" cigarette violates the core utility of the category: simplicity.
2.  **Regulatory Blocker:** Promoting it as "cleaner" invites immediate regulatory action against unverified health claims. Without the ability to market the differentiator, the product loses its only justification for switching.
3.  **Sunk Cost Fallacy:** The "very large sum" already spent is irrelevant to the forward-looking decision. Throwing good money after bad by launching a flawed product to justify past spend is a classic failure mode.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves capital. A "limited pilot" without wide promotion is the only safer alternative, but the proposal demands wide promotion now.

Blockers:
*   **Regulatory:** Marketing "cleaner" claims without explicit approval is a legal blocker that stops the proposed promotion strategy.
*   **User Acceptance:** Stated risk that users dislike the taste/mechanism directly contradicts the requirement for mass adoption.

Risks:
*   Brand damage if the product is perceived as a gimmick or fails publicly.
*   Regulatory fines.

Kill Criterion: If test market data shows <20% repeat purchase rate due to taste/usability, the project must stop. The proposal ignores this by pushing for wide launch despite known risks.

The proposal fails because it attempts to scale a product with known functional deficits and a blocked marketing pathway. The sunk cost is not a reason to launch; it is a reason to cut losses or radically pivot to a non-health-claimed niche test, not a wide launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
