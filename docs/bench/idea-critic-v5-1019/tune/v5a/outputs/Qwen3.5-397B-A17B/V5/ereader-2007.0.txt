First call: GO. The proposal solves the core friction of early e-reading (need for a PC) and aligns with the company's catalog strength, despite hardware inexperience.

**Steelman:** A dedicated, cellular-enabled device removes the primary adoption barrier (complexity) and creates a closed, publisher-safe ecosystem that drives high-margin digital sales, offsetting hardware losses.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Cedes the digital future to competitors; print sales eventually decline anyway.
2.  **Software Only:** Fails in 2007 due to poor battery life on existing devices and lack of ubiquitous Wi-Fi; requires a PC, maintaining friction.
3.  **Hardware Risk:** Valid, but manageable by partnering with existing manufacturers rather than building factories. The "cannibalization" fear is a false negative; digital saves margin on printing/shipping and captures users who would otherwise buy nothing or pirate.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the device is too expensive, the screen is hard to read, or publishers withhold content. The "free cellular" model is the critical differentiator but creates a massive operating expense risk if usage scales without corresponding book sales.

**Evidence & Math:** The proposal assumes low prices will drive volume sufficient to offset hardware subsidies and telco costs. Without a verified unit-economic model showing positive Lifetime Value (LTV) per user after hardware and connectivity subsidies, the cash burn could be catastrophic. The claim that "new releases at a single low price" satisfies publishers is unproven; they may demand higher splits to offset print loss.

**Blocker Test:**
*   **Blockers:** None explicitly fatal in the text, provided the subsidy math works.
*   **Risks:** Hardware execution, publisher revolt, subsidy burn rate.

**Kill Criterion:** If the cost to acquire a customer (device subsidy + 2 years cellular) exceeds the projected gross profit from 15 books/year within the first 12 months, the model fails.

**Decision:** The strategic necessity outweighs the operational risk. The "walled garden" approach addresses piracy fears directly. The hardware gap is a solvable execution problem, not a strategic flaw. The potential to own the entire reading experience justifies the bet.

DECISION: GO
