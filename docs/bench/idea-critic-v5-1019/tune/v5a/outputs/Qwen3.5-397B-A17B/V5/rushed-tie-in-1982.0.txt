First call: NO-GO. Compressing complex creative work into five weeks while committing to massive inventory creates a catastrophic risk profile where failure modes (poor quality, unsold stock) are guaranteed to compound the already high sunk cost of the license.

Steelman: A single elite programmer can rapidly prototype a simple film tie-in to capture immediate holiday demand, leveraging the film's existing popularity to override quality deficits.

Options:
1. **Proposed Plan:** High cost (license + large run), extreme risk (5-week dev), low reversibility (physical cartridges).
2. **Do Nothing:** Zero additional cost, zero risk, forfeits holiday revenue.
3. **Staged Pilot:** One programmer builds a minimal viable product in five weeks for a *small* test run. Cost is capped; risk is contained; reversibility is high.

Fit: The goal is maximizing holiday profit, not just shipping product. The proposed plan jeopardizes the entire investment by betting the farm on an unproven, rushed execution.

Pre-mortem: The game ships late or buggy due to unrealistic timelines. The large production run sits unsold because the quality does not match the film's hype, leading to massive write-offs and brand damage. This is a one-way door; once cartridges are pressed, capital is trapped.

Evidence & Calculation: Normal development cycles exceed five weeks significantly. Compressing this by an order of magnitude without parallel teams or prior assets implies severe corner-cutting. The "very large production run" assumes 100% market penetration or perfect sales forecasting, which is historically unsupported for new game releases, especially rushed ones. The high license fee increases the break-even point, making the inventory risk even more dangerous.

Blockers:
1. **Time/Capacity Mismatch:** Five weeks is insufficient for a quality commercial release given normal development standards. This directly contradicts the requirement for a "hit" product.
2. **Inventory Risk:** Committing to a large run before validating market reception or product quality creates a near-certain financial loss scenario if the game underperforms.
3. **No Stop Condition:** The plan lacks a kill criterion or pilot phase. It commits full capital before verifying the core assumption (that a 5-week build will sell).

Kill Criterion: If a playable demo is not ready and tested within two weeks, the project must pivot to a smaller run. The current plan has no such brake.

DECISION: NO-GO
