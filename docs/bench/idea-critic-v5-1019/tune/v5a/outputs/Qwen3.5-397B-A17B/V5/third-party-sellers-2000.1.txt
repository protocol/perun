First call: GO. The marketplace model transforms fixed inventory risk into variable fee income, essential for an unprofitable retailer scaling assortment without capital expenditure.

Steelman: By aggregating all offers (new/used, first/third-party) on single pages, the site becomes the definitive destination for selection and price, monetizing traffic via fees even when not winning the buy box.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Maintain full margin control but cap growth at inventory capital limits; likely path to continued unprofitability.
2. Closed platform: Only authorized partners; reduces risk but limits selection density and price competitiveness.
3. Proposed open marketplace: Maximizes selection and price leadership; sacrifices some margin control for volume and fee revenue.
The proposal beats "do nothing" by solving the capital constraint blocking profitability.

Fit: Aligns with the north star of becoming the "earth's biggest bookstore" (and beyond) by leveraging external inventory to expand selection infinitely without balance sheet strain.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if third-party fraud or poor shipping destroys brand trust, causing customers to flee to physical stores. This is a two-way door; seller terms can be tightened or accounts suspended. Cost of delay is high: every month without expanded selection cedes customer habit formation to emerging competitors.

Evidence: The core economic fact is that unprofitable retailers cannot fund infinite inventory expansion. External capital (sellers' inventory) is required. The risk of cannibalization is real but secondary: if a used copy sells, the retailer still earns a fee, whereas an unsold new copy yields zero revenue and incurs holding costs.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (undercutting, quality, cannibalization, supplier objection) are operational challenges, not factual impossibilities. They are mitigated by the fee structure (profit on lost sales), seller rating systems, and the sheer necessity of growth over margin protection in a loss-making state.

Risks: Brand dilution from bad actors; supplier retaliation. These are managed via strict SLAs and gradual onboarding, not by rejecting the model.

Kill criterion: If third-party defect rate (negative feedback/shipping failures) exceeds 2% within six months, pause new seller onboarding to recalibrate vetting. Leading metric: Percentage of total units sold by third parties vs. first-party.

DECISION: GO
