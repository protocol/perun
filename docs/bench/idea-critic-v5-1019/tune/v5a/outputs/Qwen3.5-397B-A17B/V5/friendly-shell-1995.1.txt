First call: NO-GO. The proposal targets a mass home market but requires hardware ("high-end PC") that the majority of that market does not possess, immediately capping adoption below viability.

Steelman: A specialized, low-friction interface could unlock computer usage for novices by leveraging spatial metaphors, justifying a premium price if it solves the complexity barrier better than general-purpose systems.

However, the attack reveals fatal structural flaws:
1.  **Market Mismatch:** The core constraint is hardware availability. Selling a $100 software layer requiring a "high-end" machine in 1995 excludes the primary demographic (new home users with existing, likely modest hardware). The addressable market is too small to sustain development.
2.  **Obsolescence Risk:** A major OS release with a new interface is imminent. Launching a standalone shell months before a platform-level shift invites immediate cannibalization or irrelevance. The "do nothing" option (waiting for the OS update) offers a similar benefit at zero marginal cost to the user.
3.  **Adoption Friction:** Experienced users (who often influence household purchases) may reject the "patronizing" nature, while the lack of third-party add-ons limits long-term utility compared to the standard desktop.

Alternatives considered:
*   **Do Nothing:** Wait for the upcoming OS release. Cost: $0. Risk: Missed early mover advantage (low, given the hardware constraint). Reversibility: N/A.
*   **Integrate, Don't Separate:** Bundle the "room" metaphor as an optional mode within the existing OS rather than a separate $100 product. This lowers the hardware barrier perception and aligns with the upcoming release.

Pre-mortem: The product launches, sells poorly to the tiny high-end niche, and is killed when the new OS arrives with built-in accessibility features. The brand is associated with an expensive, unnecessary toy.

Blockers:
*   **Hardware Gap:** The requirement for a high-end PC contradicts the goal of reaching "many new home users" who typically have standard configurations.
*   **Timing:** The imminent major OS release makes a standalone, expensive shell strategically unsound.

Risks:
*   User perception of being patronized.
*   Lack of ecosystem (add-ons).

Kill Criterion: If <5% of current installed base meets the "high-end" spec by launch date, the mass-market premise fails.

DECISION: NO-GO
