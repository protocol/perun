First call: NO-GO. The proposal ignores a critical mechanical mismatch between the product's design specifications and the target market's infrastructure.

Steelman: A small, accessible motorcycle can carve a new commuter niche in the US if it survives local operating conditions.

**Blockers:**
The proposal explicitly admits the company's larger models fail at US highway speeds. While the 50cc unit is intended for cities, US urban infrastructure in 1959 often requires merging onto high-speed arterials where 50cc engines cannot safely operate. Furthermore, "sporting-goods stores" lack the specialized service infrastructure required for vehicle maintenance. If the bikes break down (a high probability given the admitted durability issues of the brand's larger engines under US stress), there is no repair network, leading to immediate brand toxicity. The foreign currency risk is secondary to the physical inability of the product to function safely in the intended environment.

**Better Ways:**
"Do nothing" preserves capital. A superior alternative is to license the technology to an established US manufacturer with existing dealer networks and service capacity, removing the currency risk and infrastructure gap. Alternatively, test a tiny batch via existing motorcycle dealers only to verify durability before committing to a subsidiary.

**Pre-mortem:**
Six months post-launch, dealers are flooded with broken units. Sporting-goods stores refuse to accept returns or handle repairs. The brand is labeled "junk." The subsidiary burns cash with no revenue.

**Kill Criteria Check:**
The proposal lacks a stop condition. There is no pilot cap, no durability test result specific to US roads, and no defined metric for "market fit" before full subsidiary setup. Committing to a subsidiary and non-traditional distribution channels without verifying the 50cc engine's survival on US roads is an irreversible spend with no working stop condition.

**Verdict:**
The mechanical mismatch and lack of service infrastructure constitute a factual blocker. The plan fails as proposed because it assumes the product works in an environment where the manufacturer already knows their technology struggles.

DECISION: NO-GO
