First call: NO-GO. The proposal executes a high-visibility, irreversible brand change with zero validation, ignoring explicit risks of customer alienation and immediate public backlash.

**Steelman:** A simultaneous, unannounced global rebrand maximizes shock value to signal a definitive break from the past, forcing immediate market attention.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves current equity. A "pilot launch" in select markets or digital-only channels tests reception with capped risk. A "teaser campaign" builds anticipation rather than confusion. The proposal beats none of these on safety or likely success; it merely maximizes exposure to failure.
2.  **Fit:** While aiming for a "contemporary" look, alienating the loyal base that funds the transition contradicts the goal of sustainable modernization. Contemporary brands iterate; they do not gamble the entire identity on an untested hypothesis.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume the launch failed. Why? Core customers felt abandoned by the removal of the recognizable square; social media amplified confusion and anger before the company could explain the rationale; sales dipped due to brand distrust. The "no advance announcement" strategy removes the ability to manage this narrative proactively.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits the design was "not tested with customers." In 2010, social media backlash was already a proven force (e.g., Gap's failed rebrand later that same year). Proceeding without data is negligence, not boldness.
5.  **Blocker:** The combination of "no testing" and "no advance announcement" for a core asset creates a single point of failure with no rollback plan. If the reaction is negative (a high probability given the risks stated), the brand is damaged globally before a fix can be deployed. This is a factual failure of risk management, not a mere risk.

**Kill Criterion:** If a pilot in one region yields a >5% negative sentiment spike or any drop in foot traffic, the global rollout stops. The current plan has no such stop condition; it is all-or-nothing.

**Verdict:** The plan lacks a validation step and a communication strategy, turning a strategic update into a reckless gamble.

DECISION: NO-GO
