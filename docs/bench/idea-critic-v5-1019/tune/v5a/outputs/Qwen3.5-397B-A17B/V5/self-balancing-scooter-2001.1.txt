First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a $200M annualized capital outlay (40k units × $5k) before resolving fundamental legal and market viability.

**Steelman:** The device is a revolutionary urban mobility solution that will create a new market category, justifying early scale to capture first-mover advantage.

**Attack:**
1.  **Legal Blocker:** The proposal admits legal status on sidewalks and roads is "unclear." If regulators ban the device from primary transit zones (sidewalks too slow/dangerous, roads too fast/unstable), the product has no legal operating surface. This is a binary failure mode not addressed by the plan.
2.  **Market Mismatch:** At $5,000, the price exceeds a reliable used car. The admitted lack of proven use cases beyond "institutional buyers" suggests the consumer value proposition is hypothetical. Scaling to 40,000 units assumes mass consumer adoption that the high price and "conspicuous" nature directly contradict.
3.  **Irreversibility:** Building a factory for 40,000 units is a one-way door. If the legal ban hits or the luxury novelty fades, the sunk cost is total. The "risks" listed are not mitigated; they are merely acknowledged while proceeding with maximum exposure.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoids catastrophic capex loss.
*   **Pilot:** Produce 500 units for institutional trials to clarify legal status and utility before tooling a factory. This reaches the same learning upside with <2% of the cost.

**Pre-mortem:** The company launches, regulators immediately classify it as a motor vehicle requiring license plates (which it lacks) or ban it from sidewalks for safety. Sales stall at 2,000 units. The factory sits idle, bankrupting the venture.

**Blockers:**
*   **Legal Uncertainty:** No verified right-to-operate exists. Launching without this is gambling, not business.
*   **Unit Economics vs. Scale:** No evidence supports 40k volume at $5k given the limited use cases.

**Kill Criterion:** If regulatory clarification is not obtained within 90 days, the project must stop. The current proposal ignores this gate.

The plan fails the "beat do nothing" test because the cheaper alternative (pilot) validates the core risks without the existential bet. Committing to full scale now is irresponsible.

DECISION: NO-GO
