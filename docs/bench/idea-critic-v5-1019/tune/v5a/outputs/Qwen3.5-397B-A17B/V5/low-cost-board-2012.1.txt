First call: GO. A $35 price point creates a new category for education, distinct from shared family laptops, provided the missing peripherals are not a barrier to entry.

**Steelman:** A ultra-low-cost, credit-card-sized computer enables schools to deploy one device per student where budget previously allowed only one per class, fostering hands-on programming skills despite the need for users to source displays and power.

**Attack & Options:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Students continue sharing expensive laptops or using locked-down tablets, limiting deep technical exploration.
*   **Subsidized Laptops:** Higher cost ($200+) limits deployment density; less durable; less educational value in hardware manipulation.
*   **The Risk:** The "bring your own screen" requirement could kill adoption if schools lack spare monitors. However, old CRTs and discarded PC monitors are ubiquitous in 2012, making this a solvable integration challenge rather than a hard blocker. The lack of manufacturing experience is mitigated by using licensed distributors who handle logistics and compliance.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the $35 unit requires expensive proprietary cables or if supply cannot meet a sudden spike in demand, causing momentum loss. The "sell out" risk is actually a signal of product-market fit, not a flaw, provided restock speed is adequate.

**Evidence & Fit:** The proposal targets a specific gap: individual ownership for coding practice. Ordinary laptops are cheap relative to a school's total IT budget but too expensive for 1:1 student deployment in developing regions or underfunded districts. The charity model aligns with the goal of rekindling interest rather than maximizing margin.

**Blockers vs. Risks:**
*   **Blockers:** NONE. The facts provided do not show an insurmountable obstacle. The peripheral requirement is a friction point, not a stopper, given the prevalence of discarded displays.
*   **Risks:** Supply chain inexperience, potential for high support volume from novices, and reliance on third-party accessories. These are managed by the distributor model and the inherent simplicity of the hardware.

**Kill Criteria:** If the first batch does not sell out within 6 months or if support costs exceed 20% of revenue, the model requires revision.

The proposal addresses a clear market failure (high cost of 1:1 computing) with a novel, low-cost mechanism. The risks are operational, not fundamental to the value proposition.

DECISION: GO
