First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to full-scale mass production and a bold public schedule before proving battery durability or demand, with no regulatory or price pressure to force adoption.

**Steelman:** A first-mover hybrid using novel power-split transmission and NiMH batteries will define the segment and capture early adopters by 1997.

**Attack:**
1.  **Blocker (Evidence Contradiction):** The plan assumes "mass production" is viable despite "unproven" battery durability. In automotive engineering, unproven high-voltage battery life in volume production is a catastrophic warranty and reliability risk. Committing to a fixed 1997 launch without a pilot fleet proving cycle life creates an uncapped liability exposure that contradicts the goal of a sustainable program.
2.  **Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "limited pilot" alternative beats this plan. Since fuel is cheap and regulators are silent, there is no market pull. A cheaper, safer path is a limited technical pilot (e.g., 500 units) to validate the transmission and battery under real-world conditions before locking in tooling for mass volume.
3.  **Kill Criterion Failure:** The proposal lacks a stop condition. It commits to irreversible tooling and public promises before the primary technical risk (battery life) is resolved. If batteries fail at 40,000 miles, the brand suffers irreparable damage, and the cost to recall/replace packs exceeds the program's upside.

**Pre-mortem:** The program launches in 1997; within 18 months, battery degradation spikes, causing massive warranty claims. With no regulatory mandate and cheap gas, consumers reject the high premium. The company is stuck with specialized inventory and a damaged reputation, while competitors wait to license proven tech later.

**Blockers:**
*   Unproven battery durability paired with a fixed mass-production schedule creates an uncapped financial and reputational risk that the plan does not mitigate.
*   No market signal (cheap fuel, no mandates) justifies the cost of solving this technical risk at volume today.

**Risks:** High unit cost, consumer rejection of premium pricing, supply chain immaturity for NiMH.

**Kill Criterion:** Halt mass-production tooling commitment if a 200-car pilot fleet does not demonstrate <5% capacity loss after 24 months of simulated daily use by Q4 1995.

DECISION: NO-GO
