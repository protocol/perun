First call: GO. The market shift toward small computers is an existential threat to a mainframe-centric model; failing to enter this segment risks long-term irrelevance despite lower margins.

**Steelman:** A dedicated, isolated team can bypass corporate inertia to rapidly deploy a standardized, open-architecture PC via retail channels, capturing market share before competitors solidify dominance.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary objection is that open designs invite copying and erode margins. However, in a nascent mass market, speed and ecosystem adoption (via outside software) outweigh the defensibility of proprietary systems. The alternative—"do nothing" or building a proprietary system internally—fails because it cedes the growing personal computer segment to agile rivals and ignores the company's lack of retail DNA. Building internally using standard practices would likely take too long and result in a product misaligned with the new market's price/performance expectations.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This move contradicts the current vertical integration strategy but aligns with the survival imperative of addressing the "small-computer" growth vector. If we wait to build retail expertise or custom chips, the window closes. The risk of supplier dependence is real but manageable compared to the certainty of missing the market entirely by insisting on total control.

**Blockers & Risks:**
*Blockers:* NONE. The proposal explicitly accounts for the deviations from standard practice (separate team, outside software) required to succeed.
*Risks:* Lower margins and copycats are inherent to the commodity hardware business model, not fatal flaws. The lack of retail experience is mitigated by using independent stores rather than building a direct sales force.

**Kill Criterion:**
If the separate team cannot ship a prototype using off-the-shelf parts within 12 months, or if initial retail partners refuse to stock the unit due to margin structures, the project must pivot or cease. The leading metric is unit volume sold through independent retailers in the first quarter post-launch.

The proposal correctly identifies that the company's core strengths (integration, service) are liabilities in this specific new context. The only path to survival is structural separation and speed.

DECISION: GO
