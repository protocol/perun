First call: GO. The proposal leverages existing core competencies (music, design) to defend against convergence threats while flipping the carrier power dynamic via revenue sharing.

**Steelman:** A premium, keyboard-less device integrating a proven music ecosystem and full web can redefine the category if carrier constraints are traded for revenue share and design control, accepting limited initial reach to establish a beachhead.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Cede music market to phone makers; become a component supplier.
2.  **License OS:** Lose hardware margin and user experience control; commoditize the brand.
3.  **Proposed Path:** High risk, high reward. Captures full stack value and sets the standard.

**Fit:** Aligns with the north star of integrating hardware, software, and services. Reuse of the music brand mitigates the "no phone experience" risk.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the premium price plus lack of keyboard alienates business users before consumer appeal is proven, or if the single carrier cannot support the data load, causing network failures that blame the device.

**Evidence:** Assumed: The music player's brand loyalty is strong enough to transfer to a phone. Assumed: Carrier revenue share offsets exclusivity volume loss.

**Blockers:** NONE. The lack of third-party apps is a known limitation of V1, not a hidden contradiction; it defines the scope rather than breaking the logic. The premium price is a feature, not a bug, signaling quality in a sea of plastic keyboards.

**Risks:** Market rejection of touch-only input; carrier relationship strain; slow adoption due to exclusivity.

**Kill Criterion:** If pre-orders or first-quarter sell-through do not exceed the break-even point for the exclusive carrier deal (specific unit threshold to be defined internally) within 6 months, halt mass production expansion and renegotiate carrier terms.

**Strongest Attack Survived:** The claim that "business users will reject a phone without a physical keyboard" was tested against the trend of consumerization IT. The attack fails because the device targets the broader market first, using superior media and web experience to bypass the entrenched business keyboard preference, effectively creating a new category rather than fighting in the old one.

DECISION: GO
