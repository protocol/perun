First call: GO. The market leader and runner-up are locked in a costly graphics arms race targeting hardcore gamers, leaving the casual and family demographic underserved and price-sensitive.

**Steelman:** By abandoning the specs war, the company can offer a significantly cheaper, differentiated experience that expands the total addressable market rather than fighting for existing share.

**Alternatives:** 
1. *Do nothing:* Remain a distant third in a saturated high-end market; likely irrelevance.
2. *Compete on power:* Requires massive R&D spend with high risk of failure against entrenched rivals; likely financial ruin.
3. *Proposed path:* Low cost, high differentiation, new audience.

**Fit:** This pivots the strategy from "better graphics for gamers" to "fun for everyone," creating a blue ocean. It aligns with a vision of market expansion rather than share stealing.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the motion control is seen as a gimmick rather than intuitive, or if third-party developers refuse to build software, leaving the console empty. However, the low price point reduces consumer risk, and first-party titles can bridge the software gap initially.

**Evidence:** The proposal relies on the verified fact that current high-definition consoles are expensive and complex, alienating non-gamers. The "thin margin" is a strategic choice to drive install base, monetized via software and accessories, a proven model.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (skepticism, untested audience) are inherent to innovation, not factual contradictions. The lack of HD is a feature of the cost strategy, not a defect. No alternative reaches the same upside of market expansion at this cost basis.

**Kill Criteria:** If the attach rate of the motion controller falls below 80% of the installed base within 12 months, or if first-party software sales do not cover hardware losses within two quarters, the strategy must pivot. The leading metric is "weekly active non-gamer households."

The proposal survives the attack because it correctly identifies that the current definition of "console" is the barrier to growth. Changing the definition is the only path to victory for a third-place player.

DECISION: GO
