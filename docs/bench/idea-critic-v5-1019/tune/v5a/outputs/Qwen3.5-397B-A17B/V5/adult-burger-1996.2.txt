First call: NO-GO. The proposal contradicts the brand's core identity and ignores verified risks without a mitigation plan.

**Steelman:** A massive ad spend can temporarily overwrite brand perception, forcing adults to trial a premium burger despite the chain's "kids and value" reputation.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit:** The strategy attacks the brand's north star (child-centered, value) rather than leveraging it. Repositioning a deeply entrenched brand requires years, not a single product launch.
2.  **Options:** "Do nothing" preserves margin. A "better way" would be testing premium items in adult-heavy locations with minimal spend to validate demand before committing industry-record budgets.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The launch fails because the core customer base feels alienated by the price hike and image shift, while the target adult demographic rejects the brand as inherently "cheap" regardless of the new price point. The result is a loss of both segments.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits risks (price sensitivity, brand mismatch) but offers no pilot, cap, or kill criterion. It commits to the "largest budget" before proving demand.
5.  **Blocker:** The plan mandates maximum irreversible spend (industry-record ad budget) before validating the core assumption that adults will accept this brand at a premium price. This violates the "kill check": there is no working stop condition. If the ad campaign fails to convert, the financial loss is catastrophic and uncapped.

**Blockers:**
*   Commitment to maximum irreversible spend prior to demand validation.
*   No pilot, cap, or observable kill criterion to limit loss if the brand pivot fails.

**Risks:**
*   Alienation of the existing child/family customer base.
*   Permanent brand dilution making future value positioning difficult.
*   Low conversion due to deep-seated consumer perception of the brand as "low cost."

**Kill Criterion:** Do not proceed without a time-boxed, capped pilot in 50 markets with a specific conversion threshold (e.g., 15% adult mix) before releasing full budget.

DECISION: NO-GO
