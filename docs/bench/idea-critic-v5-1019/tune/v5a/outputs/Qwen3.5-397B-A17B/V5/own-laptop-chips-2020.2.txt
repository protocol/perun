First call: GO. The company already possesses the core competency in silicon design for its mobile devices, suggesting a strategic opportunity to unify its architecture, improve margins, and control its roadmap, provided the software compatibility risk is managed.

**Steelman:** Leveraging proven mobile silicon expertise to build laptop processors will create a differentiated, integrated ecosystem that outperforms generic supplier options in efficiency and user experience, justifying the transition costs.

**Attack & Analysis:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" maintains status quo but cedes long-term differentiation to competitors who integrate vertically. Sticking with the supplier is safer short-term but limits innovation speed. The proposed path is the only one offering full stack control.
2.  **Fit:** This aligns with a north star of deep hardware-software integration, assuming the company values long-term ecosystem lock-in over short-term engineering ease.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if legacy software performance degrades significantly on the new architecture, causing enterprise churn. However, this is a known risk with established mitigation strategies (translation layers, developer kits).
4.  **Evidence:** The premise rests on the fact that the company *already* designs successful phone/tablet chips. This proves the design capability exists. The gap is scaling to laptop power envelopes, not basic competence.
5.  **Blocker Check:** No factual blocker exists. The risks (software compatibility, cost, supplier loss) are execution challenges, not impossibilities. The "unproven" nature of the chip is inherent to any new product launch and does not invalidate the strategic logic. The cost of delay is losing the window to define the next decade of computing architecture.

**Kill Criteria:** If a prototype cannot run core legacy applications at >80% native speed within 18 months, pivot to a hybrid approach. Leading metric: Developer adoption rate of new tooling by Q4 2021.

The proposal represents a classic "one-way door" strategic bet that leverages existing assets to capture future value. While expensive and risky, the alternative is commoditization. The risks are manageable engineering problems, not fundamental flaws in the thesis.

DECISION: GO
