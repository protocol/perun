First call: NO-GO. The proposal forces price-sensitive youth to buy a full data plan for a device with no apps, no games, and no app store, while the company's main platform launches months later, guaranteeing internal conflict and market irrelevance.

**Steelman:** Launching a restricted, carrier-locked social phone now captures the youth market before the main platform arrives, leveraging the acquired team's specific expertise despite higher costs.

**Attack:**
1.  **Economic Mismatch:** Requiring a full smartphone data plan for a device without apps or games creates a cost structure identical to a smartphone but with significantly lower utility. Price-sensitive youth will reject a "dumb" phone at "smart" prices.
2.  **Strategic Cannibalization:** Launching two niche devices months before the company's major platform release splits marketing focus and confuses carriers, likely depressing pre-orders for the flagship launch.
3.  **Product Void:** A "social phone" in 2010 without an app store or games offers no stickiness against competitors who are rapidly expanding ecosystems. It is a feature, not a platform.
4.  **Operational Friction:** The history of internal conflict between the core team and the acquisition suggests high execution risk, especially under a rushed timeline to beat the main launch.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Wait for the main platform launch, integrating social features there. Cost: $0. Risk: Delayed entry. Reversibility: High.
*   **Software Only:** Release the social network as a web app or future app on the main platform. Cost: Low. Risk: Dependency on main launch. Reversibility: High.
*   **Current Proposal:** High cost, high risk of failure, low reversibility once hardware is committed.

**Pre-mortem:** The devices launch, carriers complain about the expensive data requirement, youth ignore the device due to lack of games/apps, and the internal conflict causes missed deadlines, forcing a fire sale that undermines the main platform's upcoming launch.

**Blockers:**
*   **Unit Economics:** The cost of the data plan + device exceeds the willingness to pay of the target demographic for a device with zero third-party utility.
*   **Strategic Timing:** Launching a niche product immediately prior to a flagship platform launch guarantees resource contention and market confusion.

**Risks:** Internal team conflict; carrier resistance.

**Kill Criterion:** If carrier pre-orders do not cover 50% of hardware BOM costs within 30 days of announcement, cancel immediately.

DECISION: NO-GO
