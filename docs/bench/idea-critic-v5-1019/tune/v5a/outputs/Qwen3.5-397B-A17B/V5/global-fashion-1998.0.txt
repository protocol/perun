First call: NO-GO. Simultaneous global launch in 1998 with unproven demand and capital constraints is operationally reckless.

**Steelman:** Aggressively capture global market share before competitors by leveraging VC to build instant scale in fashion e-commerce across Europe and America.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Evidence:** In 1998, online fashion buying is explicitly "unproven." The proposal admits this yet commits to maximum complexity (multi-country, multi-office) immediately. This violates the basic startup principle of validating the core mechanism (selling clothes online) before scaling geography.
2.  **Better Way:** The "do nothing" alternative regarding global expansion is superior. A single-market pilot (e.g., UK or Germany) validates the logistics and customer appetite with a fraction of the burn rate. The proposal fails to compare against this cheaper, safer path.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 18 months. Cause: Venture capital ran out while logistics networks in five countries were bleeding cash, and no single market achieved profitability to sustain operations. The "running many countries at once" risk multiplies costs linearly while revenue remains uncertain.
4.  **Blocker Test:** The proposal states "venture money may run out before sales catch up." This is a stated fact within the proposal itself. Combined with "unproven" demand, this creates a definitive blocker: the plan commits to a fixed, high-burn cost structure (offices in several countries) without a validated revenue engine. The math does not work as proposed because the upside is hypothetical while the downside (cash exhaustion) is probable.
5.  **Kill Criterion:** If the plan proceeds, the kill criterion is "cash runway < 6 months without positive unit economics in at least one primary market." Since the proposal launches everywhere at once, it likely hits this wall simultaneously across all regions, leaving no fallback.

**Decision Logic:**
*   **Blockers:** Yes. The plan relies on unproven demand to sustain a maximalist cost structure, explicitly acknowledging the risk of capital exhaustion. A cheaper alternative (single market) exists that reaches the same learning upside with lower ruin risk.
*   **Risks:** Logistics complexity, marketing inefficiency, cultural misalignment.

The proposal fails the "beat do nothing" test. It bets the company on a hypothesis that should be tested cheaply.

DECISION: NO-GO
