First call: NO-GO. The core mechanism trades sensitive user data for ad revenue while relying on an opt-out model for consent, a pattern that historically triggers severe backlash.

Steelman: Sharing purchase data with friends increases ad relevance and viral reach, driving revenue, while notifications and an opt-out provide a minimal privacy safeguard.

Attack: The proposal fails the blocker test on its own stated facts.
1. **Incentive Mismatch:** Users treat purchases (especially sensitive ones) as private. An opt-out default assumes apathy, but privacy violations trigger active hostility, not passive acceptance. The "notification step" is a friction point that highlights the intrusion rather than mitigating it.
2. **Partner Risk:** Partner sites face embarrassment from public reactions to sensitive purchases appearing in feeds. They have a strong incentive to block this integration to protect their brand, undermining the "partner website" premise.
3. **Irreversibility:** The proposal admits the feature is "hard to withdraw." Launching a system that exposes sensitive data creates a permanent trust deficit. Once users feel betrayed, regaining trust is exponentially harder than gaining it.
4. **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves current trust levels. A strictly opt-in model (user explicitly chooses to share) captures willing users without alienating the majority, though it yields lower initial volume.

Pre-mortem: The feature launches, users discover sensitive purchases (e.g., medical, adult, financial) are visible to friends, media outlets frame it as a surveillance scandal, partners demand immediate removal to avoid liability, and the company faces regulatory scrutiny. The cost of delay is zero compared to the cost of a trust collapse.

Blockers:
- The default "share unless declined" model for sensitive data contradicts basic user expectations of privacy, creating an immediate, high-probability failure mode.
- The admitted difficulty of withdrawal means the risk cannot be capped; a mistake becomes permanent.

Risks: User churn, partner exodus, regulatory fines, brand destruction.

Kill Criterion: If >0.1% of users opt-out within 24 hours or any major partner threatens legal action, the feature must be killed immediately. However, given the irreversible nature and the sensitivity of the data, the only safe path is to not launch this specific mechanism.

DECISION: NO-GO
