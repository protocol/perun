First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a fixed public launch date and full-scale mass production while admitting unproven battery durability, no near-term demand signal, and cheap fuel removing the customer value proposition.

**Steelman:** This is a strategic preemptive strike to own the "future car" narrative and force regulatory alignment, betting that being first creates an insurmountable moat despite current economics.

**Attack:**
1.  **Economic Mismatch:** With cheap fuel and no mandates, the price premium for a hybrid cannot be justified by operating savings. The target market has no financial incentive to buy.
2.  **Technical Blocker:** Nickel-metal-hydride durability is unproven in volume. Committing to mass production before solving cycle-life failure modes invites catastrophic warranty costs that dwarf R&D savings. A public schedule removes the option to delay launch if testing fails.
3.  **Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "limited pilot" alternative is superior. A low-volume technical showcase (e.g., 500 units) validates the power-split transmission and battery life without exposing the corporation to mass-market warranty liability or locking capital into a product with no demand.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume it is 1998. The cars are sitting on lots because they cost 20% more than gas equivalents. Early adopters report battery failures after 15,000 miles. The company faces a massive recall or brand reputation hit for selling unreliable technology. The bold public schedule forced a launch before the bugs were fixed.

**Blockers:**
*   **Unproven Core Component:** Mass production commitment precedes battery durability validation. This is a factual gap in the readiness state that makes the "mass production" claim impossible to execute safely.
*   **No Demand Signal:** The proposal admits no market pull. Launching a premium-priced product with no economic benefit to the user violates basic product-market fit logic.

**Kill Criterion:** If battery cycle testing does not show <5% degradation after 1,000 equivalent cycles by Q4 1995, the mass production tooling spend must be halted. The current plan lacks this stop condition, committing to full scale regardless of test outcomes.

**Decision:** The plan fails the blocker test. It conflates "technical feasibility" with "commercial viability" and removes the safety valve of a pilot phase.

DECISION: NO-GO
