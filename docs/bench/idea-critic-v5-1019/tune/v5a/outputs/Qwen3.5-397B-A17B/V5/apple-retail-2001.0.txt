First call: NO-GO. A loss-making PC maker with single-digit share proposing high-fixed-cost retail in a low-margin sector during a recession violates basic unit economics.

**Steelman:** Direct retail is the only way to demonstrate the new digital-music strategy and stop third-party staff from pushing cheaper competitors, thereby saving the core business.

**Attack:**
1.  **Financial Suicide:** The company is already losing money. Adding prime mall leases (high fixed costs) to a low-margin hardware business creates massive operating leverage against a recession. One quarter of missed sales targets triggers insolvency.
2.  **Channel Warfare:** Opening stores directly competes with the existing reseller network. Resellers, already incentivized to push cheaper rivals, will now actively demonize the brand to protect their own showrooms. The company lacks the volume to replace lost reseller revenue with direct sales quickly enough.
3.  **Track Record:** The industry has a "poor retail track record." Entering a capital-intensive, operationally distinct business (retail management) while failing at the core business (manufacturing) is a classic diversification trap.
4.  **Better Way:** The "demonstration" goal can be achieved via low-cost kiosks inside existing partner stores or dedicated "shop-in-shop" zones, avoiding lease liability and channel conflict.

**Pre-mortem:** Two years later, the company is bankrupt. The stores drained cash flow during the downturn, resellers dropped the brand due to conflict, and the digital-music strategy failed because the hardware base shrank faster than the retail footprint could compensate.

**Blockers:**
*   **Cash Flow:** The proposal adds high fixed costs to a loss-making entity during a recession without a guaranteed immediate margin shift.
*   **Contradiction:** The plan relies on retail margins to fix losses, but admits computer retail has "low margins."
*   **Strategic Mismatch:** A manufacturing turnaround requires focus and cash conservation, not expansion into a notoriously difficult operational domain.

**Kill Criterion:** If the company cannot prove a path to positive cash flow within 12 months *without* new debt, this expansion is impossible. The current facts show losses; adding leases guarantees deeper losses.

The risk is not just failure; it is accelerated collapse. The "digital-music strategy" does not justify betting the entire remaining equity on a retail experiment that contradicts the company's financial reality.

DECISION: NO-GO
