First call: GO. The proposal targets a distinct, price-sensitive segment with a simpler, cheaper solution, a classic disruptor pattern where "good enough" beats "perfect" at a fraction of the cost.

Steelman: A randomized, low-cost robot captures the mass market by prioritizing affordability and convenience over complete coverage, rendering mapping features unnecessary for most users.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Cede the market to expensive mapping rivals; high risk of missing the volume opportunity.
2. **Build mapping tech:** Prohibitive cost and delay; likely failure given lack of experience.
3. **Launch low-cost random bot (Proposed):** Lowers barrier to entry, leverages existing simple tech, accepts coverage trade-off for price advantage.

Fit: Aligns with a strategy of market penetration via disruptive pricing. Assumes consumers value "automatic cleaning" more than "perfect cleaning."

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if random motion leaves visible debris clusters that trigger returns, or if the brand cannot overcome the "toy" perception. However, these are execution risks, not fundamental flaws. The door is two-way; a failed launch can be pivoted or halted with limited loss compared to developing mapping tech. Delay costs first-mover advantage in the budget segment.

Evidence: The lack of interest from floor-care companies is a positive signal; incumbents often reject disruptive, low-margin innovations that cannibalize their high-end models (the innovator's dilemma). Their rejection validates the disruptive nature of the proposal. The risks (no consumer experience, trust) are real but manageable through pricing, warranty, and iterative design, not reasons to kill the project.

Blockers: NONE. No fact presented proves the unit economics fail or that the target customer does not exist.
Risks: Brand perception, return rates due to coverage gaps, lack of distribution channels. These are mitigated by the low price point and the "good enough" value proposition.

Kill Criterion: If return rates exceed 15% due to performance complaints within the first 6 months, halt production and re-evaluate the motion algorithm. Leading metric: Units sold vs. returns in the first quarter.

DECISION: GO
