First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks to commit to full-scale manufacturing and a premium price point without evidence that buyers will pay extra for a bagless machine or that the inventor can overcome zero manufacturing history.

Steelman: A superior, bag-free technology justifies a premium price and can disrupt incumbents if direct-to-consumer channels bypass retail gatekeepers.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** License the technology to an incumbent for royalties (lower risk, lower upside).
2. **Pilot first:** Build a small batch to validate the "willingness to pay" hypothesis before founding a company.
3. **Proposed plan:** Found company, build factory, launch at premium immediately.

Fit: Assumes a market desire for durability/convenience over upfront cost. This is unverified.

Pre-mortem: The company fails because retail chains refuse to shelf an unknown, expensive brand, and direct sales costs exhaust capital before unit economics are proven. Incumbents release a cheap copycat while the startup is stuck with unsold inventory. This is a one-way door; capital spent on tooling and inventory is unrecoverable.

Evidence: The proposal explicitly states "buyers may not pay extra" and "no manufacturing record." These are not risks to be managed later; they are foundational gaps in the current plan. The cost disadvantage against established brands is a stated fact, not a speculation.

Blockers:
1. **Unproven Demand:** The core assumption (users will pay a premium for bagless) is listed as a risk, not a validated fact. Launching full-scale before testing this is a blocker.
2. **Capability Gap:** Zero manufacturing record combined with high capital requirements creates an execution blocker.
3. **No Stop Condition:** The plan commits to "found a company" and "manufacture" without a capped pilot or kill criterion to test the price sensitivity first.

Risks: Incumbent copying, distribution blockade, cash flow collapse.

Kill Check: If the plan proceeds as proposed, there is no mechanism to stop after spending $1M if only 100 units sell. The lack of a time-boxed, capped pilot to verify the price premium before full commitment is a fatal flaw. The proposal must be revised to include a small-batch market test with a specific sales threshold before founding the full company.

DECISION: NO-GO
