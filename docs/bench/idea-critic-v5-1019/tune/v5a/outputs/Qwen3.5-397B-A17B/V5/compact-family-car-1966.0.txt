First call: GO. The new plant requires volume to amortize; a dedicated compact model utilizes capacity while the existing small car line likely saturates its niche.

Steelman: A dedicated 1100 cc family car, sold via exclusive dealers, fills the gap between entry-level small cars and larger sedans, ensuring the new plant runs at efficient capacity to secure long-term profitability.

Attack:
1.  **Dealer Cost vs. Control:** Building a dedicated network is capital intensive. However, existing rivals' low-price dominance suggests the current channel may be commoditized. A dedicated network allows premium positioning and brand control, potentially yielding higher margins per unit to offset setup costs. If the network cannot be funded without cannibalizing the core business, this is a blocker; the proposal assumes capital availability.
2.  **Market Fit (1100 cc):** The risk of buyers wanting "cheaper or larger" is real. Yet, in 1966, the "family car" segment is the growth engine. An 1100 cc engine likely hits the sweet spot for tax brackets and fuel efficiency versus usability. If market data shows the 1100 cc segment is shrinking or the price point exceeds the "family" budget, the plan fails. The proposal lacks specific price elasticity data, but the strategic fit for a "new plant" implies a need for a high-volume, mid-tier product.
3.  **Plant Capacity:** The new plant *must* run near capacity. If this single model cannot generate sufficient volume alone, the plan relies on optimistic sales forecasts. However, introducing a new segment is the only way to generate *new* volume rather than fighting for share in the crowded "small car" market.

Pre-mortem: Sales miss targets by 20% because the dedicated dealer network limits geographic reach compared to rivals' established networks. The plant operates at 60% capacity, bleeding cash.
Door: Two-way. If sales lag, the dealer network can be merged with existing channels, and the engine size adjusted in future model years.
Delay Cost: High. Every month the plant sits underutilized increases the per-unit fixed cost, eroding competitiveness against established rivals.

Blockers: NONE. The risks are executional (sales volume, network build-out speed), not fundamental flaws in the logic of using a new plant for a new segment. The "cheaper or larger" risk is mitigated by targeting the specific "family" niche which often values features over rock-bottom price or maximum size.
Risks: Dealer network ROI, 1100 cc market acceptance.
Kill Criterion: If first-year sales do not reach 60% of the plant's dedicated capacity line, halt network expansion and pivot to shared dealerships.

DECISION: GO
