First call: GO. The marketplace model turns fixed inventory risk into variable fee revenue, critical for an unprofitable retailer facing cash constraints.

Steelman: Aggregating third-party supply creates the "everything store" selection customers want without capital expenditure, monetizing traffic through fees even when undercutting own retail prices.

Options analysis:
1. **Do nothing:** Retain control but cap growth at inventory capital limits; likely path to continued losses.
2. **Acquire competitors:** Prohibitive cost for an unprofitable entity; high integration risk.
3. **Marketplace (Proposed):** Sacrifices some retail margin and control to capture transaction fees and dominate selection. It beats "do nothing" by removing the capital ceiling on growth.

Fit: Aligns with the survival imperative of an unprofitable retailer needing asset-light scaling.

Pre-mortem & Door: Failure occurs if trust collapses due to rampant fraud or if suppliers boycott. This is a two-way door; bad sellers can be removed, and the program capped if metrics fail. Cost of delay is losing the network effect race to early movers.

Evidence & Math: The proposal relies on the assumption that fee revenue from increased volume outweighs lost retail margin on cannibalized sales. Given the "not yet profitable" status, the cash flow shift from buying inventory to collecting fees is mathematically superior for survival, provided take-rates cover operating costs.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (undercutting, quality, cannibalization, supplier objection, trust) are execution risks, not factual blockers. They are inherent to the model but do not prove the model fails as proposed.
- *Undercutting/Cannibalization:* Accepted trade-off for fee revenue and selection density.
- *Quality/Trust:* Managed via policy, not a reason to reject the strategy.
- *Supplier objection:* A political hurdle, not a logical impossibility.

Risks: Supplier retaliation (boycotts), brand dilution from bad actors, complexity in customer service.

Kill Criteria: If third-party defect rate exceeds 2% or customer trust scores drop >10% within 6 months, pause new seller onboarding. Leading metric: Gross Merchandise Value (GMV) from third parties vs. operating cost of marketplace division.

DECISION: GO
