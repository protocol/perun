First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a costly nationwide launch based on test market data that the proposal itself admits may overstate demand, while ignoring the core risk that the product's fundamental premise (clear cola) conflicts with established consumer expectations of flavor.

Steelman: A clear cola can successfully carve out a "purer" niche without destroying the flagship brand if the novelty converts to repeat purchases.

Attack: The proposal fails the "cheaper alternative" test. It jumps straight to a "large marketing campaign" and nationwide distribution. Given the admitted risks of non-repeat novelty buyers and potential cannibalization, the sound operator move is a staged regional rollout with a capped budget to verify repeat purchase rates before committing to national scale. The current plan lacks a stop condition; if novelty fades in month two, the company is locked into expensive national ad spend for a failing product.

The pre-mortem is clear: The launch happens, initial sales spike due to curiosity, then plummet as consumers reject the taste-flavor mismatch. Meanwhile, the flagship brand loses shelf space and focus to the extension. Rivals quickly copy the "clear" gimmick with lower costs, diluting the first-mover advantage. The cost of delay is low; waiting to confirm repeat behavior prevents a massive sunk cost in inventory and advertising.

Blockers:
1. No kill criterion: The proposal commits to full-scale launch without a defined metric to stop the bleed if repeat purchases fail to materialize.
2. Ignored cheaper alternative: A staged rollout is not considered, violating the principle of minimizing exposure to unproven demand.

Risks:
- Consumer rejection of taste-flavor dissonance.
- Cannibalization of the core revenue stream.
- Rapid competitive imitation eroding margins.

Kill Criterion: If repeat purchase rate in test markets (or a phased regional launch) is below 15% within 90 days, halt national expansion immediately.

DECISION: NO-GO
