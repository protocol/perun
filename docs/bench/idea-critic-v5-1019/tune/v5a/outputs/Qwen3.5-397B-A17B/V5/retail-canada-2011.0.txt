First call: NO-GO. The plan commits to a massive, irreversible capital outlay (130–150 stores) before validating the core assumption that the retailer's domestic value proposition translates to a market with different competitors, regulations, and price sensitivities.

**Steelman:** Rapid saturation creates immediate scale to amortize the high fixed costs of a new cross-border supply chain, preempting the entrenched competitor's response.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** The "do nothing" or "pilot" options are superior. Opening 5–10 stores first would test the bilingual labeling, logistics, and price perception risks with <10% of the capital exposure. The proposal offers no reason why learning must happen at maximum scale.
2.  **Fit & Pre-mortem:** Entering a market with higher prices on some goods contradicts the "discount" identity unless the assortment is radically different. If the new inventory system fails (a stated risk), 150 stores become 150 liabilities simultaneously. There is no "off-ramp"; the lease commitments lock the company into a failing strategy before the first data point arrives.
3.  **Evidence Gap:** The proposal admits the supply chain and systems are "not yet proven." Betting the entire international expansion on unproven infrastructure is not strategy; it is gambling. The entrenched competitor's dominance suggests high switching costs for customers, making a "blitzkrieg" opening less effective than a targeted proof-of-concept.

**Blockers:**
*   **Unproven Systems at Scale:** Deploying a new, unproven inventory system across 150 sites simultaneously creates a single point of failure that could cripple the entire expansion instantly.
*   **Lack of Reversibility:** Leasing 150 sites creates a massive fixed-cost burden that cannot be reversed if the "price perception" risk materializes.
*   **Missing Kill Criteria:** The plan lacks a pilot phase or observable metric to validate demand before full commitment.

**Kill Criterion:** A viable plan must cap the initial rollout to a number of stores (e.g., <20) sufficient to validate the supply chain and price elasticity, with a stop condition if same-store sales do not meet a specific threshold within 6 months. As proposed, the plan has no working stop condition.

DECISION: NO-GO
