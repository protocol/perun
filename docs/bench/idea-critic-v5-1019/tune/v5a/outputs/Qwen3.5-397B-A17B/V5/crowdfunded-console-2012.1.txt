First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on a developer ecosystem and supply chain execution that the team explicitly admits it cannot secure, while entering a market dominated by incumbents with deep pockets.

Steelman: A niche, open-software console can succeed by serving underserved hobbyists and indie developers ignored by major players, using crowdfunding to validate demand before mass production.

Options:
1. **Proposed Plan:** High risk. Requires simultaneous success in hardware manufacturing, developer adoption, and marketing with zero budget and no track record.
2. **Do Nothing:** Avoids certain capital loss; status quo remains.
3. **Software-First Alternative:** Build the open software platform for existing PCs/consoles first. Zero hardware risk, instant access to existing user bases, proves developer interest before touching supply chains.

Fit: Assuming the goal is "establish a new gaming platform," skipping the hardware barrier is more aligned with success than forcing it immediately.

Pre-mortem: The project fails because hardware delays drain the limited crowdfunding funds before shipping, leaving backers angry. Simultaneously, developers ignore the platform due to its tiny installed base, creating a dead ecosystem. This is a one-way door for the invested capital; reversibility is near zero once tooling is ordered.

Evidence: The proposal itself states the team has "no marketing budget" and "hardware delays are common." In 2012, the mobile market (iOS/Android) and established consoles (PS3/Xbox 360) created an incredibly high bar for new entrants. The claim that developers will build for an unproven, niche platform without financial incentive contradicts the stated risk that "developers may not build games."

Blockers:
1. **Economic Contradiction:** The plan requires developer adoption to succeed, yet admits developers are unlikely to build for it. No mechanism is proposed to solve this chicken-and-egg problem other than hope.
2. **Capability Gap:** The team acknowledges "hardware delays are common" but proposes a fixed one-year ship date with no buffer, no manufacturing experience, and no budget for expedited fixes. This guarantees failure to deliver.

Risks: Market saturation, lack of brand awareness, supply chain collapse.

Kill Criterion: If a working software prototype cannot attract 500 committed developers on existing hardware within 3 months, the hardware venture should not proceed.

The proposal asks to bet everything on the hardest part (hardware) before validating the easiest part (software interest), despite admitting fatal weaknesses in both areas.

DECISION: NO-GO
