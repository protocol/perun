First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a retailer with no developer platform experience to enter an unproven market against entrenched incumbents while investors are already hostile to its capital spending model.

**Steelman:** Leveraging existing spiky infrastructure to create a new high-margin revenue stream could amortize fixed costs and diversify beyond low-margin retail.

**Attack:**
1.  **Capability Gap:** Running internal infrastructure for spiky retail loads is fundamentally different from selling reliable, multi-tenant compute to developers. The latter requires billing systems, API governance, SLA enforcement, and 24/7 support teams the retailer does not possess.
2.  **Market Risk:** The market is explicitly "unproven." Entering first requires educating customers while burning cash, a luxury unavailable when investors are already questioning capital intensity.
3.  **Strategic Distraction:** Building a developer platform demands a culture shift incompatible with retail operations. Failure here could crater confidence in the core business, which remains the primary cash engine.
4.  **Competition:** Established hardware and software vendors have deeper pockets, existing developer relationships, and specialized sales channels. A retailer lacks brand credibility in this domain.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Focus on optimizing retail margins and proving the core model to calm investors.
*   *Partner:* License excess capacity to an existing provider rather than building a standalone business.
*   *Internal Only:* Optimize infrastructure strictly for retail efficiency without external sales complexity.

**Pre-mortem:** The launch fails because reliability slips during a retail peak, causing external clients to flee and generating negative press that scares retail customers. Investors pull funding due to compounded losses in both divisions.

**Blocker:** The combination of "unproven market," "no relevant experience," and "investor hostility to capital spend" creates a fatal alignment. The company cannot afford the experimentation period required to validate the market or build the necessary platform capabilities without jeopardizing the core retail survival. The risk of margin dilution and distraction is immediate; the upside is speculative and distant.

**Kill Criterion:** If the company cannot secure a dedicated team with prior platform experience without diverting retail leadership attention within 30 days, the initiative must stop. However, given the current constraints, the premise itself is flawed.

DECISION: NO-GO
