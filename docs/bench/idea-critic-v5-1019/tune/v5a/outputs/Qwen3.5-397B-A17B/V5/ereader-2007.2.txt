First call: GO. The proposal solves the core friction of digital books (need a PC, slow download) and aligns with the company's catalog strength, despite hardware inexperience.

**Steelman:** A dedicated, cellular-enabled device removes technical barriers for mass-market readers, creating a closed ecosystem that reassures publishers on piracy while driving high-volume, low-margin digital sales that complement rather than replace print.

**Attack & Options:**
1.  **Hardware Risk:** Valid, but "do nothing" cedes the emerging digital format to competitors. Building a walled garden is superior to licensing software to fragmented hardware makers.
2.  **Margin/Publisher Fear:** Low prices are necessary for adoption; the loss leader strategy (subsidizing hardware or margins) captures lifetime customer value. Piracy fears are mitigated by the closed ecosystem and cellular control.
3.  **Cannibalization:** Inevitable but preferable to losing the customer entirely to a third-party device. Print and digital often coexist; the goal is total share of reading time.
4.  **Past Failures:** Previous e-readers failed due to poor UX (slow, required PC) and limited content. This proposal directly addresses both with cellular connectivity and a massive catalog.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the device is too expensive, the screen is unreadable, or publishers withhold key titles. The "free cellular" cost could bleed cash if usage models are wrong.

**Blockers:** None found in the stated facts. The risks are executional, not fundamental flaws in the thesis. The "hardware inexperience" is a hurdle, not a blocker, given the clear value prop.

**Kill Criteria:** If publisher participation remains below 50% of top titles by launch, or if the unit cost exceeds the subsidized target by >20%, the project must pivot or halt. Leading metric: Units sold per active user per month.

The proposal correctly identifies that owning the reading experience is essential for a bookseller entering the digital age. The risks are manageable compared to the existential risk of irrelevance in a digital future.

DECISION: GO
