First call: GO. The friction of per-order fees directly suppresses repeat purchase frequency, which is the primary lever for lifetime value in retail. Removing this friction via a prepaid annual fee aligns customer incentives with the retailer's goal of frequency, despite the apparent margin risk.

**Steelman:** The $79 fee acts as a sunk-cost psychological trigger, compelling members to maximize utility by ordering more often, thereby increasing total gross profit dollars even if per-order margins decrease.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Competitors offering free shipping thresholds will capture high-frequency buyers; retention declines.
2.  **Free Shipping Thresholds:** Less effective than a membership at locking in loyalty; customers still hesitate at the margin for small orders.
3.  **Economics Risk:** The fear that heavy buyers will cause losses is valid only if the incremental gross profit from increased order volume and basket size does not exceed the shipping subsidy. However, the $79 upfront cash flow improves working capital immediately.
4.  **Reversibility:** While hard to reverse mid-year, the program can be discontinued for *renewals* if unit economics fail after a quarterly review. The "unproven" nature requires a pilot or strict monitoring, but waiting for perfect data cedes market share.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the attraction of "free shipping" brings in low-basket-size customers whose shipping costs exceed $79 annually without generating sufficient incremental profit.
**Kill Criterion:** If the average shipping cost per member exceeds $79 within the first 6 months without a corresponding 20% lift in gross profit dollars year-over-year, terminate renewals.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (margin hit, unproven economics) are inherent to the strategy's mechanism, not factual contradictions. The short-term margin hit is the intended investment to buy frequency.
**Risks:** Adverse selection of only heavy buyers; inability to raise prices later. These are managed via the kill criterion and pricing power.

The proposal correctly identifies the core friction and offers a standard, high-upside solution used successfully in the industry. The risks are manageable through monitoring, not reasons to halt.

DECISION: GO
