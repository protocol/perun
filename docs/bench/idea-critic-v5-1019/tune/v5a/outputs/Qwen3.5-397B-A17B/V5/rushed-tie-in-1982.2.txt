First call: NO-GO. The compression of a complex creative build into five weeks, coupled with an irreversible large-scale production commitment before any quality validation, presents a catastrophic failure mode that outweighs the seasonal opportunity.

**Steelman:** A single elite developer can replicate a hit film's essence in a minimal viable product within five weeks, and the brand's strength guarantees sales sufficient to clear a massive inventory run despite potential quality compromises.

**Attack:**
1.  **Capacity Mismatch:** Normal development cycles exist for a reason; reducing months of work to five weeks for one person forces the exclusion of core gameplay loops, bug fixing, and polish. The result will likely be unplayable or critically panned, damaging the brand rather than leveraging it.
2.  **Inventory Trap:** Ordering a "very large" run based on a hypothesis of quality that has not been tested is financial negligence. If the game is poor (highly probable given the timeline), the company is left with high fixed costs (license + manufacturing) and zero revenue, compounding the loss.
3.  **Single Point of Failure:** Relying on one programmer creates a critical bottleneck. Illness, technical debt, or scope creep immediately misses the Christmas window, rendering the inventory worthless.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Preserve capital, avoid brand damage, but lose the Christmas revenue window.
*   **Staged Release:** Build a minimal prototype in five weeks for internal review. If quality passes, authorize a smaller initial print run for Christmas, with re-orders planned for Q1. This caps downside risk.
*   **Delay:** Miss Christmas but deliver a quality product for the following year, preserving long-term franchise value.

**Pre-mortem:** The game ships in December, reviews are scathing due to bugs and lack of content, retailers refuse re-orders, and the company writes off the entire manufacturing cost plus the license fee. The "large run" becomes landfill.

**Blockers:** The proposal commits to full-scale manufacturing *before* verifying if the 5-week constraint allows for a shippable product. There is no kill criterion or pilot run to validate quality before the irreversible spend.

**Kill Criterion:** If a playable prototype is not reviewed and approved by internal stakeholders within 3 weeks, manufacturing must be capped at a minimal test batch (e.g., 5,000 units) rather than the proposed "very large" run.

DECISION: NO-GO
