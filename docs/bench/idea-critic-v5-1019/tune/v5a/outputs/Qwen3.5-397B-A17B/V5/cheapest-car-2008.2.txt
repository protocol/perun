First call: GO. The claim addresses a massive, verified market gap: families transitioning from two-wheelers to four-wheelers in an emerging economy.

Steelman: A dedicated, high-volume factory can achieve a ~$2,500 price point, creating a new automotive category for Indian families priced out of existing options.

**Options:**
1.  **Proposed Plan:** High risk (land protests, safety perception) but highest upside (category creation, volume dominance).
2.  **Do Nothing:** Cede the "first car" segment to used markets and scooters; miss the demographic shift.
3.  **Incremental Upgrade:** Modify an existing platform. Lower risk, but unlikely to hit the $2,500 psychological threshold required to convert scooter owners.

**Fit:** Aligns with the vision of motorizing the masses. The "cheapest" label is a double-edged sword: it drives volume but invites safety scrutiny.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if land protests delay the factory indefinitely, forcing production into a higher-cost interim facility that breaks the pricing model. Alternatively, a single high-profile safety incident cements the "cheap equals dangerous" narrative, stalling adoption despite the low price.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits the land conflict is active. This is a tangible threat to the "dedicated factory" requirement essential for the cost target. Safety concerns are hypothetical but plausible given the price constraint.

**Blockers:** NONE. The land protest is a risk to timeline, not a proof of impossibility. The safety concern is a marketing and engineering challenge, not a factual disqualifier. The existence of cheaper scooters does not negate the value proposition of a car for families.

**Risks:** Brand perception ("cheapest" = "unsafe"), land acquisition delays, margin erosion if volume targets slip.

**Kill Criterion:** If the factory site is not secured within 6 months, or if initial crash-test ratings fall below the legal minimum, halt the specific "cheapest" marketing angle and reposition as "value-safe."

**Decision:** The opportunity to define a new category outweighs the execution risks, provided the pricing holds. The risks are manageable through aggressive PR and legal resolution of land disputes.

DECISION: GO
