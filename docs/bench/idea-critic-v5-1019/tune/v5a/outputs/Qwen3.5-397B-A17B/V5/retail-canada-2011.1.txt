First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a massive, irreversible capital expenditure (130–150 leases) and operational complexity (new supply chain, new IT system) before proving the core assumption: that the retailer's domestic value proposition translates to a market with different competitors, regulations, and price sensitivities.

**Steelman:** Rapid saturation prevents competitor reaction and achieves immediate scale economies, leveraging existing capital to dominate a neighboring market where prices are currently higher.

**Attack:**
1.  **Blocker (Unproven Core Claim):** The plan assumes demand exists for this specific retailer's model in a market with an entrenched dominant player and different consumer habits. Opening ~140 stores simultaneously creates a "single point of failure" for the entire international strategy. If the value proposition fails in this specific context (e.g., local preferences differ, regulatory delays stall openings, or the incumbent retaliates aggressively), the company is left with massive fixed costs and no viable exit strategy. A sound operator tests the model with a pilot (e.g., 5–10 stores) before signing leases for 150.
2.  **Better Way:** Enter via a joint venture with a local partner to share risk and gain regulatory/logistical knowledge, or open a small cluster of flagship stores to validate the supply chain and brand fit before scaling. The "do nothing" or "slow roll" option preserves capital while gathering data.
3.  **Fit & Pre-mortem:** The strategy ignores the "liability of foreignness." Assuming failure: The new inventory system crashes under cross-border complexity, bilingual labeling causes legal fines, and the entrenched competitor engages in a price war the newcomer cannot sustain due to unoptimized logistics. The cost of delay (waiting a year to pilot) is negligible compared to the cost of a failed $100M+ expansion.
4.  **Evidence Gap:** No data is provided on local consumer testing, supply chain stress tests, or regulatory approval timelines. The "risks raised" in the prompt are admitted but not mitigated by the proposed "all-in" approach.

**Kill Criterion:** If a pilot of 5 stores does not achieve positive same-store sales growth within 12 months, the expansion halts. The current proposal lacks this stop condition, committing to full scale before validation.

**Verdict:** The absence of a pilot phase and the commitment to irreversible leases before validating the core market fit constitutes a blocker.

DECISION: NO-GO
