First call: NO-GO. The price is astronomical for an asset with zero revenue, massive liabilities, and no proven monetization path.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the dominant market leader immediately secures the category, eliminates a rival, and captures network effects that organic growth cannot match, justifying a premium for speed and monopoly potential.

**Options:**
1.  **Buy:** Costs $1.65B stock + unlimited legal/bandwidth liability. Upside: Market dominance. Reversibility: Near zero; integration destroys the target's culture, and stock spend is permanent.
2.  **Do Nothing/Build:** Cost is internal engineering + marketing. Risk: Losing the war for user attention. Reversibility: High; can pivot or stop spending.
3.  **License/Partner:** Cost is revenue share. Risk: Competitor buys them anyway. Reversibility: High.

**Fit:** Assumes "video is the future of search" is the north star. If true, owning the library is essential. However, the financial mechanics contradict survival.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-close, media giants file coordinated lawsuits demanding statutory damages exceeding the purchase price. Advertisers flee due to brand safety concerns around unmoderated content. Bandwidth costs double as traffic grows, burning cash faster than ads can replace it. The original founders leave, killing the innovation engine. This is a one-way door; once the stock is issued and the entity merged, the damage is irreversible.

**Evidence & Math:** The proposal admits "no meaningful revenue." At $1.65B, even if the site generated $50M annually (optimistic), the multiple is 33x revenue, unheard of for an unproven, loss-making model in 2006. The "copyright litigation" is not a risk; it is a certainty given the "large volume" of infringing content. The buyer's own smaller service proves the technical capability exists; the missing piece is content, but buying this specific legal time-bomb is not the only way to get it.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Financial Suicide:** Paying $1.65B for negative cash flow and undefined liabilities violates basic capital preservation.
2.  **Existential Legal Threat:** The core asset (user uploads) is legally toxic. Buying it imports the lawsuit directly into the buyer's balance sheet.
3.  **No Stop Condition:** The proposal commits to full acquisition without a pilot, cap, or mechanism to test ad viability or legal settlement costs first.

**Kill Criterion:** If legal counsel cannot quantify a maximum liability exposure below $500M prior to signing, the deal must die.

DECISION: NO-GO
