First call: GO. The removal of the headphone jack creates an immediate, forced market for a wireless alternative; doing nothing cedes this newly created demand to third parties.

Steelman: Launching a proprietary, seamlessly pairing wireless solution at $159 captures the entire installed base forced off wired audio, turning a hardware limitation into a high-margin ecosystem lock-in.

Alternatives considered:
1. Do nothing: Loses the accessory revenue and forces users to competitors' dongles or buds, weakening ecosystem stickiness.
2. Sell wired dongles only: Cheap, but fails to solve the "future of audio" narrative or capture high-margin recurring hardware revenue.
3. License tech to others: Dilutes the unique pairing experience and brand premium.

Fit: Aligns with a strategy of moving users to wireless ecosystems and increasing average revenue per user (ARPU) through high-margin accessories.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the "odd look" becomes a cultural joke that outweighs the convenience of automatic pairing, or if battery anxiety (5 hours) prevents daily adoption. However, the charging case mitigates the battery risk. The door is two-way; if sales flop, the company can revert to including wired buds or lowering prices later, though the jack removal itself is the primary one-way constraint.

Evidence: The proposal states the jack is being removed. This fact alone guarantees a baseline demand. The $159 price point is high compared to free wired ears, but justified by the convenience of auto-pairing and the lack of direct competition with the same integration level at launch.

Blockers: NONE. No factual contradiction exists between the jack removal and the need for a wireless solution. The risks (appearance, loss, price) are execution challenges, not fundamental flaws that prevent the product from working or selling to a captive audience.

Risks: Public ridicule of design; high attach-rate friction due to price; battery life perception. These are managed by the charging case and the inevitability of the jack's absence.

Kill criterion: If pre-orders or first-month attach rates fall below 15% of new phone sales, pause marketing spend and evaluate a price drop or bundled promotion. Leading metric: Attach rate of earbuds to new phone activations.

DECISION: GO
