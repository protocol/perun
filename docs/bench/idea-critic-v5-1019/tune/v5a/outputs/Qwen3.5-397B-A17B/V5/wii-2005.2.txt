First call: GO. The proposal targets an uncontested market segment (casual/non-gamers) by trading raw performance for accessibility and price, a valid blue-ocean strategy when trailing in a saturated high-fidelity race.

Steelman: By abandoning the expensive graphics arms race, the console can be sold at a profit immediately while undercutting competitors, using a novel interface to unlock a massive, ignored demographic rather than fighting for hardcore gamers.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Remain a distant third, squeezed by two giants fighting over the same shrinking hardcore slice; likely eventual exit from hardware.
2. Compete on power: Requires massive R&D spend with no guarantee of overtaking leaders; high risk of financial ruin.
3. Proposed path: Lower capex, unique value proposition, potential to expand the total market size.

Fit: Aligns with the goal of survival and growth by redefining the battlefield rather than losing on the current one.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the "toy" perception becomes absolute, preventing any software sales beyond the bundle, or if third-party developers completely ignore the platform, leaving a content vacuum. However, the low hardware cost mitigates financial bleed during this phase.

Evidence: The proposal relies on the assumption that non-gamers are priced out or intimidated by complex controllers, not just lack of interest. While unproven, the low entry price reduces the barrier to trial.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (skepticism, no HD, thin margin) are strategic trade-offs inherent to the differentiation, not factual contradictions. Thin margins are acceptable if volume compensates, which is the core hypothesis.

Risks: Third-party abandonment, niche categorization, controller fatigue.

Kill Criterion: If unit sales do not exceed the combined install base of the top two competitors within 18 months of launch, the "market expansion" hypothesis is false, and the strategy must pivot to cost-cutting or exit. Leading metric: Weekly active users among non-traditional demographics.

The plan survives the attack because the risks are manageable consequences of the strategy, not flaws that invalidate it. The potential upside (market creation) vastly outweighs the status quo (slow decline).

DECISION: GO
