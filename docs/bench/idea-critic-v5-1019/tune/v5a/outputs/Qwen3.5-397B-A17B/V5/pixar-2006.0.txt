First call: NO-GO. The $7.4 billion price tag for a studio producing one film annually creates an unsustainable unit economics profile that no near-term slate can justify, regardless of creative talent.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the studio is the only way to secure its creative pipeline and prevent a rival from gaining those assets, immediately revitalizing the conglomerate's struggling animation division by installing proven leadership.

**Attack:**
1.  **Valuation Blocker:** At ~$7.4B for one film a year, the acquisition cost per unit of output is astronomically higher than industry norms. Even with massive box-office hits, the payback period extends decades, violating basic capital allocation discipline. The "all-stock" deal dilutes existing shareholders massively for an unproven volume scale-up.
2.  **Key Person Risk:** The value is entirely concentrated in a few creative leaders and a founder who becomes a major shareholder. If they leave or underperform, the $7.4B asset becomes worthless shell. There is no industrial moat, only human capital that cannot be contractually guaranteed forever.
3.  **Culture Clash:** Integrating a hit-driven, creative-led studio into a large conglomerate historically stifles the very innovation being purchased. The risk of talent exodus post-acquisition is extreme.
4.  **Alternative:** A long-term distribution-only partnership or a minority stake with first-look rights secures the content flow without the catastrophic balance sheet risk of a full buyout at this multiple.

**Pre-mortem:** Two years post-deal, the creative leaders depart due to corporate interference, the film slate delays, and the stock price collapses under the weight of goodwill impairment. The founder uses their significant shareholding to block necessary restructuring.

**Blockers:**
*   **Economic Impossibility:** The revenue potential of one film per year cannot mathematically support a $7.4B valuation within a reasonable investment horizon.
*   **Concentration Risk:** Total dependence on non-transferable creative talent makes the asset too fragile for such a massive outlay.

**Risks:** Culture clash, integration failure, shareholder dilution.

**Kill Criterion:** If the studio cannot contractually guarantee a minimum release schedule of 2-3 films annually with the current leadership tied for 5+ years, the deal destroys value.

DECISION: NO-GO
