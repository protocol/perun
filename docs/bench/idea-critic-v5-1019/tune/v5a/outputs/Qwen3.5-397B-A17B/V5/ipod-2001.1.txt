First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on a tiny installed base, an unproven legal music supply, and a premium price in a market where cheaper devices have already failed to gain traction.

**Steelman:** A high-capacity, premium device could define a new category if it offers a vastly superior user experience that justifies the price and creates a halo effect for the company's core computer business, eventually expanding beyond the initial walled garden.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Avoid capital risk; let competitors fight over the low-margin, low-adoption market.
2.  **License software:** Build for Windows immediately to address the market size issue, sacrificing the integrated ecosystem hook.
3.  **Wait:** Delay until legal downloads are licensed and the market matures, risking being a follower.

**Fit:** This assumes a strategy of ecosystem lock-in and brand elevation rather than immediate unit volume. Without a stated North Star of "ecosystem expansion," this fails the immediate financial logic.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches, but users cannot legally fill the 5GB drive because licensing deals fall through. Sales stall at the early adopter phase. The high price alienates the small computer user base. The project drains resources from the core struggling computer line. It is a two-way door only if the hardware can be repurposed or sold cheaply later; otherwise, it is inventory write-down.

**Evidence & Blockers:**
*   **Blocker 1 (Market):** Cheaper players exist and have "not sold well." Adding a higher-priced option with a smaller compatible base contradicts current demand signals.
*   **Blocker 2 (Content):** The music industry has "not yet licensed legal downloads." A 5GB drive is useless without a streamlined way to fill it legally. The proposal admits this gap.
*   **Blocker 3 (Base):** "Small market share" + "only owners of its own computers" creates an addressable market too small to sustain component sourcing economies or justify the development risk.

**Kill Criteria:** If legal licensing is not secured before prototype completion, or if initial sales to the existing computer base do not exceed 10% attach rate within 6 months, the project must pivot to cross-platform support or cease.

The absence of a content pipeline and the restriction to a tiny hardware base are fatal flaws in the current plan.

DECISION: NO-GO
