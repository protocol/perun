First call: NO-GO. The plan contradicts the core behavior of the chain's existing revenue base without evidence they will adapt.

**Steelman:** Replacing a discount-dependent model with everyday low pricing (EDLP) and branded shops will modernize the brand, restore margins, and attract new shoppers, provided the current base follows or is replaced quickly enough to offset initial volume loss.

**Attack:**
1.  **Behavioral Mismatch:** The proposal admits most sales come from discounted items and customers are "trained to wait." Abruptly removing coupons removes the primary purchase trigger for 100% of the current revenue base. There is no bridge strategy.
2.  **Execution Risk:** The CEO's past success was in *electronics* (high velocity, different demographic), not mid-market apparel/home goods with an aging demographic. This is a category mismatch.
3.  **No Pilot:** Rolling out a total model shift across the entire chain simultaneously eliminates the ability to measure elasticity or refine the "branded shop" concept before burning capital. If the hypothesis is wrong, the chain collapses before correction is possible.
4.  **Cost of Delay vs. Cost of Error:** While delay loses time, the cost of error here is existential. A failed pilot costs limited capital; a failed chain-wide rollout costs the company.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Continued sales erosion, but preserves cash flow while a gradual transition is tested.
*   *Pilot:* Test EDLP in 10% of stores to validate if the existing base converts or if new traffic arrives before committing to a full rebuild.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** Most sales are discount-driven.
*   **Fact:** No pilot exists.
*   **Contradiction:** The plan relies on a customer behavior change (stopping coupon waiting) that the proposal itself identifies as deeply entrenched, yet offers no mechanism to verify if this change will occur before implementation.

**Kill Criterion Check:** The proposal commits to full scale immediately. If the customer base refuses to accept EDLP (highly probable given the facts), there is no stop condition; the chain will have alienated its only buyers before realizing the error. This lack of a capped, reversible first step is a fatal flaw.

**Decision:** The absence of a pilot for a strategy that directly attacks the known purchasing trigger of the entire customer base makes this an unjustifiable gamble.

DECISION: NO-GO
