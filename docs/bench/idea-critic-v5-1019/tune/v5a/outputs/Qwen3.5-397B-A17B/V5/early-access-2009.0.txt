First call: NO-GO. Releasing an unfinished, unsupported game to a paying public without marketing or a support plan invites immediate reputation damage and refund churn that a solo developer cannot survive.

**Steelman:** Early revenue funds development, and player feedback ensures market fit, turning customers into co-creators before the product is polished.

**Attack:**
1.  **Support Collapse:** A "large audience" (implied by public sale) creates a support ticket volume a solo dev cannot handle. Unanswered bugs lead to negative reviews, killing future sales. The proposal admits "cannot support a large audience" yet proceeds anyway.
2.  **Value Mismatch:** In 2009, "Early Access" as a trusted model did not exist. Players paying full or near-full price expect a complete experience. An "unfinished" state with no roadmap transparency looks like a scam, not a partnership.
3.  **Visibility Paradox:** With "no publisher or marketing budget," how does the audience find the game? Relying on organic discovery for a rough product is a failure strategy; it exposes the flaws to the few who find it without the volume needed to generate sustaining revenue.
4.  **Better Way:** Release a free, limited demo to gather feedback and build a mailing list. Use that validation to secure a publisher or small seed funding before asking for money. This tests demand without risking reputation or overwhelming support channels.

**Pre-mortem:** Three months post-launch, the store page is flooded with 1-star reviews citing game-breaking bugs and zero developer response. Sales halt completely. The developer is burned out from trying to answer hundreds of emails alone, and the game is dead on arrival.

**Blocker:** The explicit admission that the developer "cannot support a large audience" contradicts the action of selling to the public. Selling a product one cannot support is a fundamental breach of the transaction, guaranteeing failure.

**Kill Criterion:** If a free demo does not generate 1,000 active users and 200 constructive feedback entries within 30 days, do not attempt a paid release.

The risk of reputational destruction outweighs the potential for early cash flow given the lack of support infrastructure.

DECISION: NO-GO
