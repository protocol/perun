First call: GO. The company already possesses the core competency (chip design) in its mobile division, suggesting high feasibility for replication in laptops to capture margin and integration benefits.

**Steelman:** Leveraging proven mobile silicon architecture for laptops will unify the ecosystem, improve performance-per-watt, and eliminate supplier dependency, outweighing transition costs.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Maintain status quo; safe but cedes long-term margin and differentiation to competitors who integrate vertically.
2.  **Hybrid:** Run dual architectures; reduces risk but increases engineering complexity and confuses buyers (highest cost, lowest upside).
3.  **Full transition (Proposed):** High upfront engineering cost and software compatibility risk, but offers maximum long-term control, margin, and product cohesion.

**Fit:** Aligns with a strategy of vertical integration and ecosystem lock-in. Assumes mobile architecture scales to laptop workloads.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if legacy software emulation degrades performance below acceptable thresholds, causing pro users to churn. This is a two-way door technically (can revert to supplier), but brand damage is sticky. Cost of delay: Competitors integrate first, capturing the "efficient" narrative.

**Evidence:** Based on stated facts: company already designs phone/tablet chips. No data contradicts the scalability of this design to laptops.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (software compatibility, unproven status, cost, supplier loss) are execution challenges, not factual impossibilities. The proposal acknowledges them. No evidence suggests the mobile design *cannot* be adapted, only that it is unproven.

**Risks:** Software fragmentation during transition; temporary performance gaps; supplier retaliation.

**Kill Criteria:** If prototype benchmarks show <80% of supplier performance on core professional workflows by Q4 2021, halt mass production. Leading metric: App compatibility rate (%) at beta launch.

**Strongest Attack Survived:** The argument that "existing software must keep running" could block the move if emulation proves too costly. However, given the company's control over both OS and silicon, binary translation layers are a solvable engineering problem, not a hard blocker, as evidenced by their existing mobile/tablet unification.

**Dissent Ledger:** Transition will likely cause 12-18 months of customer confusion and elevated support tickets. Supplier relationship loss may limit short-term supply flexibility.

**Remaining Risk:** Niche enterprise software incompatibility driving segment churn.

DECISION: GO
