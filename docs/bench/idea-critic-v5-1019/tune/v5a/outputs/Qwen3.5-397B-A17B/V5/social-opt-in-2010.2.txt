First call: NO-GO. Automatic enrollment with default public visibility violates the core trust contract of an email service, where users expect confidentiality, not social broadcasting.

**Steelman:** Leveraging the existing user base and default settings is the only way to achieve critical mass quickly enough to compete with dedicated social networks, turning a utility into a platform before the window closes.

**Attack:**
1.  **Trust & Privacy:** Email is a private channel. Making connections visible by default treats user data as a social graph asset without consent. This is not a "risk"; it is a fundamental breach of the service's primary value proposition. The backlash would likely exceed acquisition gains.
2.  **Regulatory & Reversibility:** Automatic enrollment invites immediate regulatory scrutiny regarding consent and data usage. Once connections are exposed and users react negatively, the "hard to reverse" nature means the brand damage is permanent. You cannot un-ring the bell on privacy violations.
3.  **Usage Fit:** An inbox is for task completion (zero-inbox), not social browsing. Forcing social interaction into a productivity tool creates friction, likely causing users to abandon the email service entirely for competitors who respect boundaries.
4.  **Alternatives:** An opt-in model with strict default privacy (invisible until connected) sacrifices speed for safety. Given the catastrophic potential of the proposed default, the slower, consensual path is the only viable one. The proposed plan bets the company on a growth hack that alienates the entire existing customer base.

**Blocker:** The plan relies on violating user trust (automatic enrollment + visible defaults) to function. This contradicts the fundamental purpose of webmail. The stated risk of "users feeling privacy is disregarded" is an understatement; it is a guaranteed churn driver and legal liability. No kill criterion can fix a launch strategy predicated on non-consensual data exposure.

**Risks:** Regulatory fines, mass user exodus, brand destruction, low engagement due to context mismatch.

**Kill Criterion:** If >0.1% of users opt-out or complain within 24 hours of announcement, the project must terminate immediately. However, the proposal lacks an opt-in mechanism, making even this metric too late.

DECISION: NO-GO
