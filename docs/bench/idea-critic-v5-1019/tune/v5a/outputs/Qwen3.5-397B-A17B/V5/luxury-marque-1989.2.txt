First call: GO. The proposal attacks a clear market gap: luxury quality at a non-luxury price, leveraging existing engineering efficiency to undercut entrenched European rivals.

Steelman: A distinct brand identity, decoupled from the economy parent, can overcome badge prejudice if the product's objective quality matches or exceeds incumbents, making the price advantage an irresistible value proposition rather than a signal of cheapness.

Alternatives analysis:
1. Do nothing: Cede the high-margin luxury segment entirely to Europeans; remain capped at mid-market volumes.
2. Badge engineering: Sell a premium version under the main brand. High risk of brand dilution and failure to command luxury pricing due to "economy" associations.
3. Acquisition: Buy an existing luxury maker. Prohibitively expensive and integration-heavy compared to building on existing low-cost manufacturing DNA.

Fit: Moves the north star from "volume leader in small cars" to "full-spectrum manufacturer," capturing margin growth without altering the core brand's value promise.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the separate dealer network burns cash before volume scales, or if the single flagship model fails to convince buyers of the brand's breadth. However, these are execution risks, not fundamental flaws. The "thin line-up" is mitigated by focusing all marketing on one perfect entry point. The "separate network" cost is the price of admission for perceived exclusivity; sharing showrooms would doom the luxury perception immediately.

Evidence check: The proposal admits heavy sunk costs in development. Stopping now guarantees a total loss of that capital. Proceeding offers a path to ROI if the pricing strategy holds. The risk of buyers rejecting the brand is real but is the only variable; the product quality and price advantage are stated as facts.

Blockers: NONE. No stated fact proves the plan cannot work. The risks (brand perception, dealer cost) are inherent to the strategy but do not invalidate the core logic of undercutting incumbents on price while matching quality.

Risks: Brand stigma persists despite separation; dealer network profitability lags; single-model vulnerability.

Kill criterion: If first-year sales of the flagship sedan do not reach 60% of the break-even volume projected for the new dealer network within 18 months of launch, halt network expansion and pivot to shared premium showrooms.

DECISION: GO
