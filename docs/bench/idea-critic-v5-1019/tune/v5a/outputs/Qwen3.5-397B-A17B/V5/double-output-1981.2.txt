First call: NO-GO. Doubling production while sales lag by 60% during a recession with rising interest rates accelerates cash burn without addressing demand.

Steelman: The claim posits that flooding the market now secures future dominance or forces dealer acceptance, betting that economies of scale will lower unit costs enough to outlast the downturn.

Attack:
1.  **Financial Reality:** Producing 4,500 additional unsold units requires upfront capital for parts and labor. With high interest rates, borrowing costs are prohibitive. With only 3,000 sold out of 7,500 produced, cash conversion is broken. Doubling output doubles the cash drain immediately, likely triggering insolvency before the economy recovers.
2.  **Channel Capacity:** Dealers are already struggling to finance existing stock. Forcing more inventory onto them risks dealer bankruptcies, destroying the distribution network needed when demand returns.
3.  **Demand Mismatch:** The core constraint is lack of buyers, not lack of cars. Increasing supply when demand is contracting due to macroeconomic factors (recession, rates) creates a larger, more expensive liability pile.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves remaining cash. Reducing production aligns burn with sales. A cheaper alternative is incentivizing current stock clearance rather than building new liabilities.

Pre-mortem: The company runs out of cash in six months. Warehouses are full of depreciating assets. Dealers refuse further deliveries. The company enters bankruptcy with double the inventory overhead and no liquidity.

Blockers:
-   **Cash Flow:** The proposal requires significant immediate capital expenditure while revenue is stagnant; the company has "limited cash." This mathematically guarantees faster insolvency.
-   **Distribution Break:** Dealers cannot finance more stock; forcing it breaks the sales channel.

Risks:
-   Inventory depreciation.
-   Increased storage costs.
-   Brand damage from distressed dealer networks.

Kill Criterion: If cash reserves drop below 3 months of operating expenses at the current burn rate, production must halt immediately. The leading metric is "Days Cash on Hand," reviewed weekly.

This plan ignores the binding constraint (demand/cash) and exacerbates the fatal flaw (inventory overhang). It is financially suicidal under the stated conditions.

DECISION: NO-GO
