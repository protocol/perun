First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to solve a software ecosystem deficit with a hardware acquisition, misaligning the remedy with the core failure mode.

Steelman: Acquiring the struggling manufacturer secures the sole remaining hardware partner and prevents immediate defection to the rival platform, buying time to fix the app gap.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Manufacturer likely defects; OS share collapses to near zero immediately.
2. **Subsidize developers:** Directly attack the app gap without hardware baggage; lower integration risk, but no guarantee of stopping the partner's defection.
3. **Acquire (Proposed):** Stops defection but inherits falling sales, massive integration drag, and alienates other partners who may fear being next or disadvantaged.

Fit: The goal is ecosystem survival. However, owning a shrinking hardware division does not inherently attract developers; developers follow users and revenue, not corporate ownership structures. If the devices continue to sell poorly under new management, the app gap widens regardless of who owns the factory.

Pre-mortem: Two years post-acquisition, the company is bogged down in restructuring a large, unprofitable workforce. Other handset makers, fearing competition or loss of neutrality, abandon the OS for the rival platform. The app gap remains because the total installed base continues to shrink. The €5.4 billion is sunk, leaving less capital to incentivize developers.

Evidence & Math: The handset maker's sales are already falling. Paying a premium (€5.4B) for a declining asset to solve a non-hardware problem (app availability) contradicts basic turnaround logic. The market leader's dominance is driven by network effects (apps/users), which hardware ownership alone cannot replicate.

Blockers:
1. **Wrong Lever:** The primary blocker is the mismatch between the problem (lack of apps) and the solution (buying hardware). Ownership does not fix the developer incentive structure if user volume remains low.
2. **Partner Alienation:** Acquiring one manufacturer signals to other partners that the OS owner is now a competitor, likely accelerating their exit to the rival platform, shrinking the ecosystem further.

Risks: Integration failure, continued sales decline, capital depletion.

Kill Criterion: If total OS market share does not stabilize within 12 months post-acquisition, the strategy has failed to arrest the bleed.

DECISION: NO-GO
