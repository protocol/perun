First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits a dominant running brand to an unproven rookie with a budget-breaking sum, relying on rule-breaking colorways that invite immediate league sanctions, while historical data suggests signature lines rarely survive beyond a season.

**Steelman:** Secure a generational talent before rivals do, using controversy (rule-breaking colors) to generate free media attention that offsets the high cost, creating a cultural shift rather than just selling shoes.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Maintain status quo; likely continue losing basketball share to established players.
2.  **Sign established star:** Lower risk, but prohibitively expensive and less cultural upside; likely yields standard sales, not a movement.
3.  **Proposed Plan:** Highest risk/reward. Beats "do nothing" only if the player becomes a superstar and the ban creates demand.

**Fit:** Assumes the brand needs a cultural disruption to enter basketball, not just product placement.

**Pre-mortem:** The player suffers a career-ending injury in game one; the league bans the shoes immediately without public outcry; the budget is exhausted with zero ROI. The door is one-way (five-year commitment); delay costs the chance to sign this specific rookie.

**Evidence:** All critical claims are `assumed`. There is no evidence this specific rookie will succeed, no data confirming fines will generate profit, and no proof the budget can absorb total loss. The "rarely sold beyond a season" statistic directly contradicts the five-year commitment.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Financial Ruin:** The cost is a "large share of the marketing budget." If the player fails or is injured (high probability for rookies), the brand loses its entire basketball marketing capability for five years.
2.  **Regulatory Stop:** The plan relies on breaking uniform rules. If the league bans the shoes effectively (confiscation, refusal to allow play), the product cannot be sold or worn, rendering the inventory worthless.
3.  **Historical Precedent:** The proposal admits signature shoes rarely last a season; a five-year deal ignores this baseline failure rate without a mitigation strategy.

**Risks:** Injury, obscurity, brand dilution.

**Kill Criterion:** If the player does not start 50% of games in Year 1, terminate endorsement.

The proposal lacks a cap on financial exposure and relies on speculative regulatory rebellion. As proposed, it threatens the company's stability on an unverified asset.

DECISION: NO-GO
