First call: GO. The proposal secures the franchise's survival against an inevitable shift to digital distribution, even if the initial execution is flawed.

**Steelman:** Offering free, limited streaming now acts as a defensive moat, retaining subscribers who might otherwise churn when competitors launch superior services, while gathering critical data on user behavior at minimal marginal cost since the core subscriber base already pays for DVDs.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Preserves short-term DVD margins but cedes the digital future to tech giants, likely causing rapid obsolescence once broadband penetration crosses a tipping point.
*   **Paid Standalone Streaming:** Fails because the catalog is too weak to command a new price point, and friction reduces adoption.
*   **Wait for Better Tech/Content:** Suicidal; by the time quality matches consumer expectations, brand relevance will be lost to first-movers.

**Pre-mortem:** The initiative fails if licensing costs spiral before user engagement validates the model, or if DVD revenue collapses faster than streaming value is perceived. However, the "no extra charge" model mitigates immediate price resistance.

**Evidence & Constraints:** The proposal admits high licensing costs and poor quality. These are not blockers but known constraints of an early-stage market. The small catalog is a feature, not a bug, for a low-cost entry trial. Cannibalization is a strategic necessity; it is better to cannibalize your own profitable business than have a competitor do it for you.

**Blockers:** NONE. The stated risks (cost, quality, competition) are industry conditions, not fatal flaws in the specific tactic of bundling free access for existing users. The math works because the marginal cost of streaming to an existing paying subscriber is near zero, while the retention value is high.

**Kill Criteria:** If DVD revenue declines by more than 15% year-over-year without a corresponding increase in total subscriber retention or lifetime value within 18 months, the strategy must pivot to a tiered pricing model or content acquisition overhaul.

**Decision:** The cost of delay exceeds the cost of a flawed launch. This is a two-way door; the service can be restricted or priced later, but it cannot be launched instantly if delayed today.

DECISION: GO
