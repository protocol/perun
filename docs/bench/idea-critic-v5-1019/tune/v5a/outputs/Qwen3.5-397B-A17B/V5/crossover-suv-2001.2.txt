First call: NO-GO. The proposal admits the core value driver (styling) failed validation and the underlying economics are compromised by platform age and cost-cutting.

Steelman: Launching a budget-friendly, utility-focused crossover on an existing minivan platform can capture a niche youth/camping market if the distinctive angular design resonates and break-even volume (30k) is achievable despite platform limitations.

Attack:
1.  **Demand Failure:** Early customer clinics explicitly rated the styling poorly. Since the "distinctive angular body" is the primary differentiator for a "youthful" demographic, negative validation here is a fatal blocker. You cannot sell a style-led product to style-conscious buyers who reject the style.
2.  **Platform Mismatch:** An aging minivan platform contradicts the "crossover/SUV" positioning, especially with raised risks about "no real off-road ability." If the target expects any ruggedness (implied by camping options), a compromised minivan chassis offers neither the utility of a van nor the capability of an SUV.
3.  **Economic Fragility:** Cost cuts during development that "compromised the design" suggest the product is already degraded before launch. Achieving 30,000 sales requires a compelling value proposition; a poorly styled, compromised vehicle on an old platform is unlikely to hit this volume, making the break-even target unreachable.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves capital. A cheaper alternative is to refresh the existing minivan or wait for a dedicated platform. The current plan beats neither.

Blockers:
*   **Validated Style Rejection:** Customer clinics rejected the core aesthetic required for the target demographic.
*   **Compromised Integrity:** admitted design compromises due to cost-cutting on an aging platform.

Risks:
*   Missing the 30k sales target due to weak product-market fit.
*   Brand damage from launching a perceived "cheap" or "failed" design.

The proposal contains internal contradictions: it relies on distinctive styling that was tested and rejected, and promises a crossover experience on a platform admitted to lack capability. The risks are not theoretical; the negative evidence (clinic results, design compromise) is already in hand. There is no kill criterion that can fix a product whose fundamental appeal was invalidated before production.

DECISION: NO-GO
