First call: NO-GO. The proposal combines a premium price with a smaller app store, carrier exclusivity, and unproven 3D hardware in a saturated two-platform market, creating a fundamental mismatch between cost and value.

Steelman: A unique 3D interface and instant product identification could carve a niche for shoppers, justifying a flagship price despite the app gap.

Attack: The "app gap" is a fatal blocker for a device priced alongside market leaders. Consumers paying flagship prices expect access to mainstream applications; a forked OS without them renders the device non-competitive regardless of novel hardware. The "one-touch" shopping feature is a single-use case that cannot offset the daily utility loss of missing core apps. Furthermore, carrier exclusivity severely limits the addressable market for a niche product, while the unsubsidized price point removes the primary incentive for carrier-driven adoption. The 3D display adds cost and complexity without proven consumer demand, repeating historical failures of gimmicky display technologies.

Alternatives: Doing nothing preserves capital. A cheaper tablet integrating the shopping camera feature leverages existing success without entering the hyper-competitive, high-churn smartphone arena. A software-only shopping solution for existing iOS/Android devices reaches the entire market immediately.

Pre-mortem: The device launches to poor reviews citing the lack of apps; sales stall within three months due to the high price and limited carrier reach; inventory is written off. This is a one-way door with high financial loss.

Evidence: The proposal admits the market is saturated and dominated by two platforms. It explicitly states the device lacks mainstream apps yet charges flagship prices. History shows that hardware novelty alone (3D) fails to overcome ecosystem deficits at premium price points.

Blockers:
1. Pricing a device with a deficient app ecosystem at flagship levels contradicts basic market dynamics.
2. Carrier exclusivity caps growth potential for a product that already lacks broad appeal.

Risks: High manufacturing costs for 3D hardware; consumer rejection of head-tracking interfaces.

Kill Criterion: If pre-orders do not reach 50,000 units in the first month, halt production immediately.

DECISION: NO-GO
