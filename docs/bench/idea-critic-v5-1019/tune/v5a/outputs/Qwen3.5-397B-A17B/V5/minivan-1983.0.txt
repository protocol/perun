First call: GO. The proposal leverages existing assets (compact platform, converted factory) to solve a specific customer constraint (garage fit) while addressing a survival-level need for cash generation.

**Steelman:** A minimal-capital entry into an uncontested space by repositioning a car platform as a family hauler, avoiding commercial van stigma through car-like driving dynamics.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Rely on station wagons; risk continued decline as family needs outgrow current offerings.
2.  **New large van:** Prohibitive cost; fails garage constraint; competes directly with entrenched rivals.
3.  **Proposed mini-van:** Low capex via conversion; creates a new category; utilizes idle capacity.

**Fit:** Aligns with survival stage and capital constraints. Reuses known technology to minimize technical risk.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if marketing fails to distinguish the vehicle from commercial work vans, or if the compact platform proves too narrow for three-row seating. However, the "car-like" driving claim directly counters the commercial perception.

**Evidence:** The government guarantee provides the necessary runway. The "no category exists" risk is actually an opportunity for first-mover advantage. The "rivals could copy" risk is mitigated by the time required for them to retool, granting a crucial window of exclusivity.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (no category, cash poor, copycats, platform limits) are inherent to disruptive innovation in a turnaround scenario, not factual contradictions. The platform limit is a trade-off for garage compatibility, not a failure mode. The lack of cash makes the low-cost conversion strategy the *only* viable path, not a reason to stop.

**Kill Criteria:** If pre-orders or early clinic testing show >60% of respondents perceive the vehicle as strictly "commercial/work" rather than "family," pivot marketing or halt. Monitor production yield at the converted factory; if it exceeds 150% of the compact car's build hours, the cost advantage evaporates.

**Decision:** The proposal turns constraints (low cash, small platform) into advantages (low cost, garage fit). It beats "do nothing" by creating a market rather than fighting for share.

DECISION: GO
