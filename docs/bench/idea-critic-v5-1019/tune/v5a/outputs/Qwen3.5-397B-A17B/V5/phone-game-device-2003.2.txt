First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to enter a mature, specialist-dominated market with a hybrid device that compromises the core utility of a phone while failing to match the competitor's price or library.

**Steelman:** A converged device leverages existing mobile distribution to offer casual gaming at a premium price point, betting on convenience over dedicated performance.

**Attack:**
1.  **Usability Blocker:** The requirement to remove the battery to change games is a fundamental design failure. It creates friction incompatible with casual play and risks data loss or hardware damage, rendering the "game console" value proposition broken.
2.  **Economic Mismatch:** At $300, the device is priced significantly above the market leader's dedicated handheld. Consumers will not pay a premium for a compromised phone and an inferior gaming ecosystem (fewer titles, awkward form factor).
3.  **Form Factor Conflict:** A device "awkward to hold for calls" fails its primary function. Alienating the existing mobile user base to chase uncommitted game publishers is a strategic error.
4.  **Content Gap:** Without a committed library, the hardware is a shell. The market leader's moat is content; this proposal offers neither exclusive content nor a better way to access existing content.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Avoid brand damage from a flawed hybrid.
*   *Software Only:* Partner with the market leader to put games on existing phones (lower risk, immediate library).
*   *Dedicated Device:* Build a proper console later, once battery/card tech improves.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches, reviewers pan the battery-swap mechanic, gamers reject the high price for limited titles, and phone users complain about ergonomics. Sales flatline; the brand is associated with "gimmick" rather than innovation.

**Blockers:**
*   **Functional Defect:** Removing the battery to swap media is an unacceptable user experience that invalidates the product category.
*   **Price/Value Disconnect:** Higher price than the superior, dedicated alternative with no compensating advantage.

**Risks:** Publisher apathy, ergonomic rejection.

**Kill Criterion:** If user testing shows >50% of users refuse to swap a game card due to the battery removal step, the project must terminate immediately.

The proposal fails on basic ergonomics and economic logic. It solves no user problem effectively and introduces severe friction.

DECISION: NO-GO
