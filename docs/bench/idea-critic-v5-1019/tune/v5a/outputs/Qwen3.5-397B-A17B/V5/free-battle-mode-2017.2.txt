First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to enter a saturated market with an unproven business model and insufficient development time, directly contradicting the studio's core competencies.

**Steelman:** Reusing assets allows a rapid, low-cost entry into the popular last-player-standing genre to capture users via a free-to-play model, diversifying revenue beyond the initial paid sale.

**Attack:**
1.  **Capability Mismatch:** The studio has zero experience running a free-to-play economy. Designing cosmetic loops that drive revenue without "pay-to-win" accusations requires specific expertise the team lacks. Building this in two months is operationally impossible while ensuring quality.
2.  **Market Reality:** A rival already dominates the genre. Entering as a late, unpolished clone from a paid-game studio offers no unique value proposition to displace the incumbent.
3.  **Cannibalization:** Releasing a free version of the core gameplay loop directly devalues the paid product. Without a distinct separation of content or a proven retention strategy, this risks eroding the existing revenue base rather than expanding it.
4.  **Timeline Delusion:** Two months is insufficient to build, balance, and launch a stable multiplayer mode, let alone integrate a secure monetization backend and anti-cheat systems required for a competitive shooter.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Protect the paid game's value and reputation; avoid sinking costs into a likely failure.
*   **Pilot/Internal Test:** Spend two months prototyping the economy internally to validate feasibility before committing to a public release.
*   **Partnership:** License the mode to a studio with F2P expertise rather than building it in-house.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** The team has no prior F2P experience.
*   **Fact:** The timeline (2 months) is inadequate for the scope (new genre + new business model).
*   **Fact:** Direct competition with an entrenched rival without a differentiator is a losing strategy.

**Risks:** Brand damage, loss of paid sales, wasted development resources, technical instability at launch.

**Kill Criterion:** If a prototype cannot demonstrate a viable cosmetic revenue projection within 4 weeks, the project must terminate. However, given the current proposal lacks even a prototype phase, the risk is immediate.

The proposal fails the "capability" and "timeline" tests based on stated facts. It asks the studio to execute a complex pivot in a timeframe that guarantees technical debt and a poor product, against a competitor who defines the category.

DECISION: NO-GO
