First call: NO-GO. A search-advertising firm with no hardware DNA proposing a $50M acquisition of a six-person team to build an OS in a carrier-dominated market is a strategic mismatch with no near-term revenue path.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the team secures a free, open-source mobile platform to accelerate mobile web adoption, directly expanding the search inventory and ad revenue base before competitors lock down the ecosystem.

**Options:**
1.  **Acquire ($50M+):** High cost, extreme execution risk, relies on convincing hostile carriers/handset makers to adopt a new OS. Payback is multi-year and indirect.
2.  **Partner/License:** Lower cost, but offers less control over the stack and slower integration with search services.
3.  **Do Nothing:** Preserve capital; wait for the market to mature or for a handset maker to demand a neutral OS.

**Fit:** Theoretically aligns with "organizing the world's information" on mobile, but assumes the company can successfully execute a platform strategy outside its core competency (ads/software services) against entrenched telecom gatekeepers.

**Pre-mortem:** The project fails because handset makers refuse to adopt an OS backed by an ad company (fearing data privacy issues or brand dilution), carriers block non-standard devices, and the $50M burns away with no market share gained after 3 years. This is a **one-way door**; the capital and focus are lost.

**Evidence & Blockers:**
*   **Blocker:** The proposal admits **no revenue model** for the OS itself and relies entirely on a speculative future increase in search volume.
*   **Blocker:** The market structure (carrier/handset control) creates a distribution bottleneck that a tiny team and an ad company cannot easily break.
*   **Blocker:** The cost ($50M for 6 people) is astronomical relative to current traction, valuing potential over proof.
*   **Kill Criterion:** If no major handset maker commits to shipping the OS within 18 months, the bet fails. The proposal lacks a capped pilot or a verified path to distribution.

This is a high-risk platform bet disguised as an acquisition, lacking a validated route to market or a direct revenue mechanism. The absence of a hardware strategy and the hostility of the distribution channel make this a "no-go" as proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
