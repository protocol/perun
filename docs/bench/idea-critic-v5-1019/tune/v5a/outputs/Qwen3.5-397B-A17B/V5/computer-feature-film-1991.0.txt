First call: GO. The offer solves the company's two hardest problems—capital for unproven tech and access to distribution—while the "toy" concept mitigates narrative risk by limiting scope to a manageable, relatable world.

**Steelman:** Partnering allows the company to leverage its unique technical moat to create a new market category (all-CG feature) that it could never fund or distribute alone, using a contained story to prove the medium's viability.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Remain a short-film/commercial shop; risk being outpaced as tech commoditizes or key talent leaves for larger studios.
2.  **Self-fund:** Impossible given rendering costs and lack of feature-production cash flow; high probability of bankruptcy before completion.
3.  **The Deal:** Accepts lower margin per unit for guaranteed production capacity and global reach. It beats "do nothing" by creating an asset (the film) that defines the category.

**Fit:** Aligns with the north star of proving computer animation as a viable feature medium. The studio partnership is the only bridge from "shorts" to "features."

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the rendering pipeline cannot scale to 80 minutes within budget, or if the "toy" story feels too small for a theatrical runtime. However, the studio partner absorbs the distribution risk, and the contained setting actually aids rendering efficiency compared to human crowds or complex environments. This is a two-way door for the industry (if it fails, CG features are delayed; if it succeeds, a monopoly is formed), but a one-way door for this specific company's trajectory.

**Evidence:** The lead animator was targeted by a competitor, proving high market value for the specific skill set. The studio's offer validates confidence in the tech despite the lack of precedents.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (inexperience, cost, partner leverage) are inherent to the opportunity, not facts that make the plan impossible. The partner's strong hand is the price of admission for distribution.
**Risks:** Production bottlenecks; creative friction with the publisher. These are managed via the production deal structure, not reasons to reject the opportunity.

DECISION: GO
