First call: GO. The friction of per-order fees directly suppresses repeat purchase frequency, which is the primary lever for lifetime value in retail.

**Steelman:** An annual prepaid fee ($79) eliminates marginal shipping friction, locking in loyalty and increasing order frequency enough to offset the margin hit via volume and upfront cash flow.

**Attack & Options:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Retain margin per order but cap frequency due to shipping friction. Competitors offering frictionless shipping will capture high-value customers.
*   **Alternative (Free Shipping Threshold):** Requires larger basket sizes, potentially deterring small, frequent top-up orders. Less effective at locking in annual loyalty than an upfront sunk cost.
*   **Pre-mortem Failure:** Heavy users drive logistics costs above $79/year before generating sufficient incremental gross profit. The program becomes a loss leader with no exit strategy due to customer backlash on reversal.
*   **Door Type:** Two-way door. While reputationally sensitive, the program can be modified for future renewals or eligibility criteria adjusted if unit economics fail.

**Evidence & Verification:**
The proposal admits economics are "unproven." Relying on the assumption that increased frequency will offset costs is a gamble without a pilot. However, the strategic fit for an online retailer aiming to dominate repeat purchases is sound. The risk of heavy buyers is real but is the necessary cost of acquiring the most valuable segment.

**Blockers vs. Risks:**
*   **Blockers:** NONE. The lack of proven economics is a risk, not a factual contradiction of the core claim (that friction reduces repeats).
*   **Risks:** Margin erosion from over-usage; inability to reverse publicly; unproven elasticity of demand.

**Kill Criteria:**
To proceed safely, the launch must be capped.
*   **Metric:** If the average shipping cost per member exceeds $95 within the first 6 months, the program fails.
*   **Action:** Pause new enrollments and renegotiate carrier rates or adjust eligibility.
*   **Leading Metric:** Repeat order frequency per member vs. non-member cohort.

The proposal identifies the correct problem (friction) and a potent solution (prepaid removal). The risks are manageable via monitoring, not reasons to block. The potential upside of locking in annual loyalty outweighs the unproven nature of the specific price point, which can be tuned.

DECISION: GO
