First call: GO. The proposal avoids a direct hardware war against a superior graphics rival by pivoting to a unique form factor (dual-screen, touch) that targets an underserved demographic (broad audience) at a lower price point, while mitigating risk by maintaining the existing profitable product line.

**Steelman:** Differentiation via novel interaction (touch/dual-screen) and price creates a new market segment rather than fighting for the same core gamers on graphical fidelity, leveraging the installed base as a safety net.

**Alternatives:**
1.  *Do nothing:* Cede the high-end market to the rival; likely erosion of core share over time.
2.  *Match hardware:* Enter a costlier arms race against a well-funded rival with superior media features; high risk of being the "also-ran" with worse specs.
3.  *Proposed:* Lower price and unique gameplay potential offer a defensible niche.

**Fit:** Aligns with broadening the audience beyond core players, a necessary move when core hardware supremacy is threatened.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if developers cannot easily adapt to two screens or if the touch interface is seen as a toy rather than a tool. However, the "gimmick" risk is mitigated by the lower price point and the retention of the older handheld for traditionalists. The cost of delay is high; waiting allows the rival to solidify dominance.

**Evidence:** The proposal relies on the strategic logic of asymmetric competition. The rival's strength (graphics) is the proposer's weakness; the proposer's strength (portfolio depth, cost control) becomes the vector of attack.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (developer confusion, unproven touch market) are execution challenges, not factual contradictions. The plan explicitly addresses the cost risk by targeting a lower price and keeping the old unit on sale to hedge bets. No internal contradiction exists in the stated facts.

**Risks:** Developer adoption speed; consumer perception of "gimmick." These are managed by the dual-product strategy and price positioning.

**Kill Criterion:** If pre-launch developer kits do not yield at least three viable flagship titles demonstrating dual-screen utility within 6 months, reconsider the software subsidy strategy.

**Decision:** The plan successfully turns a hardware disadvantage into a market differentiation opportunity without sacrificing the current revenue stream.

DECISION: GO
