First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on a cyclical peak and ignores fatal cultural friction.

**Steelman:** A combined entity leverages US truck profits to fund German engineering while achieving global scale through platform sharing.

**Attack:**
1.  **Cyclical Trap:** The US partner's profitability hinges entirely on a light-truck boom. Merging at the peak of a cycle locks the German firm into an asset destined for mean reversion. When the cycle turns, the "equals" balance sheet becomes a liability, dragging the premium brand into a downturn it cannot control.
2.  **Cultural Incompatibility:** "Merger of equals" is a fantasy between a hierarchical, consensus-driven German engineering culture and a quarterly-profit-focused US mass-market operator. Integration costs will explode as decision-making paralyzed by conflicting labor laws and management styles.
3.  **Brand Dilution:** Platform sharing between a premium luxury marque and a mass-market truck manufacturer risks eroding the exclusive perception required for the German brand's pricing power. The mass market does not elevate the premium; the premium rarely lifts the mass without distinct separation, which this merger undermines.
4.  **Governance Risk:** A combined board with divergent priorities (long-term engineering vs. short-term shareholder return) creates a deadlock scenario. The "takeover" dynamic hidden behind "equals" rhetoric will surface immediately during the first earnings miss, leading to internal warfare rather than synergy.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Preserve the premium brand's margin and avoid exposure to the US cyclical crash.
*   **Joint Venture:** Test platform sharing on specific components without merging balance sheets or cultures.
*   **Minority Stake:** Gain market insight without assuming full liability for labor and cultural integration.

**Pre-mortem:** Two years post-merger, the truck boom bursts. The US division requires massive capital injection. The German division's cash reserves are drained to plug the hole. Culture clashes prevent rapid restructuring. The stock collapses, and the "global scale" becomes a global burden.

**Blockers:**
*   The US partner's profits are cyclical, not structural; merging at the peak guarantees future write-downs.
*   No viable mechanism exists to reconcile fundamentally opposed corporate governance and labor cultures within a single board.

**Risks:** Brand dilution, integration paralysis, capital misallocation during downturn.

**Kill Criterion:** If US light-truck sales drop >10% within 18 months, the merger thesis fails; immediate divestiture required.

DECISION: NO-GO
