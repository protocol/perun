First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits massive, irreversible capital to a non-existent market while the company fights for survival in its core business against a determined rival.

Steelman: Embedding general-purpose compute in every consumer chip creates a ubiquitous parallel supercomputer, unlocking scientific markets that CPUs cannot touch, provided the software ecosystem can be built before cash runs out.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Protect margins, focus on winning the gaming race. Cost: $0. Risk: Missed future. Reversibility: N/A.
2. **Workstation-only:** Add features only to high-margin professional cards. Cost: Low. Risk: Slow adoption. Reversibility: High.
3. **Proposed (All chips):** Subsidize R&D via every gamer. Cost: High per-unit penalty. Risk: Catastrophic if market fails. Reversibility: None (silicon is permanent).

Fit: This contradicts the immediate survival need to win the graphics race. It assumes a "north star" of universal computing that is not yet validated by revenue.

Pre-mortem: The program burns cash for three years. Gamers pay more for features they don't use, causing price-sensitive sales to collapse to the rival. The scientific community sticks to clusters because the consumer drivers are unstable. The company runs out of cash before the software matures. This is a one-way door; once silicon area is allocated, it cannot be reclaimed without respinning chips.

Evidence: The proposal admits "no large paying market exists" and relies on "academic users experimenting." There are no committed customers. The cost is immediate; the revenue is hypothetical.

Blockers:
1. **Unit economics failure:** Raising unit costs on 100% of volume to serve a 0% revenue segment destroys the margin needed to fund the core war against the rival.
2. **No validation:** Zero committed customers for a multi-year burn is a fatal gap for a public company in a competitive hardware race.

Risks: Engineering distraction, competitor undercutting on price, software adoption failure.

Kill check: If no enterprise customer signs a letter of intent within 12 months, the loss is uncapped because the cost is baked into every shipped unit. There is no stop condition; the spend is automatic with volume.

DECISION: NO-GO
