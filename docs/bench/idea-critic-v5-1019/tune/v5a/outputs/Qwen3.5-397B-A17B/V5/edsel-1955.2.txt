First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a massive, irreversible infrastructure build (new dealer network) and a fixed 1957 launch date despite an explicit, unquantified recession risk and a segment that "could shrink."

**Steelman:** Capturing the growing mid-priced gap requires a distinct brand to avoid cannibalization, justifying the dealer investment to compete with the market leader.

**Attack:**
1.  **Macro Mismatch:** Launching a capital-intensive luxury-adjacent brand exactly when a recession may hit is structurally unsound. In a downturn, the mid-priced segment often collapses as buyers trade down to economy cars or hold existing vehicles. The proposal admits this risk but proceeds with a "massive" spend anyway.
2.  **Infrastructure Trap:** Building a new dealer network is a high-fixed-cost, one-way door. If the segment shrinks or the recession bites, these dealers become stranded assets that cannot be easily liquidated or repurposed, bleeding cash while the parent company's core volume brands need support.
3.  **Timing Rigidity:** A fixed 1957 debut ignores the economic cycle. A "go-if" approach (launch only if leading indicators hold) is absent. The plan bets the company's liquidity on a specific economic window that is already flagged as unstable.
4.  **Alternatives Ignored:** The proposal fails to consider using the existing premium or volume dealer networks to sell a new model line under a sub-brand, which would drastically reduce capital exposure and allow for rapid contraction if the market turns.

**Blocker:** The combination of a potential recession, a shrinking segment possibility, and a mandatory new dealer network creates a scenario where the company cannot cap losses. There is no stop-condition; the spend is front-loaded before market validation in a downturn.

**Kill Criterion:** If consumer confidence indices or new car sales drop by >5% in Q4 1956, the dealer network build must halt immediately. The current plan lacks this brake.

**Decision:** The risk of locking capital into a specialized network right before a potential economic contraction outweighs the opportunity. The plan must be revised to utilize existing channels or delay infrastructure until the recession risk clears.

DECISION: NO-GO
