First call: NO-GO. The plan assumes asset reuse and a two-month timeline will yield a competitive product in a genre dominated by an incumbent, despite the studio having zero prior free-to-play (F2P) operational experience.

**Steelman:** Reusing assets allows a rapid, low-cost entry into the battle royale market to capture users via a free model, monetizing later through cosmetics without harming the core paid business.

**Attack:**
1.  **Capability Gap:** Running a F2P economy (anti-cheat, live ops, server scaling, cosmetic pipelines) is fundamentally different from selling a paid cooperative game. The proposal admits no prior experience yet schedules a full launch in two months. This is an execution mismatch, not just a risk.
2.  **Cannibalization vs. Value:** If the assets are good enough to sustain a standalone F2P title, separating them devalues the paid product. If they are not, the F2P title will fail against the rival's polished, popular offering. The plan offers no mechanism to prevent the free version from rendering the paid version obsolete.
3.  **Market Reality:** Entering a "winner-take-most" genre two years post-inception (2017 context) against an established rival requires a unique hook or superior quality. "Reused assets" and "two months" suggest neither. The cost of delay is low; the cost of a failed launch brand-damage is high.

**Blockers:**
*   **Operational Inexperience:** The proposal commits to a live-service launch without evidence of the required infrastructure or team skill set. This is a factual gap in the plan's feasibility.
*   **Timeline Fallacy:** Two months is insufficient to build, test, and launch a networked competitive shooter capable of competing with a market leader, even with asset reuse.

**Risks:** Cannibalization of paid sales; failure to monetize cosmetics; brand damage from a subpar free product.

**Kill Criterion:** If a prototype cannot demonstrate stable networking and a unique gameplay loop distinct from the rival within 4 weeks, the project must stop.

The plan fails the feasibility test on its own stated constraints (timeline, experience level). It requires a pivot to a capped prototype phase before any commitment to a separate title release.

DECISION: NO-GO
