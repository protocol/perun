First call: GO. The proposal targets a distinct market segment (portability/durability) rather than competing directly on specs, leveraging a lower price point and strong software bundle to drive adoption.

**Steelman:** Win by offering superior battery life and affordability, making the device accessible to a mass market that values playtime over graphical fidelity, using a killer app to establish ecosystem lock-in.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Cede the portable market to the rival; risk long-term brand erosion as gaming becomes mobile.
2.  **High-end handheld:** Match rival specs. Result: Higher cost, shorter battery life, higher retail price, likely lower volume.
3.  **Proposed strategy:** Low cost, long battery, mass appeal. Beats "do nothing" by capturing new users; beats high-end by prioritizing the core constraint of portability (battery/price).

**Fit:** Aligns with dominating the home console market by extending the brand into portable living. Assumes the "north star" is total user base growth, not just hardware margin per unit.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if third-party developers ignore the platform due to perceived technical limits, or if consumers reject monochrome displays entirely. However, the bundled puzzle game mitigates the software library risk at launch. This is a two-way door; a failed launch can be discontinued without destroying the core console business. Cost of delay is high: the rival is already establishing user habits.

**Evidence:** The proposal relies on assumed market willingness to trade color for battery life and price. The $20M marketing spend suggests confidence in creating demand rather than reacting to it. Margins are thin, but the model relies on volume and software attach rates, not hardware profit.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks (monochrome screen, modest hardware) are features of the cost-saving strategy, not contradictions of it. The "single-game" history is a process constraint to be solved by the new cartridge model, which is implied by the "bundled" nature and market context.

**Risks:** Developer adoption, consumer perception of "inferior" tech.
**Kill Criterion:** If pre-orders or first-month sales do not reach 500,000 units by Q2 1990, halt production expansion. Leading metric: Weekly sell-through rate vs. rival.

DECISION: GO
