First call: NO-GO. Raising prices 60% and immediately fracturing the user experience into two brands, two logins, and two bills compounds a single grievance into an existential crisis.

**Steelman:** Separating a declining legacy asset (DVD) from a growth engine (streaming) allows distinct strategies and prevents the legacy cost structure from dragging down the valuation of the future.

**Attack:** The proposal ignores the immediate context: customers are already reeling from a massive price hike. Executing a complex, friction-heavy split (new accounts, separate queues, lost data continuity) immediately after a 60% price increase signals hostility rather than strategic clarity. The "loss of cross-service recommendations" is not just a feature loss; it destroys the unified value proposition that justified the combined plan initially. While the "one-way door" argument suggests speed is needed, the cost of delay is lower than the certainty of mass churn caused by perceived nickel-and-diming. The alternative—maintaining a unified brand while internally ring-fencing the P&L—achieves the management separation without the customer-facing trauma. The stated risk of "customer confusion" is an understatement; this is customer alienation.

**Blockers:**
1. **Compound Shock:** The 60% price increase (July) combined with a forced account split (September) creates a compounding negative event that historically triggers mass exodus. The proposal offers no mitigation for this timing.
2. **Value Destruction:** Explicitly removing shared ratings and recommendations degrades the product utility for the very customers being asked to pay more, contradicting the goal of retaining them for streaming.

**Risks:** Brand fracture, irreversible churn, competitor exploitation of the confusion.

**Kill Criterion:** If internal modeling shows >15% churn in the first 30 days post-split, the separation must be paused and the brands reunited under a single login immediately.

The plan fails because it optimizes for internal accounting convenience while ignoring the external customer reality of price gouging followed by service fragmentation.

DECISION: NO-GO
