First call: NO-GO. Releasing an unfinished, unsupported product to the public without marketing or a support plan invites immediate reputation damage that a solo developer cannot recover from.

**Steelman:** Early access validates demand and funds development through direct user feedback, bypassing traditional publisher gatekeeping.

**Attack:**
1.  **Support Collapse:** A "large audience" for a buggy game generates overwhelming support tickets. A solo dev cannot code and answer tickets simultaneously; development halts, angering buyers.
2.  **Reputation Poison:** In 2009, "unfinished" often meant "broken." Negative reviews on launch day are permanent. Without a marketing budget to drown out noise or a publisher to manage PR, the game dies on arrival.
3.  **Revenue Illusion:** Players rarely pay for promises. If the core loop isn't fun yet, sales will be negligible, failing to fund the very features needed to make it sellable.
4.  **Better Way:** Release a free, limited demo to gather feedback without financial transaction friction. Build a mailing list. Secure a small publisher deal for marketing/support before asking for money. Or, finish the core loop privately before any public exposure.

**Pre-mortem:** The game launches, receives 50 negative reviews citing bugs and lack of content, the developer burns out answering emails, and the project is abandoned within three months.

**Blockers:**
*   **Capacity Mismatch:** The proposal admits the dev "cannot support a large audience" yet proposes a public sale that risks creating one. This is a self-contradictory operational plan.
*   **Market Reality:** Selling an unfinished product without marketing relies on organic discovery, which rarely happens for unknown titles in a crowded market.

**Kill Criterion:** If a free demo fails to retain 20% of players for 30 minutes within two weeks, the core loop is not ready for monetization.

**Decision:** The operational risk of reputational destruction and support overload outweighs the potential cash flow. The plan lacks a mechanism to handle success or mitigate the stated risks.

DECISION: NO-GO
