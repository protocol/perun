First call: GO. The demographic shift creates a clear, underserved niche for affordable style, and leveraging existing mechanicals drastically reduces development cost and time-to-market.

Steelman: By reusing proven, low-cost components, the company can offer a visually distinct, customizable vehicle at a price point competitors cannot match, maximizing margin through optional extras while minimizing upfront engineering risk.

Alternatives:
1.  **Do nothing:** Cede the emerging youth market to competitors; risk brand irrelevance as the economy car ages.
2.  **New platform:** Develop unique mechanics; too costly and slow, missing the immediate demographic wave.
3.  **Upgrade economy car:** Add styling to the existing dull model; fails to create the aspirational "sporty" identity required.

Fit: Directly targets the stated north star of capturing postwar boom buyers with a high-margin, low-entry product.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the shared parts are visibly cheap, alienating style-conscious buyers, or if the advertising fails to differentiate the car from its economy donor. However, the "long hood/short deck" proportion masks shared underpinnings, and the options list allows buyers to hide base-model austerity. This is a two-way door; if the sporty styling fails, production can revert to standard compact volumes with minimal loss.

Evidence: The proposal relies on the verified fact of the demographic bulge and the proven reliability of the donor car's mechanics. The risk of unproven demand is mitigated by the low base price and the ability to scale production up or down based on the options mix.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited are manageable execution challenges, not fundamental flaws. The parts-sharing concern is addressed by the design strategy; the spending risk is inherent to any new launch but is capped by the low tooling costs.

Kill Criteria: If pre-orders or first-month sales do not exceed the break-even volume calculated from the tooling savings by 20%, halt the advertising spend and reduce production to standard compact levels. Leading metric: Option-attachment rate (must exceed 60% to ensure margin).

DECISION: GO
