First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a hardware manufacturer with zero platform experience to enter a duopoly dominated by entrenched competitors with loyal user bases and iconic software libraries, while simultaneously fighting a direct 3D console launch from one incumbent.

Steelman: A CD-based console could succeed by leveraging lower manufacturing costs and superior developer tools to attract third-party studios, undercutting the expensive cartridge model of incumbents.

However, the attack reveals fatal structural flaws based strictly on the provided facts. First, the "better way" analysis fails: the cheapest alternative is "do nothing" or wait, as the proposal admits no experience as a platform holder. Entering a market where two companies hold all leverage, without a unique hook beyond "cheaper discs," ignores the necessity of must-have exclusive software to drive hardware adoption. Second, the timing is catastrophic; launching against an incumbent's own 3D console release guarantees a direct feature war the proposer is ill-equipped to fight. Third, the economic model is unproven for this entity; selling hardware at negative margins requires massive capital reserves and a guaranteed software attach rate to recoup losses, neither of which is evidenced here.

The pre-mortem is clear: the console launches, fails to secure key third-party exclusives because developers prefer the established install bases, and the company bleeds cash on unsold hardware inventory. The risk of "no experience" combined with "loyal customers" for rivals creates a barrier to entry that "easier tools" cannot overcome. There is no stated path to acquiring the critical mass of users required to make the developer ecosystem viable. The proposal relies on assumptions that outside developers will flock to an unproven platform over established ones, despite the lack of a installed user base to sell to.

Blockers: The combination of zero platform experience, immediate competition from an incumbent's 3D launch, and the inability to guarantee developer adoption without an existing user base makes success impossible as proposed.
Risks: Massive financial loss from negative hardware margins without software recoupment; total failure to attract third-party titles.

DECISION: NO-GO
