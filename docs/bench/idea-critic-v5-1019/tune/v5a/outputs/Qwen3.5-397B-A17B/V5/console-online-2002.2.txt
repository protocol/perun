First call: NO-GO. The proposal mandates a paid subscription and broadband requirement in a 2002 market where competitors offer free online play and broadband penetration is minimal, creating an immediate barrier to entry that contradicts stated user expectations and infrastructure reality.

Steelman: A unified, premium online service creates a sticky ecosystem and recurring revenue stream if the market shifts rapidly to broadband and values convenience over cost.

However, the attack reveals fatal flaws in the current context:
1.  **Infrastructure Mismatch:** Requiring broadband in 2002 excludes the vast majority of the addressable market, capping the player base before launch.
2.  **Pricing Dissonance:** Charging for a service competitors provide free, while offering no exclusive content to justify the fee, invites immediate rejection.
3.  **Operational Naivety:** The company lacks experience in running large-scale hosting and support operations, introducing high execution risk for a service that must be flawless to retain paying subscribers.
4.  **Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "publisher-led" approach aligns with current market norms and shifts infrastructure costs to game developers. A hybrid model (free basic, paid premium) or delaying the mandatory paywall until broadband adoption crosses a critical threshold (e.g., 30%) would mitigate adoption risk.

The proposal fails the "fit" test against the 2002 reality: it solves a 2005 problem with 2002 constraints. The risks are not merely theoretical; they are structural barriers confirmed by the low broadband adoption rates and established free alternatives. The lack of a pilot, a phased rollout, or a fallback to a free tier makes this an all-or-nothing bet on unproven consumer willingness to pay.

Blockers:
-   Market infrastructure (broadband) is insufficient to support a mandatory requirement.
-   Pricing model contradicts established competitor offerings and user expectations without added value.
-   Operational capability gap in hosting/support is unaddressed.

Risks:
-   Low adoption due to cost and access barriers.
-   High churn if service stability issues arise.

Kill Criterion: If broadband adoption does not exceed 15% within 12 months, the mandatory subscription model fails.

DECISION: NO-GO
