First call: GO. The proposal solves the core friction of digital books (instant access without a PC) while addressing publisher piracy fears via a closed, cellular-only device.

**Steelman:** A dedicated, cellular-enabled e-reader creates a frictionless ecosystem that locks in customers and reassures publishers, enabling a new high-margin revenue stream that outweighs hardware risks.

**Attack & Options:**
1.  **Fit:** Entering hardware contradicts the "no experience" constraint, but doing nothing cedes the digital future to competitors. The "do nothing" option guarantees stagnation as print declines.
2.  **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if hardware costs bleed cash before adoption hits critical mass, or if publishers withhold content fearing the low-price model.
3.  **Evidence:** The claim relies on the assumption that "paper-like screens" and "cellular connectivity" are mature enough for mass production at a consumer price point in 2007. Earlier failures suggest the market isn't ready, but those devices lacked seamless connectivity and content integration.
4.  **Blockers vs. Risks:**
    *   *Risk:* Hardware inexperience. *Mitigation:* Partner with existing manufacturers; treat the device as a content delivery vehicle, not a profit center initially.
    *   *Risk:* Publisher backlash. *Mitigation:* The closed system prevents easy file sharing, directly addressing piracy fears.
    *   *Risk:* Cannibalization. *Mitigation:* Digital margins often exceed print; shifting volume preserves gross profit.
    *   *Blocker Check:* No factual blocker exists. The risks are executional, not fundamental flaws in the logic. The "cheaper alternative" (selling PDFs for PC) fails to solve the user experience gap that killed previous e-readers.

**Kill Criterion:** If the device cannot be manufactured at a cost allowing a sub-$400 retail price within 18 months, or if two major publishers refuse to license content due to pricing, the project halts.

**Verdict:** The proposal transforms a bookstore into a platform. The hardware risk is real but necessary to capture the digital shift. The integrated cellular model is the differentiator that makes this viable where predecessors failed.

DECISION: GO
