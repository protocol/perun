First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to launch a high-friction, high-cost hardware product into a mass market without solving its fundamental adoption blockers.

Steelman: An invitation-only, high-price launch will generate enough revenue and feedback to refine the product for mass-market viability before privacy and social norms become deal-breakers.

**Options:**
1.  **Proposed Plan:** $1,500 consumer launch. High cost, high reputational risk, low reversibility once public perception sours.
2.  **Do Nothing:** Avoid brand damage from a failed consumer rollout; wait for battery/social tech to mature. Zero cost, zero risk.
3.  **Enterprise Pivot:** Target specific industrial/medical use cases where the camera is a feature, not a bug. Higher unit economics, clear utility, contained privacy scope.

**Fit:** Positioning as an "everyday consumer product" contradicts the stated lack of clear everyday use. A product without a daily utility cannot sustain a $1,500 price point regardless of exclusivity.

**Pre-mortem:** The device fails because early adopters are ostracized in public ("Glassholes"), creating a negative social norm that permanently poisons the brand. The "invitation-only" gate creates artificial scarcity but does not solve the utility deficit. Battery life renders it unusable for "everyday" wear, leading to churn.

**Evidence & Verification:** The proposal itself admits "no clear everyday use" and "privacy objections." These are not hypothetical risks; they are stated facts that directly contradict the core value proposition of an "everyday consumer product." The $1,500 price amplifies the expectation of utility which the device cannot meet.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Utility Mismatch:** The plan targets the mass market ("everyday consumer") while admitting there is "no clear everyday use." This is a fatal logical contradiction.
2.  **Social/Privacy Hostility:** The stated risks (privacy, social acceptance) are intrinsic to the form factor in a public setting. An invitation-only program does not mitigate public backlash when users enter general society.
3.  **Hardware Limits:** Short battery life prevents the "always-on" utility required for the proposed positioning.

**Risks:** High price sensitivity, competitor imitation, supply chain costs. (These are secondary to the blockers).

**Kill Criterion:** If pilot users do not report >4 hours of daily active use within 30 days, the "everyday" premise is false. The current plan lacks a pivot trigger before the general public launch.

The proposal ignores its own admitted flaws to pursue a premature mass-market strategy. It must pivot to enterprise or delay until utility is proven.

DECISION: NO-GO
