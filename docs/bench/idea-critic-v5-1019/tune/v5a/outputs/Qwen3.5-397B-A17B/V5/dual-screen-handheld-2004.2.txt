First call: GO. The proposal leverages asymmetric differentiation rather than engaging in a costly hardware arms race the company may not win against a well-funded rival.

**Steelman:** By introducing a dual-screen form factor with touch input at a lower price point, the company creates a distinct value proposition targeting non-core gamers, while maintaining revenue from the legacy install base.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Ceding the narrative to a rival with superior specs risks long-term brand erosion. Matching specs would likely result in a higher price point, failing to broaden the audience.
2.  **Better Way:** A cheaper, single-screen refresh is safer but offers no unique hook to attract the "broadened audience" or justify marketing spend against a "stronger" rival.
3.  **Fit:** This aligns with a blue-ocean strategy, expanding the market rather than fighting for existing core players.

**Pre-mortem & Risks:**
If this fails, it will be because developers ignore the unique hardware, leaving a library of standard games that do not utilize the second screen or touch, validating the "gimmick" criticism. The risk of developer confusion is real but manageable if first-party studios demonstrate the hardware's potential immediately. The cost of two screens is offset by using less powerful (cheaper) graphics silicon than the rival.

**Blockers vs. Risks:**
*   **Blockers:** NONE. The proposal explicitly accepts the risk of unusual hardware and unproven touch mechanics as part of the strategic bet. No internal contradiction exists; the lower price is achievable by sacrificing raw power, which is the trade-off made to hit the mass market.
*   **Risks:** Developer adoption, consumer perception of the device as a toy, and execution of the software lineup. These are execution risks, not fatal flaws in the logic. The "gimmick" label is a marketing challenge, not a structural blocker.

**Kill Criteria:**
If first-party launch titles fail to demonstrate unique gameplay impossible on the rival's machine within six months, or if the attach rate of software to hardware falls below the legacy system's baseline in the first quarter, the strategy must pivot to aggressive price cuts or software subsidies.

The proposal correctly identifies that winning requires changing the rules of engagement, not playing the rival's game better.

DECISION: GO
