First call: NO-GO. The combination of a historically hostile buyer, a loss-making target, active community distrust, and leadership instability presents immediate existential risks that outweigh the strategic value of code hosting at this specific moment and price.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the dominant platform is the only way to secure the future of developer workflows, provided the buyer can credibly firewall its historical hostility and stabilize the target's leadership quickly.

**Attack:**
1.  **Cultural Collision:** A company known for hostility cannot instantly convince a vocal, distrustful community of benevolence. The "run independently" promise rings hollow without a permanent CEO at the target to champion autonomy. Defection to free alternatives is not a risk; it is a near-certain reaction to the announcement.
2.  **Financials:** Paying $7.5B in stock for a loss-making entity with no CEO is speculative. The valuation assumes zero churn, which contradicts the stated community sentiment.
3.  **Regulatory:** Dominance plus a giant buyer invites immediate antitrust scrutiny, potentially stalling integration or forcing divestiture.
4.  **Leadership Void:** Acquiring a ship without a captain during a storm guarantees drift. The buyer inherits the crisis of finding a CEO while simultaneously fighting a PR war.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoids cultural backlash and financial drag; allows the target to stabilize its own leadership first.
*   **Minority Investment:** Gains influence without triggering community flight or antitrust alarms.
*   **Build/Buy Smaller:** Acquire a niche player to seed a new platform without the baggage.

**Pre-mortem:** The deal closes, key maintainers migrate to free competitors citing "embrace, extend, extinguish" fears, the stock price dips as losses mount, and regulators open an inquiry. The target becomes a toxic asset rather than a crown jewel.

**Blockers:**
*   **Leadership:** No permanent CEO at the target means no one to execute the "independent" strategy or reassure the community.
*   **Community Trust:** The buyer's history directly contradicts the requirement for community retention; no concrete mechanism (beyond a promise) is offered to bridge this.
*   **Profitability:** Acquiring a loss-maker at a premium during a leadership vacuum fails basic financial discipline.

**Kill Criterion:** If >5% of top-repo owners migrate to alternatives within 90 days of announcement, the acquisition thesis collapses.

DECISION: NO-GO
