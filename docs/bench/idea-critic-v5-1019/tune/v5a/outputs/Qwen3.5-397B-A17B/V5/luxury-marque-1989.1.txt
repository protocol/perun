First call: GO. A separate brand with a clean-sheet flagship priced below incumbents directly attacks the prestige barrier while leveraging underlying engineering efficiency.

**Steelman:** Creating a distinct luxury marque with its own distribution isolates the premium offering from the parent's economy reputation, allowing it to compete on merit and value rather than heritage alone.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Cede the high-margin luxury segment entirely to Europeans; remain capped at mid-market volumes.
2.  **Badge engineering:** Slap luxury trim on existing economy platforms. *Failure:* Cannot match European refinement; reinforces "cheap" perception; destroys credibility.
3.  **Acquisition:** Buy an existing luxury maker. *Failure:* Prohibitive cost in 1989; integration risks diluting the acquired brand's value.

**Fit:** Moves the north star from "volume leader" to "full-spectrum manufacturer." Reuses the parent's reliability DNA but hides the badge.

**Pre-mortem:** The brand fails if the dealer network feels like a discounted showroom or if the single flagship lacks the depth to sustain customer interest. However, a one-way door (massive CAPEX) is mitigated by pricing below incumbents to ensure initial volume. The cost of delay is ceding the growing affluent demographic to competitors for another decade.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits high development costs (sunk) and risks (reputation, network cost). These are not blockers but execution hurdles. The "economy" reputation is a blocker only if the brands share showrooms or badges; the proposal explicitly mandates separation. The thin line-up is a risk, not a fatal flaw, provided the flagship is flawless.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (prestige, cost, line-up) are inherent to market entry, not factual contradictions. The strategy correctly identifies that reputation cannot be upgraded, only bypassed via separation.

**Risks:** Dealer profitability during ramp-up; brand confusion if marketing leaks parent identity.

**Kill Criterion:** If flagship pre-orders miss 60% of capacity targets within six months of reveal, halt dealer expansion and pivot to a hybrid sales model.

**Decision:** The separation strategy neutralizes the primary objection (brand dilution). The value proposition (European quality, lower price) is sound.

DECISION: GO
