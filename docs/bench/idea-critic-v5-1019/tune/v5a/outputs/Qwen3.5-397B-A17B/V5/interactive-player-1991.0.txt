First call: NO-GO. The $1,000 price point creates an immediate mismatch between the target "living room" mass market and the cost structure of early adopter hardware, while the value proposition is squeezed by two existing, cheaper trajectories.

**Steelman:** A dedicated $1,000 appliance succeeds by offering a seamless, superior multimedia experience that general-purpose PCs and game consoles cannot match in 1991, justifying the premium through exclusivity and ease of use.

**Attack:**
1.  **Price vs. Substitutes:** At $1,000, this device costs significantly more than contemporary game consoles and competes directly with high-end PCs that are rapidly gaining multimedia capabilities. A PC offers productivity plus media; this box offers only media at a higher relative cost for the core function.
2.  **Content Chicken-and-Egg:** The proposal admits the library of interactive titles will be small. Consumers will not pay a $1,000 premium for a device with limited utility, and developers will not invest in a niche platform with high hardware barriers.
3.  **Identity Crisis:** Positioned "between" a console and a PC, the product lacks a clear job-to-be-done. Consoles are for play; PCs are for work/play. A $1,000 "middle" device risks being perceived as an expensive toy or a crippled computer.
4.  **Market Confusion:** If buyers do not understand the product's purpose, marketing costs will skyrocket. In 1991, "interactive multimedia" is not a self-evident consumer need.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Wait for PC prices to drop and multimedia standards to mature naturally. Cost: $0. Risk: Competitor moves first.
*   **License the Tech:** Sell the drive technology to PC manufacturers instead of building a standalone box. Cost: Lower capex. Risk: Lower margins, less control.
*   **Lower Price Point:** Launch a stripped-down version focused solely on one killer app (e.g., games) at $400. Cost: Lower margin per unit. Risk: Still faces console competition.

**Blocker:** The stated fact that the "library of interactive titles will be small" combined with the "$1,000" price creates a fatal economic loop. No mass-market consumer product can succeed with high price and low content availability simultaneously. This is not a risk; it is a structural failure of the proposed business model.

**Risks:** Rapid PC commoditization, format wars, consumer education costs.

**Kill Criterion:** If a prototype cannot secure commitments for 50+ exclusive interactive titles before manufacturing starts, the project must stop.

DECISION: NO-GO
