First call: GO. The core problems (update delivery, cheating) are unsolvable at scale via physical media; a mandatory client is the only viable technical path.

**Steelman:** A proprietary client is the minimal infrastructure required to secure revenue and gameplay integrity in a shifting broadband market, creating a defensible moat that physical distribution cannot provide.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Cheating erodes player base; update friction kills retention. Competitors with digital solutions win.
2.  **Third-party tools:** Cedes control, data, and revenue share; likely lacks game-specific anti-cheat depth.
3.  **Proposed Client:** High initial friction but solves root causes and captures long-term platform value.

**Fit:** Aligns with the north star of player retention and revenue security. Assumes broadband adoption will outpace resistance.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the client launches with game-breaking bugs during peak hours, causing immediate mass exodus before stability is reached. This is a two-way door (software can be patched or rolled back), but the reputation damage is severe. Cost of delay is high: every month without anti-cheat accelerates community decay.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits broadband isn't universal (2003 context), but the "very popular" status implies the core demographic is early-adopter adjacent. Retail pushback is a risk, not a blocker; digital margins eventually outweigh shelf space.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (dislike of accounts, broadband gaps) are adoption hurdles, not logical contradictions. The plan addresses the stated existential threats (cheating, updates) which the current model cannot touch.

**Risks:** Retail channel shrinkage, initial user churn due to friction, competitor cloning. These are managed via kill criteria: if active user count drops >15% post-launch without recovery in 30 days, or if retail revenue falls >20% YoY without digital offset, the mandatory requirement for *new* titles should be paused for review.

**Kill Criterion:** If the client causes a net loss of >10% of the installed base within 60 days of a major title launch, revert to optional updates for that title. Leading metric: Daily Active Users (DAU) vs. Cheat Reports ratio.

DECISION: GO
