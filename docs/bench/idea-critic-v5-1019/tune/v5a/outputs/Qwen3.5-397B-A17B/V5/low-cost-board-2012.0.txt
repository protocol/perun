First call: GO. The $35 price point creates a novel category of "disposable" computing for education, distinct from shared laptops or fragile tablets, provided the ecosystem gap is treated as a feature (learning opportunity) rather than a bug.

**Steelman:** A ultra-low-cost, credit-card-sized computer sold via licensed distributors to schools, leveraging the "bring your own peripherals" model to hit an unbeatable price, with manufacturing and support outsourced to partners to mitigate the charity's lack of experience.

**Attack & Findings:**
*   **Skeptic:** The requirement for users to source displays and power supplies is a massive friction point. If the total cost of ownership (TCO) exceeds $50, the value proposition collapses against used laptops. The charity's lack of manufacturing experience is critical; a single bad batch could bankrupt the venture.
*   **Better Way:** Why not partner with an existing manufacturer to white-label a device rather than creating a new supply chain? However, existing manufacturers may not hit the $35 target without the charity's specific volume commitments and non-profit status. Doing nothing leaves the status quo of expensive, shared IT labs.
*   **Kill Criteria:** If the first batch sells out but 20% of buyers cannot find compatible power supplies within two weeks, the project fails its educational mission. If the cost to manufacture exceeds $28, the margin vanishes.

**Blockers:** None found in the stated facts. The risks (peripheral friction, small batch, inexperience) are real but manageable through the proposed distributor model and by framing the hardware assembly as part of the curriculum. The "ordinary laptops are cheap" argument is factually weak in 2012 for a *personal*, programmable device per student; tablets are consumption-only, and laptops are too expensive for 1:1 deployment in many schools.

**Decision:** The proposal addresses a genuine gap (personal, programmable hardware at scale) that incumbents ignore. The risks are executional, not fundamental flaws in the concept. The distributor model mitigates the charity's lack of logistics experience.

Blockers: NONE
Risks: Peripheral friction causing user drop-off; manufacturing yield issues due to inexperience.
Kill Criterion: If >15% of initial buyers report inability to power the device within 14 days of receipt, halt expansion and pivot to bundled kits.

DECISION: GO
