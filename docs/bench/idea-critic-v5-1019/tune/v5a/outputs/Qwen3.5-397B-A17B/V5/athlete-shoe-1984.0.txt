First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits a dominant share of the marketing budget to an unproven asset with high injury risk and regulatory hostility, violating basic capital preservation for a public company.

**Steelman:** Secure a generational talent early to monopolize cultural mindshare before rivals react, using controversy as free media to bypass traditional advertising costs.

**Options:**
1.  **Proposed Plan:** High cost, extreme risk, low reversibility (5-year lock).
2.  **Do Nothing/Status Quo:** Maintain running dominance; accept slow basketball erosion. Low cost, zero new risk.
3.  **Proven Veterans:** Sign established stars with lower upside but verified sales data. Moderate cost, predictable ROI.
The proposal fails to beat "Do Nothing" on risk-adjusted returns given the "unproven" status.

**Fit:** Assumes "cultural disruption" outweighs "financial stability." Without verified data that this specific rookie transcends sport, this contradicts fiduciary duty.

**Pre-mortem:** The player suffers a season-ending injury in game 3. The league fines the company $50k/week. Inventory piles up unsold as the "novelty" fades. The budget is gone; running shoe marketing is starved.

**Evidence:** All critical figures (rookie's potential sales, fine amounts, budget percentage) are marked "assumed." No historical data supports signature shoes for rookies lasting beyond a season; the proposal admits this is rare.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Capital Concentration:** Allocating a "large share" of the budget to a single unproven variable creates a single point of failure for the entire marketing org.
2.  **Regulatory Hostility:** Building a product line explicitly designed to break league rules invites immediate punitive action, potentially banning the product entirely before launch.
3.  **Lack of Stop-Loss:** The 5-year term locks capital regardless of performance or injury. There is no exit clause or pilot phase.

**Kill Check:** If the player plays zero minutes due to injury in Year 1, the plan commits to four more years of payment. This is uncapped downside. A sound plan requires a performance-based escalator or an injury opt-out.

**Decision:** The absence of a pilot, cap, or fallback, combined with the admission of high probability failure modes (injury, fines, flopping), makes this a gamble, not a strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
