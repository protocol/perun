First call: NO-GO. The core value proposition (reliable handwriting recognition) is explicitly stated as unproven and unreliable in testing, while the price point is high for an unproven category.

**Steelman:** Launching a $700 pen-based device with heavy CEO-backed publicity will define a new handheld category despite current technical flaws, relying on market education to overcome reliability issues.

**Attack:**
1.  **Blocker (Core Function Failure):** The primary input method is "unreliable in testing." For a device marketed on handwriting, this is not a risk; it is a functional failure. A $700 product cannot ship with a broken primary feature.
2.  **Blocker (Unit Economics/Adoption):** The price is "high" while the category is "unproven" and software is "little." Without a killer app ecosystem or proven demand, the high price creates an insurmountable barrier to early adoption.
3.  **Form Factor:** Being "too big for a pocket" contradicts the "handheld" and "personal" nature of the device, reducing utility compared to existing notebooks or emerging smaller competitors.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" allows competitors to fail first while the company refines the technology. A cheaper, smaller prototype with limited release would validate the tech before a $700 mass launch.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches to heavy hype, users immediately encounter unusable handwriting recognition, reviews destroy the brand's credibility, and the high price prevents any forgiveness. The category is deemed a gimmick, delaying real adoption by years.

**Decision Logic:**
*   **Blockers:** Yes. The core mechanism (recognition) does not work reliably; the price/value mismatch is extreme given the lack of software.
*   **Risks:** Unproven category, size issues, execution difficulty.
*   **Kill Criterion:** If handwriting accuracy cannot reach >95% in blind user tests before mass production, the project must stop.

The proposal commits to a full-scale launch with a broken core feature and no market validation. This is not a calculated bet; it is a guaranteed failure of product-market fit.

DECISION: NO-GO
