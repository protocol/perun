First call: NO-GO. The core value proposition (reliable handwriting recognition) is explicitly stated as unproven and unreliable in testing, while the price point is high for an unproven category.

**Steelman:** Launching a $700 pen-based device with heavy CEO backing will create a new handheld category despite current technical limitations, relying on market education to overcome reliability issues.

**Attack:**
1.  **Blocker (Core Function Failure):** The primary input method is "unreliable in testing." A computer whose main interface fails fundamentally cannot succeed, regardless of publicity. Selling a broken core feature at a premium price ($700) guarantees customer rejection and brand damage.
2.  **Blocker (Form Factor):** The device is "too big for a pocket." A "handheld" computer that cannot be carried handheld defeats its own definition and utility.
3.  **Risk Amplification:** High price + unproven category + little software + unreliable tech = catastrophic adoption barrier. The "heavy publicity" increases the blast radius of the inevitable failure.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves capital. A cheaper, smaller prototype with verified recognition software is a mandatory prerequisite, not an alternative to consider later.

**Pre-mortem:** The product launches with great fanfare. Users immediately discover the handwriting recognition fails on their actual writing. The device is too bulky to carry daily. Software developers refuse to build for a failing platform. Sales collapse, returns surge, and the company becomes synonymous with overhyped failure.

**Evidence:** The proposal itself admits the recognition is unreliable, the size is wrong, the price is high, and the ecosystem is empty. These are not risks; they are stated facts that contradict the viability of the launch.

**Kill Criteria Check:** There is no stop condition mentioned. The plan commits to a full launch despite known fatal flaws.

**Blockers:**
1.  Core input mechanism (handwriting) is unreliable.
2.  Physical form factor violates the "handheld" requirement.
3.  Price is unjustified for a broken, unproven product.

**Risks:** Market rejection, brand destruction, financial loss.

The plan fails the basic test of offering a working product that fits its intended use case. Launching now is negligence.

DECISION: NO-GO
