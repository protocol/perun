First call: GO. The stated facts describe a massive, unserved market segment (families on scooters) and a price point ($2,500) that creates a new category, outweighing the listed risks which are executional, not fundamental flaws in the value proposition.

**Steelman:** Launching the world's cheapest car at $2,500 will successfully convert millions of scooter-owning families to four-wheel safety, provided volume targets are met to sustain margins.

**Attack & Options:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Families remain on unsafe scooters; competitors eventually fill the gap.
*   **Alternatives:** A used car is cheaper but lacks the "new product" warranty and specific family design; scooters are cheaper but unsafe for families. Neither matches the specific "four-seat safety at motorcycle-adjacent cost" upside.
*   **Fit:** Directly addresses the vision of motorizing the masses.
*   **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if land protests delay the factory beyond cash reserves, or if "cheapest" branding irreparably signals "unsafe," causing buyers to choose used cars instead.
*   **Evidence:** The proposal cites a developed prototype and a specific price point. The risks (protests, safety perception, alternatives) are acknowledged but do not contradict the core demand hypothesis.

**Blockers vs. Risks:**
*   **Blockers:** NONE. The land protests are a delay risk, not a proof that the factory cannot be built or the car sold. Safety doubts are a marketing challenge, not a verified defect.
*   **Risks:** Land acquisition delays, brand stigma ("cheap" = "dangerous"), and competition from used cars.

**Kill Criteria:**
If land acquisition is not resolved within 6 months, or if pre-orders (leading metric) fall below 50% of the first-year production target by the launch date, the project must pivot to a contract manufacturing model or halt.

**Decision:**
The proposal presents a viable path to a high-volume, low-margin business that solves a clear user need. The risks are manageable through standard mitigation (PR for safety, legal for land) and do not invalidate the core opportunity. The absence of a pilot or cap in the text is an omission to be fixed in execution, not a reason to reject the strategy.

DECISION: GO
