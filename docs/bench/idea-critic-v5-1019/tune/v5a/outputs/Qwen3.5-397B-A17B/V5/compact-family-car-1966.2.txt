First call: GO. The new plant creates a fixed cost imperative; filling it with a product matching the facility's design (small cars) is the only way to avoid immediate financial bleed, making "do nothing" the most expensive option.

**Steelman:** Launching an 1100cc compact is the minimum viable product to activate the new plant's capacity, leveraging existing engineering while creating a distinct market segment via a dedicated dealer network to protect brand positioning.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Leaves the new plant idle, guaranteeing capital loss. Fails the primary constraint.
2.  **Larger Car:** Risks clashing with established rivals and requires retooling the new plant, delaying capacity utilization.
3.  **Cheaper/Existing Model:** Cannibalizes current sales without solving the "new plant" utilization problem or expanding the customer base.

**Fit:** Matches the stage (new capacity) and vision (expansion). The dedicated network is a high cost but necessary to control the customer experience for a new segment, preventing channel conflict with existing larger models.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the dealer network burns cash before volume ramps, or if the 1100cc engine is perceived as underpowered compared to rivals. However, waiting for perfect market conditions guarantees the plant remains a sunk cost. The cost of delay is the daily depreciation of the idle facility.

**Evidence & Calculation:** The proposal facts state the plant *must* run near capacity to pay off. No alternative product is cited that fits the plant better than the proposed compact. The risks (cost, competition, preference) are execution challenges, not fundamental flaws in the logic of filling the factory.

**Blockers:** NONE. The stated risks are manageable execution variables, not facts proving the plan fails. The necessity of filling the plant overrides the risk of market preference uncertainty.
**Risks:** Dealer network profitability timeline; competitor price wars. These become kill criteria (e.g., stop expansion if dealer ROI < X% by date Y), not reasons to halt.

**Kill Criterion:** If the dedicated network fails to sell 5,000 units within the first 12 months, freeze further network investment and pivot to shared channels.

DECISION: GO
