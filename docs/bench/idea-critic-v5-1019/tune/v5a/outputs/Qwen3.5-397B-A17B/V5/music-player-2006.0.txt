First call: NO-GO. The proposal enters a saturated market late, abandons the company's own established copy-protection standard (alienating existing partners), and offers no price advantage against a dominant incumbent with high switching costs.

**Steelman:** The company can leverage its brand and marketing budget to overcome lateness, using superior hardware (screen) and unique features (wireless sharing) to create a differentiated premium ecosystem that labels will support despite the DRM shift.

**Options:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Preserve partner relationships and avoid high CAPEX in a low-margin hardware battle.
2.  **License Software Only:** Provide the client/store to existing device makers, avoiding hardware risk but ceding control.
3.  **Proposed Plan:** High-risk vertical integration requiring massive marketing to displace an entrenched leader without a clear value hook beyond hardware specs.

**Fit:** Assumes the market values hardware specs over ecosystem lock-in and that labels will accept a new, unproven DRM-free (or different DRM) model from a late entrant.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches to fanfare, but users refuse to re-purchase libraries; labels block wireless sharing fearing piracy; the marketing budget burns out before achieving critical mass; the company is stuck with unsold inventory and angry former partners.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits "enormous share" by the leader, "late to market," "no clear price advantage," and potential label restrictions. These are not risks; they are stated facts that contradict the path to victory.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Strategic Contradiction:** Dropping the company's own promoted copy-protection standard destroys trust with current partners without a guaranteed replacement, creating a diplomatic and technical vacuum.
2.  **No Winning Vector:** Entering late with no price advantage and a fragmented content strategy (potential label restrictions) offers no mechanism to steal share from a loyal ecosystem.
3.  **Dependency Failure:** The core feature (wireless sharing) is explicitly flagged as likely to be blocked by content owners, removing the primary differentiation.

**Risks:** High marketing spend yielding low conversion; hardware commoditization.

**Kill Criterion:** If label agreements for wireless sharing are not signed and verified before hardware tooling begins, the product value proposition collapses.

The plan relies on overcoming stated facts (lateness, no price edge) with unsubstantiated hope (marketing magic, label cooperation).

DECISION: NO-GO
