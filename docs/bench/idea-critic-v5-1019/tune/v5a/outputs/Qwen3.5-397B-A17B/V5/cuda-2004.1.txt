First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits massive, irreversible capital to a non-existent market while the company fights for survival in its core business against a determined rival.

**Steelman:** Embedding general-purpose compute capabilities in every consumer chip today creates an uncontested monopoly in scientific acceleration tomorrow, transforming the company from a component supplier into a foundational platform before competitors realize the shift.

**Options:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Maintain focus on gaming/workstation dominance; zero incremental cost; risk being disrupted later if the market materializes under a competitor.
2.  **Specialized Hardware:** Build a separate, expensive niche product for scientists; lower risk, but fails to achieve mass-market ubiquity or lower costs via volume.
3.  **Proposed Plan:** Subsidize future potential with current profits; highest risk of bankrupting the core business if the "future" arrives late or never.

**Fit:** This contradicts the immediate survival strategy required in a fierce duopoly. Resources diverted to compiler toolchains and university funding are resources subtracted from the graphics performance race, which currently pays 100% of the bills.

**Pre-mortem:** The program launches, unit costs rise, and the core gaming product loses price/performance leadership to the rival. The scientific market remains niche for another decade. The company runs out of cash, unable to sustain the R&D burn, and is acquired or collapses. The "platform" never launches because the host died.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits "no large paying market exists yet" and "academic users are experimenting." There are no committed customers. The cost is immediate and universal (every unit); the revenue is hypothetical and distant.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Capital Misallocation:** Funding a speculative decade-long horizon while engaged in a lethal, immediate price/performance war violates basic liquidity discipline.
2.  **Lack of Validation:** Zero committed customers means the demand curve is entirely assumed.
3.  **Strategic Distraction:** Engineering focus split between winning the current generation and inventing a new paradigm guarantees mediocrity in both.

**Risks:** The technology proves unusable for general science; the software stack becomes a maintenance nightmare; the rival undercuts prices while this company subsidizes researchers.

**Kill Criterion:** If no commercial customer signs a letter of intent within 18 months, the program must terminate. However, given the total lack of current market signals, the initial commitment itself is the failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
