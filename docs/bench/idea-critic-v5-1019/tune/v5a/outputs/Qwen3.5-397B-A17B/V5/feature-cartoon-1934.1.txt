First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits the studio's entire steady income to a single unproven format with a 10x cost increase, based on assumptions that directly contradict current industry consensus and insider advice.

**Steelman:** A feature-length film could unlock a new, higher-margin market if audiences accept the format, justifying the risk.
**Attack:** The core assumption—that audiences will sit for a feature—is untested and actively disputed by experts. The "do nothing" alternative preserves steady profits; the "cheaper alternative" (a series of linked shorts) tests audience appetite without the 10x upfront exposure. The proposal fails the "one-way door" test: if it fails, the studio is bankrupt, not just set back.

**Blockers:**
1. **Contradicted Premise:** Industry insiders and the founder's family advise against it, indicating a high probability of failure based on existing market knowledge.
2. **No Kill Criterion:** The proposal lacks a specific, observable metric to stop production if early tests (e.g., story reels) fail to engage viewers. Committing the full budget before validating demand is a fatal flaw.
3. **Resource Concentration:** Funding the project entirely from short-film earnings removes the safety net, making the studio vulnerable to any delay or overrun.

**Risks:** Budget overrun, audience rejection, loss of steady income stream. These are not merely risks; they are likely outcomes given the lack of validation.

**Kill Check:** The most likely failure is audience rejection. A valid kill criterion would be: "If a 10-minute test reel fails to hold 80% of a test audience's attention, stop." The proposal has no such stop condition.

**Decision Method:**
- **Fit:** Does not align with the current "steady but thin" profit model; it attempts a total pivot without validation.
- **Pre-mortem:** The studio runs out of money halfway through production because the feature format proves too expensive and unpopular.
- **Evidence:** No evidence supports the claim that audiences want this; all cited evidence (insider advice) suggests the opposite.

The proposal as stated is a gamble, not a plan. It must be revised to include a low-cost pilot, a clear kill criterion, and a phased funding approach.

DECISION: NO-GO
