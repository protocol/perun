First call: NO-GO. The unit economics of providing a gigabyte of storage per user in 2004, when competitors offer mere megabytes, create an unsustainable cost base relative to unproven text-ad revenue.

**Steelman:** A superior search interface and massive storage will disrupt a stagnant market by solving user pain points, with ad relevance funding the infrastructure.

**Attack:**
1.  **Cost vs. Revenue:** In 2004, storage hardware and bandwidth are expensive. Scaling to millions of users at 1GB each requires massive upfront capital. Text ads matched to content are unproven at this scale and may not cover the marginal cost of storage per user. The proposal admits "no direct revenue at launch," making the cash burn dangerous.
2.  **Privacy Trust:** Scanning private email content to serve ads is a fundamental violation of user trust. In a mature market dominated by entrenched players, asking users to switch based on a feature that feels invasive is a critical adoption barrier. Competitors can market themselves as "privacy-safe" instantly.
3.  **Market Dynamics:** Email is a commodity with high switching costs (network effects, archived data). Entrenched incumbents can easily match storage increases once the model is proven, leveraging their existing user base without the new entrant's heavy infrastructure debt.
4.  **Blocker:** The core financial model relies on ad revenue covering high storage costs, yet there is no evidence that ad click-through rates or CPMs in 2004 can support a 100x increase in storage allocation per user. This is a factual mismatch between cost structure and revenue potential.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoid massive capital expenditure on an unproven ad model.
*   **Cheaper Path:** Launch with a modest storage increase (e.g., 100MB) to test ad viability before committing to 1GB.
*   **Differentiation:** Focus on interface speed alone without the storage arms race.

**Conclusion:** The proposal fails the unit economics test. The cost of 1GB storage per user vastly outstrips the likely yield from text ads in the current market environment. The privacy risk further jeopardizes user acquisition. Without a proven revenue mechanism to cover the storage spike, this is a financial liability.

DECISION: NO-GO
