First call: NO-GO. The stated price is several times higher than competitors while offering slower performance and less software, a clear failure on value.

Steelman: This machine pioneers the graphical interface for business, justifying a premium for future-proofing despite current slowness and software scarcity.

Options:
1. Launch at $10k: High risk of immediate rejection due to price/performance mismatch against established text-based PCs.
2. Do nothing: Wait for hardware costs to drop and software ecosystem to mature before entering.
3. Internal alternative: Prioritize the cheaper, similar machine already in development to capture the market first.

Fit: Assuming the goal is mass office adoption, a $10k price point excludes the vast majority of targets. It fits only a niche luxury segment, not general business.

Pre-mortem: The product launches, sales teams cannot justify the cost against faster, cheaper rivals with more software. Inventory piles up, brand reputation suffers as "overpriced and slow." The internal cheaper team feels undermined, causing political friction. This is a two-way door (can stop production), but the cost of delay is low compared to the cost of a failed launch.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits the price is "several times" higher, speed is "slow," and software is "little." These are fatal flaws for a general business launch in 1983.

Blockers:
1. Price/Performance Mismatch: The core value proposition (GUI) is negated by being slower and vastly more expensive than alternatives. No business case supports this as proposed.
2. Internal Cannibalization: A cheaper, similar internal project exists. Launching the expensive version first undermines the company's own optimal path to market.

Risks: Lack of software ecosystem, slow adoption, competitor response.

Kill Criterion: If pre-orders from top 50 target enterprises do not cover 20% of initial production costs within 60 days, halt launch immediately.

The proposal fails the basic test of offering competitive value. The existence of a cheaper internal alternative makes this specific configuration redundant.

DECISION: NO-GO
