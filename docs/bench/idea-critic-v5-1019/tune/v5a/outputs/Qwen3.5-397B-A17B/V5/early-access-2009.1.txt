First call: NO-GO. Releasing an unfinished, unsupported game to a paying public without marketing or a support plan invites immediate reputation damage and refund churn that a solo developer cannot survive.

**Steelman:** Early revenue funds development while real player data dictates the feature roadmap, validating demand before full completion.

**Attack:**
1.  **Support Collapse:** A "large audience" for a buggy sandbox game generates disproportionate support tickets. A solo dev coding features cannot simultaneously triage crashes and lost saves. The result is a backlog that halts development entirely.
2.  **Reputation Lock-in:** In 2009, "unfinished" often meant "broken." Unlike modern Early Access norms, a rough alpha release risks permanent negative reviews that kill future sales, regardless of subsequent patches.
3.  **Discovery Failure:** With zero marketing budget, the game relies on word-of-mouth. If the first wave encounters bugs and no support, they spread negative word-of-mouth, ensuring no second wave arrives.
4.  **Better Way:** Release a free, limited demo to gather feedback without financial transaction friction. Use this to build a mailing list. Only charge once the core loop is stable and support load is manageable.

**Pre-mortem:** The game launches; players encounter game-breaking bugs; the developer is overwhelmed by emails; development stops for three months to handle support; the game is labeled "abandonware" on forums; sales cease.

**Blockers:**
*   **Capacity Mismatch:** The proposal admits the dev "cannot support a large audience" yet proposes a public sale that invites one. This is a direct contradiction between capability and strategy.
*   **Market Timing:** The 2009 market lacks the established "Early Access" social contract; paying for alpha is viewed as purchasing a defective product, not funding development.

**Kill Criterion:** If the first 100 buyers generate more than 10 support tickets per day that require more than 1 hour to resolve, the project fails. The proposal has no cap on sales to prevent this.

The plan commits to full-scale public sale before verifying support capacity or market readiness. It ignores the fatal constraint of solo bandwidth against public expectations.

DECISION: NO-GO
