First call: GO. The shift from desktop to web, combined with satellite imagery and routing, creates a uniquely defensible position despite 2004 technical constraints.

**Steelman:** A free, browser-based map with satellite views and routing will become the global standard by removing installation friction and offering unprecedented visual context, monetizing indirectly through search dominance.

**Attack & Options:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Retain desktop niche; cede the web to incumbents. High risk of irrelevance as bandwidth grows.
*   **Paid Web App:** Fails against free competitors; adoption too slow.
*   **Current Proposal:** High upfront licensing cost and browser performance risk. However, "slow browsers" are a transient hardware constraint, while network effects are permanent. The lack of direct revenue is acceptable if the asset drives core search engagement and ad inventory value.

**Fit:** Aligns with the acquirer's north star: organizing the world's information. Maps are structured spatial data; satellite imagery is the ultimate visual index.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if licensing costs bankrupt the project before scale, or if browser rendering remains unusable. Yet, waiting for "perfect" browsers guarantees losing the market. The cost of delay (ceding user habit formation to rivals) exceeds the cost of early technical debt.

**Evidence & Verification:**
*   **Claim:** Browsers are too slow. **Counter:** AJAX and improving JS engines (2004-2005 trajectory) will solve this faster than desktop distribution cycles.
*   **Claim:** No revenue model. **Counter:** Value is captured in increased search session duration and data richness, not direct map fees.
*   **Claim:** Licensing is costly. **Fact:** True, but creates a moat against casual clones.

**Blockers:** NONE. The stated risks (cost, speed, competition) are execution hurdles, not fundamental flaws. The "no revenue" objection ignores the strategic value to the parent search engine. No alternative reaches the same upside (global ubiquity) cheaper.

**Risks:** High burn rate; technical lag in tile rendering; potential licensing disputes.
**Kill Criterion:** If active daily users do not reach 100k within 6 months of launch, or if licensing costs exceed 20% of the projected search-ad uplift, pivot to API-only or shut down.

The proposal bets on a paradigm shift (web vs. desktop) that aligns with internet trajectory. The risks are manageable; the opportunity is existential.

DECISION: GO
