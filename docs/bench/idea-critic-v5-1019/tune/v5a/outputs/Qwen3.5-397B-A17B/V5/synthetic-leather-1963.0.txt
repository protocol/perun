First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks to spend heavily on a product that costs more than the incumbent, offers no proven comfort advantage, and faces active resistance from the very manufacturers required to adopt it.

Steelman: If the material's durability and aesthetic can command a premium that offsets higher costs and retooling friction, it could disrupt the leather market.

Attack:
1.  **Economics:** The synthetic costs *more* than leather. Shoe manufacturers operate on thin margins; without a drastic reduction in their assembly costs (which retooling increases) or a guaranteed consumer price premium, they have zero incentive to switch.
2.  **Adoption Friction:** Manufacturers are explicitly unwilling to change factories. Heavy marketing spend targeted at them ignores this hard constraint; you cannot market away a capital expenditure barrier.
3.  **Consumer Risk:** Wearers may find it less comfortable. In footwear, comfort is the primary retention driver. A "premium" label cannot sustain a product that hurts to wear.
4.  **Fashion Volatility:** Betting heavily on a single material in 1963 ignores the cyclical nature of fashion. If trends shift back to natural textures, the inventory becomes worthless.

Alternatives:
*   **Do nothing:** Avoids sunk costs in a hostile market.
*   **Niche Pilot:** Target a specific non-footwear application (e.g., upholstery, accessories) where retooling is cheaper and comfort is less critical, proving demand before approaching shoe makers.
*   **Cost-First Approach:** Delay launch until production costs drop below leather, creating an immediate economic incentive for manufacturers.

Pre-mortem: The company spends millions on marketing. Manufacturers refuse to retool due to cost. A few try it, consumers complain about comfort, returns spike, and the brand is labeled "cheap plastic" despite the premium price. The project is cancelled with total loss.

Blockers:
*   **Negative Unit Economics:** Higher cost than incumbent with no offsetting efficiency gain for the buyer.
*   **Channel Refusal:** Target customers (manufacturers) have stated unwillingness to adopt.
*   **Product Defect Risk:** Potential comfort issues in a category where comfort is king.

Risks: Fashion trends shifting; long-term durability perceptions.

Kill Criterion: If a pilot with 3 manufacturers does not yield a signed purchase order within 6 months without subsidizing their retooling costs, stop immediately.

DECISION: NO-GO
