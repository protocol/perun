First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to radically invert the core purchasing behavior of the existing customer base without validation, relying on an executive's past success in a different retail category (electronics) applied to a department store context with distinct demographics.

**Steelman:** Eliminating coupon dependency and modernizing the store layout will attract new, higher-margin shoppers while simplifying operations, replicating a proven electronics retail model.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Evidence:** The proposal admits most sales historically come from discounted items. The customer base is "older" and "coupon-driven." Abruptly removing the primary value proposition (discounts) for this specific demographic contradicts the stated facts of their behavior. What worked for branded electronics (often impulse or tech-upgrade driven) does not automatically translate to habitual department store shopping where price sensitivity is the stated norm.
2.  **Options:** The "do nothing" option preserves current revenue while testing small changes. The "cheaper alternative" is a pilot program in 5-10 stores to measure elasticity before a chain-wide rollout. The proposed "all-at-once" approach carries maximum risk with zero feedback loops.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in Q4 2012. Cause: Loyal customers stop shopping immediately due to perceived price hikes, while new customers fail to arrive fast enough to replace the lost volume. The lack of a pilot means this error is irreversible at scale before detection.
4.  **Blocker Test:** The proposal explicitly states "announced without a test." This is a factual blocker. Changing the entire chain's pricing model and physical layout simultaneously, based on an unverified assumption that the customer base will adapt instantly, violates basic operational hygiene for a company already experiencing falling sales. The risk is not theoretical; it is a direct conflict with the "coupon-driven" reality cited in the proposal.

**Kill Criterion:** If a pilot were run, a >15% drop in transaction volume in test stores within 60 days would trigger an immediate halt. Since no pilot exists, the condition to verify safety is missing, making the full rollout a blocker.

**Decision:** The plan fails the blocker test due to the absence of a pilot and the direct contradiction of known customer behavior without evidence of successful migration.

DECISION: NO-GO
