First call: GO. The cost ($50M) is negligible relative to the strategic upside of securing the mobile search channel before competitors lock it down.

**Steelman:** Acquiring this team provides the only viable path to owning the mobile operating layer, ensuring free distribution of search services in a carrier-controlled market where paid placement will become prohibitively expensive.

**Options:**
1.  **Acquire:** High execution risk, but captures the platform layer for the cost of a small engineering team.
2.  **Do Nothing:** Cedes mobile OS to incumbents (Nokia, Microsoft), risking existential marginalization as usage shifts from desktop to mobile.
3.  **Partner/License:** Likely impossible; handset makers have no incentive to adopt a new, unproven OS from a search vendor without ownership leverage.

**Fit:** Directly serves the north star of universal search access. If mobile becomes the primary interface, owning the OS is the ultimate defensive moat.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if handset manufacturers refuse to install the OS despite it being free, or if carriers block it to protect their own portals. However, the "free" aspect disrupts the existing licensing economics, creating a wedge for adoption among smaller handset makers desperate to cut costs.

**Evidence & Math:** $50M for 6 engineers is ~$8.3M per head—a premium, but cheap for a potential monopoly-grade asset. The "no revenue model" is irrelevant; the OS is not the product, search ads are. The OS is the distribution pipe. Waiting increases the price exponentially or renders the opportunity unavailable.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (adoption, carrier resistance) are execution challenges inherent to any platform play, not factual contradictions proving failure. The lack of a direct OS revenue model is a feature, not a bug, aligning with the core ad-business strategy.

**Kill Criteria:** If, after 3 years, the OS fails to secure >5% market share or does not demonstrably increase mobile search volume compared to a baseline of no-OS, the project should be pivoted or sunset. The leading metric is "Mobile Search Queries per Active Device."

The cost of delay is losing the mobile internet entirely. The cost of failure is $50M, a rounding error for a search giant preventing obsolescence.

DECISION: GO
