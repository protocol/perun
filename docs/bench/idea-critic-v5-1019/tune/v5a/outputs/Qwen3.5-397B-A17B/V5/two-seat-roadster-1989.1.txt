First call: GO. The market gap for an affordable, lightweight roadster exists precisely because incumbents deem the niche "dead," creating a low-competition entry point for a specialist maker.

**Steelman:** A mid-size manufacturer can profitably capture pent-up demand for a simple, front-engine, rear-drive convertible by leveraging existing components to keep costs low, ignoring the "safety rules make it costly" assumption through smart design rather than exotic engineering.

**Attack & Options:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Safe, but cedes the emerging "affordable enthusiast" segment to potential future competitors.
2.  **High-End Sports Car:** Higher margin but requires heritage the company lacks and violates the "affordable" constraint.
3.  **The Proposal:** Beats "do nothing" by targeting a specific, underserved demographic willing to compromise on practicality for driving purity. The risk of shrinking sales is inherent to all niche launches; the counter is that the current supply is zero.

**Fit:** Aligns with a vision of democratizing driving pleasure. The lack of heritage is an advantage, allowing freedom from legacy expectations.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the car becomes too heavy or expensive trying to meet every safety regulation perfectly, destroying the "lightweight/affordable" value proposition. This is a two-way door; a limited initial run caps exposure.

**Evidence & Blockers:** The proposal states mass-market makers have abandoned the segment (fact: reduced competition). The objection regarding safety costs is a hypothesis, not a proven blocker; lightweight design often offsets compliance costs. No internal data contradicts the existence of demand.

**Kill Criteria:** If the prototype cannot meet the target weight or price point within 12 months, the project stops. Leading metric: Pre-order conversion rate among non-enthusiast demographics to test broader appeal.

**Verdict:** The risks cited (shrinking segment, two seats) are features of the niche, not flaws in the plan. The absence of a "safety cost" proof allows the bet to proceed. The potential upside of owning a revived category outweighs the capped downside of a limited production run.

Blockers: NONE.
Risks: Misjudging price sensitivity; regulatory cost overruns.

DECISION: GO
