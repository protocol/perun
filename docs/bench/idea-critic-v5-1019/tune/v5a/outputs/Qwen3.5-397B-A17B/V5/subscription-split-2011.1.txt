First call: NO-GO. A 60% price hike followed immediately by a forced split into two brands, two logins, and two bills treats loyal customers as adversaries, guaranteeing mass churn and reputational collapse.

**Steelman:** Separating a declining legacy asset (DVD) from a high-growth one (streaming) allows distinct strategies and prevents the legacy cost structure from dragging down the future valuation.

**Attack:** The execution ignores the immediate context: customers are already reeling from a massive price increase. This proposal compounds that pain by fracturing the user experience. Forcing users to manage separate accounts and losing cross-service recommendations removes the primary value of the "combined" plan they just paid more for. The rationale cites "customer confusion" as a risk, yet the solution *creates* maximum confusion and friction. A declining business still generates cash flow; alienating its remaining base to "free" the other side is a false economy if it triggers a boycott that hits both brands. The lack of shared data (ratings/queues) destroys the product's intelligence, making both services dumber.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Keep the combined plan, absorb the declining DVD margins as a cost of acquiring streaming users, and slowly sunset DVD features without a hard brand split.
2.  **Soft Separation:** Maintain a single login and billing interface but offer distinct "tiers" or clear internal segmentation, avoiding the cognitive load of two websites.
3.  **Proposed Plan:** High friction, high anger, total data silo.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, subscriber numbers plummet 20%. Social media is flooded with stories of billing nightmares and lost queues. The stock price halves. The "new" DVD brand is dead on arrival because no one wants a separate relationship for a dying format, and the streaming brand is tainted by association with the hostile split.

**Blocker:** The proposal offers no mitigation for the compounding negative reaction to the recent 60% price hike. It assumes customers will accept higher prices *and* a fractured product simultaneously. This contradicts basic retention logic. The loss of shared data is a self-inflicted wound that reduces the core product value.

**Kill Criterion:** If customer support ticket volume regarding billing or login confusion exceeds 200% of the current baseline within 14 days of announcement, the plan fails.

DECISION: NO-GO
