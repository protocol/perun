First call: NO-GO. Doubling production while sales lag by 60% during a recession with rising interest rates destroys cash without creating demand.

**Steelman:** The claim is that flooding the channel now secures future market share or forces sales through volume, betting that the recession will end soon and inventory will be needed immediately.

**Attack:**
1.  **Cash Flow:** Producing 7,500 more units (doubling output implies massive incremental cost) requires upfront capital for parts and labor. With only 3,000 sold, cash conversion is negative. Rising interest rates make bridging this gap prohibitively expensive or impossible given "limited cash."
2.  **Demand Mismatch:** A recession suppresses demand; high interest rates specifically crush auto sales (financing dependent). Increasing supply does not create demand; it creates stranded assets.
3.  **Channel Blockage:** Dealers cannot finance more stock. If they cannot buy, the manufacturer holds the inventory, accruing storage costs and depreciation while burning cash.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves remaining cash. Reducing production aligns burn rate with sales, extending runway. Doubling production accelerates insolvency.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** The company has "limited cash." Doubling production requires significant immediate outlay before any revenue is realized.
*   **Fact:** Interest rates are rising, increasing the cost of holding the resulting massive inventory and making dealer financing harder.
*   **Fact:** Sales (3,000) are less than half of production (7,500). The gap is widening. Doubling production widens it further, guaranteeing a larger unsold stockpile that the company cannot afford to hold.

**Pre-mortem:** The company runs out of cash in 6 months, unable to pay suppliers or service debt, while sitting on a lot of 10,000+ unsold cars that dealers refuse to take. The attempt to "get ahead" of the recovery kills the firm before the recovery arrives.

**Kill Criterion:** If cash reserves cannot cover the increased production costs for 3 months without new sales, the plan fails immediately. The proposal offers no funding source.

This plan ignores the binding constraint (cash/demand) and exacerbates the primary risk (inventory overload). It is financially suicidal under the stated conditions.

DECISION: NO-GO
