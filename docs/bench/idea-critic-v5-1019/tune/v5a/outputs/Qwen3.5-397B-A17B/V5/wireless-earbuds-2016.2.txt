First call: GO. The removal of the headphone jack creates an immediate, forced vacuum for a premium audio solution that only this manufacturer can fill with seamless integration.

**Steelman:** Launching proprietary wireless earbuds at $159 captures the high-margin accessory market created by the jack's removal, leveraging automatic pairing to justify the price over free wired alternatives.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Cedes the newly created accessory market to third parties, reducing ecosystem stickiness and leaving users with inferior adapters.
*   **Cheaper Option:** Selling a $50 generic Bluetooth bud fails to monetize the "seamless" value proposition or recoup the custom chip investment.
*   **Fit:** Aligns with a "wireless future" north star; the jack removal is the catalyst that makes this viable now.
*   **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if battery anxiety (5 hours) or loss frequency triggers mass returns. However, the charging case mitigates battery limits, and the high price signals premium status, countering "odd look" ridicule through brand confidence.
*   **Evidence:** The proposal admits risks (price, loss, aesthetics) but offers no data proving these will stop adoption. Historically, convenience and ecosystem lockout outweigh initial aesthetic shock. The $159 price point is high but defensible against the "free" alternative because the free option is now functionally obsolete for the new device without an adapter.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (ridicule, loss, price, anger) are market headwinds, not factual contradictions. Anger is transient; the functional gap is permanent until filled. No evidence suggests the target users will reject the solution en masse given the lack of alternatives.

**Risks:** High unit cost sensitivity; physical loss rate exceeding replacement purchases. These are managed by the charging case (utility) and brand loyalty.

**Kill Criterion:** If pre-order conversion from phone buyers is <15% within 30 days, halt mass production expansion and pivot to a lower-cost tier. Leading metric: Attach rate per new phone sold.

The plan addresses a self-created necessity. The risks are manageable marketing and product design challenges, not fundamental flaws in the business logic. The cost of delay is losing the narrative control of the "wireless transition."

DECISION: GO
