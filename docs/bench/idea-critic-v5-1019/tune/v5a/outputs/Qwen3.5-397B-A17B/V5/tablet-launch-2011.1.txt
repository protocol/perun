First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a full-scale launch against a dominant incumbent with a fragile ecosystem and internal instability, ignoring the fatal mismatch between price parity and value deficit.

**Steelman:** Launching now leverages first-mover momentum from the acquisition to secure a foothold before the OS becomes irrelevant, betting that marketing can bridge the app gap.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Evidence:** Pricing "in line with the market leader" while offering a "small fraction" of the apps and an OS with "almost no market share" violates basic economic logic. Shoppers see the product as "unproven"; without a price discount or unique hardware advantage (not stated), there is no incentive to switch. The stated facts show target users do not want it at this price point.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" or delaying launch to grow the app catalog is superior. A cheaper, staged alternative (launching at a loss-leader price or only to developers) reaches the same upside (ecosystem growth) with less reputational risk.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The launch fails because developers do not build apps for a device with no users, and users do not buy a device with no apps. The "heavy investment" in advertising burns cash accelerating this realization.
4.  **Blocker:** The proposal states shoppers see the product as unproven and the app catalog is tiny, yet insists on premium pricing. This is a self-contradictory strategy where the core claim (market success) is negated by the stated facts (no value prop). Leadership transition adds execution risk that a "go-for-broke" launch cannot absorb.

**Kill Criterion Check:** The plan commits to full scale and heavy ad spend immediately. There is no capped pilot, no metric to stop the bleed if early sales miss targets, and no fallback if developer outreach fails. The absence of a working stop condition for a high-cost, low-probability bet is a blocker.

**Verdict:** The plan as proposed ignores the "chicken-and-egg" trap of platform markets. It bets the company on a premium launch of an inferior product during leadership instability.

Blockers: Premium pricing despite tiny app catalog and unproven status; no capped pilot or stop condition for heavy ad spend.
Risks: Developer apathy, leadership distraction, cash burn.

DECISION: NO-GO
