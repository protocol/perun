First call: GO. The pivot from "cheap plastic" to "fashion item" fundamentally changes the value proposition, turning a potential reputation liability into a market opportunity.

**Steelman:** By positioning twelve distinct models as accessible fashion accessories rather than compromised timekeepers, the company captures a new demographic without diluting its core luxury identity, making the aggressive one-million-unit target achievable through volume rather than margin.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary objection is reputation damage. However, treating the product as a disposable fashion item creates a "Chesterton's fence" separation from the brand's heritage of permanence. If marketed strictly as seasonal style, failure is expected and contained, not a quality scandal.
*Alternative 1 (Do Nothing):* Safe, but cedes the emerging low-cost, high-style segment to competitors.
*Alternative 2 (Sub-brand Launch):* Safer for reputation but slower to market and higher overhead. The proposed "collection" approach achieves similar separation with lower friction.
*Alternative 3 (Price Cutting):* Established brands cutting prices devalue their own core; this proposal creates a new category entirely.

**Pre-mortem & Door:**
If this fails, it will be because the designs were too generic to justify "fashion" status, reverting the product to "cheap plastic" in the consumer's eye. This is a two-way door; unsold inventory can be liquidated as promotional items with minimal long-term brand scar. The cost of delay is high, as fashion trends in 1983 are shifting rapidly toward colorful, lightweight accessories.

**Blockers vs. Risks:**
*Blockers:* NONE. The facts provided do not show an inability to execute the fashion positioning or a current brand constraint that forbids it.
*Risks:* Aggressive sales targets and fickle tastes are inherent to the fashion strategy, not blockers. They are managed by the twelve-model diversification (spreading taste risk) and the low unit cost (limiting financial exposure).

**Kill Criterion:**
If the first three models fail to achieve sell-through rates of 40% within six months, halt production on the remaining nine models and pivot the line to promotional giveaways.

The proposal correctly identifies that the product's weakness (plastic) is its strength in a fashion context. The risks are manageable execution details, not fundamental flaws.

DECISION: GO
