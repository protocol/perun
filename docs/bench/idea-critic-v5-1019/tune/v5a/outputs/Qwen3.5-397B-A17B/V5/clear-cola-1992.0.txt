First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a costly, irreversible nationwide launch based solely on test market data that the proposal itself admits may overstate demand, while ignoring the high probability of flagship cannibalization and low repeat purchase rates.

**Steelman:** A clear cola captures a distinct "purity" segment that test markets validated, justifying a rapid nationwide rollout to preempt competitors before novelty fades.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the blocker test. It acknowledges that "test markets can overstate demand" and "novelty buyers may not repeat," yet proceeds to a full launch without a mechanism to verify real-world retention or limit exposure. If the color-flavor association holds (a stated risk), the product fails permanently, and the massive marketing spend is wasted. Furthermore, launching as a "major brand extension" invites immediate cannibalization of the flagship without a proven net gain. The absence of a staged rollout, a cap on marketing spend, or a specific kill criterion for repeat purchase rates constitutes a blocker. A sound operator would not bet the brand on a known flawed signal (test market overstatement) without a safety valve.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Preserve flagship dominance; zero risk of cannibalization or wasted launch capital.
2.  **Staged Regional Rollout:** Launch in limited regions with a strict repeat-purchase metric before committing to national marketing. This caps loss and validates the "overstated demand" fear.
3.  **Covert Launch:** Release without "major marketing" to test organic retention, avoiding the signaling of weakness to rivals.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, sales have collapsed after the initial novelty spike. Repeat purchases are below 15%, confirming shoppers reject clear cola as "lacking flavor." The flagship lost 4% share to the new product, resulting in a net revenue decline. Rivals launched cheap copies, diluting the "purity" claim. The massive campaign spend cannot be recovered.

**Blockers:**
*   Commitment to nationwide scale despite admitted risk of overstated test data and unknown repeat rates.
*   No kill criterion or cap defined to stop the rollout if repeat purchases fail.

**Risks:** Cannibalization of flagship; novelty fade; competitor copying.

**Kill Criterion:** If repeat purchase rate in the first 90 days is <20%, halt national expansion immediately and cap total marketing spend at $5M.

DECISION: NO-GO
