First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits irreversible capital before validating the core hypothesis that unit economics can be positive in a low-margin, high-density model.

**Steelman:** Rapidly building a nationwide moat of automated warehouses prevents competitors from securing real estate and customers, leveraging abundant venture capital to dominate the market before margins matter.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" beyond the current metro preserves cash while learning unit economics. A "cheaper alternative" involves leasing existing flexible space and using third-party logistics to validate density requirements without billion-dollar sunk costs. The proposal fails to beat "do nothing" because it assumes success before evidence exists.
2.  **Fit:** The strategy contradicts the stated lack of operating track record. Expanding to a dozen cities dilutes management focus from solving the fundamental pickup/delivery cost problem in the first city.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 2001. Cause: The $1B fixed cost burden creates a break-even volume impossible to reach because customer density in new cities is lower than modeled, and grocery margins cannot cover the last-mile delivery cost at the promised 30-minute speed. The company runs out of cash while stuck with unused, hyper-specific automated warehouses.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits "no operating track record" and "low-margin business." Committing $1B based on unproven assumptions violates basic capital allocation logic. There is no data showing the first metro is profitable, let alone scalable.
5.  **Blocker:** The plan commits to full-scale irreversible spend (warehouses) before a pilot validates the unit economics. There is no working stop condition; once construction begins, the capital is gone regardless of operational failure. This is a one-way door with no exit.

**Kill Criterion:** If the first metro does not show positive contribution margin per order within 6 months, expansion must halt. The current proposal ignores this, betting the entire fund on an unproven model.

**Verdict:** The absence of validated learning combined with massive fixed-cost commitment is a fatal flaw.

Blockers: Irreversible $1B spend before unit economic validation; no pilot success metric required before expansion.
Risks: Low margins, unproven density, capital intensity.

DECISION: NO-GO
