First call: GO. The market shift toward small computers is an existential threat to a mainframe-only strategy; failing to enter this segment risks long-term irrelevance despite lower margins.

**Steelman:** A dedicated, autonomous team can bypass corporate inertia to rapidly deploy a standard-based PC via retail channels, capturing market share before the window closes.

**Attack & Options:**
The proposal admits severe risks: commoditization (copying), supply dependence, margin erosion, and channel incompetence. However, the alternative—"do nothing" or building a proprietary system internally—fails the urgency test. Internal development would take too long, missing the growth wave. Proprietary systems risk high cost and low adoption in a nascent market. The "open design" is not a bug but a feature for speed; it trades defensibility for time-to-market. Lower margins are acceptable if the volume secures a foothold in a new category.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This contradicts the current vertical integration model but aligns with the survival imperative. If we wait for perfect conditions (retail experience, proprietary software), rivals will dominate. The pre-mortem failure mode is that the mainframe culture strangles the small team or that suppliers undercut us. The proposal mitigates the first via team separation; the second is an industry-wide reality for standard parts, not a unique flaw.

**Blockers vs. Risks:**
No factual blocker exists. The lack of retail experience and lower margins are *risks*, not proof of failure. The proposal explicitly accepts these trade-offs to achieve speed. The risk of copying is inherent to the PC architecture but is outweighed by the risk of non-participation. The "kill criterion" here is implicit: if the team cannot ship within a year or secure key software partners, the strategy fails, but the *decision to start* is not blocked by these possibilities.

**Decision:**
The proposal correctly identifies that the cost of delay exceeds the cost of execution errors. It isolates the innovation from the core business to ensure speed. The risks are managed by the structural separation of the team.

DECISION: GO
