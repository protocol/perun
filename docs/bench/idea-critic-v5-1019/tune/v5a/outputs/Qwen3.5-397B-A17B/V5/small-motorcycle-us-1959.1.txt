First call: NO-GO. The proposal ignores a fatal mechanical mismatch: the product (50cc) cannot perform the core function of US transport (highway speeds), and the target channel (sporting-goods) lacks the service infrastructure for motor vehicles.

**Steelman:** A small, low-cost motorcycle can create a new "commuter" segment in the US by bypassing traditional dealers, provided the vehicle is reliable enough for local roads and the brand can overcome its obscurity.

**Attack:**
1.  **Mechanical Blocker:** The proposal admits larger models fail at US highway speeds; a 50cc engine is physically incapable of sustaining interstate travel, which is mandatory for many American commuters. If the bike cannot reach the destination, the "commuter" value proposition collapses immediately.
2.  **Channel Mismatch:** Selling complex machinery requiring maintenance, licensing, and insurance through sporting-goods stores (accustomed to tents and balls) creates a service vacuum. Without a dedicated dealer network for repairs and parts, early failures will destroy the brand before it gains traction.
3.  **Financial Risk:** Tying up scarce foreign currency in a subsidiary for a product that may be legally or practically unusable on primary roads is an unjustifiable gamble compared to licensing or waiting for market maturation.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Preserve capital; avoid reputational damage from a flawed launch.
*   *Licensing:* Partner with an existing US manufacturer to adapt the design for higher speeds without capital expenditure.
*   *Niche Only:* Target off-road/recreation use only, explicitly excluding "commuting" claims until infrastructure is proven.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, bikes are stranded on highways; sporting-goods stores refuse to handle warranty repairs; the subsidiary burns cash with zero repeat customers.

**Blockers:**
*   The 50cc engine cannot meet the speed requirements of the stated "commuter" use case in the US geography.
*   The proposed sales channel cannot support the mandatory after-sales service for motor vehicles.

**Risks:** Brand unknown; cultural preference for large bikes.

**Kill Criterion:** If a prototype cannot sustain 55mph for 30 minutes without failure, the project stops. No pilot should proceed without a dedicated service network contract.

DECISION: NO-GO
