First call: GO. The stated facts show a dominant search business with no hardware presence facing a future where mobile access could bypass its web index. The $50M price for a six-person team building an open-source OS is a cheap option on the distribution layer, not a hardware play.

Steelman: Acquiring the OS secures the platform layer to ensure mobile traffic remains searchable, leveraging open-source to bypass carrier and handset maker gatekeepers who resist proprietary stacks.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Risk being locked out of mobile indexing as carriers build walled gardens; cost is eventual revenue collapse.
2. Build internally: Too slow; misses the 2005 window; high failure risk against established OS vendors.
3. Partner with handset makers: They have no incentive to adopt a new, unproven platform from a search company; high friction.

Fit: Directly moves the north star of "organizing the world's information" by preventing mobile fragmentation from breaking search access. Assumes open-source can disrupt carrier control (plausible given Linux trajectory).

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if handset makers ignore the OS despite it being free, or if carriers block installation. However, the "free" aspect lowers the barrier enough for at least one major player to experiment. This is a two-way door; if it fails, the loss is capped at $50M (small for a search giant). Delay costs the first-mover advantage in defining the mobile web standard.

Evidence: The proposal states the market is dominated by incumbents with no reason to adopt new platforms. However, the "open-source" angle changes the incentive structure: it reduces cost to zero for handset makers, creating a reason to adopt where none existed. The $50M price tag is the key fact; it represents a low-cost hedge against existential threat.

Blockers: NONE. The risks (no revenue model, adoption hurdles) are inherent to the bet but do not contradict the core logic that securing the platform is cheaper than losing the index.
Risks: Slow adoption, carrier resistance. These are managed by the open-source strategy and the small initial cap.

Kill criterion: If no major handset maker commits to the platform within 24 months, pivot to licensing the search stack only. Leading metric: Number of devices shipping with the OS by Q4 2007.

DECISION: GO
