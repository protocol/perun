First call: GO. The shift from desktop to web, combined with satellite imagery and routing, creates a step-change in accessibility and utility that dominates the status quo, despite known risks.

**Steelman:** A free, browser-based map with satellite views and routing will become the global standard because immediate, zero-friction access outweighs the limitations of 2004 browser graphics and the lack of an immediate revenue model.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Retain the desktop niche. Result: Irrelevance as bandwidth increases and user expectations shift to instant access.
*   **Paid/Enterprise Model:** Fails to achieve critical mass; network effects require ubiquity.
*   **Wait for Tech:** Cedes the market to incumbents who are already moving.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** This aligns with organizing the world's information. Failure mode: Licensing costs bankrupt the project before ad-tech matures to monetize traffic. However, the asset (imagery/data) retains value even if the product pivots. The "slow browser" risk is transient; Moore's Law solves this faster than competitors can lock in users.

**Evidence & Economics:** The acquisition provides the seed technology. While revenue is undefined, the traffic volume potential from "free + satellite + routing" creates a unique data moat that justifies the licensing burn rate as a customer acquisition cost. The proposed features are the minimum viable product for dominance; removing any kills the value proposition.

**Blocker Test:**
*   **Blockers:** NONE. The risks (cost, competition, tech limits) are known variables, not factual contradictions. No alternative reaches the same upside cheaper. The lack of a revenue model is a timing issue, not a feasibility blocker, given the strategic value of the data.
*   **Risks:** High burn rate; browser performance friction; monetization lag. These are managed by scaling infrastructure elastically and treating licensing as an investment in market share.

**Kill Criterion:** If user growth does not exceed 100k daily active users within 6 months of launch despite marketing spend, the hypothesis of "frictionless adoption" is false, and the project must pivot to enterprise licensing or shut down.

The opportunity cost of delaying this integration exceeds the cost of failure. The market window is open now.

DECISION: GO
