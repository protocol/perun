First call: NO-GO. The plan burns cash on every unit sold while betting on perpetual capital market access to fund the loss.

Steelman: Aggressively capture dominant market share through an IPO war chest, using predatory pricing and mass media to create a monopoly that can later raise prices and achieve logistics density.

Options:
1. **Proposed Plan:** Extreme cash burn; high risk if IPO window closes or margins don't recover; irreversible once ad spend and warehouses are committed.
2. **Do Nothing:** Continue organic growth; slower capture but positive unit economics; fully reversible.
3. **Targeted Expansion:** Focus on high-margin niches without national TV or below-cost selling; moderate growth with sustainable unit economics.

Fit: Assumes "growth at all costs" overrides solvency. In early 2000, the dot-com bubble is peaking; capital markets are volatile. A strategy requiring continuous external funding to subsidize negative gross margins is misaligned with imminent market contraction risks.

Pre-mortem: The IPO window slams shut due to market correction. The company cannot fund the TV campaign or cover shipping losses on below-cost goods. Competitors with deeper pockets or better unit economics survive the price war; this company runs out of cash within months. This is a one-way door: once warehouses are built and ad contracts signed, the burn rate is fixed. The cost of delay is low; waiting preserves cash until market conditions stabilize.

Evidence: The proposal admits to selling products below cost (negative gross margin) and heavy shipping costs. Mathematically, every sale increases the loss. The plan relies entirely on "proceeds" from an IPO and future capital availability, both uncertain in early 2000.

Blockers:
1. **Negative Unit Economics:** Selling below cost + high shipping = guaranteed cash drain per transaction. No path to profitability without assuming infinite future capital.
2. **Capital Market Reliance:** The entire strategy collapses if the IPO fails or follow-on funding dries up, a high probability in early 2000.
3. **Kill Criterion Failure:** No stop condition exists for the burn rate before the monopoly upside is realized.

Risks: Competitor retaliation, inability to integrate acquisitions, shipping logistics failures.

The plan commits to full-scale irreversible spend (warehouses, national TV) before proving unit economics can ever be positive. It lacks a capped pilot.

DECISION: NO-GO
