First call: GO. The request comes from the largest customer, addressing a verified growth trajectory with a specific unit-cost target. The design features (raised cockpit, wide-body) directly enable the stated freighter conversion upside, creating a dual-market hedge.

Steelman: Building a massively scalable, convertible freighter/passenger jet secures market dominance against both subsonic growth and potential supersonic shifts by owning the high-capacity niche no other aircraft can fill.

Alternatives analysis:
1. Do nothing: Cedes the high-growth, low-cost segment to competitors; risks losing the anchor customer.
2. Supersonic only: Ignores immediate economic reality; technology is immature and capacity is low.
3. Stretch existing jet: Insufficient for the "2.5x" requirement; inefficient aerodynamics.
The proposal beats "do nothing" by capturing a unique value pool.

Fit: Aligns with the vision of dominating global air travel volume, not just speed.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the engine fails certification or if the single customer cancels before completion. However, the freighter conversion capability mitigates the risk of passenger demand shifts (e.g., to supersonic). The cost of delay is total loss of the strategic moat; a competitor will inevitably attempt this scale.

Evidence: The customer's explicit request validates demand. The "unproven" engine is a necessary step for efficiency at this scale; no existing engine suffices. The "single airline" risk is offset by the universality of the cost-per-seat problem for all carriers.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (cost, engine, supersonic threat, single buyer) are execution challenges or market uncertainties, not factual contradictions. The supersonic threat actually strengthens the case for a convertible freighter design. The massive outlay is the barrier to entry that protects the winner.

Risks: Engine development delays; over-reliance on one launch customer.
Kill Criterion: If the engine supplier fails to deliver a working prototype within 24 months, pause airframe finalization. Leading Metric: Commitment of a second launch customer before the factory tooling begins.

The proposal addresses the core economic driver (cost per seat) while future-proofing via freighter conversion. The risks are inherent to breakthrough innovation but do not invalidate the strategic necessity.

DECISION: GO
