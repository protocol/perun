First call: GO. The proposal converts a "web-only" limitation into a controlled ecosystem, directly addressing the risk of rivals locking in developers while mitigating security and stability through a single review gate.

**Steelman:** A curated native app store creates a defensible moat by attracting top-tier developers with a monetization model (70/30 split) and distribution scale, while the mandatory review process preserves the device's signature polish and stability better than open sideloading or rival fragmented ecosystems.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Retain web-only apps. Risk: Developers migrate to rivals with native APIs; device becomes a secondary toy.
2.  **Open sideloading:** Match rival openness. Risk: Malware and crashes destroy brand reputation for stability; carrier partnerships fracture.
3.  **Proposed Store:** Balanced approach. Cost: High operational overhead for reviews. Benefit: Controls quality, captures revenue, satisfies carriers via oversight.

**Fit:** Aligns with the north star of a "premium, polished user experience." Web apps cannot match native performance or hardware access required for category-defining tools.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the review bottleneck stifles innovation, causing developers to ignore the platform despite the store. Or, if carriers block the store entirely. However, the 30% fee funds the review team, and carrier objections are mitigated by the company retaining final approval authority (unlike open web).

**Evidence:** Rivals already have ecosystems; staying web-only cedes the developer mindshare. The 30% cut is standard for value provided (distribution + payment).

**Blockers:** NONE. The concerns (security, stability, carrier relations) are features of the proposed solution (review gate), not flaws in the plan. The plan explicitly addresses the risks it creates.

**Risks:** Review slowdowns; carrier pushback on specific app categories. These are execution risks, not structural blockers.

**Kill Criterion:** If the review process averages >7 days per app within 6 months, causing a measurable drop in top-developer submissions, the process must be streamlined or tiered.

**Decision:** The proposal turns the company's previous "web-only" stance into a competitive advantage by offering safety and quality that open rivals cannot match.

DECISION: GO
