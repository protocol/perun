First call: GO. The company already possesses the core competency in silicon design for its mobile devices, creating a strategic foundation for vertical integration in laptops to unify architecture and capture margin.

**Steelman:** Leveraging proven mobile silicon expertise to replace third-party laptop processors will yield superior performance-per-watt and tighter hardware-software integration, differentiating the product line despite transition costs.

**Attack & Analysis:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" maintains status quo but cedes long-term differentiation to the supplier. "Hybrid approach" delays inevitable friction. The proposed path is the only one that secures full stack control.
2.  **Fit:** Aligns with a north star of end-to-end user experience control.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if legacy software compatibility breaks or performance lags behind the supplier's high-end offerings. However, the proposal acknowledges software continuity as a risk, implying mitigation is part of the plan.
4.  **Evidence:** The premise rests on the fact that the company *already* designs successful phone/tablet chips. This is not a greenfield gamble but an expansion of existing capability.
5.  **Blockers vs. Risks:**
    *   *Risks:* Engineering cost, buyer confusion, supplier fallout, unproven laptop performance. These are execution hurdles, not fundamental flaws. They are managed via kill criteria (e.g., if benchmark X is not met by date Y, pivot).
    *   *Blockers:* None found. The risks cited are standard for platform shifts and do not contradict the core claim that the company *can* do this given its mobile success. The "unproven" nature of laptop chips is a condition to be solved, not a static fact preventing the start.

**Kill Criterion:** If the first prototype fails to run core legacy software suites at >90% efficiency of the incumbent supplier's chip within 18 months, halt the transition.

**Conclusion:** The strategic upside of owning the silicon roadmap outweighs the transitional friction. The company has the requisite skills; the risks are manageable execution details, not showstoppers.

DECISION: GO
