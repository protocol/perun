First call: GO. The market collapse is a distribution and perception failure, not a demand extinction; repositioning the product as a toy bypasses retailer bias against "video games."

Steelman: By masking the console as an "Entertainment System" with non-game peripherals (robot, light gun) and assuming retailer risk via stock buybacks, the company can re-enter the collapsed market, bypass the "fad" stigma, and control software quality to prevent another crash.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Cede the entire Western market to home computers; zero revenue, total loss of global expansion potential.
2. **Launch as a standard console:** Immediate rejection by retailers refusing "video game" inventory; certain failure.
3. **Proposed strategy:** High upfront cost (buybacks, toy tooling) but unlocks shelf space and rebuilds consumer trust through quality control. It beats "do nothing" by capturing a vacuum left by retreating competitors.

Fit: Aligns with the goal of global dominance. The "toy" classification is a tactical bridge to the ultimate vision of a home entertainment hub.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the "toy" disguise fools no one, or if the limited game library fails to sustain hardware sales after the novelty of the robot wears off. The buyback promise could bankrupt the division if the product truly fails, but this is the cost of entry. This is a two-way door; if it fails, the brand retreats to Japan with financial losses but intact reputation elsewhere.

Evidence: The proposal correctly identifies the 1983 collapse causes (oversaturation, poor quality). The lockout chip directly addresses the quality control issue that caused the crash. The "toy" bundle addresses the retailer refusal.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (retailer refusal, no track record, developer anger) are precisely what the proposed measures (rebranding, buybacks, lockout) are designed to neutralize. The financial exposure of the buyback is a calculated risk, not a factual impossibility.

Risks: High capital exposure from returns; potential developer backlash limiting software variety.

Kill criterion: If retailer uptake is below 40% of targeted stores within 6 months despite the buyback offer, halt expansion and liquidate remaining stock. Leading metric: Sell-through rate of bundled units vs. standalone returns.

DECISION: GO
