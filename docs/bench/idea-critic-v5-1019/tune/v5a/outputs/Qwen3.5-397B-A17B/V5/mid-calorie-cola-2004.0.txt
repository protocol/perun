First call: NO-GO. The proposal targets a fragmented need with a product that may satisfy neither existing segment, while charging a premium for a potentially fleeting trend.

**Steelman:** A mid-calorie cola captures young men who want taste without the full sugar load of regular cola but distrust or dislike the aftertaste of artificial sweeteners in diet cola, creating a new category before rivals do.

**Attack & Options:**
The core failure mode is the "middle trap." Consumers reducing calories typically seek maximum reduction (Diet) or accept the trade-off for taste (Regular). A half-sugar option risks tasting too sweet for dieters and not sweet enough for regular drinkers, satisfying no one. The premium price exacerbates this by raising the cost of trial for a product with an unproven value proposition.

Compared to "Do Nothing" (letting Diet grow and Regular stabilize) or "Reformulate Existing" (tweaking current lines), this launch requires massive campaign spend to educate consumers on a confusing new benefit ("half sugar") during a potentially transient low-carb fad. If the trend fades, the product becomes obsolete inventory. The rival's similar move suggests a land grab, but being second with a flawed value prop is worse than being absent.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
Assuming the goal is growth in a declining regular segment, this assumes young men care about "half sugar" specifically, rather than just "low calorie." In a pre-mortem where the launch fails: sales are flat because the product cannibalizes regular cola without pulling significant diet users, the premium price limits volume, and the low-carb trend collapses within 18 months, leaving a niche product with no audience.

**Blockers:**
The proposal admits the risk that the drink satisfies neither group yet offers no evidence (taste tests, concept scores) that a distinct "mid-calorie" preference exists. Launching a premium-priced product into a trend that may be a fad, without proof that the "half-sugar" taste profile is desirable, is a factual gap that blocks approval. The risk of cannibalization without net new growth is too high given the uncertainty.

**Kill Criterion:**
If concept testing does not show >20% purchase intent from non-diet users specifically citing "less sugar than regular but better taste than diet," the project must stop.

DECISION: NO-GO
