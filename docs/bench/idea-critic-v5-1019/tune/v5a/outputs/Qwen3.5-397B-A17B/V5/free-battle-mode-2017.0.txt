First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to enter a saturated market with a dominant incumbent using an unproven business model and an unrealistic timeline, creating immediate existential risk.

**Steelman:** Reusing assets allows a rapid, low-cost entry into the popular last-player-standing genre to capture users via a free-to-play model, monetizing through cosmetics without upfront cost to players.

**Attack:**
1.  **Market Fit:** A rival already "owns the genre." In network-effect driven multiplayer games, the second mover rarely survives unless significantly differentiated. Reusing assets from a cooperative game suggests the core loop may lack the unique polish required to displace the incumbent.
2.  **Cannibalization:** The risk of cannibalizing the paid game is not just a risk; it is a near-certainty if the free mode offers comparable content. This erodes the studio's only proven revenue stream to chase an unproven one.
3.  **Operational Reality:** Two months is insufficient to build, balance, and launch a stable competitive multiplayer title, especially for a studio with zero free-to-play experience. Live operations, anti-cheat, and server scaling are not "asset reuse" tasks; they are new competencies.
4.  **Business Model:** Running a free-to-play economy requires specific expertise in retention and monetization tuning that the studio explicitly lacks. Launching without this invites rapid failure.

**Blockers:**
*   **Capability Gap:** The studio has no experience running a free-to-play business. This is a stated fact that blocks execution of the monetization strategy.
*   **Timeline Fantasy:** A two-month window for a new competitive multiplayer title is operationally impossible given the scope, creating a high probability of a broken launch.
*   **Strategic Contradiction:** The plan relies on a free product to save the business while simultaneously threatening the only existing revenue source (the paid game) without a proven method to replace it.

**Risks:** Dominant competitor lock-in, total cannibalization of paid sales, technical failure at launch, inability to monetize.

**Kill Criterion:** If the studio cannot demonstrate a functional server stress test and a validated monetization simulation within 30 days, the project must terminate. However, given the current lack of capability, the project cannot even start safely.

The combination of a dominant rival, an unproven business model for this team, and an impossible timeline constitutes a fatal flaw.

DECISION: NO-GO
