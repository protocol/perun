First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a $200M annual capacity (40k units × $5k) before resolving fundamental market and regulatory blockers.

**Steelman:** A breakthrough urban mobility device will create a new mass market despite high cost and regulatory ambiguity, justifying immediate full-scale production.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Regulatory Void:** The proposal admits legal status on sidewalks and roads is "unclear." Launching a vehicle without defined operating grounds creates an immediate existential risk of bans or seizures, invalidating the business model before sales begin.
2.  **Demand Mismatch:** The price ($5,000) is prohibitive for mass adoption, yet the factory is sized for mass volume (40k units). With "unproven" uses beyond institutions and high social friction ("conspicuous"), there is no evidence of 40k annual buyers. Building for scale before validating unit economics is capital malpractice.
3.  **Utility Constraint:** The inability to carry cargo limits practical use cases, further shrinking the addressable market below the proposed production capacity.

**Risks:** High unit cost, niche appeal, potential public ridicule, and regulatory crackdown. These are not merely risks; combined with the blockers, they indicate a high probability of catastrophic inventory overhang.

**Analysis:** The plan fails the "do nothing" comparison. A sound operator would prototype with limited runs, lobby for regulations, and validate consumer demand before committing to heavy fixed costs. The proposal demands full-scale factory commitment while admitting the core prerequisites for success (legal clarity, mass demand) are missing. The "kill criterion" for this plan—regulatory approval or confirmed orders for 10% of capacity—cannot be met today. Proceeding as proposed guarantees a massive sunk cost with no guaranteed route to revenue.

**Verdict:** The plan commits irreversible capital based on assumptions contradicted by its own stated facts (unclear legality, unproven demand). It lacks a staged approach or pilot cap.

DECISION: NO-GO
