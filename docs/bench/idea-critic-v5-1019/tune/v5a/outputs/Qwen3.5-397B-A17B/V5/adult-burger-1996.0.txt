First call: NO-GO. The proposal contradicts the brand's core identity and customer value proposition without a mechanism to overcome them.

**Steelman:** A massive ad spend can temporarily overwrite brand perception, forcing adults to trial a premium burger despite the chain's "kids/value" reputation.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit:** The strategy fights the "North Star" (child-centered, value) rather than leveraging it. Adults visiting for kids' meals seek speed and low cost; a high-price item creates friction, not appeal.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves margin. A "better way" is introducing adult sides or coffee (low capex, fits existing traffic) rather than a flagship burger requiring identity reconstruction.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The campaign launches. Adults perceive the price as unjustified for a "kids' brand." The massive spend burns cash with low conversion. Regular value customers feel alienated by the shift in focus. The brand becomes confused: neither the best for kids nor credible for adults.
4.  **Evidence/Logic:** The proposal admits the risks (brand mismatch, price sensitivity) but attempts to solve them solely with spending volume. There is no evidence that advertising budget alone can invert deep-seated brand positioning, especially when the product (premium burger) conflicts with the operational reality (fast food).
5.  **Blocker:** The plan commits to the "largest advertising budget" *before* validating demand or brand elasticity. This is an irreversible financial exposure based on an unproven assumption that perception can be bought instantly. The lack of a pilot, test market, or phased rollout means failure results in total loss of the marketing capex with no learning loop.

**Kill Criterion Check:** The proposal lacks a stop condition. If early sales miss targets by 20% in a test region, the plan as written implies continuing the full global spend. Without a capped, time-boxed pilot to verify if adults will actually pay a premium at this specific brand, the risk is uncapped.

**Verdict:** The strategy relies on brute force spending to solve a fundamental product-market-brand mismatch. It fails the "cheaper alternative" test (test first) and presents a clear financial blocker (uncapped spend on unproven demand).

DECISION: NO-GO
