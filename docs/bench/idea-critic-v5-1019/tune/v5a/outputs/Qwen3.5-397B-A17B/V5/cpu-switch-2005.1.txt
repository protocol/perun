First call: GO. The current roadmap fails the core requirement for thin, efficient laptops, and the gap versus the dominant maker is widening; staying put guarantees product irrelevance.

**Steelman:** Migrating to the dominant architecture is the only viable path to competitive hardware within two years, and a translation layer mitigates the immediate software ecosystem risk sufficiently to bridge the gap.

**Attack & Options:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Guarantees continued thermal/performance deficits. Market share erosion is certain as competitors ship superior thin forms.
*   **Dual Architecture:** Increases BOM cost and engineering complexity without solving the root supply issue.
*   **The Plan:** Risks are real but manageable. Sales freeze is a short-term revenue dip versus long-term survival. Developer friction is inherent to any transition; the translation layer buys time. Performance loss in translation is an acceptable trade-off for native efficiency gains in new apps. Brand dilution is preferable to brand irrelevance.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the translation layer is too slow for pro users or if the timeline slips beyond two years, allowing competitors to cement dominance. However, the "painful earlier transition" proves the organization possesses the necessary, albeit painful, change management muscle memory.

**Evidence & Fit:** The premise rests on the supplier's failure to deliver cool/efficient chips. If this fact holds, the current strategy has a hard ceiling. The proposal directly addresses the "thin laptop" market requirement which the current path cannot meet.

**Blockers:** NONE. No factual contradiction exists in the proposal; the risks are execution challenges, not fundamental flaws. The alternative (staying) is a guaranteed loss.
**Risks:** Sales freeze, developer churn, translation performance, brand dilution. These become kill criteria: if translation performance drops below 80% of native for key apps by month 18, or if sales drop >30% YoY without recovery signals by month 6 post-announcement, the plan must pivot.

**Kill Criterion:** If the translation layer cannot sustain >70% native performance on core professional workflows by the 18-month mark, halt full migration and extend the bridge or re-architect. Leading metric: % of top 100 apps running natively vs. translated at 12 months.

DECISION: GO
