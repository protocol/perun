First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits the company to an existential bet on a single customer's demand while ignoring a looming technological shift (supersonic travel) and relying on unproven core components (engine, factory scale).

**Steelman:** Building a massive, convertible freighter jet now secures the dominant market position against future cargo demand and leverages the largest customer's growth, accepting high initial risk for long-term monopoly power.

**Attack:**
1.  **Technological Obsolescence:** The premise assumes subsonic jets remain the standard. If supersonic transport becomes viable as predicted, this massive capital investment becomes a stranded asset before paying back.
2.  **Single-Customer Dependency:** Committing to a factory and engine development based on one airline's order creates catastrophic concentration risk. If that airline falters or cancels, the company faces insolvency.
3.  **Unproven Supply Chain:** Relying on a supplier for an unproven high-bypass engine introduces a critical single point of failure outside the company's direct control.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" or incrementally stretching existing models avoids the existential downside while testing supersonic waters. A smaller, twin-engine wide-body (if technically feasible then) would reduce engine risk and fuel burn without the massive factory outlay.

**Pre-mortem:** The project fails because supersonic jets render subsonic giants economically unviable for passengers, and the freighter conversion market is insufficient to support the debt load. The engine supplier fails to deliver on time, causing massive penalties.

**Blockers:**
*   **Technological Displacement:** The stated expectation that supersonic airliners will replace subsonic jets directly contradicts the long-term viability of a larger subsonic airframe. This is a stated fact in the proposal that removes the route to upside.
*   **Concentration Risk:** The financial exposure exceeds any prior outlay, yet relies on a single confirmed order. This violates basic portfolio logic for a project of this magnitude.

**Kill Criterion:** If a viable supersonic prototype achieves regulatory certification within 5 years, the project must halt.

The proposal ignores the most significant threat to its core assumption (subsonic dominance) while betting the entire company on it. The risk profile is not just high; it is potentially terminal without a diversified order book or a resolved supersonic strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
