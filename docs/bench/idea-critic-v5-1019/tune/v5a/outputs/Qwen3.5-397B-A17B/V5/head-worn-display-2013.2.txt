First call: NO-GO. The proposal ignores stated, fundamental blockers that prevent the product from functioning as an "everyday consumer product" at the proposed scale.

**Steelman:** An invitation-only launch at $1,500 will validate demand and refine the device for a mass-market release by filtering for tech-tolerant users.

**Attack:**
The proposal explicitly lists risks that are not merely "execution difficulties" but structural contradictions to the stated goal of an "everyday consumer product."
1.  **Social & Privacy Blocker:** A device with a always-on camera facing "privacy objections" and "social acceptance" issues cannot function as an everyday item. Unlike a smartphone (camera pointed away or explicitly raised), this form factor creates immediate interpersonal friction and potential legal liabilities in private spaces. The proposal offers no mitigation for a feature that alienates the wearer's immediate social circle.
2.  **Utility Gap:** The admission of "no clear everyday use for most people" contradicts the mass-market positioning. High price ($1,500 is ~$2,000+ today) is defensible only for solved, critical problems. Paying a premium for a device with undefined utility and social stigma is irrational for the target mass market.
3.  **Hardware Constraint:** "Short battery life" renders an "everyday" wearable unusable. If it cannot last a standard waking day without anxiety or tethering, it fails the core definition of the category.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoids reputational damage from a failed consumer push and legal battles over privacy.
*   **Enterprise Pivot:** Target specific industrial use cases (logistics, surgery) where the camera is a feature, privacy is controlled, and cost is secondary. This bypasses social acceptance and utility gaps.

**Pre-mortem:**
The product launches, early adopters wear it once to novelty, then stop due to battery anxiety and hostile social reactions. Media narratives cement the device as a privacy nightmare ("Glassholes"). The brand becomes toxic for consumer wearables, killing future iterations before they mature.

**Blockers:**
*   Stated fact: "No clear everyday use" for the mass market.
*   Stated fact: "Privacy objections" and "social acceptance" issues inherent to the form factor.
*   Stated fact: "Short battery life" incompatible with all-day wear.

These are not risks to be managed; they are disqualifiers for the proposed strategy. The plan attempts to sell an unfinished, socially hostile, expensive solution to a problem that does not exist for the general public.

DECISION: NO-GO
