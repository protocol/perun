First call: GO. The market gap for an affordable, lightweight roadster is distinct, and the proposed specs directly address the void left by disappearing competitors.

**Steelman:** A focused, low-cost rear-drive convertible can profitably serve a neglected niche by minimizing complexity and leveraging existing components, ignoring the "dead niche" consensus of mass-market rivals.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary objection is the shrinking segment. However, a shrinking total market often indicates a lack of supply rather than zero demand; mass-market exits create a vacuum for a specialist product. The "do nothing" alternative guarantees zero revenue from this demographic. A "safe" front-drive family car alternative fails to capture the specific emotional utility of a roadster. The lack of heritage is irrelevant if the price-to-fun ratio is compelling; enthusiasts prioritize driving dynamics over badge history.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
Assuming the goal is brand revitalization and margin improvement rather than volume leadership, this fits. If we assume failure, it stems from over-engineering (violating "lightweight") or pricing too high, not the two-seat layout itself. The cost of delay is ceding the remaining enthusiast loyalty to used markets or imports.

**Evidence & Blockers:**
The proposal relies on the factual premise that competitors have exited. No internal data contradicts the feasibility of front-engine, rear-drive layouts for a mid-size maker. The risks cited (safety costs, limited buyers) are inherent to the class, not unique flaws in this plan; they are managed by the "affordable" and "lightweight" constraints which lower the break-even point.

**Decision:**
There are no factual blockers. The risks are manageable execution challenges, not reasons to stop. The plan beats "do nothing" by capturing an uncontested niche. The kill criterion is clear: if initial pre-orders do not cover tooling costs within six months, the project stops. As proposed, the logic holds.

DECISION: GO
