First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks to commit to full-scale manufacturing and direct sales without evidence that buyers will pay a premium for an unproven mechanism, facing entrenched incumbents who can copy the idea instantly.

Steelman: A superior, bagless technology justifies a premium price and can disrupt the market if early adopters validate the value proposition before incumbents react.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** License the patent to an incumbent for immediate royalties, avoiding manufacturing risk.
2. **Pilot first:** Build a limited run to test price elasticity and retention in a controlled region before founding a full company.
3. **Proposed plan:** Full company launch immediately. This fails to beat "do nothing" or "pilot" on risk/reversibility given the lack of sales data.

Fit: Assumes a market desire for higher upfront cost in exchange for long-term savings (no bags), but this is unverified. No stated north star is moved by speculation alone.

Pre-mortem: The company launches, burns capital on tooling and inventory, and fails because consumers refuse to pay 2x the price for a strange-looking machine, or because a major brand releases a cheaper clone within months. This is a one-way door with high capital loss. The cost of delay is low; validating demand first prevents total loss.

Evidence: The proposal admits buyers "may not pay extra" and incumbents "could copy." These are not risks; they are stated probabilities that contradict the core assumption of immediate viability. There is no evidence of a waiting list, pre-orders, or successful prototype testing with paying strangers.

Blockers:
1. **Unit economics unproven:** The claim that buyers will pay a premium is an assumption contradicted by the admitted risk that they "may not."
2. **No stop condition:** The plan commits to founding a company and manufacturing without a capped pilot or kill criterion. If no one buys it, the loss is total, not capped.
3. **Competitive vulnerability:** Launching without a moat (brand, scale, or locked distribution) against copy-capable incumbents is suicide without first establishing a foothold.

Risks: Manufacturing defects, supply chain failure, marketing inefficiency.

Kill check: The plan lacks a metric to stop. A sound version would require: "Sell 500 units at full price in 90 days; if <200, pivot to licensing." Without this, the bet is uncapped.

DECISION: NO-GO
