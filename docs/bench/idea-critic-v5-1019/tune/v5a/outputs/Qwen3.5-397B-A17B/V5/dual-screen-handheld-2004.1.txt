First call: GO. The proposal avoids a direct hardware arms race against a well-funded rival by leveraging asymmetric innovation (dual screens, touch) to access an underserved demographic (broad audience) at a lower price point, while maintaining revenue from the legacy install base.

Steelman: Differentiation through novel interaction models and price accessibility will expand the total addressable market faster than marginal graphics improvements, securing long-term software sales despite initial developer friction.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (developer confusion, unproven touch mechanics, gimmick perception) are execution challenges, not factual contradictions. The proposal explicitly accepts the cost of two screens but offsets it with a lower consumer price target, implying a viable margin structure. No internal evidence contradicts the claim that this approach broadens the audience.

**Risks:**
1.  **Developer Friction:** Unusual hardware may delay software library growth.
2.  **Market Perception:** The device may be dismissed as a toy rather than a serious competitor.
3.  **Unproven Mechanics:** Touch-screen gaming lacks a established hit portfolio.

**Kill Criteria:**
If, within 12 months of launch, third-party software titles utilizing the touch interface do not exceed 20% of the total new release catalog, or if the attach rate of software per unit sold falls below 4.0, the strategy is failing to convert hardware novelty into sustainable ecosystem value. The leading metric to watch is "weekly active users playing touch-native titles" with a review date set for the end of the first fiscal quarter post-launch.

**Strongest Attack Survived:**
The argument that "stronger graphics define market leadership" was tested against the premise of "broadening the audience." Historical data in this domain suggests that casual expansion often prioritizes accessibility and price over fidelity. The proposal's decision to keep the older handheld on sale mitigates the risk of alienating core gamers who demand higher fidelity, effectively segmenting the market rather than ceding it. The lack of proven touch games is a gap this specific hardware launch is designed to fill, not a reason to abstain.

DECISION: GO
