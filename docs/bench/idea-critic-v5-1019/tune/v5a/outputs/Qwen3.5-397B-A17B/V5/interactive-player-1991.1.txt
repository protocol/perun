First call: NO-GO. The $1,000 price point creates an immediate mismatch between the vague value proposition ("interactive multimedia") and the cost, especially when cheaper alternatives (game consoles, evolving PCs) already serve adjacent needs.

Steelman: A dedicated $1,000 appliance successfully bridges the gap between passive TV viewing and complex PC usage by offering superior interactive media playback that consoles cannot match, justifying the premium through exclusive disc formats.

Attack: The proposal fails the "cheaper alternative" test. Personal computers are rapidly gaining multimedia capabilities at a similar or lower effective cost (since they offer general utility), while game consoles provide entertainment at a fraction of the price. The "interactive disc" format is unproven with a tiny library, creating a chicken-and-egg problem: users won't pay $1,000 without content, and developers won't create content without users. The risk that "buyers may not understand what the product is for" is fatal at this price; high-cost items require clear, immediate utility, not education.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Wait for PC multimedia to mature or console makers to add disc drives. Cost: $0. Risk: Missing first-mover advantage. Reversibility: N/A.
2. Partner/License: License the disc technology to existing console or PC manufacturers. Cost: Low. Risk: Lower margins. Reversibility: High.
3. Lower-cost pilot: Launch a stripped-down player under $500 with a bundled hit title. Cost: Medium. Risk: Hardware loss leader. Reversibility: Medium.

The proposed $1,000 standalone unit beats none of these. It is more expensive than the "do nothing" option (which avoids hardware risk), less flexible than the PC path, and lacks the ecosystem of consoles.

Blockers:
1. Price-to-Value Mismatch: $1,000 is unjustifiable for a single-function device with a small library when multi-function PCs are competing directly.
2. Undefined Audience: The admission that buyers may not understand the product confirms there is no clear market fit for a premium appliance.

Risks: Small title library, rapid PC obsolescence of the dedicated player, consumer confusion.

Kill Criterion: If pre-orders do not reach 50,000 units within 3 months of announcement at the $1,000 price point, cancel hardware production.

DECISION: NO-GO
