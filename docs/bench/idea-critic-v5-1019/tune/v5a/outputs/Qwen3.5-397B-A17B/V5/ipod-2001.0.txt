First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a niche computer maker with no consumer electronics experience to enter a stagnant market with an unproven, expensive device that works only for its tiny existing user base, while facing supply chain hurdles and a lack of legal music content.

Steelman: A tightly integrated, high-capacity device could offer a superior user experience that justifies a premium, potentially expanding the computer brand's appeal if the ecosystem lock-in drives loyalty.

However, the blockers are fatal under the stated facts. First, the addressable market is effectively zero for growth: restricting usage to owners of a "small market share" computer, when the device itself is intended to be the growth engine, creates a circular dependency that prevents adoption. Second, the value proposition collapses without content; selling a $400 hardware vessel when "the music industry has not yet licensed legal downloads at scale" means the device has no legitimate utility superior to cheaper competitors or CDs. Third, the "no consumer-electronics experience" constraint combined with a "short timeline" for complex hard-drive sourcing presents an execution risk that cannot be mitigated by strategy alone.

Alternatives: Doing nothing preserves capital. Licensing the software to Windows/Mac immediately would solve the addressable market issue but contradicts the "sold only with its own" constraint. Waiting for legal download infrastructure to mature reduces hardware obsolescence risk but misses the "under a year" window.

The pre-mortem is clear: The device launches, finds no legal music library, alienates non-computer owners due to compatibility locks, and fails to sell against cheaper, more compatible units. The cost of delay is low because the market has not yet sold well; being first in a non-existent market is a liability, not an asset. The lack of a content pipeline is a factual blocker, not a risk; the hardware cannot succeed without it, and the proposal admits it does not exist.

Blockers: No legal music supply chain; incompatible with any computer outside the small existing base; no consumer hardware experience to meet the timeline.
Risks: High price point rejection; component sourcing delays.

DECISION: NO-GO
