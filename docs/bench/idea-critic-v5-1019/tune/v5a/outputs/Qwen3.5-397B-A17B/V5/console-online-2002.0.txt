First call: NO-GO. The proposal mandates a paid subscription for a service (online play) that rivals offer for free, while the primary enabling technology (broadband) is not yet widely adopted in the target market.

**Steelman:** A unified, premium online ecosystem creates superior user retention and a new recurring revenue stream that justifies the infrastructure investment despite low initial broadband penetration.

**Attack:**
1.  **Market Fit:** In 2002, broadband adoption is too low to support a "console-wide" mandatory service. Requiring broadband excludes the vast majority of the installed base using dial-up, artificially capping the addressable market for every game.
2.  **Pricing Power:** Charging for a feature competitors give away free is a direct value subtraction. Without a massive library of "must-play" exclusive titles available at launch, users will reject the double cost (console + subscription).
3.  **Operational Risk:** The company has zero history running hosting or support operations. Building this from scratch while simultaneously launching hardware creates a high probability of service failures, damaging the brand immediately.
4.  **Alternatives:** A "freemium" model (free basic matchmaking, paid for premium features) or deferring the mandatory requirement until broadband penetration hits a critical threshold (e.g., >30%) would mitigate adoption risk. The current "all-or-nothing" paid approach ignores the "do nothing" or "phase-in" options which preserve the player base.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** Broadband penetration in 2002 is insufficient to sustain a mandatory, console-wide service without alienating the majority of users.
*   **Fact:** Competitors offer online play for free; no evidence is presented that this specific console has enough leverage to force a paid model immediately.
*   **Fact:** The company lacks the operational capability to run the service, creating a high risk of launch failure.

**Risks:** Low adoption, brand damage from service outages, developer resistance due to small user base.

**Kill Criterion:** If broadband adoption does not exceed 20% in the target demographic within 12 months, the mandatory model fails.

The proposal fails because it attempts to force a mature market behavior (paid, broadband-only online) onto an early-stage market without a bridge strategy. It bets the console's success on unproven operations and unfavorable economics.

DECISION: NO-GO
