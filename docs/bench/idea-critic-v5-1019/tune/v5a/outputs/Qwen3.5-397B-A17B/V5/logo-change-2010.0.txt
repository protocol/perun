First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to an irreversible, high-blast change with zero evidence of customer acceptance and explicit, unmitigated risks of alienating the core base.

**Steelman:** A silent, immediate global rebrand modernizes the image by shedding dated visual weight, betting that design sophistication outweighs legacy attachment.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Strategy:** The goal is to look "contemporary," not to disappear. Removing the distinctive blue square—the primary recognition asset—risks making the brand invisible, not modern. The proposal admits the square is what the brand is recognized by; discarding it contradicts the goal of maintaining market presence while updating style.
2.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume the launch failed. Why? Loyal customers felt betrayed by the silent removal of a decades-old symbol, interpreting the lack of announcement as arrogance. Social media amplified this into a "death of the brand" narrative before the company could explain the rationale. Sales dipped as trust eroded.
3.  **Evidence Gap:** The proposal explicitly states the new design was "not tested with customers." Launching a total rebrand without validation is speculation, not strategy. The risks raised (backlash, attachment) are treated as obstacles to ignore rather than variables to measure.
4.  **Alternatives:** The "do nothing" or "staged pilot" options are superior. A tested rollout in a single channel or region would provide data on customer sentiment. A transitional phase keeping the blue square but updating the typeface would balance heritage with modernity. The current plan beats neither do nothing (safety) nor a pilot (learning).

**Blocker:** The combination of "no advance announcement," "rolled out to every channel at once," and "not tested with customers" constitutes a factual failure of the plan's own success criteria. The stated risks (backlash, loss of recognition) are direct, probable outcomes of this specific approach, not mere possibilities. There is no stop condition; the damage occurs instantly upon launch.

**Kill Criterion:** If customer sentiment scores drop >10% in the first week post-launch, the brand equity is permanently damaged. The current plan offers no way to check this before the damage is done.

**Decision:** The plan fails the blocker test. It requires immediate revision to include customer testing and a phased rollout.

DECISION: NO-GO
