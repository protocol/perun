First call: NO-GO. The unit economics and market timing contradict the debt structure.

**Steelman:** A premium, niche service for global travelers can generate enough high-margin revenue quickly to service massive debt before terrestrial networks saturate the market.

**Attack:**
1.  **Market Shrinkage:** The core assumption is a large, underserved population of travelers needing voice service. However, ground cellular is spreading "fast." The window to capture users before they get cheap, convenient local SIMs or roaming agreements is narrowing daily. The "several dollars a minute" price point accelerates this churn; users will tolerate inconvenience for 10x cost savings.
2.  **Product-Market Fit:** Bulky handsets that fail indoors severely limit utility. Business travelers need reliability in airports, hotels, and conference centers—exactly where satellite signals fail without external antennas. A $3,000 handset is a prohibitive CapEx for a secondary, unreliable device.
3.  **Financial Mismatch:** The $5 billion debt load demands immediate, massive scale. The high price and product limitations inherently cap the Total Addressable Market (TAM) to a tiny fraction of what is needed to service that debt quickly. You cannot scale a luxury niche fast enough to pay off infrastructure built for mass adoption.
4.  **Distribution:** Sales channels are "not ready." Building a global retail and support network for a complex, expensive device takes years the company does not have due to debt payments.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoid sinking further capital into a launch likely to trigger immediate default.
*   **Pivot to B2B/Government:** Ignore consumers; sell only to maritime, aviation, and defense where price is irrelevant and outdoor use is standard. (Requires restructuring debt and delaying commercial launch).
*   **Wait/Partner:** Delay launch until handsets are smaller, or partner with terrestrial carriers for hybrid devices rather than competing directly.

**Pre-mortem:** Two years post-launch, subscriber growth is 10% of projections because travelers refuse to carry bulky phones for $5/min calls. Cash flow cannot cover interest. The company defaults, and the satellites become space junk.

**Blockers:**
*   The stated debt payment schedule requires mass adoption speeds that the high price and poor indoor coverage physically prevent.
*   The rapid expansion of ground cellular invalidates the "urgent need" premise for the target demographic.

**Risks:** Execution delay, handset supply chain issues, regulatory hurdles in key countries.

**Kill Criterion:** If signed pre-launch orders do not cover 12 months of debt service by Q4 1998, abort.

DECISION: NO-GO
