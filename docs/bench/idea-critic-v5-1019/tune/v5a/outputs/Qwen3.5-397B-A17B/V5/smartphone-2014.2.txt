First call: NO-GO. The proposal combines a premium price with a fragmented ecosystem and a niche hardware feature in a saturated two-platform market, creating a fundamental mismatch between cost and value.

Steelman: A differentiated shopping device leveraging 3D navigation and instant product identification could capture high-value retail customers despite a smaller app store.

Attack: The "app gap" is a fatal blocker at the proposed price point. Consumers paying flagship prices expect flagship utility (mainstream social, banking, and media apps), which a forked OS explicitly lacks. The 3D display is a novel feature, not a platform substitute; history shows hardware gimmicks cannot sustain premium pricing without software parity. Carrier exclusivity further caps the addressable market to users already committed to one network, while the unsubsidized price removes the primary incentive for carrier switching.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve capital and brand equity; avoid entering a low-margin hardware war without leverage.
2. Low-cost hardware: Release a budget device subsidized by retail margins to drive shopping volume, matching the company's successful tablet strategy.
3. Software-only approach: Build the "one-touch identify" and 3D-like features as an app for existing dominant platforms, reaching 100% of the market immediately.

Pre-mortem: The device launches to initial press curiosity but fails to gain traction as reviewers cite missing apps. Sales miss targets within Q1. The carrier, seeing low uptake, refuses to renew the exclusivity deal. Inventory is written off, and the brand is associated with a failed "also-ran" phone.

Blockers:
1. Price-Value Mismatch: Flagship pricing requires a flagship ecosystem. The proposal admits the absence of mainstream apps, making the price unjustifiable.
2. Market Structure: A two-platform duopoly leaves no room for a third, incomplete OS at a premium price point.

Risks: Execution complexity of 3D tracking; carrier relationship dependence.

Kill Criterion: If pre-orders do not exceed 500k units in the first month (indicating strong carrier push and consumer interest despite app gap), halt production immediately. However, given the structural blockers, the plan should not proceed as proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
