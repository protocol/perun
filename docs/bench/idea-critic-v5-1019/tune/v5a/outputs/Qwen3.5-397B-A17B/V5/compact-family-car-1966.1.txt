First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a high-fixed-cost channel (dedicated dealers) and a specific product (1100cc) without evidence that this combination beats existing low-price rivals or utilizes the new plant's capacity, creating immediate exposure to volume shortfalls.

**Steelman:** A dedicated 1100cc compact sold via exclusive dealers will capture sufficient family-market share to fill the new plant and outperform rivals on service quality, justifying the network build-out cost.

**Attack:**
1.  **Capacity vs. Demand:** The new plant requires near-capacity operation to be viable. Building a dealer network from scratch is slow; sales ramp-up will lag, leaving the plant underutilized and burning cash before the network matures.
2.  **Channel Cost:** Rivals already sell small cars at low prices, likely using established, amortized networks. Adding the heavy fixed cost of a *new* dedicated network forces higher prices or destroys margins, contradicting the "low price" competitive necessity.
3.  **Product Fit:** With buyers split between "cheaper" and "larger," an mid-spec 1100cc car risks being too expensive for budget buyers and too small for families, especially if the dealer markup is needed to fund the network.
4.  **Reversibility:** Building a dealer network is a one-way door with high sunk costs. If the 1100cc segment does not materialize as predicted, the network becomes a stranded asset, worsening the plant's utilization crisis.

**Better Way:** Utilize existing distributor relationships or shared showrooms to test the 1100cc model with minimal fixed investment. This lowers the break-even volume, allowing the plant to ramp gradually without the immediate burden of a proprietary network.

**Kill Criterion:** If projected monthly sales do not cover the combined depreciation of the new plant and the operating cost of the dedicated network within 12 months, the model fails. The proposal offers no such capped pilot; it commits to full scale immediately.

**Blockers:**
*   **Economic Mismatch:** The cost structure of a new dedicated network contradicts the need to compete with established low-price rivals while filling a new plant immediately.
*   **Missing Evidence:** No data proves the 1100cc segment is large enough to support both the plant capacity and the new network costs simultaneously.

**Risks:** Consumer preference shifts to larger cars; price war with incumbents erodes margins needed to fund the network.

DECISION: NO-GO
