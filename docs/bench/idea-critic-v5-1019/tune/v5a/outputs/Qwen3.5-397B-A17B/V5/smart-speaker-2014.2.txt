First call: NO-GO. The company's last hardware launch failed badly, and this proposal repeats the core risk of entering an unproven category with new form-factor complexity while ignoring the stated failure of their previous hardware effort.

Steelman: A cylindrical, always-listening speaker at $199 ($99 for members) creates a new home category, leveraging invitation-only scarcity to overcome privacy concerns and past hardware failures.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Avoids repeating hardware failure; cost zero; fully reversible.
2. Software-only assistant on existing phones: Uses established hardware; lower risk; higher reversibility; reaches users immediately without manufacturing risk.
3. Proposed speaker: High manufacturing cost; unproven category; low reversibility once inventory is built; relies on overcoming fresh privacy backlash.

Fit: Assumes "hardware ownership" is a north star, but the strategy context shows a recent, specific failure in hardware launches. Entering a non-existent category contradicts a cautious post-failure stance.

Pre-mortem: The product fails because privacy fears stall adoption, voice recognition frustrates users in real home environments, and the cylindrical form factor proves impractical for mass production, repeating the previous hardware launch disaster. This is a one-way door with high capital commitment. Cost of delay is low; waiting allows competitors to validate the category first.

Evidence: The proposal admits "no product category like it exists" and "company's last hardware launch went badly." These are stated facts, not assumptions. The risks (privacy, recognition failure) are acknowledged but unmitigated in the plan.

Blockers:
1. The stated fact that the "company's last hardware launch went badly" directly contradicts the readiness to launch another complex hardware product without a stated pivot in strategy or team.
2. The "no product category like it exists" fact means demand is unproven; combined with the "always-on microphone" privacy risk, the path to upside is removed by the proposal's own stated negatives.

Risks: Voice failure rates, privacy acceptance, inventory overhang.

Kill criterion: If pre-orders (invitation phase) do not reach 50% of production capacity within 30 days, halt manufacturing. However, the plan commits to full launch mechanics before this can be tested.

The proposal commits to a full hardware launch despite a recent, specific failure in the same domain and an unproven category with high privacy barriers. The stated facts show the target users may not want it (privacy fears) and the company has not demonstrated hardware competence recently.

DECISION: NO-GO
