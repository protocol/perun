First call: NO-GO. The proposed $700 price point is prohibitively high compared to existing consoles, and the licensing model creates a fatal cost structure conflict.

**Steelman:** Licensing shifts manufacturing risk to partners while securing royalty streams, leveraging multiple distributors to capture market share quickly with superior technology.

**Attack:**
1.  **Price vs. Value:** At $700, the console costs nearly double or triple competitors (who often sell at a loss to subsidize game sales). Early adopters rarely pay a 200%+ premium for unproven hardware without an exclusive killer app library, which a new platform lacks.
2.  **Incentive Misalignment:** Established competitors subsidize hardware to maximize game sales. Here, licensees *must* profit on hardware sales to survive. This forces the retail price up, reducing volume. Lower volume means fewer game sales, reducing the start-up's royalty revenue and making the platform less attractive to game developers.
3.  **Market Dynamics:** Entering a crowded market with a premium-priced, licensed hardware strategy ignores the "razor-and-blades" standard. Licensees will hesitate to commit to a high-cost, low-volume product when they can manufacture established, volume-driven consoles.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoids capital destruction in a hostile market.
*   **Subsidized Model:** The start-up absorbs hardware losses (requiring significant capital) to lower the price to ~$300, driving volume and game royalties.
*   **Exclusive Hardware:** Build and sell directly to control pricing and margins, though this increases operational risk.

**Pre-mortem:** The project fails because licensees cannot sell enough units at $700 to cover costs, leading to production cancellations. The start-up earns negligible royalties, developers ignore the small install base, and the brand dies as an expensive curiosity.

**Blocker:** The economic model requires consumers to pay a massive premium for hardware that competitors give away cheaply, while simultaneously requiring licensees to maintain high margins in a volume-driven market. This dual constraint makes market penetration impossible.

**Kill Criterion:** If market research shows less than 5% of early adopters are willing to pay $700 for a non-exclusive console without a bundled killer app, the plan is dead.

DECISION: NO-GO
