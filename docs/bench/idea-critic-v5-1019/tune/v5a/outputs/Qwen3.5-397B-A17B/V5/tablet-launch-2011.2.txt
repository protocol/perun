First call: NO-GO. Launching a premium-priced tablet with a tiny app catalog against a dominant incumbent, while the OS has negligible share and leadership is in flux, ignores the primary driver of tablet adoption: ecosystem depth.

**Steelman:** The brand's hardware prestige and heavy marketing can temporarily overcome app scarcity, buying time for developer outreach to close the gap before shoppers reject the device.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Economics:** Tablet users prioritize content consumption over hardware specs. A "small fraction" of apps means missing core use cases (media, productivity). Pricing "in line with the leader" removes the only viable alternative strategy: undercutting on cost to compensate for lower utility.
2.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in Q4 2011. Cause: Consumers buy the market leader for the apps, not the chassis. Marketing spend burns cash acquiring users who churn immediately upon realizing the content gap. Leadership transition stalls critical developer partnerships needed to expand the catalog.
3.  **Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "wait" option preserves capital. A cheaper, stripped-down device (loss leader) to drive OS adoption would be safer, but the proposal explicitly mandates premium pricing. Entering a winner-take-most market with a me-too product at parity price is historically fatal.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits the app catalog is small and OS share is near zero. These are not risks; they are current facts that dictate failure at the proposed price point. No kill criterion can save a product launched with a fundamental value deficit against an entrenched network effect.

**Blockers:**
*   **Value Mismatch:** Premium price + small catalog vs. Market Leader + huge catalog. The math does not work; users will not pay equal money for significantly less utility.
*   **Timing:** Leadership transition creates execution risk exactly when aggressive developer outreach is required to survive.

**Risks:** High marketing burn with low conversion; permanent brand damage if the device is perceived as "broken" due to lack of apps.

**Kill Criterion:** If developer sign-ups do not triple within 3 months of launch, halt ad spend immediately. However, this is post-launch; the structural flaw exists today.

DECISION: NO-GO
