First call: NO-GO. Paying $1 billion for a pre-revenue app with no proven business model violates basic capital discipline, regardless of strategic fear.

**Steelman:** The acquisition is a defensive necessity to neutralize the primary mobile threat before the IPO locks in the buyer's stagnation, buying immediate market leadership and talent that cannot be built internally fast enough.

**Options:**
1. **Acquire:** Cost $1B cash/equity. Risk: Massive overpayment, culture clash, regulatory block. Reversibility: Low (capital spent).
2. **Do Nothing:** Cost $0. Risk: Losing mobile photo dominance to a competitor. Reversibility: High (can still build or buy later).
3. **Build/Partner:** Cost <$100M. Risk: Slower time-to-market. Reversibility: High.

**Fit:** Assumes "mobile dominance" is the north star. However, paying a 100x+ premium over comparable valuations without revenue contradicts sustainable growth strategies.

**Pre-mortem:** The deal closes, but integration destroys the app's unique culture, causing user exodus. Regulators scrutinize the "killer acquisition." The buyer's stock dips post-IPO due to the massive write-down risk. The core mobile product remains weak because the acquisition was a patch, not a fix.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits "no revenue" and "no business model." The valuation is purely speculative based on "tens of millions of users" and "growth." There is no data proving these users monetize or that the threat is existential rather than competitive.

**Blockers:**
1. **Valuation Mismatch:** $1B for zero revenue is a factual financial anomaly that cannot be justified by "threat" alone without a clear path to monetization.
2. **No Business Model:** Acquiring a company with no way to generate ROI is a direct failure of fiduciary duty.
3. **Cultural Destruction Risk:** The proposal explicitly flags this; history shows such clashes often destroy the acquired value.

**Risks:** Regulatory intervention, user churn post-acquisition, failure to integrate technology.

**Kill Criterion:** If user growth slows by >20% within 6 months post-acquisition, the thesis fails.

The absence of a revenue model and the extreme valuation constitute a blocker. This is a panic buy, not a strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
