First call: GO. The pivot to fashion transforms a liability (plastic/cheap) into an asset (accessible/trendy), addressing the reputation risk by redefining the brand's scope rather than diluting its core. The aggressive target is justified only if the price point creates a new mass market, not by stealing share from established players.

**Steelman:** Launching 12 diverse models as disposable fashion accessories creates a new volume category where low cost is a feature, insulating the parent brand's luxury reputation while capturing youth market share.

**Attack & Options:**
1.  **Reputation:** If marketed as "toys" or "accessories" distinct from the main line, reputation risk is neutralized. If blurred, it is a blocker. The proposal specifies "fashion items," which supports separation.
2.  **Sales Target:** One million units requires ~83k/month. For a low-cost item, this is feasible only with massive distribution, not traditional jewelry channels. The risk of missing the target is financial, not existential, given the "inexpensive" production.
3.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" cedes the emerging plastic-watch market to competitors. "Price cutting" by incumbents hurts their margins more than this entrant's. A smaller pilot (e.g., 3 models) reduces upside and fails to test the "fashion collection" hypothesis adequately.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the watches are perceived as "cheap junk" rather than "fun fashion," or if distribution fails to reach non-traditional buyers. The "fashion" angle mitigates the quality concern but introduces inventory risk if tastes shift. However, low unit cost limits exposure.

**Blockers vs. Risks:**
*   **Blockers:** NONE. The proposal explicitly frames the product as "fashion items," which solves the reputation contradiction. The sales target is aggressive but not mathematically impossible given the price point.
*   **Risks:** Inventory obsolescence due to trend shifts; channel conflict. These are managed by the low cost of goods and the specific positioning.

**Kill Criterion:** If first-month sell-through is <5% of initial stock in pilot regions, halt expansion and re-evaluate the fashion positioning.

The plan converts a potential brand dilution into a segmented growth engine. The risks are standard for fashion retail, not manufacturing defects.

DECISION: GO
