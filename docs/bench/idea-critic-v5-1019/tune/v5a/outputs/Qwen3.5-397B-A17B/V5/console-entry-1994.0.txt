First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a hardware manufacturer with zero platform experience to enter a duopoly dominated by entrenched competitors with loyal user bases and first-party software moats, while simultaneously fighting a direct 3D launch from an incumbent and accepting negative hardware margins.

Steelman: A CD-based console leveraging cheaper media and accessible tools can disrupt the cartridge monopoly if third-party developers flock to the platform faster than incumbents can react.

Attack:
1. **Capability Gap:** The firm has no history as a platform holder. Winning developers requires trust and a installed base, neither of which exist. "Easier tools" are insufficient without a guaranteed audience.
2. **Competitive Timing:** Launching 3D hardware the same year as an incumbent's 3D console invites a feature war the newcomer cannot win on brand or library depth.
3. **Economic Trap:** Selling hardware at a loss requires massive capital reserves to subsidize units until software royalties kick in. Without a hit franchise or exclusive partnerships, cash burn will be catastrophic before critical mass is reached.
4. **Developer Inertia:** Established developers are already optimized for cartridges or courted by incumbents. Switching costs and risk aversion make them unlikely to bet on an unproven outsider.

Alternatives:
- **Do Nothing:** Avoid massive R&D and marketing spend; preserve capital for core electronics business.
- **Partner/License:** Provide CD drives or chips to existing console makers rather than building a walled garden.
- **PC Market:** Target the open computer market where 3D and CD-ROM are growing without the need for a curated ecosystem.

Pre-mortem: The console launches, developers hesitate due to low install base projections, sales miss targets, hardware losses accumulate, and the division is shuttered within two years.

Blockers:
- No proven ability to secure must-have third-party software exclusives.
- Direct head-to-head launch timing with an incumbent's next-gen 3D hardware.
- Negative margin model without a confirmed path to rapid scale.

Risks: Brand damage, massive financial loss, distraction from core business.

Kill Criterion: If signed third-party titles do not exceed 20 major releases by launch date, abort.

DECISION: NO-GO
