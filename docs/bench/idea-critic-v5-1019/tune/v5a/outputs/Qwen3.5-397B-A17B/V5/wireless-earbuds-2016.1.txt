First call: GO. Removing the jack forces a wireless future; owning the chip and case solves pairing and battery anxiety, creating a new high-margin category.

**Steelman:** Bundling a custom wireless audio ecosystem with the hardware removal creates a seamless, premium user experience that competitors cannot match without similar vertical integration.

**Options:**
1. **Do nothing:** Keep the jack, delay wireless transition, lose design differentiation and water-resistance potential.
2. **License standard Bluetooth:** Cheaper R&D, but pairing friction remains; no ecosystem lock-in.
3. **Proposed plan:** High upfront cost and price risk, but maximum control over UX and recurring revenue potential.

**Fit:** Aligns with a "wireless future" north star. Assumes customers value convenience over legacy ports.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if battery life (5 hours) proves insufficient for daily commutes or if the $159 price point triggers a consumer revolt leading to phone returns. It is a two-way door; the jack could be reinstated in the next cycle if backlash is severe, though brand damage would linger. Cost of delay: ceding the "first mover" narrative to rivals.

**Evidence:** Based on stated facts: proprietary chip enables auto-pairing (solving friction), case addresses battery/loss risks. $159 is high vs. free, but justified if perceived as a premium accessory rather than a replacement.

**Blockers:** NONE. No stated fact proves the market will reject the form factor or price outright; these are risks, not impossibilities.
**Risks:** Aesthetic ridicule, loss of earbuds, price sensitivity, anger over jack removal.

**Kill Criterion:** If pre-orders for the earbuds are <10% of phone pre-orders within 30 days of launch, halt mass production of the earbuds and bundle a dongle adapter immediately. Leading metric: Attachment rate of earbuds per phone sold. Review date: 45 days post-launch.

The proposal survives the attack because the risks are manageable via marketing and the kill criterion caps the downside. The strategic upside of forcing the industry standard outweighs the transient anger.

DECISION: GO
