First call: NO-GO. Launching early with high price, scarce software, and alienated retailers contradicts basic market entry logic for a hardware platform dependent on ecosystem support.

**Steelman:** An early, limited launch secures mindshare and tests the channel before the rival's September arrival, accepting short-term friction for long-term positioning.

**Attack:**
1.  **Ecosystem Failure:** Consoles live or die by software. Shipping with "few games" while developers are unprepared guarantees negative reviews and weak attach rates. Early adopters burned by empty shelves will not wait for the full library.
2.  **Channel Poisoning:** Excluding retailers to manage limited supply creates immediate hostility. Unlike digital goods, physical distribution requires shelf space and salesperson advocacy. Alienating partners before the main launch ensures poor placement when volume actually arrives.
3.  **Price Disadvantage:** Being $100 more expensive than a rival launching shortly after, with inferior software availability, offers no value proposition. The "premium" perception cannot hold without exclusive hit titles, which are explicitly missing.
4.  **Technical Debt:** If the machine is already "hard to program," rushing developers exacerbates the quality gap. Poor early titles will define the console's reputation permanently.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing (Wait for September):* Launches with fuller library, unified retail support, and competitive pricing parity. Risk: Rival steals headlines.
*   *Soft Launch (Non-public):* Test logistics with one partner without public announcement or pricing exposure.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, the system is deemed a failure. Critics cite the "hollow launch" of May as proof the hardware was not ready. Retailers prioritize the competitor's September release due to prior slights. The $399 price point becomes a punchline compared to the rival's $299 value.

**Blockers:**
*   **Software Gap:** No mechanism to force game readiness; early units will demonstrate the console's weaknesses, not strengths.
*   **Retail Hostility:** The plan explicitly trades partner goodwill for limited units, a net negative for mass-market distribution.
*   **Price/Value Mismatch:** Higher cost + fewer games = irrational consumer choice against the incoming rival.

This is not a strategic pivot; it is a self-inflicted wound that hands the competitor a narrative of incompetence.

DECISION: NO-GO
