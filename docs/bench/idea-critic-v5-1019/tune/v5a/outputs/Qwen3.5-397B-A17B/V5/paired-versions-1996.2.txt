First call: GO. The mechanic directly solves the core engagement problem (completion) by manufacturing social dependency, turning a single-player limitation into a viral loop.

**Steelman:** Releasing two distinct versions forces interpersonal trade to achieve 100% completion, leveraging the existing install base's link cables to create organic marketing and double unit sales per dedicated player.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
*   *Skeptic:* Declining hardware popularity reduces the absolute ceiling. However, the strategy maximizes revenue per remaining user. If the console is dying, extracting maximum value from the loyal base is superior to a single release that might sell half as many units.
*   *Better-way:* "Do nothing" (one version) eliminates the trade mechanic, reducing replayability and social buzz. A "expansion pack" model is technically impossible on read-only cartridges. The two-version approach is the only viable way to enforce trading on this hardware.
*   *Pre-mortem:* Failure occurs if players refuse to buy two cartridges or cannot find trading partners. Yet, the "gotta catch 'em all" psychological drive historically overrides price sensitivity in this genre. The risk of looking like a "price grab" is mitigated if the creature differences are perceived as genuine variety rather than mere recolors.
*   *Evidence:* The proposal relies on the psychological assumption that completionists value collection over cost. While the publisher is unsure, the mechanic creates its own demand signal; without it, the game is just another title on a dying platform. With it, it becomes a social event.

**Blockers vs. Risks:**
*   *Blockers:* NONE. The hardware constraint (link cable) is a feature, not a bug. The declining popularity is a market fact, not a plan failure.
*   *Risks:* Publisher hesitation and potential consumer backlash regarding price. These are managed by positioning the versions as distinct experiences. The lack of a second player is a social hurdle the game itself incentivizes users to overcome.

The plan turns the platform's isolation into a connector. The risk of doing nothing (mediocre sales on a dead console) outweighs the risk of the dual-release strategy.

DECISION: GO
