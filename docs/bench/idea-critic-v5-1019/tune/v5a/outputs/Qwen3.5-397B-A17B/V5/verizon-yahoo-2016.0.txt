First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to solve a scale deficit by acquiring a second declining asset, creating a larger entity with falling revenue rather than a growing competitor.

**Steelman:** Combining two distressed media portfolios creates necessary scale to negotiate with dominant ad platforms and leverage the carrier's mobile distribution.
**Attack:** Scale without growth trajectory is a value trap. The target suffers from multi-year traffic and revenue declines, repeated management instability, and an unproven fit with a carrier lacking consumer media DNA. The "declining portal" status suggests structural obsolescence, not a temporary dip. Merging two falling curves rarely yields an upward trajectory; it typically accelerates cultural friction and integration costs while the market shifts further toward the dominant duopoly.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Preserve $4.8B capital; avoid integrating two failing cultures.
2.  **Build/Organic:** Invest the $4.8B in developing native mobile ad tech on the carrier's own network data, avoiding legacy debt.
3.  **Buy Growth:** Acquire a smaller, growing mobile-first entity rather than a shrinking legacy portal.

**Fit:** The strategy assumes "scale" equals "competitiveness" in ad tech. In 2016, data relevance and algorithmic superiority (owned by the duopoly) matter more than raw legacy inventory volume. This move chases the past market structure.

**Pre-mortem:** Integration drags on for 18 months; key talent flees due to culture clash; ad revenue continues double-digit decline post-merger; the combined entity is sold for parts within three years at a significant loss. This is a one-way door with high irreversible capital commitment.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Negative Momentum:** The target's core metrics (traffic, revenue) are structurally declining, contradicting the requirement to build a competitive, scaled business.
2.  **Capability Gap:** The acquirer has zero history in consumer media, making the integration of a troubled asset highly probable to fail.
3.  **Economic Logic:** Paying a premium ($4.8B) for a "declining" asset to fix a "declining" previous acquisition compounds risk rather than mitigating it.

**Risks:** Continued ad-revenue collapse, inability to integrate distinct legacy cultures, failure to compete with data-rich duopoly.

**Kill Criterion:** If combined ad revenue does not stabilize (stop declining) within 12 months of close, initiate immediate divestiture of non-core assets.

DECISION: NO-GO
