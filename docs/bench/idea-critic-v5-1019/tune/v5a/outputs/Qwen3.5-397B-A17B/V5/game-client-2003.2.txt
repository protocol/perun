First call: GO. The proposal solves two existential threats (update delivery, cheating) that retail distribution cannot address, while creating a new revenue channel.

Steelman: A mandatory client is the only viable mechanism to enforce integrity and ensure version synchronization for a live-service PC title in 2003, with the store as a high-upside optionality.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Cheating destroys the player base; patching remains slow and fragmented.
2. Optional launcher: Fails to stop cheaters or guarantee update parity.
3. Retail-only patches: Logistically impossible at scale; high cost, low reach.
The proposal beats "do nothing" because the status quo leads to product death.

Fit: Aligns with the north star of maintaining a healthy, competitive multiplayer ecosystem. Assumes broadband adoption will grow fast enough to support the shift.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the client is so buggy at launch that it prevents gameplay entirely, or if retail backlash causes a immediate revenue cliff before digital sales mature. This is a one-way door for new titles; reversing it means abandoning anti-cheat efforts. Cost of delay is immediate loss of players to cheaters.

Evidence: Based on stated facts: retail struggles with updates/cheating; broadband is growing but not universal.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (dislike of accounts, broadband gaps, retail friction) are execution challenges, not facts proving the plan fails. The "universal broadband" concern is mitigated by targeting *new* games, allowing a transition period, and the necessity of the tool outweighs the friction for the core audience.

Risks: Retail partner retaliation; initial client instability; alienating dial-up users.

Kill Criterion: If the client causes >15% of new installs to fail launch in the first 30 days, or if retail revenue drops >40% quarter-over-quarter without digital offset, pause mandatory enforcement for new titles and revert to optional status while fixing stability. Leading metric: Daily Active Users (DAU) retention and cheat report incidence rate. Review date: 90 days post-launch.

The absence of a pilot is not a blocker; the mandatory nature for *new* games acts as the controlled rollout. The potential upside (solving cheating, creating a store) vastly outweighs the transition costs.

DECISION: GO
