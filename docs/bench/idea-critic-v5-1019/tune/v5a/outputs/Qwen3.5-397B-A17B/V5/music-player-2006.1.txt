First call: NO-GO. The proposal enters a saturated market late, dismantles existing partner trust by dropping copy-protection, and offers no price advantage against a dominant incumbent with high switching costs.

Steelman: A superior hardware device with wireless sharing and a unified store can disrupt the market by prioritizing user experience over legacy DRM, leveraging marketing to overcome lateness.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Preserve partner relationships; avoid high burn rate; accept status quo.
2. License software: Partner with existing players to provide the desktop client/store without hardware risk.
3. The Proposal: High capex hardware + store + marketing. Highest risk, highest potential reward, but currently lacks a mechanism to secure content or justify switching.

Fit: Assumes the goal is market disruption. However, dropping the industry copy-protection standard alienates label partners essential for store inventory. Without content, the device is useless.

Pre-mortem: The device launches, but labels restrict sharing or withhold key catalogs due to the dropped DRM standard. The "wireless sharing" feature becomes a liability rather than a differentiator. Marketing spend burns cash acquiring users who cannot find desired music. The project fails due to content starvation, not hardware quality.

Evidence: The proposal admits the leader has an "enormous share and loyal ecosystem" and that "labels may restrict sharing." It offers no confirmed licensing deals to counter this. The removal of the copy-protection standard previously promoted to partners is a strategic contradiction that invites retaliation.

Blockers:
1. Content Access: The plan relies on a store, yet explicitly notes labels may restrict sharing and the company just abandoned the protection standard partners require. No evidence suggests labels will cooperate under these new terms.
2. Differentiation: With "no clear price advantage" and a late entry, the value proposition rests entirely on features (screen, wireless) that may be negated if the music library is incomplete.

Risks: High marketing burn, ecosystem lock-in of competitors, hardware inventory obsolescence.

Kill Criterion: If exclusive licensing agreements with major labels are not signed and announced prior to hardware manufacturing commitment, the store cannot compete.

The absence of secured content rights in the face of antagonizing partners is a fatal flaw.

DECISION: NO-GO
