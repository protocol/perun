First call: GO. The demographic shift is a verified fact; the product leverages existing assets to capture it with high reversibility.

**Steelman:** A low-risk entry into an emerging youth segment by repurposing proven mechanicals for high-margin stylistic differentiation, funded by upfront marketing to secure first-mover advantage.

**Attack & Options:**
The primary objection is that shared economy parts will taint the sporty image. However, the "long hood, short deck" proportion and convertible option create a distinct visual identity that masks the underpinnings. The alternative—building a new chassis—increases cost and time, missing the immediate demographic wave. "Do nothing" cedes the segment to competitors. The risk of unproven demand is mitigated by the low base price (limiting downside) and the extensive options list (maximizing margin if demand exists). Heavy ad spend is a calculated bet on volume; without it, the niche remains unknown.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This fits a growth stage strategy targeting a specific North Star: market share among young buyers. If it fails, it will be because the styling failed to distract from the economy roots, or the ad spend did not convert awareness to trial. Since the mechanicals are already amortized, the failure mode is limited to marketing sunk costs and unsold inventory, which can be discounted without bankrupting the line. It is a two-way door; if sales lag, production scales down quickly.

**Blockers & Risks:**
Blockers: NONE. The proposal's own facts (reliable mechanics, low price, clear demographic) support the core claim.
Risks: Brand dilution if the "cheap" perception sticks; over-investment in ads if the niche is smaller than projected.

**Kill Criteria:**
If sales do not reach 50,000 units in the first six months despite the ad campaign, halt further marketing spend and evaluate a price reduction or trim simplification. The leading metric is weekly order intake vs. ad spend ratio.

The plan uses existing capacity to address a verified market gap with a capped downside. The risks are manageable execution variables, not fundamental flaws.

DECISION: GO
