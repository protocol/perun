First call: NO-GO. The proposal trades certain, high-margin licensing revenue for speculative subscriber growth while intentionally burning cash to build an unproven technology platform, creating a fatal mismatch between immediate financial destruction and distant, uncertain upside.

**Steelman:** Withdrawing content from rivals to launch a discounted proprietary service is the only way to capture full customer lifetime value and secure direct distribution before the market consolidates behind the current leader.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" (continue licensing) preserves billions in immediate, risk-free cash flow. A "hybrid model" (licensing non-core content while launching a premium niche service) captures some upside without triggering total revenue collapse or massive burn. The proposed "all-in" approach fails to beat "do nothing" on risk-adjusted returns.
2.  **Fit & Evidence:** The plan targets "tens of millions" of subscribers against a competitor with "over a hundred million." Even at success, the scale gap remains massive. The proposal admits zero experience running a technology platform; building this from scratch while funding multi-year losses introduces extreme execution risk not mitigated by the plan.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in Year 3. Cause: Licensing revenue vanished immediately, but subscriber acquisition stalled due to market saturation and low pricing preventing profitability. Cash reserves depleted by tech build-out and operating losses force a distress sale or shutdown before the "five-year" target is reached.
4.  **Blocker:** The plan relies on funding multi-year losses while simultaneously losing existing licensing income. This creates a double-cash-crunch (lost inflow + new outflow) that threatens solvency before the audience is built. The proposal offers no cap on losses, no interim kill criteria, and no fallback if subscriber growth lags. It commits to full-scale irreversible spend without a verified stop condition.

**Verdict:** The absence of a capped pilot, a defined kill criterion for the cash burn, or a viable alternative to total licensing withdrawal makes this a reckless bet on unproven execution capabilities.

DECISION: NO-GO
