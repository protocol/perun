First call: NO-GO. The proposal sacrifices core usability and comfort for a gimmick, targeting a non-existent market segment between handheld and home consoles with a physically painful experience.

**Steelman:** A low-cost ($180) bridge product leveraging stereoscopic 3D to extend the brand lifecycle between major generations.
**Attack:** The "bridge" fails because the user experience is fundamentally hostile. Red monochrome graphics limit appeal; mandatory eyepiece usage destroys social play and comfort; reported eye strain creates a hard ceiling on engagement. Being neither truly portable nor a home console means it loses both use cases.

**Options:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Preserve brand equity and wait for the next home console (due in 2 years). Cost: $0. Risk: Revenue gap. Reversibility: N/A.
2.  **Enhance Handheld:** Iterate on the existing portable line. Cost: Moderate. Risk: Low. Reversibility: High.
3.  **Proposed Tabletop 3D:** Cost: High dev/manufacturing. Risk: Brand damage, health liabilities, inventory bloat. Reversibility: Low (hardware sunk cost).
The proposal does not beat "Do Nothing" because the revenue risk is outweighed by the reputational risk of shipping a product known to cause headaches.

**Fit:** Assumes the goal is "hardware unit sales" rather than "ecosystem health." If the North Star is user engagement, this fails immediately.

**Pre-mortem:** The product launches, reviewers pan the discomfort, parents return units due to children's headaches, and developers refuse to port titles due to the niche install base. It becomes a cautionary tale, distracting resources from the upcoming home console.

**Evidence:** The proposal itself admits the critical flaws: "neither portable nor comfortable," "eye strain or headaches," "few games." These are not risks; they are confirmed defects in the value proposition.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Physiological Harm:** The admission that users "may get eye strain or headaches" is a blocker for a consumer electronics device intended for prolonged use.
2.  **Value Mismatch:** At $180, the cost is too high for a "toy" with red-only graphics and no comfort, yet too low to justify the R&D for a failed form factor.
3.  **Software Void:** "Few games at launch" guarantees commercial failure regardless of hardware price.

**Risks:** Brand dilution, channel conflict with existing handhelds.

**Decision:** The confirmed physical discomfort and lack of software support constitute fatal flaws that cannot be mitigated post-launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
