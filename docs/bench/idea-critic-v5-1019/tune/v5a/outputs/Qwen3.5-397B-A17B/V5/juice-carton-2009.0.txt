First call: NO-GO. The proposal discards the brand's primary shelf cue for an abstract design while targeting an older, loyal demographic, creating an immediate recognition failure risk that outweighs the aesthetic benefits.

Steelman: A modernized package will attract new buyers and signal premium quality without alienating existing customers if supported by massive advertising.

Attack: The "minimalist" design removes the single most effective visual heuristic (orange with straw) used by the core demographic for instant identification. Replacing a concrete fruit image with an abstract glass and a rotated logo increases cognitive load at the point of sale. For older, loyal shoppers, this is not an upgrade but an obstruction. The proposal admits the risk of failed recognition yet plans a "big bang" nationwide launch. If recognition fails even temporarily, sales collapse before advertising can retrain consumer behavior. The cost of re-acquiring lost shelf presence exceeds the cost of the redesign.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Maintains current sales and recognition; zero risk.
2. Staged pilot: Test the new design in a limited region to measure recognition loss before national rollout.
3. Hybrid design: Evolve the current orange image rather than replacing it entirely.

Fit: Assuming the north star is revenue growth or market share, alienating the loyal base to chase a vague "modern" aesthetic contradicts the stability required for a mature brand.

Pre-mortem: Three months post-launch, sales have dropped 15%. Investigation reveals older shoppers walked past the cartons, unable to locate the brand quickly. The advertising campaign was insufficient to overcome the loss of the immediate visual cue. The company is forced to revert packaging, wasting the entire print run and ad spend.

Evidence: The proposal itself cites "old image is the brand's main shelf cue" and "buyer base is loyal and older." These facts directly contradict the strategy of removing that cue simultaneously across all stores.

Blockers: The plan commits to an irreversible, nationwide removal of the primary recognition asset without a validation step. The stated facts confirm the target audience relies on this asset. Launching everywhere at once removes the ability to learn and adjust, capping no loss.

Kill Criterion: If a regional pilot shows a >2% drop in repeat purchase rate within 4 weeks, the project stops. Since no pilot is proposed, the stop condition cannot be met before total exposure.

DECISION: NO-GO
