First call: GO. The shift in buyer demographics toward American-brand customers seeking smaller sedans represents a clear market signal that the current lineup ignores. Ignoring this segment cedes ground to competitors while the brand's prestige remains anchored in its existing flagship models.

Steelman: Leveraging a parent company's mass-market platform allows for rapid entry into the compact segment with controlled costs, while premium trim and exclusive branding maintain the perception of luxury, effectively capturing a new demographic without diluting the core brand's high-end status.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Retain only large European-focused models. Risk: Continued loss of the emerging American compact-buyer segment; zero revenue from this demographic.
2. Build a ground-up compact platform: Cost: Prohibitive R&D and time-to-market; Risk: High financial exposure if the segment proves temporary.
3. Acquire a smaller brand: Cost: High capital outlay; Risk: Integration challenges and brand confusion.
The proposed shared-platform approach beats "do nothing" by capturing revenue and beats ground-up development by minimizing risk and cost.

Fit: Assumes the brand's north star includes volume growth and demographic expansion, not just exclusivity. If the strategy is purely exclusivity, this fails; however, the proposal explicitly targets a new buyer type, implying a growth mandate.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the "premium price" cannot mask the mass-market underpinnings, leading buyers to reject the value proposition. This is a two-way door; the model can be discontinued if sales lag. The cost of delay is the loss of early adopters in this shifting demographic to rivals who act faster.

Evidence: The proposal cites a factual shift in buyer origin (American-brand customers) and preference (smaller sedan). No internal data contradicts this trend.

Blockers: NONE. The risks raised (platform visibility, price justification, salesperson behavior, prestige dilution) are execution challenges, not factual impossibilities. They are mitigated by the "premium trim" strategy and distinct model naming.
Risks: Brand dilution if the quality gap is too visible; sales channel resistance.

Kill Criterion: If first-year sales do not exceed 15,000 units or if customer satisfaction scores regarding "value for price" drop below 80%, the program halts. The leading metric is the ratio of new American-brand buyers to total sales.

DECISION: GO
