First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks to launch a premium-priced hardware company with no manufacturing history, facing entrenched incumbents who can copy the technology and leverage superior distribution, while consumer willingness to pay a premium for an unproven bagless mechanism remains unverified.

Steelman: A superior cyclone technology justifies a premium price and can disrupt the market if direct manufacturing allows quality control that licensing would forfeit.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** License the technology to an established manufacturer for royalties, avoiding capital risk and distribution battles.
2. **Pilot first:** Manufacture a limited run to validate the premium price point and build a brand before full-scale factory investment.
3. **Proposed plan:** Immediate full-scale company founding and manufacturing.

Fit: Assumes a market desire for high-end innovation over convenience/cost, which contradicts the risk that buyers won't pay extra.

Pre-mortem: The company runs out of cash within 18 months because retail channels refuse shelf space to an unknown brand, and early adopters reject the high price once the novelty fades. Incumbents release a cheaper cyclone alternative using their existing supply chains. This is a one-way door with high capital irreversibility. The cost of delay is low; waiting to validate demand prevents total loss.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits zero manufacturing record, higher costs, and unproven buyer willingness. These are not hypothetical risks but stated facts that currently invalidate the business case.

Blockers:
1. **No manufacturing capability:** The plan requires building a factory/product line with zero prior record, creating a high probability of production failure or cost overruns.
2. **Unverified demand:** The core revenue assumption (buyers paying a premium) is explicitly listed as a risk with no data to support it.
3. **Incumbent threat:** Large competitors can copy the mechanical idea faster than a startup can build distribution, rendering the first-mover advantage null without a patent moat (not mentioned).

Risks: Cash flow insolvency, retail rejection, price sensitivity.

Kill criterion: If a pilot run of 500 units does not achieve a 20% repeat/referral rate or sell out at the premium price within 90 days, the project must stop. As proposed, there is no pilot, only a full commitment. Committing to full-scale manufacturing without validating the price premium or production viability is a blocker.

DECISION: NO-GO
