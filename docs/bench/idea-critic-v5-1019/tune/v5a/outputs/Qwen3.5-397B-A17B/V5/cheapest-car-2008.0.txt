First call: GO. The stated facts describe a clear market gap: moving families from scooters to cars at an unprecedented price point. The volume targets and dedicated factory align with the cost requirements to hit $2,500.

Steelman: Launching the world's cheapest car enables mass motorization for Indian families, leveraging a dedicated high-volume factory to achieve a $2,500 price point that undercuts all four-seat alternatives.

Options:
1. **Proposed Plan:** High risk due to land protests and safety perceptions, but offers first-mover advantage in a new category.
2. **Do Nothing:** Maintain status quo; families remain on scooters, missing the safety and comfort upgrade.
3. **Used Car Market:** Cheaper than new, but lacks warranty, standardized safety, and the "new ownership" status symbol.
The proposal beats "do nothing" by solving a specific mobility constraint. Used cars cannot match the scale or specific design intent for scooter upgraders.

Fit: Assuming the north star is mass adoption and market creation, this fits. If the goal was premium branding, it would fail.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if land protests halt construction indefinitely, or if safety incidents confirm "cheap equals dangerous," destroying brand trust. This is a two-way door for product features but a one-way door for factory capital expenditure. Delay costs first-mover advantage to competitors or entrenches used car dominance.

Evidence: The proposal states the car exists and the price target is set. Farmer protests and safety doubts are stated risks, not proven blockers. The existence of cheaper scooters is a feature of the target demographic, not a contradiction.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (protests, safety perception, alternatives) are execution challenges and market dynamics, not factual contradictions that make the plan impossible as proposed. The land protest is a local hurdle, not a fundamental flaw in the product-market fit. The "cheapest" label is a positioning risk, not a blocker.

Risks: Safety reputation, land acquisition delays, consumer stigma.

Kill Criterion: If land acquisition is not resolved within 6 months, or if initial crash tests yield a safety rating below the legal minimum, halt the factory build-out. Leading metric: Pre-order volume from current scooter owners.

DECISION: GO
