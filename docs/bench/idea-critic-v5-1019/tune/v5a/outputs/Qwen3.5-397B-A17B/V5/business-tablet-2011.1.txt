First call: NO-GO. The proposal sacrifices the company's core differentiator (business email) on an untested OS while matching a competitor's price for an inferior hardware size and ecosystem.

**Steelman:** A niche, phone-tethered 7-inch device creates a new category for loyal existing users, validating the new OS before investing in full tablet apps.

**Attack:**
1.  **Value Destruction:** The company leads in business email. Shipping a tablet *without* native email, calendar, or contacts unless paired with a phone destroys the primary use case for its core demographic. Business users will not carry a phone just to enable tablet email.
2.  **Pricing Failure:** Matching the price of a rival's larger tablet with a superior app store, while offering smaller hardware, no native apps, and an untested OS, creates zero value proposition.
3.  **Ecosystem Risk:** Developers ignore platforms without users; users ignore platforms without apps. Without native productivity apps, developer commitment is impossible to secure.
4.  **Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "wait" option is superior. Rushing an incomplete product damages the brand. If launching, the device must include native productivity apps or be priced significantly lower to compensate for the lack of ecosystem.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches, reviewers pan the lack of native email and high price, sales flatline, and the new OS is declared dead on arrival, wasting the acquisition cost.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** The proposal explicitly removes the company's #1 competitive advantage (native business email) from the device experience.
*   **Fact:** Price parity with a superior competitor (larger screen, established store) ensures market rejection.

**Kill Criterion:** If the device cannot run native email independently at launch, it fails the core business requirement.

**Decision:** The plan as proposed guarantees failure by negating the brand's strength and overpricing the weakness.

DECISION: NO-GO
