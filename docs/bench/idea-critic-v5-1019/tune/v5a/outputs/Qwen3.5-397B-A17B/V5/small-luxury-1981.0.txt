First call: NO-GO. The plan relies on a mass-market foundation to sustain a luxury premium, creating an immediate contradiction between cost structure and brand promise.

**Steelman:** A compact luxury vehicle can succeed on a shared mass-market platform if premium trim and features sufficiently mask the underlying commonality, allowing the brand to capture a new demographic without diluting core prestige.

**Attack:**
1.  **Platform Transparency:** Luxury buyers purchase status and engineering exclusivity. A shared platform with a parent's mass-market car is a tangible defect that trim cannot hide. Once discovered (and it will be), the "premium" price becomes indefensible fraud, not value.
2.  **Brand Dilution:** The proposal admits salespeople won't use the main brand name. This confirms the product damages the brand's equity. If the sales force distances the product from the badge, the badge loses its power.
3.  **Competitive Moat:** Rivals offer dedicated small sedans. Competing on "added trim" against competitors' purpose-built engineering is a losing position on performance and perception.
4.  **Economic Logic:** The "premium price" is the only path to margin, yet the "mass-market platform" is the primary cost saver. This creates a value gap: the customer pays luxury prices for non-luxury roots. The risks listed (buyers seeing through it, prestige suffering) are not possibilities; they are inevitable outcomes of the architecture.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do nothing:* Preserve brand integrity; miss the segment.
*   *Dedicated Platform:* Build a true compact luxury car. Higher cost, but defensible price and brand safety.
*   *Acquisition/Rebadge:* Buy an existing small luxury competitor rather than cannibalizing own prestige.

**Pre-mortem:** The car launches, critics reveal the shared underpinnings, the core customer base feels betrayed by the "cheapened" brand, and the new buyers reject it as an overpriced economy car. Sales collapse, and the main brand's resale value plummets.

**Blocker:** The core claim (premium price justification) is directly contradicted by the stated method (mass-market shared platform). The value proposition is negative.

**Risks:** Brand erosion, sales failure, channel confusion.

**Kill Criterion:** If consumer testing reveals >15% recognition of the mass-market donor platform, the project must terminate before tooling.

DECISION: NO-GO
