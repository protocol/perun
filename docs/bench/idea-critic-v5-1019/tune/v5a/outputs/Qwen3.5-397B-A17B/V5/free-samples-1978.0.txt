First call: NO-GO. The initial four-city test demonstrated weak market demand; repeating the experiment with a different name and a non-representative sample (free recipients) ignores the primary evidence that the product lacks value.

**Steelman:** Renaming might fix a branding mismatch, and free sampling could generate word-of-mouth if the product's utility is only realized upon physical interaction, making a small-scale office test a low-risk validation before national spend.

**Attack:**
1.  **Evidence Contradiction:** The proposal dismisses the "disappointing sales" of the first test as a naming issue without proof. If the core adhesive utility were strong, sales would not have failed solely due to a name. Changing the name is an assumption, not a strategy based on data.
2.  **Invalid Metric:** Relying on recipients saying they "would buy" after receiving a free item is a flawed metric. It measures politeness or hypothetical interest, not actual willingness to pay. The previous failure was in *sales*, not *intent*.
3.  **Sample Bias:** Targeting offices in one mid-sized city ignores where the product might actually fit (e.g., homes, schools) and assumes office workers are the primary user base without verification.
4.  **Cost vs. Value:** Free samples incur manufacturing and distribution costs. If the conversion rate from "say they would buy" to actual purchase is low (as suggested by the general lack of need), this step burns capital without resolving the fundamental demand question.
5.  **Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "kill project" option is stronger because the existing evidence points to product-market fit failure. A cheaper alternative is a focused survey on *why* the first test failed before spending on renaming and sampling.

**Blocker:** The plan relies on a hypothetical metric ("say they would buy") that does not correlate with the proven failure metric (actual sales). Proceeding assumes the first test's failure was superficial (naming) rather than fundamental (utility), contradicting the available evidence.

**Risks:** Wasting budget on a national rollout based on false positive feedback from free users; failing to identify the true reason for the initial failure.

**Kill Criterion:** If <10% of free sample recipients purchase the product at full price within 30 days, stop. However, the current proposal lacks this real purchase gate, relying instead on verbal intent.

DECISION: NO-GO
