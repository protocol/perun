First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a fixed public schedule and full-scale mass production despite unproven battery durability, no demand signal, and cheap fuel removing the primary user incentive.

Steelman: Being first with a novel power-split hybrid creates an insurmountable brand moat and defines the segment before regulators force the issue.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Preserve capital, wait for battery data or regulatory mandates. Low risk, zero upside.
2. Pilot program: Limited release to fleets to test durability and gather data without public schedule risk. Moderate cost, high learning.
3. Proposed plan: Full mass production commitment now. Extreme cost, extreme warranty risk, unproven demand.

Fit: Assumes a future where fuel efficiency is valued despite current cheap prices and lack of regulation. This contradicts stated market facts.

Pre-mortem: The battery pack fails prematurely in cold weather or high cycles, triggering massive warranty claims that exceed the vehicle's margin. The public schedule prevents delaying launch to fix the issue, forcing a recall that destroys brand trust. The car is a two-way door technically but a one-way door financially due to the fixed tooling and public commitment.

Evidence: The proposal admits battery durability is "unproven" and demand is absent. Committing to volume production before proving the core mechanism (battery life) violates basic engineering economics.

Blockers:
1. Unproven core technology: The battery durability is unknown, yet the plan commits to volume production. A failure here collapses the entire business case.
2. No demand signal: With cheap fuel and no mandates, the premium for this technology cannot be justified by the market today.
3. Fixed public schedule: This removes the ability to delay launch if testing reveals issues, turning a technical risk into a guaranteed reputation disaster.

Risks: High warranty exposure, competitor fast-following with better batteries, stranded asset costs.

Kill criterion: If fleet pilot data shows <80% battery capacity retention after 50,000 miles by Q4 1996, the program must pivot to R&D only. The current proposal lacks this stop condition and commits to full scale before verification.

DECISION: NO-GO
