First call: NO-GO. The initial four-city test demonstrated weak demand; repeating the experiment with a different name and a free sample distribution in a single city does not address the fundamental lack of product-market fit or the core risk that office workers do not perceive a need for the item.

Steelman: Renaming and targeted sampling might uncover a specific use case (internal office communication) that general retail missed, validating demand before a costly national launch.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. The primary blocker is the reliance on stated intent ("say they would buy it") rather than actual purchasing behavior as the success metric for a national rollout. History and behavioral economics show a massive gap between claimed intent in survey settings and actual wallet-opening behavior, especially for low-necessity items. The proposal explicitly admits the first test showed "little demand" and that workers "may not see a need." Giving the product away for free to a captive audience (offices) creates artificial usage that will not translate to organic retail sales. The cost of sampling one mid-sized city is sunk capital with no guarantee of conversion, and the criterion for success is scientifically invalid for predicting national performance.

The "better way" is to identify *why* the first test failed before spending more on sampling. Is it price, awareness, or genuine lack of utility? A cheaper alternative is a focused qualitative study or a small-scale paid trial where users must purchase the item at a discount, proving willingness to pay.

The pre-mortem reveals that the company rolls out nationally based on positive survey feedback from free users, only to find zero retail traction because the free distribution masked the lack of inherent value. The cost of delay is minimal compared to the cost of a failed national launch. The proposal lacks a kill criterion based on actual sales conversion rates from the sample, relying instead on vanity metrics of intent.

Blockers: Success metric is stated intent rather than actual purchase; fundamental demand question remains unanswered by free distribution.
Risks: Wasted sampling budget; false positive leading to larger loss.

DECISION: NO-GO
