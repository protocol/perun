First call: GO. The proposal addresses a critical vulnerability: commodity pricing due to buyer ignorance. If buyers cannot distinguish the component, they will not pay a premium, and computer makers will switch to cheaper clones without resistance.

Steelman: By shifting demand pull to the end-user, the chip maker forces computer manufacturers to include their specific processor to satisfy customer requests, thereby securing pricing power and blocking clones.

Alternatives considered:
1. Do nothing: Continue losing margin to clones as differentiation erodes.
2. B2B incentives only: Paying manufacturers directly lacks consumer leverage and does not stop clone confusion.
3. Legal action against clones: Costly, slow, and does not solve the root cause of buyer indifference.

Fit: Aligns with the goal of maintaining market leadership and premium pricing in a commoditizing environment.

Pre-mortem: The campaign fails if computer makers refuse to co-advertise due to resentment. However, if consumers begin asking for the brand by name, manufacturers will be forced to comply to move units. The cost of delay is immediate margin erosion as clone confusion solidifies.

Evidence: The proposal correctly identifies that buyers currently do not know which chip they own. This information asymmetry is the sole enabler of the clone threat.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (unprecedented nature, budget share, manufacturer resentment) are execution challenges, not factual contradictions. Unprecedented moves are often required to break commodity traps. Manufacturer resentment is a manageable negotiation friction compared to the existential threat of commoditization.

Risks: High budget consumption; potential channel conflict. These are mitigated by tying payments to actual ad placement (performance-based) and framing the program as a demand-generation service for manufacturers.

Kill criterion: If, after 12 months, consumer awareness surveys show less than 10% unaided recall of the brand logo, or if computer maker participation drops below 50% of key partners, the program must be halted or restructured. Leading metric: Percentage of co-branded ads in major retail circulars within 6 months.

The proposal solves the root cause of the threat. The risks are inherent to any strategic shift but do not invalidate the core logic.

DECISION: GO
