First call: NO-GO. A $6 billion cash outlay at a large premium in 2007 for a fast-moving display ad tech target, primarily to react to a competitor's move, fails basic capital discipline without proof of synergy or retention.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the platform instantly grants scale to challenge the search leader's impending dominance, bypassing years of organic build time.

**Attack:**
1.  **Capital Risk:** $6 billion cash is immense for 2007. If the "fast-changing" market shifts (e.g., to programmatic or mobile), this asset becomes a stranded cost with no liquidity.
2.  **Channel Conflict:** The buyer serves existing agency customers. Owning the ad-server creates an immediate conflict of interest, likely driving those agencies to competitors, eroding the core business to chase a speculative one.
3.  **Reactive Strategy:** Buying because a rival did so is a classic "fear of missing out" error, not a strategic fit. The premium suggests overpayment for hype rather than cash flow.
4.  **Integration Failure:** Merging a massive cash-heavy acquisition into a software culture during a market transition historically leads to talent flight and technology rot.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Preserve cash; build organic capabilities or partner without equity risk.
*   **Minority Stake:** Gain insight and optionality without full balance sheet exposure or channel conflict.
*   **Build:** Invest the $6B R&D budget internally to create a conflict-free solution.

**Pre-mortem:** By 2009, the acquired agency clients have left due to conflict concerns, the technology is obsolete due to a market shift, and the company faces a massive write-down, distracting leadership from its core search dominance.

**Blockers:**
*   **Conflict of Interest:** No stated plan to retain existing agency customers who will view the buyer as a competitor.
*   **Valuation Disconnect:** "Large premium" in a "fast-changing" market with no defined kill criteria or cap on loss.
*   **Reactive Motive:** The primary driver is competitor reaction, not verified customer demand or unit economics.

**Kill Criterion:** If agency churn exceeds 5% within 12 months post-close, the strategic premise is broken.

DECISION: NO-GO
