First call: GO. A space-fantasy franchise with existing theme park, product, and film synergy potential justifies a premium acquisition to secure long-term IP control, despite near-term creative and financial risks.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the entire franchise ecosystem for $4B secures exclusive rights to revitalize a dormant but culturally massive IP, enabling direct monetization across parks, merchandise, and new films, outweighing the loss of the founder and recent mixed reception.

**Options:**
1.  **Acquire:** High upfront cost ($4B), high integration risk, but captures 100% of future upside and strategic control. Reversible only via resale at a likely loss.
2.  **License content:** Lower cost, avoids integration, but leaves core IP fragmented, limits park/story control, and caps upside.
3.  **Do nothing:** Zero cost, zero risk, but forfeits the only available asset capable of feeding all three business units simultaneously.

**Fit:** Directly serves the north star of "franchises that feed all businesses." No other available asset offers this specific tripartite synergy.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if new films flop, alienating the core fanbase and rendering the $4B spend a sunk cost with no park or product lift. This is a one-way door; capital is gone. Cost of delay: The founder is retiring now; waiting likely means the asset becomes unavailable or the cultural moment passes.

**Evidence:** Facts state the founder is retiring (urgency), the price is $4B (sizable but feasible for a "large" company), and risks are known (mixed reviews, licensing constraints). No data contradicts the strategic fit.

**Blockers:** NONE. The high price and creative risks are significant but do not factually prevent the plan from working; they are execution challenges, not impossibilities. The licensing constraints actually *support* the acquisition thesis (need to reclaim rights).

**Risks:** Creative failure, fan rejection, overpayment. These are managed by the proposed trilogy strategy (betting on revitalization).

**Kill Criterion:** If the first film of the new trilogy fails to recoup its production budget within 6 months of release, halt further spin-off production and re-evaluate park integration spend.

**Decision:** The strategic necessity of owning a unified IP to drive all three business lines outweighs the financial and creative risks, which are inherent to any major content bet.

DECISION: GO
