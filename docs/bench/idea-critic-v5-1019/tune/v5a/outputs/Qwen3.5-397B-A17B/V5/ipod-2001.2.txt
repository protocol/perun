First call: NO-GO. The proposal combines a premium price with a severely restricted ecosystem (small computer market share, no consumer experience) in a category where cheaper alternatives are already failing. The addressable market is too small to justify the component sourcing risk and development cost.

Steelman: A high-capacity, integrated ecosystem play could create a defensible premium niche if the user experience is vastly superior, forcing adoption of the computer platform.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Avoid capital burn in a failing category.
2. License software: Expand addressable market immediately, reducing ecosystem risk.
3. Cheaper hardware: Match competitors to gain volume, sacrificing margin.
The proposal beats "do nothing" only if the ecosystem lock-in drives computer sales, which is unproven.

Fit: Assumes a strategy of vertical integration to drive hardware differentiation. However, without an existing large user base, this is a bet on creating demand from scratch.

Pre-mortem: The device fails because the $400 price point excludes the mass market, and the requirement to own a specific computer eliminates 95% of potential buyers. Component sourcing delays push launch past the holiday window, leaving inventory stranded.

Evidence: The prompt states competitors are cheaper and selling poorly. The music industry has not licensed downloads, limiting the value proposition to CD ripping. The computer maker has no consumer electronics experience, increasing execution risk.

Blockers:
1. Market Size: The addressable base (owners of small-share computers) is insufficient to support the volume needed for a $400 premium device in a nascent category.
2. Value Mismatch: Without legal downloads, the value of 5GB is limited to CD ripping, which cheaper players already handle adequately for most users.
3. Experience Gap: No consumer electronics experience increases the likelihood of missing the tight timeline, leading to missed market windows.

Risks: Component sourcing, industry licensing, price sensitivity.

Kill Criterion: If pre-orders from the existing computer base do not cover 50% of the initial production run within 60 days of announcement, the project must stop.

The lack of a broader software compatibility plan and the high price relative to the tiny installed base constitute a fundamental flaw in the unit economics and market fit.

DECISION: NO-GO
