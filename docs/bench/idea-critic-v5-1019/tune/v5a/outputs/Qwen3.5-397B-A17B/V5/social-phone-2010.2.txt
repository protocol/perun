First call: NO-GO. The proposal ignores basic market fit: teenagers are price-sensitive, yet the data plan is prohibitively expensive, and the lack of an app store contradicts the core "social network" value proposition when rivals offer vast catalogs.

**Steelman:** Leveraging the acquired brand to lock in teens via a exclusive carrier deal could secure immediate volume and offset OS development risks through tight integration.
**Attack:** This collapses because the value proposition is inverted. Teens need apps to socialize; offering a phone with *no* app store while charging premium data rates makes the device useless for its intended purpose. The "sunk cost" of two years' development is a trap; throwing good money after bad by launching a product that fails the primary user need (apps) guarantees a larger write-off.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Preserve capital, avoid brand damage from a failed launch.
2.  **Port existing platform:** Use the company's established mobile OS to gain immediate app compatibility, sacrificing the "new OS" dream but saving the product.
3.  **Delay launch:** Build an app ecosystem before hardware release (high risk, slow).

**Fit:** The strategy assumes teens will prioritize a specific messaging phone over the utility of a full smartphone ecosystem. In 2010, with rivals holding large catalogs, this assumption is fatal. The high data cost further excludes the target demographic.

**Pre-mortem:** The phone launches, teens realize they cannot install Instagram, TikTok precursors, or games, and the high monthly fee drains their allowance. Sales flatline immediately. The carrier, stuck with unsold inventory, terminates the exclusivity deal. The new OS becomes a graveyard.

**Blockers:**
1.  **No App Store:** A social phone without apps cannot compete in 2010. This is a functional failure, not a risk.
2.  **Price Mismatch:** High data costs directly contradict the teenage demographic's budget constraints.

**Risks:** Carrier exclusivity limiting reach; new OS bugs.

**Kill Criterion:** If pre-orders do not exceed 50,000 units in the first week due to lack of app availability, halt production immediately. However, given the structural flaws, the plan should not proceed to launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
