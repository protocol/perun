First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to solve an unproven demand hypothesis (online fashion buying) with maximum operational complexity (multi-continent launch) and maximum cash burn (multiple offices), creating a single point of failure in funding before product-market fit is established.

**Steelman:** Simultaneous global launch captures first-mover advantage across key markets before competitors emerge, leveraging venture capital to build an insurmountable brand moat among young buyers immediately.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Evidence:** The core claim relies on "online fashion buying" being viable. In 1998, this is an assumption, not a fact. No evidence is provided that users will trust online transactions for fit-sensitive goods. Launching globally assumes the solution works everywhere before proving it works anywhere.
2.  **Options:** The "do nothing" alternative is safe but yields zero growth. A superior alternative is a single-market pilot (e.g., UK or US only) with a centralized warehouse. This reduces logistics complexity by an order of magnitude, lowers marketing spend to test channel efficacy, and extends the runway. If the model fails, the loss is capped; if it succeeds, expansion follows. The proposed "all-in" approach lacks this reversibility.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 18 months. Cause: Venture capital runs out due to slower-than-expected adoption in one region dragging down the whole, while logistics costs in multiple jurisdictions bleed cash. The delay risk materializes because fixing issues in five countries takes five times longer than in one.
4.  **Blocker Test:** The proposal states "online fashion buying is unproven." Proceeding with full-scale global infrastructure before proving this unit economics model is a factual contradiction. You cannot scale an unproven mechanism; you must validate it first. The risk of running out of money is not a "risk" here; it is a near-certainty given the burn rate of multi-country offices against an unproven revenue stream.

**Kill Criterion:** If a single-market pilot cannot achieve positive unit economics within 12 months, the global thesis is invalid. The current proposal has no such stop condition; it commits to full scale immediately.

**Decision:** The plan fails the blocker test. It treats an unproven hypothesis as a reason for maximum expenditure rather than minimum validation.

DECISION: NO-GO
