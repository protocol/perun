First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits irreversible capital to an unproven model in a low-margin industry before validating unit economics.

**Steelman:** Secure dominant market share by building insurmountable infrastructure moats early, betting that speed-to-market outweighs the risk of operational inefficiency.

**Attack:**
1.  **Unit Economics Unknown:** The proposal explicitly admits the unit economics of the first city are unknown. Groceries have notoriously thin margins; without proof that a single order is profitable after delivery costs, scaling losses by 12x guarantees catastrophic burn.
2.  **Irreversibility:** Building "highly automated, very large" warehouses is a one-way door. If customer density fails to materialize or picking logic flaws emerge, these assets become stranded costs with no liquid market.
3.  **Premature Scale:** Entering new cities rapidly dilutes management focus and capital exactly when the team should be iterating on the flaws of the first location. The "learn fast" principle is violated by locking in hardware before learning.
4.  **Alternative:** A cheaper, safer path exists: lease smaller, flexible fulfillment centers or use manual picking in existing retail spaces to validate density and margins before committing $1B to automation.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** Unit economics are unproven.
*   **Fact:** Margins are low.
*   **Contradiction:** The plan requires massive fixed costs before proving variable profit contribution. The stated facts show the route to profitability is hypothetical, yet the spend is concrete and irreversible.

**Kill Criterion Check:** The proposal commits to full-scale construction and multi-city expansion before a pilot can demonstrate positive unit economics. There is no working stop condition; the $1B spend happens *before* the learning. This lack of a staged cap is a blocker.

**Risks:** (Non-blocking but severe) Customer adoption of 30-minute slots may be insufficient to cover delivery costs; automation may fail with diverse grocery SKU counts.

**Decision:** The plan fails the blocker test. It bets the company on unverified assumptions with irreversible capital. The correct move is to validate the first city's unit economics using flexible, low-capital infrastructure. Only upon proving a profitable per-order model should automation and expansion be considered.

DECISION: NO-GO
