First call: GO. The data-driven case for original content to offset rising licensing costs and differentiate the service is sound, provided the data actually supports the specific creative choices.

**Steelman:** Leveraging proprietary viewing data to greenlight a high-profile original series without a pilot minimizes development friction and capitalizes on proven audience preferences for specific talent and genres, creating a unique asset that licensing cannot provide.

**Alternatives:**
1.  *Do nothing:* Continue paying rising licensing fees; risk commoditization as competitors withdraw content.
2.  *Pilot first:* Delays launch, incurs upfront cost without guarantee of series order, and signals lack of confidence to top-tier talent.
3.  *Cheaper originals:* Lower prestige may fail to drive the necessary subscription lift or brand repositioning.

**Fit:** Moves the north star from "aggregator" to "destination." Essential for long-term margin improvement.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the internal data misinterprets niche interest as broad appeal, or if the "all-at-once" model confuses users accustomed to weekly schedules. However, the attachment of a film star and director mitigates quality risk.

**Evidence Check:** The proposal relies entirely on "the service's viewing data." If this data explicitly shows high completion rates for the attached director's films or the star's previous work, and strong retention for the specific genre, the risk is calculated, not blind. The cost, while large, is a necessary bet to stop the bleeding of rising licensing fees.

**Blockers:** NONE. The absence of a pilot is a strategic choice, not a factual error, given the data backing. The "all-at-once" release is a differentiator, not a defect.
**Risks:** Audience uncertainty and high upfront cost. These are inherent to innovation.

**Kill Criterion:** If the first season's completion rate drops below 60% within 30 days of release, halt production on Season 2 immediately to cap losses. Leading metric: New subscriber acquisition cost (CAC) vs. Lifetime Value (LTV) of cohorts exposed to the show.

The proposal represents a logical evolution for a data-centric platform facing market headwinds. The risks are real but managed by the very data cited as the foundation.

DECISION: GO
