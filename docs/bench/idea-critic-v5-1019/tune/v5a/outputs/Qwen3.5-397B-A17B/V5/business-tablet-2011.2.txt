First call: NO-GO. The proposal removes the company's primary competitive advantage (business email) from a device targeting that very segment, while pricing it equally to a superior, established rival product with a mature app ecosystem.

**Steelman:** A 7-inch device on an untested OS can carve a niche by leveraging phone pairing for business users, delaying native app development to save costs.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Value:** The core value proposition is "business email." Shipping without built-in email, calendar, or contacts unless paired with a specific phone creates a fractured experience. Business users demand standalone reliability. This directly contradicts the stated market leadership in business email.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" or waiting to integrate native apps is safer. The rival's larger tablet with a full app store dominates the value equation at the same price point. A cheaper, smaller alternative only works if it offers *better* utility in its niche; here, it offers *less* utility (no native email) at the *same* cost.
3.  **Evidence & Logic:** The proposal admits the OS is untested and developers may not commit. Relying on an unproven platform while stripping core features is a double penalty. The risk of developer abandonment is high if the initial user base is handicapped by missing core apps.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** The device launches, business users reject it due to the lack of native email, developers ignore the platform due to low adoption, and the company loses brand equity as a business leader. The price parity with the rival makes the value deficit glaring.

**Blockers:**
-   **Core Contradiction:** The plan targets business users but removes the defining feature (native email) that defines the company's leadership in that sector.
-   **Price/Value Mismatch:** Matching the rival's price while offering significantly less functionality (no native apps, untested OS, smaller screen) guarantees market rejection.

**Risks:** Untested OS stability, lack of developer support, brand dilution.

**Kill Criterion:** If market research shows <20% of target business users are willing to rely on phone-pairing for core email functions at this price point, the project must stop.

DECISION: NO-GO
