First call: NO-GO. The unit economics and behavioral friction of a $699 connected juicer relying on proprietary, subscription-only inputs create an insurmountable barrier to mass adoption in 2016.

**Steelman:** A premium, hassle-free nutrition system where device subsidies are recouped via high-margin recurring revenue, justified by convenience and freshness verification.

**Attack:**
1.  **Price & Value:** At $699, the device costs ~10x a standard high-end blender. The "convenience" premium requires the per-bag cost to be competitive with fresh produce, yet proprietary packaging and logistics inherently inflate margins. If the subscription is priced to recover the hardware subsidy, the cost-per-juice will likely exceed $8–$10, alienating all but the wealthiest niche.
2.  **Connectivity Friction:** Requiring Wi-Fi for a countertop appliance in 2016 introduces unnecessary failure points (setup complexity, network drops) for a core mechanical function. If the device refuses to operate without a handshake, it creates immediate user resentment and support overhead.
3.  **Market Size:** The intersection of consumers willing to pay $699 upfront, commit to a subscription, and accept limited SKU variety is vanishingly small. Hardware startups of this capital intensity require mass-market velocity to survive; this model targets a luxury niche insufficient to sustain the burn rate implied by "tens of millions" raised.
4.  **Alternatives:** Consumers can buy a $200 blender and fresh fruit for 1/4 the cost with zero lock-in. The "freshness check" via app is a solution in search of a problem; visual inspection is free and instantaneous.

**Blocker:** The business model relies on a subscription margin high enough to subsidize complex custom hardware while remaining cheaper than the trivial alternative (buying fruit). The math does not work at a scale required to justify the funding raised. The internet dependency adds cost and risk without proportional value.

**Risks:** Supply chain complexity for proprietary bags; high churn if subscription costs spike; device becomes a brick if servers fail.

**Kill Criterion:** If pre-orders do not cover 20% of the tooling costs within 60 days at the proposed price point, the unit economics are proven false.

DECISION: NO-GO
