First call: GO. The current roadmap fails the core requirement for thin, efficient laptops, and the dominant architecture offers a clear path to competitiveness.

**Steelman:** Transitioning to the dominant architecture within two years, bridged by a translation layer, is the only viable strategy to secure hardware performance parity and long-term ecosystem relevance, despite short-term friction.

**Attack & Options:**
The primary blocker risk is the "freeze" effect on current sales and the pain of a prior transition. However, "do nothing" guarantees product irrelevance as thermal and efficiency gaps widen. Staying on the legacy path is a slow death; the proposed move is a sharp, survivable shock. The "distinctiveness" argument is weak if the distinct product cannot run coolly. A translation layer mitigates the immediate app gap, buying time for native recompilation.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the translation layer performs too poorly, validating developer skepticism, or if the announcement timing cannibalizes Q4 revenue without a clear "coming soon" hook. The cost of delay is losing the next hardware cycle entirely.

**Evidence & Blockers:**
The proposal admits the current supplier lags and cannot deliver "cool and efficient" chips. This is a hard factual blocker for the status quo. The risks listed (sales freeze, developer effort, speed penalty) are execution challenges, not fundamental flaws. They are managed by the two-year timeline and the inclusion of a translation layer. No fact presented shows the target users reject the dominant architecture; indeed, they likely demand its efficiency.

**Kill Criteria Check:**
The plan includes a specific timeline (~2 years) and a mitigation mechanism (translation layer). The risk of slower translated software is acknowledged, not hidden. There is no evidence that the company *cannot* execute this, only that a past transition was painful. Pain is not a blocker; impossibility is. Since the current path is impossible for thin laptops, the switch is mandatory.

**Decision:**
Blockers: NONE. The status quo is blocked by physics/supply; the proposal clears this.
Risks: Sales dip, developer friction, performance overhead. These are managed by the phased approach and translation tech.

DECISION: GO
