First call: NO-GO. The proposal demands high costs from price-sensitive users while stripping away the primary value drivers (apps, games) of a smartphone data plan.

Steelman: Launching a locked-down, carrier-exclusive social phone leverages an acquired team's expertise to capture the youth market quickly, avoiding direct competition with the main platform's later launch.

Attack:
1.  **Economic Mismatch:** Requiring a full smartphone data plan for a device without apps or games forces users to pay a premium for unused utility. Since the target demographic is price-sensitive, the total cost of ownership (device + plan) will likely exceed their budget, especially when competitors offer similar social features on cheaper hardware or existing devices.
2.  **Value Proposition Void:** A "social phone" with no app store and no games is functionally inferior to any standard smartphone available in 2010. Without third-party extensibility, the device relies entirely on built-in features, which rarely sustain long-term engagement for young users.
3.  **Cannibalization & Timing:** Launching two niche devices before the main platform creates market confusion and dilutes marketing spend. If the main platform launches later the same year, these dedicated devices will appear obsolete almost immediately.
4.  **Execution Risk:** The history of internal conflict between teams suggests integration will be slow and contentious, exacerbating the already admitted delays.

Alternatives:
*   **Do Nothing:** Wait for the main platform launch and integrate social features as a software layer, avoiding hardware fragmentation and carrier lock-in.
*   **Software-Only:** License the social features to existing carrier devices, removing the hardware risk and upfront capex.

Pre-mortem: The project fails because carriers refuse to subsidize a device with no broad appeal, and teens reject the high monthly fees for a "dumb" smartphone. The internal conflict causes missed launch windows, leaving inventory unsold when the main platform arrives.

Blockers:
*   The unit economics fail: Price-sensitive users cannot justify a full data plan for a device with strictly limited functionality (no apps/games).
*   Strategic contradiction: Launching a limited hardware line immediately prior to a major platform release undermines the main launch and fractures the brand.

Risks: Internal team conflict delaying launch further; carrier rejection due to low projected uptake.

DECISION: NO-GO
