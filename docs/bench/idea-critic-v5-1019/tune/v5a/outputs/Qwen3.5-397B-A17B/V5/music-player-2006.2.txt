First call: NO-GO. The proposal enters a saturated market late, abandons the company's own established copy-protection standard (alienating existing partners), and offers no price advantage against a dominant incumbent with a loyal ecosystem.

**Steelman:** A superior hardware experience (larger screen, wireless sharing) and integrated store can disrupt the market leader despite lateness, provided the ecosystem shift doesn't fracture partner relations.

**Attack:**
1.  **Strategic Contradiction:** Dropping the copy-protection standard the company previously promoted to partners is a betrayal of trust. This invites retaliation from labels and device makers who invested based on the old standard, potentially blocking content licensing essential for the store.
2.  **Market Reality:** Being "late" with "no clear price advantage" against a dominant player with high switching costs (loyal ecosystem) is a fatal commercial flaw. Hardware features alone rarely overcome network effects without a disruptive price or exclusive content, neither of which is present.
3.  **Regulatory/Label Risk:** Wireless sharing and removing DRM explicitly conflict with label restrictions mentioned in the risks. Launching a feature labels may restrict guarantees a post-launch crippled product or legal blockade.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves partner relationships. A "cheaper alternative" would be licensing the existing dominant platform rather than building a conflicting ecosystem.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** The proposal admits dropping the company's own promoted copy-protection standard. This contradicts the requirement for partner support to secure music labels, creating an immediate supply-side failure.
*   **Fact:** No price advantage + late entry + dominant competitor = negative expected value. The marketing budget cannot buy market share if the value proposition (price/content) is inferior.

**Risks:** Label restrictions on sharing could render the key differentiator (wireless sharing) illegal or unusable at launch.

**Kill Criterion:** If major labels refuse licensing terms due to the DRM shift before the store launch, the project must stop.

**Decision:** The plan relies on partners supporting a standard the company just abandoned and assumes users will switch ecosystems for features that may be legally restricted. The internal contradiction on DRM is a hard blocker.

DECISION: NO-GO
