First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a capital-intensive path while explicitly admitting the company lacks the necessary funds for the factory and batteries.

**Steelman:** A focused EV-only strategy with a repurposed large factory could accelerate production if capital were secured and infrastructure risks mitigated.

**Attack:**
1.  **Capital Mismatch (Blocker):** The proposal demands buying a large factory and funding battery packs, yet states the company has "far less capital than needed." Committing to an exclusive EV lineup without solving this funding gap guarantees insolvency before production begins. You cannot build a capital-heavy moat with empty pockets.
2.  **Infrastructure Void:** In 2008, charging stations are scarce. A full-size sedan requires reliable long-distance charging to compete with established内燃机 (ICE) vehicles. Without a partnered charging network or home-charging incentives, the product is functionally limited, reducing market viability.
3.  **Execution Risk:** Mass production experience is low. Buying a large, complex factory amplifies this risk rather than reducing it. A smaller pilot or contract manufacturing would be safer, but the proposal insists on a large owned facility.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves cash. A "cheaper alternative" would be contract assembly or a limited regional pilot to prove demand before buying real estate. The proposal rejects these by demanding immediate, total commitment.

**Pre-mortem:** The company buys the factory, burns cash on retooling, fails to secure battery supply due to cost, and cannot sell cars because customers fear range anxiety. The company enters bankruptcy with a stranded asset.

**Decision Logic:**
*   **Blockers:** The plan requires capital the company explicitly does not have. This is a factual contradiction between the goal (build large factory) and the constraint (insufficient funds).
*   **Risks:** Market readiness, competitor response, and operational inexperience are high but secondary to the immediate lack of funds.
*   **Kill Criterion:** If the company cannot secure financing equal to the factory purchase plus 18 months of operating costs *before* signing the purchase agreement, the plan must stop.

The proposal asks to jump off a cliff and build a plane on the way down, without the materials for the plane.

DECISION: NO-GO
