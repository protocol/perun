First call: NO-GO. The proposal bets $1B+ on unproven demand for premium short-form subscriptions amid known fatigue and abundant free alternatives.

Steelman: A high-budget, mobile-native platform featuring A-list talent and adaptive vertical/horizontal technology can carve a profitable niche by offering superior quality that free user-generated content cannot match, justifying a subscription despite market saturation.

Attack:
1.  **Demand Mismatch:** The core assumption—that users will pay for "premium" short-form—is contradicted by the prevalence of free, high-engagement short video (TikTok, YouTube Shorts) and rising subscription fatigue. Short-form consumption is habit-driven and infinite; gating it behind a paywall removes the frictionless utility that defines the format's success.
2.  **Cost Structure vs. Value:** High production budgets and A-list talent are optimized for long-form retention or theatrical events, not sub-10-minute clips. The unit economics likely fail: the cost per minute of content vastly exceeds what users pay per minute in subscription fees, especially when competitors offer similar duration content for free.
3.  **Feature Irrelevance:** Adaptive orientation is a baseline expectation, not a differentiator worth a billion-dollar moat. It does not solve the fundamental value proposition gap.
4.  **Competition:** The proposal admits competition for "phone attention" but ignores that incumbents already own the short-form habit. Displacing a free, algorithmic feed with a paid, curated library requires behavior change the market has historically rejected for this format.

Blockers:
-   **Economic Contradiction:** The plan combines "high production budgets" with "short-form" in a subscription model where the lifetime value (LTV) of a user interested only in short clips is unlikely to cover the Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) and content amortization. The math implies a negative margin per viewer unless volume is impossible to achieve given the paywall.
-   **Unproven Core Premise:** The proposal explicitly lists "no proven demand for premium short-form" as a risk yet proceeds with a $1B+ launch. This is not a risk to mitigate; it is a fatal flaw in the thesis.

Kill Criterion: If a pilot cannot demonstrate that users will pay specifically for *short-form* (vs. long-form) at a price point that covers the high production cost per minute within 6 months, the venture fails. The current plan skips this validation.

DECISION: NO-GO
