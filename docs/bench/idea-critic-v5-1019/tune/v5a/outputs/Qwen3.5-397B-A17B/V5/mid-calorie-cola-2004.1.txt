First call: NO-GO. The proposal targets a shrinking trend with a product that likely satisfies no one, at a premium price, while ignoring the established diet alternative.

**Steelman:** A mid-calorie cola captures young men wanting taste without the full sugar load of regular cola, differentiating from the "artificial" perception of diet drinks, justifying a premium before the low-carb trend fades.

**Options:**
1.  **Proposed:** High risk. Alienates diet loyalists (too many calories) and regular loyalists (wrong taste/price). Competes directly with a rival in a potentially dying category.
2.  **Do Nothing:** Maintain current portfolio. Diet cola is already growing; regular cola serves the taste-first segment. Zero investment risk.
3.  **Cheaper Alternative:** Reformulate existing regular cola slightly or market the existing diet variant more aggressively to young men if taste is the barrier.

**Fit:** If the north star is volume growth in a health-conscious market, this misfires by straddling two distinct value propositions. It assumes a "middle" exists where consumers historically choose extremes (all sugar or no sugar).

**Pre-mortem:** The launch fails because the low-carb trend proves to be a short-term fad, leaving a premium-priced product with no enduring niche. Young men reject the premium price for a "compromise" taste, while the rival captures the small remaining trend-chaser market. The cost of delay is minimal since the trend's expiration date is unknown and likely near.

**Evidence:** The proposal itself admits the risks: diet cola is already growing (proving the market has a solution), and the core fear is satisfying neither group. The "half-sugar" value proposition is unproven and contradicts typical binary consumer behavior in this category.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Value Proposition Failure:** The stated risk that the drink satisfies neither group is a fundamental product flaw, not just a risk. Without evidence of demand for *exactly* half-sugar at a *premium*, the core claim collapses.
2.  **Trend Timing:** Launching a premium product based on a trend explicitly identified as a potential "fad" with a rival already entering is a strategic error.

**Risks:** Brand confusion, wasted campaign spend, cannibalization of existing SKUs.

**Kill Criterion:** If a blind taste test shows <20% preference over diet cola among young men, stop immediately.

DECISION: NO-GO
