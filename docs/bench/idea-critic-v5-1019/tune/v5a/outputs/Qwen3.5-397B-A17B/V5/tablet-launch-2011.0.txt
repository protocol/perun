First call: NO-GO. Launching a premium-priced tablet with a tiny app catalog against a dominant incumbent, while the OS has negligible share and leadership is unstable, ignores the primary driver of tablet adoption: ecosystem depth.

**Steelman:** The brand's hardware quality and marketing spend can overcome the app gap quickly enough to attract developers before cash runs out.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves capital. A "low-price entry" strategy could offset the app deficit with value, but the proposal insists on matching the leader's price. Without a price advantage or app parity, the value proposition is empty.
2.  **Fit:** If the north star is ecosystem growth, a premium price point creates a barrier to entry that contradicts the need for rapid user acquisition to lure developers.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The device launches; reviewers praise the hardware but pan the "app desert." Consumers, seeing no unique utility and high cost, stick to the leader. Developers see low sales and ignore the outreach. The product is discontinued in 18 months, wasting the acquisition cost.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits the app catalog is a "small fraction" and the OS has "almost no market share." History shows platforms fail when they cannot solve the chicken-and-egg problem immediately. Heavy advertising cannot fix a lack of utility (apps).
5.  **Blockers:** The pricing strategy is a factual blocker. Charging market-leading prices for a fraction of the value (apps) is economically unsound. The "leadership transition" adds execution risk to an already fragile plan. There is no kill criterion or pilot; it is a full-scale launch commitment.

**Kill Criterion:** If developer sign-ups do not triple within 90 days post-launch, the platform fails. The proposal commits to heavy spend before validating this.

**Verdict:** The plan demands premium pricing without premium ecosystem value. It bets on marketing to solve a product deficit. This is a capital destruction event.

Blockers: Pricing parity with inferior utility; no validated path to app parity; leadership instability during critical launch.
Risks: Consumer rejection; developer apathy; cash burn.

DECISION: NO-GO
