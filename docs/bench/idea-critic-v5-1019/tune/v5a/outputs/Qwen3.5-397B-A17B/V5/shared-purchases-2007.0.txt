First call: NO-GO. The proposal trades long-term user trust for short-term ad data, introducing a systemic reputational risk that an "opt-out" cannot neutralize.

**Steelman:** Transparency via notifications and opt-outs legitimizes sharing purchase data, turning social proof into a revenue stream while respecting user agency.

**Attack:**
1.  **Incentive Mismatch:** Users join to connect, not to broadcast consumption. Framing purchases as "news" conflates social interaction with commercial surveillance. Sensitive purchases (medical, financial, gifts) create immediate personal harm if leaked, regardless of an opt-out default.
2.  **Opt-Out Fallacy:** Privacy research consistently shows default settings dictate behavior; most users never change them. Relying on users to "decline" shifts the burden of privacy protection to the victim, signaling that the platform values data extraction over safety.
3.  **Partner Backlash:** Partners fear public embarrassment from negative reviews or awkward social exposures attached to their brands. They will resist a feature that makes their sales data a public spectacle, threatening the very ad ecosystem this aims to boost.
4.  **Irreversibility:** As noted, this is a one-way door. Once users feel surveilled, trust evaporates and cannot be restored by later tweaking settings. The "notification step" is a temporary安抚 (soothing measure) that will likely be removed to increase adoption, accelerating the backlash.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Preserve trust; seek less invasive ad targeting (contextual).
*   **Aggregated Data:** Share anonymized trends with partners, not individual user actions with friends.
*   **Explicit Opt-In:** Only share if a user explicitly clicks "share this purchase." (Lower volume, higher trust).

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, a high-profile user's sensitive purchase is mocked in their feed, sparking a media firestorm. Users mass-delete accounts; partners suspend campaigns. The feature is quietly killed, but the brand is permanently tagged as "untrustworthy."

**Blockers:** The core mechanism (broadcasting private transactions to social graphs) fundamentally conflicts with user expectations of privacy and creates unrecoverable reputational liability. The proposed mitigations (notifications, opt-out) are insufficient to cap this risk.

**Kill Criterion:** If >0.1% of users opt out within the first week or a single sensitive purchase leaks publicly, the project fails. The proposal lacks a pre-launch cap on data sensitivity.

DECISION: NO-GO
