First call: NO-GO. The $6 billion cash price for a fast-moving technology asset, paid at a large premium while alienating existing agency customers, fails the basic operator test of capital preservation and strategic fit.

Steelman: Acquiring the platform is the only remaining path to catch the search leader after they secured a competing asset, making the high cost a necessary entry fee.

Alternatives: Doing nothing avoids immediate conflict and cash burn but cedes the display market. Building internally is slower but cheaper and avoids customer conflicts. Buying a smaller, non-conflicting tech stack offers a safer, staged entry. None of these require a $6 billion immediate outlay that jeopardizes relationships with current revenue sources.

Fit: While the goal to compete in display ads aligns with broader ambitions, the method directly undermines the company's existing agency ecosystem, a core strength. The strategy assumes ownership is superior to partnership, an unproven premise in 2007's fragmented market.

Pre-mortem: The acquisition fails because integrated agency clients defect to competitors due to perceived conflict, revenue collapses, and the technology becomes obsolete within two years due to the market's rapid shift, leaving the company with a $6 billion stranded asset. The door is one-way; cash spent cannot be recovered.

Evidence: The proposal admits to a "large premium" and "fast-changing technology." No evidence suggests the buyer can manage the conflict with existing customers or that the target's tech will remain relevant long enough to justify the price. The competitor's move creates pressure, not a validated business case for this specific price tag.

Blockers:
1. The $6 billion cash price for a volatile tech asset lacks a verified ROI model in the proposal.
2. The stated conflict with existing agency customers threatens the core business without a mitigation plan.
3. The "fast-changing" nature of the market makes the long-term value of the specific technology highly uncertain, rendering the premium unjustifiable.

Risks: Execution failure, cultural clash, and regulatory scrutiny.

Kill criterion: If agency client churn exceeds 5% within 12 months post-acquisition, the strategic premise is invalidated.

DECISION: NO-GO
