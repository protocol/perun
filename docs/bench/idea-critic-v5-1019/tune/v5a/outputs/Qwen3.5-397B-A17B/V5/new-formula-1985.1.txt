First call: NO-GO. Replacing a heritage asset based solely on blind taste data ignores the brand's emotional equity.

**Steelman:** A superior-tasting product will reverse share loss by satisfying revealed consumer preference for sweetness.
**Attack:** The evidence is flawed. Blind tests isolate flavor from brand meaning, measuring only physical sensation, not purchase intent. Consumers buy the symbol, not just the liquid. Removing the original eliminates the baseline for comparison and triggers a psychological reactance: the "endowment effect" makes the lost formula instantly more valuable. The testing protocol failed to disclose the consequence (total replacement), invalidating the data for this specific strategic move.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Retain share erosion but preserve brand trust.
2.  **Line extension:** Launch the sweeter formula as a new variant alongside the original. This captures the "sweetness" preference segment without alienating heritage loyalists.
3.  **Proposed plan:** Total replacement. Highest risk, irreversible in the short term.

**Fit:** This contradicts the north star of brand heritage. A century-old formula is not a bug; it is the product's core differentiator against generic competitors.

**Pre-mortem:** The launch triggers a public outcry. Loyalists perceive the removal as a corporate betrayal of their identity. Media narratives shift from "taste" to "loss." Sales plummet not because the new drink tastes bad, but because the old one is gone. Bottlers face riots; inventory of the old formula becomes black-market gold.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits testing did not inform participants of the removal. This is a fatal experimental design flaw for a replacement strategy. The risks (emotional attachment, no fallback) are not hypothetical; they are direct consequences of the plan's structure.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Invalid Data:** The core evidence (taste tests) explicitly excluded the primary variable (removal of choice), rendering it useless for a "replace entirely" decision.
2.  **No Stop Condition:** The plan withdraws the original entirely with no fallback. If the reaction is negative (highly probable given the emotional risk), there is no immediate pivot path other than a humiliating reversal.

**Kill Criterion:** If consumer sentiment turns negative within 14 days, the brand equity damage will exceed any taste-preference gains. The lack of a fallback makes this a one-way door with no safety net.

DECISION: NO-GO
