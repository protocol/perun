First call: NO-GO. The proposal demands committing the entire company to a single, unproven product line while lacking the capital for essential infrastructure (factory, batteries) and facing a non-existent charging network.

Steelman: A bold, exclusive focus on EVs could create a first-mover brand monopoly if executed perfectly. However, attacking this reveals fatal flaws in the 2008 context.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve cash, wait for battery costs to drop and charging networks to expand.
2. Hybrid transition: Produce limited EVs while maintaining ICE revenue to fund infrastructure.
3. Proposed plan: All-in EV with undercapitalized factory purchase.
The proposed plan fails to beat "do nothing" because the lack of capital and charging infrastructure makes mass production impossible, not just risky.

Fit: Assuming the goal is survival and eventual market share, this plan ignores the immediate constraint: liquidity. A strategy requiring billions in upfront capex without secured funding contradicts basic operational viability.

Pre-mortem: The company buys the factory but cannot tool it for battery production due to cash flow failure. With no charging network, consumer adoption stalls at zero. Established makers, having deeper pockets, wait for the startup to bankrupt itself before entering the segment aggressively. The cost of delay is low; the cost of immediate execution is total insolvency.

Evidence: The proposal admits the company lacks capital for the factory and batteries. In 2008, public charging was negligible. These are stated facts, not assumptions.

Blockers:
1. Capital mismatch: The plan requires buying a large factory and developing battery packs, but the proposal explicitly states the company has "far less capital than needed." This is a mathematical impossibility to execute as proposed.
2. Infrastructure void: Mass adoption of a full-size electric sedan in 2008 is blocked by the absence of charging stations, a fact the proposal acknowledges as a risk but offers no solution for.

Risks: Inexperience in mass production, competitor response.

Kill criterion: If the company cannot secure funding equal to the factory purchase price plus tooling costs before signing the acquisition, the plan must stop. No such funding path is identified.

The proposal asks to jump off a cliff and build a plane on the way down, without the materials to build the plane.

DECISION: NO-GO
