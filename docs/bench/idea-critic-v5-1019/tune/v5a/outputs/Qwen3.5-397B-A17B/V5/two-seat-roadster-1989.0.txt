First call: GO. The market void and low capital requirements for a lightweight, front-engine, rear-drive layout offer a high-reward entry point despite segment shrinkage.

**Steelman:** A low-cost, lightweight roadster exploits a neglected niche where mass-market makers fear to tread, leveraging simplicity to bypass high development costs and safety complexity.

**Attack & Options:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves capital but cedes the emerging enthusiast niche to future competitors. A heavy, feature-rich GT contradicts the "affordable/lightweight" thesis and increases risk. The proposed path is the only one fitting the "affordable" constraint.
2.  **Fit:** Assumes the north star is brand revitalization through emotional halo products rather than volume. This fits a mid-size maker needing differentiation.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if safety compliance destroys the cost advantage or if the "two-seat" limitation proves absolute rather than a filter for dedicated enthusiasts. However, the shrinking segment suggests low competition, not zero demand.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal states the segment has shrunk (fact) and mass-makers have exited (fact). This creates a supply vacuum. The lack of heritage is irrelevant if the price/value proposition is sound; heritage is built by the product, not just history.

**Blockers vs. Risks:**
*   **Blockers:** NONE. No fact provided proves the unit economics fail or that safety rules make the project impossible, only "costly." Cost can be managed via lightweighting.
*   **Risks:** Sales fading after early adopters; safety costs; two-seat limitation. These are execution risks, not fundamental flaws. They are mitigated by the "affordable" cap and the specific targeting of a gap left by giants.

**Kill Criteria:** If prototype safety compliance costs exceed 20% of the total BOM, or if pre-orders from the enthusiast press fall below 500 units within three months of reveal, the project pauses.

**Conclusion:** The risks are manageable constraints that define the product's character (light, simple, affordable). The absence of competition is a stronger signal than the shrinking market size, which likely reflects a lack of suitable product rather than lack of desire. The plan survives the attack.

DECISION: GO
