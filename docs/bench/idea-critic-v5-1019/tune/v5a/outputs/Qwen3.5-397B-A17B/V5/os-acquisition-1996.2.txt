First call: NO-GO. The proposal bets the company's survival on an unproven, low-revenue asset while ignoring the massive, unquantified cost of migrating the existing developer ecosystem, which is the true source of value.

Steelman: Acquiring this specific modern OS is the only remaining path to technical relevance because internal attempts failed, and the founder's unique vision justifies the premium despite past friction.

Options analysis shows "Do Nothing" leads to continued share erosion but preserves cash. "Licensing" offers immediate stability without integration risk. The proposed acquisition carries extreme integration risk (migrating developers) and cultural risk (founder return) for an asset with negligible market validation (few units sold). The price-to-revenue ratio is indefensible without assuming a future upside that the current facts do not support.

Pre-mortem: The integration fails because the acquired OS lacks the necessary drivers and application library, causing key developers to abandon the platform during the transition. The founder's return reignites internal conflict, distracting leadership during the critical migration window. The company runs out of cash before the new OS gains traction, resulting in total collapse. This is a one-way door; once the cash is spent and the old OS deprecated, there is no return.

Evidence check: The proposal admits the target has "sold few units" and the price is "high against small revenue." There is no evidence provided that this specific OS can support the existing hardware or software base, nor that the founder can collaborate effectively. The risk of developer migration is stated but unmitigated.

Blockers:
1. The core value of a computer maker is its developer ecosystem; the proposal offers no plan to migrate them, only a technical core with no apps.
2. The financial terms (high price/low revenue) violate basic capital preservation principles given the company's falling share.
3. The "difficult history" of the founder introduces a high-probability execution risk that is not addressed.

Risks: Cultural clash, technical incompatibility, market rejection of the new OS.

Kill criterion: If the acquired OS cannot run 50% of the top 100 existing applications within 6 months, the project must be abandoned. No such stop condition is proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
