First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits $36 billion to integrate two fundamentally incompatible network technologies while explicitly acknowledging the necessity of running legacy equipment with limited data capability.

Steelman: The merger acquires a sticky, high-value business segment via a unique push-to-talk service that justifies the $36 billion price tag and the pain of integrating incompatible networks.

Attack: The core value driver (push-to-talk) relies on the very legacy technology that creates the integration bottleneck. Running dual networks indefinitely destroys the synergies required to justify the purchase price, while migrating the specific business users to a new, unproven (for them) technology risks churning the exact customer base being bought. The "large goodwill" indicates the price already assumes successful integration, leaving no margin for the inevitable technical friction of merging incompatible 2005-era standards.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Avoids $36B cash burn and integration chaos; forgoes the business segment.
2. Build/Buy compatible PTT: Develop or license a push-to-talk solution on the buyer's existing network for a fraction of $36B.
3. The Proposal: Pay a premium to acquire a technical debt burden that threatens the core network's stability.

Fit: In 2005, the industry north star is data scalability and network simplification. This deal moves in the opposite direction by cementing legacy, data-poor infrastructure.

Pre-mortem: Integration stalls for years; the dual-network operational cost swallows the margin from the new business users; competitors launch superior IP-based push-to-talk services on modern networks, rendering the acquired legacy tech obsolete before migration completes.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits "incompatible technologies," "limited for data," and "run one on legacy equipment." These are not hypothetical risks but stated facts of the deal structure.

Blockers:
1. The stated necessity to run legacy equipment contradicts the strategic imperative of network simplification and data growth.
2. The $36B valuation relies on synergies that the admitted technical incompatibility makes impossible to realize without destroying the acquired product's utility.

Risks: Customer churn during migration, inflated CapEx for dual maintenance, technology obsolescence.

Kill Criterion: If a cost-benefit analysis shows the Net Present Value of migrating the legacy users to the main network exceeds the cost of building a native PTT solution, the deal fails.

DECISION: NO-GO
