First call: NO-GO. The proposal discards the brand's primary shelf cue for an unproven aesthetic, risking immediate sales collapse among a loyal, older demographic.

**Steelman:** A modernized design will attract new buyers without alienating existing ones if supported by heavy advertising.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Evidence:** The proposal admits the old image is the "main shelf cue" and the base is "older." Removing the sole visual anchor for this demographic contradicts the stated facts. No evidence suggests the new "minimalist" design functions as a cue.
2.  **Options:** "Do nothing" preserves revenue. A "staged pilot" in one region would test recognition before a nationwide rollout. The proposal rejects staging for a "all stores at once" launch, maximizing downside risk.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure: Shoppers scan the aisle, miss the familiar orange-with-straw, and assume the brand is out of stock or discontinued. They buy a competitor. The ad campaign runs too late to stop the initial shelf rejection.
4.  **Door & Delay:** This is a one-way door; printing millions of cartons is irreversible. The cost of delay (waiting for a pilot) is negligible compared to the cost of a nationwide delisting by confused customers.
5.  **Blocker Test:** The stated fact that the "old image is the brand's main shelf cue" directly contradicts the plan to remove it without a verified replacement cue. The plan offers no metric or pilot to prove the new design works before committing the entire inventory.

**Kill Criterion:** If a regional pilot shows a >2% drop in repeat purchases within 4 weeks, stop immediately. The current proposal lacks this stop condition, committing to full scale before verification.

**Verdict:** The plan fails the blocker test. It ignores the explicit risk that shoppers will fail to recognize the product, relies on an unproven cue, and eliminates the possibility of a safe, staged learning loop. The "large advertising campaign" cannot compensate for immediate shelf invisibility.

Blockers: Removes stated primary shelf cue without validation; irreversible nationwide commitment before testing.
Risks: Alienating older loyalists; high re-print costs if failed.

DECISION: NO-GO
