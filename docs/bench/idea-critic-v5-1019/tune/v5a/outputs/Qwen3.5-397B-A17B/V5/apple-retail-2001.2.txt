First call: NO-GO. A loss-making manufacturer with 3–5% share entering a low-margin, high-fixed-cost business during a recession contradicts basic survival logic.

**Steelman:** Direct retail is the only way to demonstrate the new digital-music strategy and stop third-party staff from pushing competitors, potentially restoring brand viability.

**Attack:**
1.  **Financial Suicide:** The company is already losing money. Adding high mall leases and inventory costs creates massive fixed overhead. In a recession, sales volatility could trigger immediate insolvency.
2.  **Channel Warfare:** Opening stores directly competes with the existing reseller network. Resellers, already prone to pushing cheaper rivals, will retaliate by dropping the brand entirely, collapsing the remaining 95% of sales volume before the new stores gain traction.
3.  **Track Record:** The industry has a documented history of failed manufacturer-led retail. Ignoring this precedent without a unique, proven operational advantage is reckless.
4.  **Opportunity Cost:** Capital spent on real estate and store staffing diverts resources from the core product and the digital-music strategy itself. A cheaper alternative (shop-in-shop displays, dedicated training for reseller staff, or online demos) achieves the "demonstration" goal without the existential risk.

**Blockers:**
*   **Cash Flow:** The proposal adds high fixed costs to a loss-making entity during a downturn. No evidence suggests the company has the liquidity to sustain years of retail losses while building scale.
*   **Channel Collapse:** The plan ignores the near-certain reaction of resellers who will view this as hostile, likely resulting in a net loss of total market access.

**Risks:**
*   Recession deepening, reducing discretionary spending on premium PCs.
*   Inventory obsolescence in a fast-moving tech market.
*   Failure to replicate the "experience" that justifies the cost.

**Kill Criterion:** If a pilot store does not generate enough incremental gross profit to cover its specific lease and operating costs within 6 months, the entire rollout must stop. However, given the current financial state, even a successful pilot may not save the core business if the channel revolt occurs.

The plan bets the entire company on a high-cost, unproven distribution channel while alienating existing partners. It fails the "do no harm" test for a distressed firm.

DECISION: NO-GO
