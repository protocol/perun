First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a full-scale launch ("add an option") despite admitting the core value drivers (catalogue size, video quality, broadband penetration) are currently weak and the unit economics (licensing costs) are hostile, while explicitly risking the cash cow (DVDs) against future giants without a defined stop condition.

**Steelman:** Launching now secures early user behavior data and bargaining leverage with studios before competitors lock the market, treating the loss as a necessary R&D cost to survive the inevitable shift from physical media.

**Attack:**
1.  **Blocker (Unit Economics & Value):** The proposal admits the catalogue is "small and mostly older" with "poor" quality while licensing costs are "high." Without a mechanism to lower rights costs or improve quality, the service offers negative value relative to its cost structure. Launching this specific product configuration burns cash to deliver a subpar experience that accelerates churn rather than building loyalty.
2.  **Blocker (Missing Kill Criteria):** The plan proposes immediate inclusion for "seven million subscribers" with no cap, pilot group, or metric to validate demand before scaling. If cannibalization occurs immediately (subscribers dropping DVD rentals for the inferior stream), there is no stated threshold to halt the rollout. A bet this large requires a "go-if" condition (e.g., "pilot with 50k users until retention > X%"), which is absent.
3.  **Strategic Exposure:** Entering a high-cost, low-quality market against "big technology" entrants without a differentiated wedge invites immediate destruction. The "first mover" advantage is nullified if the product is objectively worse than the status quo (DVDs) and the coming competition.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, DVD revenue has dropped 15% due to cannibalization, streaming retention is near zero due to poor content/quality, and the company has burned significant cash on licenses, leaving it too weak to negotiate when broadband adoption finally matures.

**Verdict:** The proposal fails the blocker test because it mandates full-scale execution of a financially negative and strategically vulnerable product without a pilot, cap, or kill criterion. It bets the profitable core on an unproven, inferior iteration.

DECISION: NO-GO
