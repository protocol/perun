First call: GO. The growth of small-computer makers signals a market shift that a "build everything itself" model cannot capture quickly enough.

Steelman: To survive the shift to personal computing, the company must bypass its slow, integrated processes by creating an autonomous unit that leverages external speed (off-the-shelf parts, outside software) and distribution (retail), accepting lower margins and copycat risks as the cost of entry.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Retain high margins but cede the emerging mass market to agile rivals, risking long-term irrelevance.
2. Integrated build: Attempt to build custom parts and sell direct; too slow to catch the growth wave, missing the window.
3. Proposed approach: Fast entry via open architecture and retail. It beats "do nothing" by securing a foothold before the market consolidates.

Fit: This contradicts the current "vertical integration" north star but aligns with the survival imperative implied by the rivals' growth. The "open design" is a necessary trade-off for speed, not a strategic drift.

Pre-mortem: The project fails if the internal culture strangles the separate team's autonomy or if suppliers prioritize competitors. It is a two-way door; if it fails, the company loses only the investment of a small team, not its core mainframe business. The cost of delay is total exclusion from the personal computer era.

Evidence: The premise relies on the observed fact of rival growth. No internal data contradicts the need to act.

Blockers: NONE. The risks (copying, supplier dependence, lower margins, no retail experience) are inherent to the new market structure, not flaws in the plan. The plan explicitly accepts these to gain speed. There is no factual evidence provided that the company *cannot* execute this specific structure; the lack of experience is a gap to be filled by the "outside software" and "independent stores" partners.

Risks: Cultural contamination of the mainframe business; brand dilution; supplier failure. These are managed by keeping the team "small" and "separate."

Kill criterion: If the separate team cannot ship a prototype within 12 months or secure 10 retail partners within 6 months, halt the initiative. Leading metric: Units sold through independent channels in Q1 post-launch.

DECISION: GO
