First call: NO-GO. The proposal trades a scarce strategic asset (the computer platform brand) for negligible upside (small royalties) while ceding control of the consumer experience to an unproven partner in a dominated market.

**Steelman:** Licensing allows rapid, low-capital entry into the consumer console market by leveraging a toy manufacturer's distribution, generating immediate royalty revenue without the licensor needing to master consumer marketing or hardware manufacturing at scale.

**Attack:**
1.  **Economics:** "Small royalty per unit" in a low-cost hardware segment likely fails to cover the opportunity cost of diluting the primary brand or supporting the ecosystem. The upside is capped; the downside (brand confusion) is uncapped.
2.  **Incentive Mismatch:** The licensee bears marketing costs but pays only a small royalty. If the market is already dominated by established players (as stated), the licensee has little incentive to spend heavily on marketing a marginal product with thin margins. The proposal admits this risk ("licensee may not market the product hard") but offers no mitigation.
3.  **Strategic Disconnect:** Leaving marketing to the licensee guarantees the "consumers may not connect the product with the licensor's brand" outcome. This defeats the purpose of a computer maker seeking a consumer presence; it creates a generic white-label device that cannibalizes prestige without building equity.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves brand integrity. A joint venture with shared marketing control would align incentives better than a passive royalty model.

**Blockers:**
*   **Incentive Failure:** The stated risk that the licensee will not market hard is a structural flaw of the compensation model (small royalty). As proposed, the plan relies on the partner to solve a problem they are financially disincentivized to fix.
*   **Brand Dilution:** The plan explicitly accepts that consumers will not connect the product to the brand. For a computer maker with a "small share" trying to grow, launching a product that actively fails to build brand recognition is counter-productive to the core goal.

**Risks:** Market dominance by incumbents makes failure likely even with perfect execution; here, execution is structurally compromised.

**Kill Criterion:** If the licensee's projected marketing spend per unit is less than 15% of the wholesale price, the product will not gain traction against established rivals.

DECISION: NO-GO
