First call: GO. The $35 price point creates a unique economic category distinct from laptops or tablets, directly addressing the charity's goal of low-cost access for schools despite peripheral requirements.

Steelman: A $35 single-board computer is the only viable option to saturate schools with programmable hardware where budget constraints preclude even "cheap" laptops, accepting that users must source peripherals separately.

Options analysis:
1. Do nothing: Programming interest continues to decline due to hardware costs.
2. Cheap laptops/tablets: Still too expensive for 1:1 deployment in tight budgets; closed ecosystems limit low-level programming.
3. This proposal: Unbeatable unit cost, open hardware, though it shifts peripheral burden to the buyer.

Fit: Aligns perfectly with the north star of rekindling student interest through affordability and hands-on hardware access.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the "bring your own peripheral" requirement creates friction too high for non-technical schools, or if the charity cannot manage supply chains, leading to stockouts that kill momentum. However, these are execution risks, not fundamental flaws. The door is two-way; unsold inventory can be liquidated, and manufacturing can be scaled up or down.

Evidence: The proposal states a $35 price point. Even assuming $20 for peripherals, the total ($55) remains significantly below the lowest-cost laptop (~$200+ in 2012 context). The risk of sell-outs indicates demand, not a flaw. Lack of experience is a hurdle, not a blocker, solvable by hiring or partnering with the licensed distributors mentioned.

Blockers: NONE. No fact presented proves the model fails; the low price is a definitive advantage that existing alternatives do not match.
Risks: Supply chain inexperience, peripheral friction, potential stockouts.

Kill criterion: If the first batch does not sell out within 6 months or if return rates due to "missing peripheral" confusion exceed 15%, halt mass production and pivot to bundled kits. Leading metric: Units sold to educational institutions per quarter.

DECISION: GO
