First call: NO-GO. The proposal combines maximum engineering risk with maximum deployment risk while alienating the core user base that drives traffic.

Steelman: A complete rewrite with a new algorithm and big-bang migration is the only way to reverse traffic decline and compete with a rival.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Traffic continues to slow; rival gains share. Low risk, negative trajectory.
2. **Iterative update:** A/B test surfacing changes on the current stack; gradual migration. Lower engineering risk, preserves rollback, validates community response.
3. **Proposal (Big Bang):** High engineering cost, zero rollback, high probability of community revolt due to changed voting mechanics.

Fit: The plan contradicts the stated fact that the community values existing mechanics. Changing these without validation risks destroying the network effect.

Pre-mortem: The launch fails because the new algorithm surfaces publisher content over community favorites, causing power users to leave. Simultaneously, the rewrite introduces critical bugs. Because the migration is instant, there is no way to revert, resulting in total service degradation and mass exodus to the rival.

Evidence: The proposal admits the community values current mechanics yet plans to change them globally. It admits "large engineering risk" and "no rollback" yet proceeds. No data suggests users want publisher-favored content.

Blockers:
1. **No Rollback:** Moving all users at once with no rollback capability is a fatal flaw for a core product change. If the new version fails, the site dies.
2. **Unvalidated Core Change:** Altering the voting mechanics (the community's primary value) without a pilot or A/B test ignores the explicit risk of alienating the base.
3. **Resource Misallocation:** A full rewrite diverts all engineering capacity to building new features rather than fixing the specific traffic leakage, while a cheaper alternative (iterative testing) exists.

Risks: Community revolt, technical debt in new codebase, rival captures market during downtime/instability.

Kill Criterion: If a beta of the new surfacing logic to 5% of users results in a >10% drop in return visitor frequency within 48 hours, the algorithm change is rejected.

The absence of a pilot, a rollback plan, or validation of the new voting mechanics constitutes a blocker. The plan must be revised to include iterative testing and a phased migration.

DECISION: NO-GO
