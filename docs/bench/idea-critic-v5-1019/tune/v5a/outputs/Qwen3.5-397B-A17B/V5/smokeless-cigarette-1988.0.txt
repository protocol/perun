First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks to launch and promote widely based on test markets that have already surfaced fundamental adoption barriers (taste, lighting mechanism, complexity) without a plan to resolve them.

Steelman: This heated product offers a genuine reduction in smoke and could satisfy health-conscious smokers if the experience matches the convenience of burning tobacco.

Attack: The proposal admits smokers may dislike the taste and find the lighting/usage complicated. In 1988, cigarette switching is driven by habit and sensory consistency; a "complicated" ignition and inferior taste are fatal to mass adoption. Promoting widely before fixing these core usability and sensory flaws guarantees a high-visibility failure. Regulators will likely challenge "cleaner" claims absent definitive health data, creating legal exposure. The sunk cost of development is irrelevant to the decision to launch; throwing good money after bad by funding a wide launch for a flawed product compounds the loss.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids reputational damage and regulatory scrutiny; preserves capital.
2. Pause and iterate: Fix taste and simplify ignition before any wide promotion.
3. Limited regional rollout: Test the specific "cleaner" claim with strict legal guardrails, not a wide launch.

Pre-mortem: The product launches widely, smokers try it once, hate the taste and the awkward lighting, and return to standard cigarettes. Regulators issue cease-and-desist orders on health claims. The brand is associated with a failed, gimmicky product, and the large development spend is totally written off with added marketing losses.

Blockers:
- Core user value (taste/convenience) is acknowledged as potentially inferior to the incumbent product.
- Regulatory risk regarding health claims is high and unmitigated in the proposal.
- "Wide promotion" commits significant capital to a product with known, unresolved defects.

Risks:
- Consumer rejection due to habit inertia.
- Legal costs from regulatory pushback.

The proposal commits to a full-scale launch despite admitting the product may be worse than the current standard in the two areas that matter most to smokers: taste and ease of use. This is a guaranteed failure mode.

DECISION: NO-GO
