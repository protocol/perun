First call: NO-GO. The stated price is several times higher than leading competitors while offering inferior speed and a barren software ecosystem, making it commercially non-viable as proposed.

**Steelman:** This machine offers a revolutionary graphical interface that will define the future of office computing, justifying a premium price for early adopters despite current performance limitations.

**Attack:**
1.  **Price vs. Value:** At $10,000, the unit costs multiples of established business PCs. Without a critical mass of third-party software, the value proposition collapses. Office workers cannot justify the cost for a slower machine with fewer tools.
2.  **Internal Cannibalization:** A cheaper, similar internal alternative is imminent. Launching the expensive version first alienates customers who will shortly see a better deal, destroying brand trust and sales momentum.
3.  **Performance Bottleneck:** "Runs slowly" is a fatal flaw for productivity tools. A graphical interface demands responsiveness; slowness negates the usability benefit of the mouse and GUI.
4.  **Software Void:** "Little third-party software" means the hardware is a solution looking for a problem. Enterprises buy ecosystems, not just boxes.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Wait for the cheaper internal model or let competitors define the market.
*   **Pivot:** Slash the price to match or beat competitors, accepting lower margins to drive software adoption.
*   **Delay:** Postpone launch until performance is optimized and key software partners are secured.

**Pre-mortem:** The product launches at $10,000, sells poorly due to price and speed, developers ignore the tiny install base, and the cheaper internal model renders it obsolete within months, resulting in a total write-off.

**Blockers:**
*   **Price Disparity:** The price is explicitly stated as "several times" the competition with no compensating advantage (speed is worse, software is scarce).
*   **Internal Competition:** The existence of a cheaper, similar internal machine makes the current pricing strategy indefensible.
*   **Performance:** Slow performance contradicts the core utility of a business tool.

**Risks:** Market rejection, developer apathy, brand damage.

**Kill Criterion:** If the price cannot be reduced to within 20% of leading competitors before launch, the project must stop.

DECISION: NO-GO
