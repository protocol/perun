First call: NO-GO. The proposal targets a shrinking demographic with a product that satisfies neither core need (taste vs. calorie reduction) at a higher price point during a potential fad.

**Steelman:** A mid-calorie option captures young men wanting reduced sugar without the "diet" stigma, leveraging a premium price to signal quality before the low-carb trend fades.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Demand:** The core conflict is unresolved: calorie-conscious consumers already have diet cola; taste-focused consumers want full sugar. A "half-sugar" drink risks alienating both. The target (young men) is notoriously resistant to premium-priced, compromised taste profiles in this category.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves margin while the trend validates. Investing in existing diet brand equity or waiting for the rival to test the market offers lower risk. The rival's similar launch validates the threat but also the uncertainty; being second allows learning from their failure if the trend is a fad.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 12 months. Cause: The low-carb trend proved transient, leaving a premium product with no loyal base. The "half-sugar" formulation failed blind taste tests against full-sugar rivals and lacked the zero-calorie appeal for dieters. High marketing spend yielded low repeat purchase.
4.  **Evidence Gaps:** The proposal admits the trend "may be a fad" and that a rival is entering. There is no evidence provided that young men will pay a premium for less sugar if they don't perceive a health benefit equivalent to diet, or a taste benefit equivalent to regular.
5.  **Blocker:** The stated risk that the drink "may satisfy neither group" is a fundamental product-market fit failure, not a manageable risk. Without data proving a specific segment demands *exactly* half sugar at a *premium*, the core economic thesis collapses. The "low-carb fad" uncertainty combined with a "satisfy neither" formulation creates a high probability of inventory write-downs and brand dilution.

**Kill Criterion:** If blind taste tests show <40% preference over existing diet/full-sugar options among young men, or if the rival's launch shows <5% market share retention after 6 months, the project must stop.

**Decision:** The proposal relies on unproven assumptions about a niche preference while admitting the product might fail to satisfy anyone. The blocker (potential total lack of product-market fit) outweighs the speculative upside.

DECISION: NO-GO
