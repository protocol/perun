First call: NO-GO. The proposal contradicts the brand's core identity and customer value proposition without a mechanism to bridge the gap.

**Steelman:** A massive ad spend can temporarily override brand perception, forcing adults to trial a premium product despite the chain's "kids/value" reputation.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit:** The strategy ignores the "north star" of the current brand: child-centered value. Adults do not visit for premium experiences; they visit for speed and low cost. A premium price point actively repels the existing base while failing to attract the target demographic who associate the brand with children's entertainment, not culinary quality.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves margin. A "better way" would be testing a premium item in adult-demographic locations with minimal spend to validate demand before committing the "largest budget in industry history."
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The campaign launches, costs are sunk, and adults perceive the high price as unjustified for a "kids' place," while regulars feel alienated by the shift in focus and pricing. The brand becomes confused: too expensive for kids, too "kiddy" for adults.
4.  **Evidence:** The risks raised in the proposal itself (customers come for kids/value, adults see it as fast/not premium) are accepted facts that directly contradict the plan's core assumption that advertising alone can shift deep-seated brand equity.
5.  **Blocker:** The proposal commits to the "largest advertising and promotional budget the industry has seen" *before* demonstrating demand. This is an irreversible, uncapped spend on an unproven hypothesis that conflicts with verified brand perception. There is no pilot, no cap, and no kill criterion.

**Blockers:**
*   Commitment to maximum industry spend prior to demand validation.
*   Direct conflict between brand identity (kids/value) and product positioning (adult/premium) with no bridge strategy.

**Risks:**
*   Alienation of core customer base.
*   Failure to convert adult demographic due to brand stigma.

**Kill Criterion:** If a limited regional test does not achieve 15% adult mix within 90 days, halt national rollout. (Note: The proposal lacks this; it demands full scale immediately).

The plan fails the blocker test by betting the company on an unproven shift in brand perception using a blunt, uncapped financial instrument.

DECISION: NO-GO
