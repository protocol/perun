First call: NO-GO. The proposal admits fatal flaws in its own evidence: poor styling ratings, an aging platform, and compromised design integrity.

Steelman: A budget-friendly, camping-oriented crossover leveraging existing minivan assets to capture a niche youth market at a low break-even volume.

Attack: The core claim relies on "distinctive angular body" appealing to a "youthful" demographic, yet early clinics explicitly rated the styling poorly. In the automotive sector, styling is the primary purchase driver for this segment; a negative clinic result is a leading indicator of market failure, not a surmountable hurdle. Furthermore, marketing a vehicle with "no real off-road ability" alongside camping gear creates a credibility gap that invites consumer ridicule and returns. The aging platform suggests rising warranty costs and limited engineering flexibility to fix the identified design compromises without blowing the mid-$20,000s price point.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserves capital and brand reputation; avoids launching a known defective product.
2. Refresh existing minivan: Lower risk, utilizes the platform honestly without pretending to be an SUV.
3. Clean-sheet compact SUV: Higher cost, but addresses the styling and capability gaps properly.

Pre-mortem: The vehicle launches, sales hit 10,000 (one-third of break-even) in year one due to negative reviews on styling and lack of utility. The division incurs massive losses per unit, the brand suffers long-term perception damage as "cheap" rather than "youthful," and the project is cancelled after two years with significant write-offs.

Blockers:
1. Validated negative consumer demand: Early clinics already proved the target audience rejects the styling.
2. Contradictory value proposition: Selling "camping" capability on a vehicle with no off-road ability undermines the core use case.
3. Economic failure: The break-even target (30k) is highly unlikely given the identified product defects.

Risks: Platform reliability, brand dilution, cost overruns to fix design compromises.

Kill criterion: If a repeat clinic with the final prototype does not show a >20% improvement in styling preference over the current minivan by Q3, terminate immediately. However, given the admitted design compromises, this is unlikely to succeed.

DECISION: NO-GO
