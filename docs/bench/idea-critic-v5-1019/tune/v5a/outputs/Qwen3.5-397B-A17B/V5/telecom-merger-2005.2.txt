First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits $36 billion to acquire a network explicitly described as incompatible and data-limited, requiring the buyer to run legacy equipment while bearing massive goodwill risk.

**Steelman:** The merger is justified solely by acquiring the target's high-value business customers who rely on push-to-talk services, assuming their retention outweighs the technical debt.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fatal Incompatibility:** The core asset (the network) cannot be integrated with the buyer's existing infrastructure. Running two disparate networks indefinitely destroys the synergies typically required to justify a $36 billion premium.
2.  **Data Limitation:** Acquiring a "limited for data" network in 2005, just as mobile data adoption is accelerating, purchases a depreciating asset. The cost to migrate these specific users to a modern, compatible data-capable network is not quantified but will be astronomical.
3.  **Goodwill Risk:** The $36 billion valuation likely creates immense goodwill on the balance sheet. If integration fails or customers churn due to service degradation during the complex migration of incompatible tech, a massive write-down becomes inevitable.
4.  **Missing Alternative:** The proposal ignores the "build vs. buy" analysis. Could the buyer replicate the push-to-talk service on their own compatible, data-ready network for a fraction of $36 billion? Without evidence that this specific legacy network is the *only* way to keep these customers, the price is unjustified.

**Blocker:** The proposal admits the target's technology is incompatible and data-limited yet offers no concrete, costed plan to migrate these users to a unified, future-proof platform. Paying a premium for a network that must effectively be scrapped or siloed forever contradicts the logic of a merger. The "large goodwill" combined with "technology incompatibility" creates a near-certain path to value destruction.

**Kill Criterion:** If the cost to migrate the acquired business customers to the buyer's native technology exceeds 20% of the deal value ($7.2 billion) within 24 months, the deal thesis fails. No such cap or migration roadmap exists in the facts provided.

**Verdict:** The plan fails the basic operator test: do not buy a broken, incompatible factory at a premium price hoping to fix it later without a defined repair cost.

DECISION: NO-GO
