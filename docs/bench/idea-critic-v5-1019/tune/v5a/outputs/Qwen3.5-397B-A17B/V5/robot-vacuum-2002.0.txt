First call: GO. The proposal targets a distinct, price-sensitive segment with a simpler, cheaper solution, accepting trade-offs that the market may value over perfection.

**Steelman:** A randomized, low-cost robot can achieve acceptable cleanliness for most users at a price point mapping rivals cannot match, creating a new mass market rather than competing in the premium niche.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Cede the entire consumer robotics market to incumbents; high risk of irrelevance.
2.  **Build mapper:** Requires massive R&D, higher price, direct competition with established players; high failure risk given lack of experience.
3.  **Launch randomized (Proposed):** Lowers barrier to entry, leverages cost advantage, tests real consumer appetite for "good enough" automation.

**Fit:** Assumes the north star is market creation and volume adoption, not technical superiority. This fits a disruption strategy.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if "random" is perceived as "broken" rather than "simple," or if trust issues prevent any purchase. However, the low price mitigates the trust barrier. This is a two-way door; a failed launch provides critical data without bankrupting the firm. Cost of delay is high: competitors may lower prices or simplify their own tech.

**Evidence:** The lack of interest from floor-care companies suggests the *incumbent* channel sees no fit for their high-margin models, not that consumers won't buy a cheap alternative. The risks (no consumer experience, trust) are real but are execution challenges, not fundamental blockers. The "random" limitation is a feature of the cost structure, not a defect, provided expectations are managed.

**Blockers:** NONE. No fact presented proves the unit economics fail or that the target demographic explicitly rejects the value proposition. The risks cited are standard for new categories and do not constitute a "no-go" without evidence of impossibility.

**Risks:** Brand damage if the product is truly defective (not just simple); inability to scale manufacturing or support. These become kill criteria: if return rates exceed 15% in the first quarter or if unit costs cannot stay below 50% of the sale price, stop.

**Kill Criterion:** Halt if first-month return rate > 15% or if negative press regarding "safety/trust" exceeds 10% of total coverage. Leading metric: Units sold vs. marketing spend in the first 90 days.

DECISION: GO
