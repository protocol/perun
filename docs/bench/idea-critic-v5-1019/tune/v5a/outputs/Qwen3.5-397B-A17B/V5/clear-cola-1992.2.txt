First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a full nationwide launch and large marketing spend based solely on test market data that the proposal itself admits "can overstate demand," while facing unproven repeat purchase behavior and direct cannibalization of the flagship brand.

Steelman: A clear cola captures a new "purity" segment that color-loyalists cannot, justifying a national rollout before rivals copy the concept.

Attack:
1.  **Evidence Failure:** The proposal explicitly flags that test markets may overstate demand. Relying on inflated data for a "large marketing campaign" violates basic capital allocation logic. The novelty effect in test markets often masks the lack of repeat purchases, which is the second raised risk.
2.  **Cannibalization vs. Growth:** Without evidence that the "purer" positioning expands the total category, shifting sales from the flagship (high margin, established) to a novelty item (uncertain retention) destroys value if the net lift is negative.
3.  **Better Way:** The "cheaper, safer alternative" is a phased regional rollout with a capped marketing budget. This tests the "repeat purchase" hypothesis in the wild without exposing the entire brand to a potential flop or giving rivals a clear national target to counter-attack immediately.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** The launch fails because initial curiosity spikes sales, but consumers reject the taste long-term due to the psychological link between color and flavor. The large national spend is sunk, the flagship brand is diluted, and rivals launch improved copies using our failed market education.

Blockers:
*   **Fact:** The proposal admits test markets can overstate demand yet proceeds to a national launch anyway.
*   **Fact:** No mechanism is proposed to verify repeat purchase rates before committing to the "large marketing campaign."
*   **Kill Criterion Missing:** There is no stop condition. If repeat rates fall below a specific threshold in the first month, the plan offers no way to halt the national spend.

Risks:
*   Consumer rejection of flavor due to lack of color cues.
*   Rapid competitor imitation once the concept is proven nationally.

The plan as proposed ignores its own stated risks regarding data validity and repeat behavior by scaling immediately. It lacks a kill switch.

DECISION: NO-GO
