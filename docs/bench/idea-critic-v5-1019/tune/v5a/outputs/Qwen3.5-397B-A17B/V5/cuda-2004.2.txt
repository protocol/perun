First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits massive, certain capital to build a market that does not yet exist, directly threatening the company's survival in its only proven revenue stream (gaming) during a fierce competitive war.

**Steelman:** Embedding general-purpose compute capability in every consumer chip creates a ubiquitous parallel processing platform, positioning the company to monopolize a future scientific computing market before rivals can react, transforming a commodity graphics vendor into an essential infrastructure provider.

**Options:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Maintain focus on gaming/workstation performance; preserve margins and engineering bandwidth for the core rivalry.
2.  **Specialized Hardware:** Build a separate, expensive niche product for scientists without bloating mass-market consumer chips.
3.  **Proposal:** Subsidize R&D via every consumer unit, betting on a non-existent mass market.
*Analysis:* Option 3 fails to beat "Do Nothing" because it imposes a guaranteed cost penalty on every unit sold today for a speculative return years away, while distracting engineering from the immediate existential threat of the core rival.

**Fit:** The strategy contradicts the immediate north star of winning the gaming market share war. It assumes a future scientific demand that is currently unproven and unwilling to pay.

**Pre-mortem:** The program burns cash for three years; the gaming division loses performance parity with the rival due to divided engineering focus; market share collapses; the new computing features remain unused by academics who lack tools and by consumers who don't need them; the company runs out of capital. This is a one-way door with high delay cost (losing the gaming war now).

**Blockers:**
1.  **Economic Contradiction:** The proposal raises unit costs in a price-sensitive mass market without a committed customer base or immediate revenue stream to offset it.
2.  **Strategic Distraction:** Engineering resources are finite; diverting them to build a compiler and toolchain for a zero-revenue segment directly undermines the core business race against the main rival.
3.  **Missing Market:** No large paying market exists; relying on "hacked APIs" is not a validated demand signal sufficient to justify re-architecting the entire product line.

**Risks:** Niche demand remains niche; software adoption fails; competitor captures gaming lead.

**Kill Criterion:** If no commercial scientific software vendor commits to the platform within 12 months of toolchain release, the program must terminate. However, the initial commitment is too large to justify even a pilot given the core business risk.

DECISION: NO-GO
