First call: NO-GO. The $6 billion cash price for a fast-moving technology asset, paid at a large premium without a stated path to resolve channel conflicts, fails basic capital discipline.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the platform is the only way to rapidly close the gap with the search leader who just secured a competing asset, preventing permanent exclusion from the display market.

**Attack:**
1.  **Financials:** Paying ~$6B cash (a massive premium) for ad-tech in 2007 risks catastrophic impairment if the market shifts or integration stalls. The proposal lacks a payback period or ROI model to justify burning this much liquidity.
2.  **Channel Conflict:** The buyer serves existing agency customers. Owning an ad-server creates an immediate, unaddressed conflict of interest. If agencies fear data leakage or bias, they will flee, destroying the buyer's core revenue while chasing a speculative display future.
3.  **Timing & Tech:** The market is "fast-changing." Buying a static platform at a peak premium is often slower and riskier than building or partnering. The competitor's move does not force a panic buy; it forces a strategic response, which this expensive acquisition may not be.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves cash for organic build or smaller tuck-ins. A partnership avoids the conflict and capex. The proposal fails to prove this specific deal beats these safer options.

**Blockers:**
*   **Unresolved Channel Conflict:** No mechanism provided to retain existing agency clients post-acquisition. This threatens the core business to chase a secondary one.
*   **Undefined Economics:** No evidence that the $6B price can be recovered given the volatility of display ad tech.

**Kill Criterion:** If the combined entity loses >5% of existing agency clients within 12 months due to conflict concerns, the acquisition thesis is broken.

**Decision:** The plan commits irreversible capital to a high-risk asset while ignoring a fatal flaw in the buyer's current business model (channel conflict). It must be revised to address customer retention and pricing logic before approval.

DECISION: NO-GO
