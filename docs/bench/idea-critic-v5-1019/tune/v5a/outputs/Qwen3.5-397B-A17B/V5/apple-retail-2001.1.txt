First call: NO-GO. A loss-making manufacturer with 3–5% share entering a low-margin, high-fixed-cost business during a recession violates basic unit economics.

**Steelman:** Direct retail is the only way to demonstrate the integrated music strategy and stop third-party staff from pushing competitors, potentially reversing brand decline.

**Attack:**
1.  **Financial Suicide:** The proposal admits recent losses, low industry margins, and high lease costs. Adding massive fixed overhead (prime mall leases) while revenue is shrinking creates a lethal cost structure.
2.  **Channel Warfare:** Opening stores directly competes with the only existing sales force (third-party retailers). These partners will retaliate by deprioritizing the brand further, causing an immediate revenue cliff before new stores profit.
3.  **Timing:** Launching capital-intensive retail during a recession, when consumer discretionary spending on PCs drops, maximizes downside risk.
4.  **Track Record:** The industry has a "poor retail track record." Ignoring this base rate without a unique operational advantage is speculation, not strategy.
5.  **Alternative:** A "shop-in-shop" demonstration program within existing retailer locations or a limited online configuration tool achieves the "demonstration" goal without the fixed cost burden or channel conflict.

**Blockers:**
*   **Cash Flow:** The company is already losing money; this plan increases burn rate dramatically with no guaranteed immediate revenue lift.
*   **Channel Conflict:** The plan actively undermines the primary distribution channel required to sustain volume while stores ramp up.
*   **Base Rate Neglect:** Industry history shows failure; the proposal offers no evidence why this specific execution would succeed where others failed.

**Risks:** Recession deepening, inventory obsolescence, lease lock-ins.

**Kill Criterion:** If a pilot store cannot achieve positive unit economics (covering lease + staff + COGS) within 6 months, the entire chain must be abandoned. However, given the current loss status, even a successful pilot may not save the core business from the cash drain of expansion.

The plan requires capital the company likely lacks, attacks its only sales partners, and ignores negative industry base rates. The "demonstration" benefit does not justify the existential financial risk.

DECISION: NO-GO
