First call: GO. The hybrid form factor directly addresses the erosion of the handheld line by phones while leveraging the maker's strong first-party IP, creating a unique market position that rivals cannot immediately copy despite their power advantage.

**Steelman:** A differentiated hybrid device captures both living-room and portable play, monetizing a dedicated user base with high-margin first-party software, rendering raw GPU power secondary to gameplay versatility.

**Attack & Options:**
The primary attack is that "modest-power" hardware will alienate core gamers accustomed to HD fidelity, and a "thin" launch lineup fails to justify the price against powerful rivals. However, doing nothing guarantees continued handheld erosion and irrelevance in the home market. Building a powerful home console repeats the previous failure mode (financial loss) without solving the mobile threat. A cheaper, mobile-only device cedes the living room entirely. The hybrid approach is the only path that defends the handheld franchise while re-entering the home market without massive R&D risk.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This fits a survival strategy: pivot from competing on specs to competing on experience. If this fails, it will be because third-party support never materializes and the first-party pipeline misses its window. This is a two-way door; if the hardware sells, developers will follow. The cost of delay is total loss of the handheld segment to smartphones.

**Blockers & Risks:**
Blockers: NONE. The proposal acknowledges the risks (specs, supply, lineup) but offers a viable strategic pivot where the alternative is stagnation. The "confusing positioning" risk is mitigated by the clear utility of the hybrid mode itself.
Risks: Third-party abandonment, supply chain bottlenecks, software delays. These are execution risks, not strategic blockers. The "modest-power" constraint is a feature, not a bug, enabling the hybrid form factor and lower price point.

**Kill Criterion:**
If pre-orders or first-quarter sell-through do not exceed the previous console's launch by 20% within 6 months, halt further hardware revisions and pivot to a pure software/IP licensing model for other platforms.

DECISION: GO
