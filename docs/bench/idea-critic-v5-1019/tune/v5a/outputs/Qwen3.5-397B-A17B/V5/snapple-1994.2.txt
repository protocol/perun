First call: NO-GO. The plan to force a cult brand into a mass-market distribution and advertising model ignores the specific assets driving its value: quirky independent distributors and loyal niche appeal.

Steelman: The acquisition captures growth by leveraging the buyer's scale to expand reach faster than the target could alone.
Attack: The proposal explicitly plans to replace the "small, quirky network" with "supermarket-focused distribution" and apply "mass-market advertising." This directly attacks the brand's differentiators. History shows that sanitizing a niche brand's edge to fit a mass template often destroys the very loyalty that justified the premium price. The risks listed (distributor loyalty, cult image survival) are not merely possible; they are probable outcomes of the stated strategy.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Avoids $1.7B cash outlay and integration risk; status quo preserved.
2. Acquire and integrate (Proposal): High cost, high risk of brand erosion, potential distributor revolt.
3. Acquire and isolate: Keep independent distributors and niche marketing intact; higher operational complexity but preserves brand equity.

Fit: Moving a premium, cult product to mass markets contradicts the "premium" positioning unless the strategy is to commoditize it, which invites the very competition from large soft-drink companies mentioned as a risk.

Pre-mortem: One year post-acquisition, sales flatline as core fans abandon the "sold out" brand, while mass consumers reject the still-premium price point. Independent distributors drop the line due to conflicting mandates. The $1.7B becomes a write-down.

Evidence: The proposal admits the brand relies on "independent distributors" and "cult image" yet plans to remove both. No evidence suggests mass marketing works for this specific category without losing the premium aura.

Blockers: The strategy requires destroying the target's core competitive advantages (niche distribution, cult status) to achieve its synergies. This is a fundamental contradiction. The plan fails as proposed because it solves for scale at the expense of the asset's value.
Risks: Brand dilution, distributor churn, failure to compete with established soft-drink teas once the niche shield is dropped.

Kill criterion: If post-acquisition tracking shows a >15% drop in repeat purchase rate among core users within 6 months of the distribution switch, the integration must halt and revert to the legacy model.

DECISION: NO-GO
