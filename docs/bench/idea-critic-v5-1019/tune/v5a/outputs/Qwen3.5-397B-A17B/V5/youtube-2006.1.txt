First call: NO-GO. The price is unjustifiable for an asset with no revenue, massive liabilities, and existential legal threats.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the dominant video platform instantly secures market leadership, neutralizes a future competitor, and captures the cultural shift to online video before it matures, justifying the premium as a strategic monopoly play.

**Options:**
1.  **Buy:** Costs $1.65B stock + assumed liability. High risk of bankruptcy via litigation. Irreversible capital burn.
2.  **Build/Organic:** Continue existing smaller service. Low cost, zero legal exposure, but cedes market dominance.
3.  **Do Nothing:** Wait for litigation to crush the target or force a fire sale. Zero cost, high risk of missing the window if they survive.

**Fit:** Assumes "dominance" outweighs "solvency." Conflicts with fiduciary duty to avoid known, catastrophic liability without a revenue hedge.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-close, media giants sue simultaneously. Courts rule against fair use for commercial platforms. Damages exceed $2B. The acquired entity is shut down; the stock price collapses due to dilution and legal overhang. The bandwidth costs continue to bleed cash with no ad model mature enough to cover them.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits "no meaningful revenue" and "threatened lawsuits." Copyright law in 2006 does not clearly protect hosting infringing content at this scale. The math fails: $1.65B for negative cash flow and undefined legal debt is speculation, not investment.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Existential Liability:** The "threatened lawsuits" regarding copyrighted TV/film are not a risk; they are a near-certain event that could bankrupt the combined entity. There is no indemnity or legal shield proposed.
2.  **Economic Incoherence:** Paying a billion-dollar multiple on zero revenue with infinite marginal cost (bandwidth) violates basic valuation principles.
3.  **No Stop Condition:** The deal is an all-or-nothing stock swap with no pilot, cap, or escape clause if litigation begins immediately.

**Kill Criterion:** If any major media file suit within 90 days causing a stay of monetization, the integration must halt and the deal unwound. Since the deal is a stock swap, unwinding is likely impossible once closed.

The absence of a legal solution to the copyright blocker makes this a reckless gamble, not a strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
