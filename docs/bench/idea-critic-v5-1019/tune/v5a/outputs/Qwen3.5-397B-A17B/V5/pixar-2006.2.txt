First call: GO. The alternative is losing the franchise to a rival while the internal unit underperforms; the premium buys certainty and creative leadership.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the studio is the only viable path to secure the franchise's future, replace failing internal capabilities with proven hit-makers, and prevent a competitor from gaining the asset, justifying the $7.4B premium as the cost of survival and dominance.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Partnership expires; rival likely signs studio; internal unit continues struggling. High risk of revenue collapse.
2.  **Renegotiate partnership:** Talks already failed over economics; studio holds leverage to seek rivals. Low probability of success.
3.  **Acquire (Proposal):** High upfront cost, but secures IP, talent, and pipeline. Reversible only via divestiture later, which would be costly.

**Fit:** Aligns with the north star of dominating family entertainment. Internal production is broken; external partnership is ending. Acquisition is the logical fix.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the creative leaders leave post-acquisition or if the stock price collapses, making the deal accretive only on paper. Culture clash could stall production. However, the deal structure (stock) aligns the founder's interests with the conglomerate's success.

**Evidence:** The proposal states the internal unit has "recent underperformers" and talks "broke down." The studio has a "string of hits." The math relies on the studio continuing its hit rate.

**Blockers:** NONE. The high price is a function of the asset's scarcity and the bidder's desperation, not a factual error. The risks (culture, dependence) are manageable via the proposed leadership transition and stock alignment.

**Risks:** Overpayment if hit rate declines; integration friction; founder influence. These are mitigated by the "go-if" nature of securing the talent (putting them in charge) and the stock-based alignment.

**Kill Criterion:** If the studio fails to deliver a box-office hit within 3 years of acquisition, or if key creative leadership departs within 18 months, the thesis is broken.

**Decision:** The cost of inaction (losing the franchise to a rival) exceeds the cost of the premium. The deal solves the immediate existential threat to the animation division.

DECISION: GO
