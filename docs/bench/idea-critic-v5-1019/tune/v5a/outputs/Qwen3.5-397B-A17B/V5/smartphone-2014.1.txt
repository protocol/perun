First call: NO-GO. The proposal combines a premium price with a smaller app store and carrier exclusivity in a saturated two-platform market, a combination that historically fails to gain traction without a massive ecosystem or subsidy.

Steelman: A differentiated hardware experience (3D display, visual search) can carve a niche among early adopters despite app limitations if the price aligns with perceived value.

Attack: The "unsubsidised-equivalent price in line with leading flagship phones" is a fatal blocker. In 2014, consumers paying flagship prices expect full access to mainstream apps and network flexibility. A forked OS lacking leading apps destroys the value proposition at that price point. Carrier exclusivity further shrinks the addressable market for a device already hampered by software limitations. The 3D feature is a novelty, not a core utility sufficient to overcome the app gap for mass-market buyers.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve capital; focus on e-readers/tablets where the company succeeds.
2. Low-cost strategy: Offer the phone at a loss or heavy subsidy to drive ecosystem adoption (standard playbook for new entrants), but this contradicts the "unsubsidised" proposal.
3. App-first approach: Secure key apps before hardware launch, delaying the project but fixing the core deficit.

Pre-mortem: The device launches to fanfare but returns surge within 90 days due to missing apps. Carrier sales targets are missed, triggering contract penalties. Inventory piles up, requiring a fire sale that damages the brand's premium perception.

Blockers:
1. Pricing mismatch: Flagship price + limited app store = no rational consumer choice.
2. Distribution constraint: Single carrier + niche OS = insufficient scale to sustain developer interest.

Risks: High manufacturing costs for 3D tech; consumer confusion over "fire phone" gimmicks.

Kill Criterion: If pre-orders do not exceed 5% of the carrier's active smartphone base in Q1, halt production immediately. However, given the structural flaws, even this metric is unlikely to be met.

The plan fails because it asks customers to pay a premium for a compromised experience. The lack of a subsidy or a complete app ecosystem makes the value proposition negative compared to incumbents.

DECISION: NO-GO
