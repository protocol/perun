First call: GO. The proposal leverages a structural cost advantage to solve a verified user pain point (storage scarcity) with a differentiated capability (full-text search), funded by an existing, high-margin model (text ads).

**Steelman:** By offering 1,000x the standard storage and instant search, the service renders competitors' deletion habits obsolete, creating a defensible moat via data gravity while monetizing attention through contextual ads.

**Alternatives:**
1.  *Do nothing:* Cede the strategic beachhead of user identity and data to incumbents; lose the long-term ad inventory value.
2.  *Paid tier only:* Fails to achieve the network effects and data volume required to train superior search and ad matching.
3.  *Standard storage:* Commoditizes the offering; fails to disrupt user behavior or justify switching costs.

**Fit:** Aligns with a strategy of organizing information and monetizing via relevance. The "invitation-only" approach creates artificial scarcity to manage infrastructure load while validating demand.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if privacy backlash regarding content scanning becomes regulatory or reputational poison, or if storage costs decline slower than user growth, burning cash faster than ad revenue scales. However, these are risks, not blockers. The market is mature only because incumbents are constrained by old hardware economics; they cannot easily match the offer without cannibalizing paid tiers.

**Evidence & Math:** Storage costs are falling (Moore's Law applied to disks). One gigabyte is expensive today but will be negligible tomorrow; locking users in now captures lifetime value. Ad targeting based on intent (email content) yields higher CPMs than generic display ads.

**Blocker Test:**
*Blockers:* NONE. No fact presented proves the unit economics fail or that users reject the trade-off of privacy for utility.
*Risks:* Privacy perception, infrastructure scaling speed, ad relevance accuracy.

**Kill Criterion:** If privacy complaints trigger a user churn rate >5% monthly post-launch or if cost-per-user exceeds ad-revenue-per-user by month 18, halt expansion.

**Decision:** The asymmetry is clear: the downside is capped (infrastructure cost), while the upside (dominating global communication and data) is uncapped. The proposal correctly identifies that "mature" markets are vulnerable to order-of-magnitude improvements.

DECISION: GO
