First call: NO-GO. The proposal trades certain, high-margin licensing revenue for speculative subscription growth while intentionally burning cash to undercut a dominant incumbent with over 100 million subscribers.

**Steelman:** Aggressively withdrawing content to force migration to a proprietary, low-priced platform is the only way to capture direct consumer relationships and long-term valuation, accepting short-term losses as an investment.

**Attack:**
1.  **Revenue Math:** Licensing revenue is immediate, high-margin, and risk-free. Replacing it requires tens of millions of subscribers just to break even on lost licensing fees, ignoring the massive operating losses required to fund the "low price" strategy. The cash burn creates a fragile runway where any execution slip forces a retreat.
2.  **Capability Gap:** The conglomerate has zero history of running a global streaming technology platform. Building this while simultaneously marketing against an entrenched leader introduces extreme execution risk. Failure here means losing licensing revenue *and* failing to gain subscribers.
3.  **Cannibalization:** The plan explicitly targets existing profitable streams (cable, theatrical, licensing). Without a guaranteed, rapid migration rate (unproven), this is value destruction, not transformation.
4.  **Competitive Reality:** Undercutting a market leader with >100M subscribers assumes price is the primary driver. However, the leader's scale allows them to absorb price wars or bundle services, potentially starving the new entrant before it reaches the "tens of millions" target.

**Blockers:**
*   **Financial Contradiction:** The proposal relies on "multi-year operating losses" funded by the very licensing revenue it proposes to eliminate. There is no bridge showing how to sustain the burn rate once licensing checks stop arriving.
*   **Unproven Core Competency:** The central mechanism (the streaming platform) is something the company "has never run." Betting the company's future on an untested operational capability while destroying existing revenue streams is a fatal flaw.

**Risks:** Execution failure, insufficient subscriber acquisition speed, permanent loss of licensing partners, and technology instability.

**Kill Criterion:** If subscriber growth does not cover 50% of lost licensing revenue within 18 months, the cash burn becomes unsustainable. The proposal lacks a capped pilot or a verified technology partner, making the "go" condition impossible to satisfy safely.

DECISION: NO-GO
