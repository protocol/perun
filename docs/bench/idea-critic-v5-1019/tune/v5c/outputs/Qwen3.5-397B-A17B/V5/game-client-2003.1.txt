First call: GO. The core failure modes (updates, cheating) are existential; the proposed cure directly addresses them, and the risks are manageable friction, not blockers.

**Steelman:** A mandatory client is the only viable mechanism to secure revenue and game integrity in a digital-distribution future, turning a distribution liability into a platform asset.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Continue losing sales to pirates and support costs from broken updates; eventual irrelevance.
2.  **Patch-only updates:** Fails to stop cheating or unify purchases; fragmented user experience.
3.  **Mandatory Client (Proposed):** High initial friction, but secures the ecosystem, enables recurring revenue, and creates a new distribution channel. It beats "do nothing" because the status quo is bleeding value.

**Fit:** Aligns with the north star of sustainable profitability and IP protection. Assumes broadband adoption will outpace client rollout (valid trend in 2003).

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the client is so buggy at launch that it renders games unplayable, causing a retail revolt. This is a two-way door; bugs can be patched, and retail relationships repaired with incentives. The cost of delay is immediate loss of control over the software lifecycle and continued piracy.

**Evidence:** The proposal states the current struggle to deliver updates and fight cheating. These are verified operational failures. The risks (broadband limits, retail pushback) are known market conditions, not contradictions of the plan.

**Blockers:** NONE. No fact presented proves the plan fails as proposed. The lack of universal broadband is a constraint to manage via caching/LAN parties, not a stopper. Retail pushback is a negotiation hurdle, not a technical impossibility.

**Risks:** Player churn due to friction; competitor cloning. These become kill criteria: if active user count drops >20% post-launch without a corresponding drop in piracy/support costs, pivot.

**Kill Criteria:**
1.  **Metric:** >90% successful update delivery rate within 3 months.
2.  **Date:** 6 months post-launch.
3.  **Action:** If cheat incidence does not drop by 50%, re-architect the anti-cheat module.

The plan solves the stated existential threats. The risks are execution challenges, not fundamental flaws.

DECISION: GO
