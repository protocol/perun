First call: NO-GO. The proposal doubles down on a recent 60% price hike by adding severe friction (separate logins, billing, and brands) immediately after angering the customer base.

**Steelman:** Splitting the brands allows streaming to grow without being dragged down by the declining DVD economics and enables distinct management strategies.

**Attack:**
1.  **Timing & Compounding Shock:** Executing a complex, confusing split two months after a massive price increase is catastrophic. Customers perceive the price hike as greed; the split will be interpreted as a degradation of service value for the same higher cost. This invites mass churn.
2.  **Value Destruction:** Separating ratings and queues destroys the core utility of a "combined" plan. If the services do not talk to each other, the justification for a bundled premium vanishes, making the 60% increase indefensible.
3.  **Operational Friction:** Forcing users to manage two accounts and bills creates immediate resentment, directly contradicting the goal of retaining subscribers during a pricing transition.
4.  **Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "slow roll" alternative is superior. Absorb the DVD decline within the main brand while quietly shifting investment to streaming. Do not institutionalize the split until streaming revenue clearly offsets DVD losses and customer sentiment stabilizes.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume the split launches in Q4 2011. Result: A PR firestorm citing "double billing" and "broken experience," leading to a stock collapse and subscriber exodus because the market views the move as punishing loyal users rather than innovating.

**Blocker:** The proposal ignores the compound negative impact of the July price hike. Launching a fragmented experience immediately after raising prices removes all perceived value synergy, guaranteeing customer backlash. The lack of shared data (ratings/queues) makes the combined offer logically incoherent at the new price point.

**Risks:** Brand confusion and churn are not just risks here; they are near-certainties given the execution window.

**Kill Criteria:** If customer sentiment scores do not stabilize within 30 days of the price hike, no structural split should occur. The current plan fails this precondition.

DECISION: NO-GO
