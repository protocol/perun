First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a costly, dedicated dealer network while admitting rivals already dominate the low-price small car segment, creating an immediate cash-flow mismatch before volume is proven.

Steelman: Launching an 1100cc compact at the new plant captures family demand, but only if the dedicated network secures market share faster than the burn rate destroys liquidity.

Options:
1. **Proposed:** Build dedicated network. High cost, high risk, low reversibility. Fails if volume lags.
2. **Do Nothing:** Run existing lines. Zero new cost, but misses growth and leaves new plant underutilized.
3. **Better Way:** Sell the new car through *existing* dealer networks or shared showrooms. Drastically lowers upfront capex, tests price/size acceptance immediately, and keeps the option to specialize later open.

Fit: Assumes the goal is volume to fill the plant, not brand isolation. A dedicated network contradicts the "low price" necessity implied by rival pressure.

Pre-mortem: The plant runs below capacity because the dedicated network takes too long to build and too much capital to sustain. Buyers reject the price because margins were eaten by network overhead. This is a one-way door; once the network leases are signed, the cost is fixed regardless of sales.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits the network is "costly" and rivals are "low price." These facts contradict the strategy of adding heavy fixed costs to compete on price. No data shows buyers specifically demand a *separate* buying experience for this engine size.

Blockers:
1. **Economic Contradiction:** Competing against low-price rivals while voluntarily adding a costly distribution layer makes the unit economics impossible at launch.
2. **Capacity Risk:** The plant needs near-capacity runs to pay off, yet the go-to-market strategy (building a network from scratch) guarantees a slow ramp-up, ensuring the plant sits idle during the critical payoff window.

Risks: Buyer preference for cheaper/larger cars becomes a certainty if the price is inflated by network costs.

Kill Criteria: If a shared-network pilot cannot secure 70% of projected volume within 6 months, stop the dedicated build.

DECISION: NO-GO
