First call: GO. The stated facts show a low-cost entry ($50M) into a distribution bottleneck (carriers/handset makers) using a lever (open source) that directly aligns with the core business goal (increasing mobile web search volume).

Steelman: Acquiring this team provides the only viable path to bypass carrier/handset gatekeepers via open source, securing future search ad inventory at a fraction of the cost of building a proprietary stack or paying access fees later.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Cede mobile search to OS vendors who will favor their own or paid search defaults; long-term revenue collapse.
2. Build internal OS: Prohibitively slow and expensive; misses the window; lacks credibility with handset makers.
3. Pay for placement: Unsustainable at scale; carriers eventually raise prices once dependency is established.
The acquisition beats "do nothing" by creating a defensive moat and offensive growth engine simultaneously.

Fit: Directly serves the north star of maximizing search queries. Without an OS foothold, the company is a tenant on someone else's land.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if handset makers ignore the OS despite it being free, or if carriers block it. However, the "free" aspect creates an irresistible economic incentive for smaller handset makers to differentiate against market leaders. The cost of delay is total exclusion from the mobile era.

Evidence: The $50M price tag is negligible for a search giant relative to the potential loss of the mobile channel. The "no revenue model" risk is irrelevant because the OS itself is not the product; search ads are. The open-source model specifically mitigates the adoption risk by removing licensing friction.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (adoption, carrier resistance) are execution challenges inherent to any new platform, not factual contradictions of the plan. The low entry cost and strategic necessity outweigh the uncertainty.

Risks: Slow adoption, carrier pushback. These become kill criteria: if no major handset partner signs within 18 months, pivot or shut down the project.

DECISION: GO
