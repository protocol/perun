First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks to pay a premium ($4B) for a film studio with a short, unproven slate and stripped assets, relying on a cyclical hit model the buyer lacks experience in, while the target's most valuable characters are locked away from the buyer.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the publisher secures long-term IP ownership to fix the buyer's gender gap, betting that internal film production will eventually outperform the current licensing model despite short-term encumbrances.

**Attack:**
1.  **Asset Hollowing:** The "most famous characters" are licensed to rivals. The buyer pays $4B primarily for secondary IP and a film division with one hit. The core value proposition (fixing the boy-demographic gap) is legally blocked for the duration of existing deals.
2.  **Execution Mismatch:** The target's film division is nascent ("short and unproven slate"). The buyer admits weakness in this demographic; adding an unproven film studio does not solve the distribution/marketing gap, it compounds it with production risk.
3.  **Financial Exposure:** Paying $4B in cash/stock for a company with "cyclical" revenue and "creative-control disputes" creates immediate downside. If the next few films fail (high probability given the short slate), the stock price dilutes and the cash reserve depletes without securing the promised audience reach.
4.  **Better Way:** License specific boy-targeted characters from the publisher (or others) for merchandise/toys immediately. This addresses the demographic gap without the $4B capital outlay, film production risk, or integration headaches. Wait for the long-term licenses to expire before considering an acquisition of the IP owner.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** Core characters are licensed to rivals. **Impact:** The primary strategic goal (reaching boys with famous characters) cannot be executed immediately post-acquisition.
*   **Fact:** Film slate is short/unproven. **Impact:** The valuation relies on future hits that are not yet in the pipeline, making the $4B price speculative rather than asset-backed.

**Risks:** Creative disputes, cyclical box office failure, overpayment for encumbered IP.

**Kill Criteria:** Do not proceed unless the purchase price is reduced to reflect the net present value of only the *unencumbered* IP and the current film slate, excluding value from characters under rival contracts. As proposed at $4B, the math fails.

DECISION: NO-GO
