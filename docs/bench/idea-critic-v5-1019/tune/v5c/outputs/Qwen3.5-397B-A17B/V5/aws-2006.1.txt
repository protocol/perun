First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a retailer with no developer platform experience to enter an unproven market against entrenched incumbents while investors are already hostile to its capital spending.

Steelman: Leveraging existing spiky infrastructure to create a high-margin revenue stream that amortizes fixed costs, turning a liability into a market-leading utility.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Avoids distraction and capital risk but leaves infrastructure costs purely as a drag on retail margins.
2. Internal optimization: Focus solely on smoothing internal spikiness; lower risk, but no new revenue.
3. Proposed external cloud: High upside if successful, but risks catastrophic distraction and margin dilution if the unproven market fails to materialize.

Fit: This contradicts the immediate constraint of investor skepticism regarding heavy capital spending. Entering a new, unproven category requires *more* capital and focus, not less.

Pre-mortem: The initiative fails because the organization lacks the DNA to support developer relations and enterprise SLAs. Reliability issues alienate early adopters, while the core retail business suffers from engineering distraction during a critical build-out. Investors pull back due to perceived lack of focus, starving both lines of capital.

Evidence: The proposal admits zero experience in this sector, an unproven market, and active investor resistance to the required spending model. These are not risks to be managed; they are foundational blockers to viability as proposed.

Blockers:
1. Capital contradiction: Investors question heavy spending; this plan requires massive additional investment in an unproven area.
2. Capability gap: No existing experience running a developer platform at scale creates an insurmountable execution risk against established vendors.
3. Market uncertainty: The core value proposition (hourly server rental) is explicitly unproven.

Risks: Distraction from core retail, margin dilution, reliability failures.

The proposal asks to bet the company's focus and capital on an unproven hypothesis while under fire for spending. A "go" decision here ignores the stated lack of experience and the specific investor constraints. The plan must first prove the market exists and secure a path to capital that does not alienate current stakeholders before launching a distinct business line.

DECISION: NO-GO
