First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on a tiny installed base, an unproven legal music supply, and a premium price in a market where cheaper devices have already failed to gain traction.

Steelman: A tightly integrated, high-capacity device could create a unique user experience that justifies a premium, potentially expanding the computer platform's appeal.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Avoid capital risk; status quo remains a niche computer maker.
2. **Cheaper alternative:** License software to Windows immediately to access the dominant market share, sacrificing integration for volume.
3. **Proposed plan:** High risk, high cost, restricted compatibility. It fails to beat "do nothing" because the addressable market (own computer owners) is too small to sustain the supply chain costs or justify the R&D without immediate mass adoption, which the closed ecosystem prevents.

Fit: This contradicts the reality of early 2001. Without a massive existing user base or legal digital music infrastructure, a closed, expensive hardware play is a strategy for obscurity, not dominance. The "north star" of selling more computers is not served by a peripheral only 5% of the population can use.

Pre-mortem: The device launches, but users cannot legally fill the 5GB drive. Sales stall due to the $400 price point and Windows incompatibility. The company writes off inventory and damages its brand as out of touch.

Evidence: The proposal admits the computer share is small, consumer electronics experience is non-existent, and legal downloads are unavailable. Competitors with lower prices have already failed. The math does not work: Small Base × Low Adoption Rate × High Price < Development Cost.

Blockers:
1. **Market Size:** The compatible user base is insufficient to recoup tooling and sourcing costs for a custom hard drive unit within a year.
2. **Content Void:** No legal mechanism exists to fill the device, rendering the capacity useless.
3. **Pricing:** $400 is prohibitive without a proven value proposition that the current ecosystem cannot support.

Risks: Supply chain failure, brand dilution, cash burn.

The plan attempts to force a ecosystem lock-in before the ecosystem exists.

DECISION: NO-GO
