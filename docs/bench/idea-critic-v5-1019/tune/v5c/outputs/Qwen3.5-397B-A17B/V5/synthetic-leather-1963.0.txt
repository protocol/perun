First call: NO-GO. The proposal's own facts state the synthetic costs more than leather while offering unproven comfort and facing high switching costs for manufacturers.

Steelman: A durable, leather-like synthetic can capture the premium market if marketing overcomes manufacturer inertia and comfort concerns.

Attack:
1.  **Economics:** The material costs *more* than leather. In 1963, "premium" shoes were defined by genuine leather quality. Charging a premium for a synthetic that is arguably inferior in comfort and prestige is a fundamental value mismatch. Marketing cannot sustainably bridge a gap where the core product is more expensive and perceived as lower quality.
2.  **Manufacturer Friction:** Shoe makers are unwilling to retool factories. Without a massive cost advantage or superior performance, they have zero incentive to disrupt production lines for a material that costs them more upfront.
3.  **Consumer Risk:** Wearers may find it less comfortable. Comfort is the primary driver of footwear repeat purchases. A "durable" but uncomfortable shoe will generate returns and brand damage, which heavy marketing cannot fix.
4.  **Fashion Volatility:** The risk that fashion moves away is heightened by positioning the product as a permanent "premium" solution rather than a niche alternative.

Alternatives:
*   **Do nothing:** Avoids sunk costs in a likely failing launch.
*   **Niche Industrial/Military:** Target durability-focused sectors where comfort and fashion are secondary, and cost is less sensitive than performance.
*   **Cost-Reduction Pilot:** Do not launch broadly; partner with one manufacturer to solve the cost and comfort issues before spending on marketing.

Pre-mortem: The launch fails because manufacturers refuse to adopt the expensive material, early adopters return shoes due to discomfort, and the brand becomes synonymous with "expensive plastic."

Blockers:
*   **Negative Unit Economics:** Higher cost than the incumbent (leather) with no proven willingness to pay a premium for the substitute.
*   **Adoption Barrier:** Explicit manufacturer unwillingness to change factories without a compelling economic driver, which is absent here.

Risks: Fashion trends, long-term comfort perception.

The proposal fails the basic test of offering a better or cheaper alternative to the incumbent. It asks the market to pay more for a product that may be worse, relying entirely on marketing to overcome structural disadvantages.

DECISION: NO-GO
