First call: NO-GO. The plan to phase out the only successful product before the successor proves reliable or adopted invites catastrophic revenue collapse.

**Steelman:** Launch a business-capable machine within a year to capture the enterprise market, accepting the risk of cannibalizing the current line to force necessary evolution.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Strategy:** The proposal aims to "phase out" the existing machine immediately. Given the new design is "complex," "unproven," and on a "tight schedule," this creates a single point of failure. If the new hardware fails reliability checks or misses the launch window, the company has no revenue stream.
2.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 12 months. Cause: The new machine ships late or with bugs (high probability given complexity). Because the old machine was phased out, sales hit zero. Software makers, already hesitant, refuse to port to a broken or delayed platform.
3.  **Better Way:** The "do nothing" option is weak, but the alternative is **parallel operation**. Launch the business machine as a premium tier without killing the existing successful product. Let the market decide the transition pace. This preserves cash flow while the new design matures.
4.  **Evidence & Logic:** The proposal admits hardware reliability is "unproven." Phasing out a proven revenue generator to bet entirely on an unproven, complex successor violates basic portfolio risk management. The risk of existing owners not moving is mitigated by keeping their machine available, not forced obsolescence.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fatal Flaw:** The mandate to "phase out the existing machine" *before* the new one is proven reliable or adopted creates an unacceptable risk of total revenue interruption. The proposal offers no bridge period.

**Risks:**
*   Schedule slippage due to complexity.
*   Software ecosystem fragmentation.
*   Reliability failures damaging brand reputation.

**Decision Logic:**
The core claim fails because it conflates "launching a new product" with "killing the old one." The former is a sound growth strategy; the latter is a suicidal gamble given the stated uncertainties. The plan must be revised to allow parallel sales until the new machine demonstrates stability and market traction. As proposed, the immediate phase-out is a blocker.

DECISION: NO-GO
