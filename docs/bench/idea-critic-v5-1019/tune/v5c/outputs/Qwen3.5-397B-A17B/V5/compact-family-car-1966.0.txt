First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to high fixed costs (new plant, dedicated network) without evidence that the specific 1100cc segment yields sufficient margin or volume to cover them, especially against established low-price rivals.

Steelman: A dedicated 1100cc compact leverages the new plant's capacity and captures a specific family demographic underserved by current cheap small cars or larger expensive ones.

Attack:
1.  **Economic Mismatch:** Building a *dedicated* dealer network creates massive upfront fixed costs. If the car is priced low to compete with rivals, margins may never amortize this network cost before cash runs out. If priced higher, volume drops, leaving the new plant underutilized.
2.  **Capacity Trap:** The requirement to run the new plant near capacity forces production volume. If market demand for this specific engine size/price point is soft (as the "buyers may prefer" risk suggests), the company will be forced to discount heavily to move metal, destroying profitability.
3.  **Straddle Risk:** The market hesitation between "cheaper" and "larger" indicates the 1100cc middle ground may be a graveyard. Without proof of distinct demand, this product cannibalizes nothing and captures little.

Alternatives:
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoids network debt; sells existing models.
*   **Shared Network:** Sell the new car through existing dealers to lower break-even volume.
*   **Contract/Phased:** Validate demand with a smaller batch before committing to full plant capacity and exclusive dealers.

Pre-mortem: Two years later, the plant is half-empty, the dealer network is bankrupt due to low traffic, and the brand is stuck with unsold inventory because buyers chose the cheaper rival or saved for a larger used car.

Blockers:
*   **Missing Unit Economics:** No data showing the 1100cc price point covers the *combined* cost of new plant depreciation + dedicated network overhead.
*   **Unverified Demand:** No evidence that the "family" segment specifically wants 1100cc over the available cheaper/larger options.

Risks: Price war with incumbents; preference shift to larger cars; plant underutilization.

Kill Criteria: If a pilot in existing channels cannot sell 5,000 units in six months at a margin covering 20% of network costs, stop.

DECISION: NO-GO
