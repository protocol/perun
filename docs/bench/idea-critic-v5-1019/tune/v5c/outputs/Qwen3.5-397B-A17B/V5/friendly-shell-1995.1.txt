First call: NO-GO. The proposal targets a mass home market with a product requiring hardware (high-end PC) that the majority of that market does not yet own, while facing immediate obsolescence from the vendor's own upcoming OS release.

**Steelman:** A $100 cartoon shell makes computing accessible for novices on high-end machines, creating a bridge market before the next OS arrives.

**Attack:**
1.  **Market Mismatch:** The core risk is fatal. In 1995, "most home PCs" lack the memory for this shell. Selling a $100 software layer that requires a hardware upgrade effectively doubles the entry cost for the target demographic (new home users), destroying the addressable market.
2.  **Cannibalization & Obsolescence:** The vendor admits a major OS release with a new interface is months away. Launching a stop-gap product now guarantees channel confusion, splits developer attention, and renders the $100 product worthless upon the OS launch. Users will refuse to pay for temporary utility.
3.  **Adoption Friction:** Experienced users (who influence household purchases) find it patronizing, while novices face a hardware barrier. Third-party add-ons are few, limiting long-term value.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Wait for the upcoming OS release to integrate accessibility features natively. Cost: $0. Risk: Delayed ease-of-use. Reversibility: N/A.
*   *Integrate, Don't Separate:* Incorporate the "cartoon guide" concept as an optional mode within the upcoming OS release rather than a separate $100 SKU. Cost: Development time. Risk: Feature bloat. Reversibility: High.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, sales are negligible because users bought the new OS instead of upgrading old machines for the shell. The brand is diluted by two conflicting interfaces.

**Blockers:**
*   **Hardware Reality:** The target audience (new home users) predominantly lacks the required high-end PCs. The proposal admits this creates a market size contradiction.
*   **Strategic Collision:** The imminent major OS release makes a standalone interface product strategically redundant and commercially suicidal.

**Risks:** Patronizing tone; lack of ecosystem. These are secondary to the structural blockers.

**Kill Criteria:** If the required RAM exceeds the median installed base by >20%, or if the gap to the next OS is <6 months, the project fails. Both conditions are met here.

DECISION: NO-GO
