First call: GO. The proposal trades a rapidly commoditizing cost (storage) for a defensible differentiation (searchability and permanence) in a market where incumbents are constrained by legacy cost structures.

Steelman: By offering 1,000x the standard storage, the service renders manual deletion obsolete, positioning "search" as the primary interface rather than "file management." This creates a sticky ecosystem where the high marginal cost of storage is offset by the high lifetime value of users exposed to precision-targeted advertising.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Cede the shift from local clients to cloud storage to competitors.
2. Match incumbents (few MB): Guarantees irrelevance; users will not switch for an identical, later product.
3. Paid-only model: Fails to achieve the network density required to train ad-matching algorithms or attract mass adoption quickly.

Fit: Assumes the strategic north star is organizing information, not just delivering messages. A mature market with entrenched players is vulnerable to a step-change in utility (1GB vs 4MB) that renders existing habits (deleting mail) obsolete.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if privacy backlash over content scanning becomes regulatory or reputational enough to stall adoption before network effects kick in, or if storage costs fail to drop as projected, turning every user into a loss leader. This is a two-way door; an invitation-only launch limits blast radius while validating unit economics.

Evidence & Math: In 2004, disk costs are falling exponentially. While 1GB seems expensive, the marginal cost per user is low compared to the potential ad revenue from a permanently engaged user base. The "no direct revenue" risk is mitigated by the ad-model precedent in search; the asset being monetized is attention and intent, not the storage itself.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (cost, privacy, maturity) are execution challenges, not factual contradictions. The cost curve supports the model; privacy is a messaging hurdle, not a technical blocker; market maturity invites disruption via capacity shock.

Risks: Privacy perception, slower-than-expected ad fill rates, storage cost volatility. These become kill criteria: if ad CTR does not exceed search benchmarks within 6 months, or if storage costs remain above $X/GB/year, pivot to paid tiers.

DECISION: GO
