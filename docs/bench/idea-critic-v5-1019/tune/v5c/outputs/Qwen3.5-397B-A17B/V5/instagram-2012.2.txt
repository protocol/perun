First call: NO-GO. The price implies a valuation multiple with no revenue base, violating standard capital discipline for an unproven business model.

Steelman: Buying the only credible mobile threat immediately neutralizes a competitor that could erode the core business's user base before the IPO, securing the mobile future at a premium that acts as an insurance policy against existential risk.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Risk losing mobile relevance post-IPO; stock price suffers if the target dominates.
2. Build internally: Too slow; current mobile product is already weak; likely to fail against network effects.
3. Acquire: Instantly captures users and talent, but burns $1B cash/stock on an asset with zero revenue and unverified retention outside its initial niche.

Fit: Aligns with the strategic necessity of mobile dominance prior to going public. However, the cost threatens the balance sheet and shareholder value if the integration fails or the hype cycle bursts.

Pre-mortem: The acquisition closes, but the app's culture clashes with the buyer's, causing key engineers to quit. The user growth was a fad tied to a specific phone OS shift, and engagement collapses within a year. The $1B becomes a write-down, distracting management during the critical IPO roadshow, and regulators delay the listing due to monopoly concerns.

Evidence: The proposal admits zero revenue and no business model. The valuation is purely speculative based on "threat" status rather than financial fundamentals. The buyer's own weak mobile position suggests poor integration capability.

Blockers:
1. Financial Impossibility: Paying $1B for zero revenue lacks any mathematical justification under standard operating metrics; it is gambling, not investing.
2. Integration Risk: High probability of cultural destruction given the size disparity and lack of stated retention plan for the dozen employees.

Risks: Regulatory delay, platform dependency, fad lifecycle.

Kill Criteria: If user growth slows by >20% in the next quarter post-acquisition, or if >30% of the founding team leaves within six months, the project is a failure.

The absence of a revenue model and the astronomical price relative to any measurable metric constitute a fundamental failure of the proposal as stated. It relies entirely on fear rather than value creation logic.

DECISION: NO-GO
