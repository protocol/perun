First call: NO-GO. The proposal discards the brand's primary shelf cue—the orange with a straw—without evidence that the new minimalist design conveys the same immediate recognition, directly threatening sales among the identified loyal, older demographic.

Steelman: A modernized aesthetic will attract new buyers while the cap shape and ad spend compensate for lost shelf recognition.

Attack: The "orange with straw" is a decades-old heuristic for instant identification. Removing it forces a cognitive re-learning process for the core base. The proposal admits the buyer base is "loyal and older," a segment typically resistant to abstract design changes. The "cap shaped like an orange" is a secondary cue, unlikely to function at the speed of a grocery aisle glance compared to the established graphic. The risk of shoppers failing to recognize the product is not theoretical; it is the direct result of removing the stated "main shelf cue."

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Retains shelf presence, zero execution risk.
2. Staged pilot: Test the new design in a limited region to measure recognition drop-off before a national rollout.
3. Hybrid: Evolve the current design gradually rather than a hard switch to minimalism.

Pre-mortem: Six months post-launch, sales have collapsed because loyalists bought competitor brands thinking the original product was out of stock or discontinued, and the ad campaign failed to retrain them fast enough. The cost of reprinting nationwide cartons is sunk, and shelf space is lost to rivals.

Evidence: The proposal itself states the old image is the "main shelf cue" and the base is "older." These facts contradict the strategy of removing the cue and launching everywhere at once.

Blockers: The plan explicitly removes the verified primary mechanism for customer purchase (the shelf cue) while targeting a demographic prone to confusion by change, with no pilot to validate the new cue's efficacy. This guarantees preventable revenue loss.

Risks: High cost of reprinting; brand alienation.

Kill criteria: If a regional pilot shows any statistically significant drop in repeat purchase rate within 4 weeks, the national rollout stops.

DECISION: NO-GO
