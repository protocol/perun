First call: NO-GO. The proposal trades guaranteed, high-margin licensing revenue for speculative subscriber growth funded by massive, multi-year losses, while requiring the company to build a complex technology platform from scratch—a capability it explicitly lacks.

**Steelman:** Direct-to-consumer distribution captures full lifetime value and customer data, preventing reliance on decaying cable/satellite models and rivals who may eventually exclude the library entirely.

**Attack:**
1.  **Capability Gap:** The firm has "never run" a streaming technology platform. Building a stable, scalable global service while simultaneously marketing to tens of millions is a dual-execution risk that frequently leads to catastrophic product failures during launch windows.
2.  **Revenue Destruction:** Pulling content immediately eliminates billions in guaranteed licensing fees. Replacing this cash flow requires acquiring subscribers at a rate that outpaces the loss of licensing income, which is unlikely given the "low price" point and the market leader's entrenched position (100M+ subscribers).
3.  **Cannibalization:** The plan admits to cannibalizing existing theatrical and cable revenue. Without a proven path to profitability, this shrinks the total addressable market in the short term while increasing fixed costs.
4.  **Capital Intensity:** Funding "multi-year operating losses" assumes infinite capital patience. If subscriber growth lags (a common occurrence against entrenched incumbents), the company faces a liquidity crisis with no licensing safety net to fall back on, as the content is already withdrawn.

**Better Way:** License selectively to rivals while launching a smaller, joint-venture streaming platform or a white-label solution to mitigate technology risk. Alternatively, launch the service without withdrawing all content immediately, preserving licensing cash flow to fund the transition.

**Kill Criteria:** The proposal lacks a specific cap on losses, a verified technology partner to bridge the capability gap, or a phased content withdrawal strategy. The assumption that tens of millions will subscribe quickly enough to offset billions in lost revenue is unsupported by the stated facts.

**Blockers:**
*   **Capability:** No existing technology platform; building one introduces high failure risk during critical growth phase.
*   **Economics:** Immediate loss of billions in guaranteed revenue creates a cash burn hole that speculative subscriber targets may not fill in time.

**Risks:** Execution failure, prolonged losses, inability to compete with established leader.

DECISION: NO-GO
