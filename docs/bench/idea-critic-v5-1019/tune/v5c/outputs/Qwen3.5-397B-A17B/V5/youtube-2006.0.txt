First call: NO-GO. The price is astronomical for an asset with no revenue, massive liabilities, and unproven economics.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the dominant platform instantly secures the category leader, neutralizes a future rival, and captures the entire shift to online video before competitors can react.

**Options:**
1. **Do nothing:** Continue building a smaller internal service; risk irrelevance if this site becomes the standard.
2. **Build/Buy smaller:** Acquire a cheaper, compliant niche player; miss the network effect of the market leader.
3. **The Proposal:** Pay $1.65B for immediate dominance but inherit existential legal and financial risks.

**Fit:** Aligns with organizing the world's information, but the cost threatens the core business's stability.

**Pre-mortem:** The deal closes, but media lawsuits force the site to remove all popular content, leaving an empty shell worth a fraction of the price. Bandwidth costs bankrupt the division. The stock price collapses under the weight of failed integration and legal settlements. This is a one-way door; once the stock is issued and the company absorbed, there is no undoing the dilution or the liability.

**Evidence & Math:** The target has ~$0 revenue against a $1.65B price tag. The buyer pays ~$82.5M per month in value for zero current cash flow. Copyright litigation is not a "risk" but a near-certainty given the content library; damages could exceed the purchase price. The bandwidth cost structure is unproven at this scale.

**Blockers:**
1. **Financial Suicide:** The valuation assumes a monetization model that does not yet exist. Paying this multiple on $0 revenue contradicts basic fiduciary duty.
2. **Existential Liability:** The core asset (copyrighted videos) is illegal contraband. Buying it buys the lawsuit, potentially crippling the profitable parent company.

**Risks:** Ad model failure, bandwidth insolvency, cultural clash. These are secondary to the blockers.

**Kill Criteria:** If legal counsel cannot quantify a cap on liability or if projected bandwidth costs exceed 20% of the buyer's current operating budget within 12 months, the deal must terminate.

The proposal fails the basic test of solvency and legal safety. The "first mover" advantage is not worth betting the entire corporation on an unproven, legally toxic asset.

DECISION: NO-GO
