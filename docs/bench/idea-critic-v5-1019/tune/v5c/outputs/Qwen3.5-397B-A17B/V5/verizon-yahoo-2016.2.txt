First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to solve a scale deficit by acquiring a second declining asset, creating a larger entity with falling revenue rather than a growing one.

**Steelman:** Combining two legacy media assets creates sufficient mobile scale to compete with dominant ad platforms by consolidating audience and inventory.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fundamental Trajectory:** Both the acquirer's existing media unit and the target are in structural decline. The carrier lacks consumer media DNA. Merging two shrinking, mismanaged businesses does not generate the growth required to challenge incumbents; it aggregates decay.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves capital. Investing $4.8B in building native mobile capabilities or acquiring a growing tech firm offers a plausible path to scale. This plan buys yesterday's problems at a premium.
3.  **Fit:** The strategy contradicts the goal of competing with dominant platforms. Those platforms win via innovation and network effects, not by consolidating legacy portal traffic which is migrating to apps and walled gardens.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** Integration fails due to cultural clash between a wireless utility and chaotic media startups. Ad revenue continues to plummet post-merger, leaving the carrier with a $4.8B impairment and no competitive moat.
5.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits falling traffic, falling revenue, repeated management failures, and zero acquirer experience. These are not risks to mitigate; they are disqualifiers.

**Blockers:**
*   **Negative Synergy:** The core claim requires growth to compete. The stated facts (falling revenue/traffic for both units) guarantee contraction. You cannot mathematically reach scale by adding two negative integers.
*   **Capability Gap:** The acquirer has no history in consumer media. Buying a failing example of the very thing you cannot do is not a strategy; it is expensive speculation.

**Risks:** Integration complexity, further revenue decline, management distraction.

**Kill Criteria:** None needed; the premise is flawed. A valid plan would require at least one asset with proven growth or a clear technical differentiator, neither of which exists here.

**Dissent Ledger:** The only argument is "scale," but the scale being purchased is losing relevance by the quarter.

**Remaining Risk:** High probability of total capital loss and strategic distraction.

DECISION: NO-GO
