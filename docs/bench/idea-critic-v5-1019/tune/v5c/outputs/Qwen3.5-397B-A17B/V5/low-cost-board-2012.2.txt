First call: GO. A $35 price point creates a new category for education, distinct from shared laptops or expensive tablets, directly addressing the charity's goal despite the lack of peripherals.

**Steelman:** A ultra-low-cost, bare-board computer forces students to engage with hardware and code, creating an affordable personal device for schools where shared resources fail.

**Attack & Options:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Schools continue sharing expensive laptops; student engagement remains low due to lack of personal ownership.
*   **Subsidized Laptops:** Cost prohibitive ($200+), high theft risk, less educational value regarding hardware internals.
*   **The Risk of Peripherals:** Requiring users to source displays/power is a barrier, but schools often have spare monitors/TVs (HDMI/VGA) and USB chargers, making the marginal cost near zero compared to a full PC.
*   **Manufacturing Inexperience:** Valid risk, but mitigated by using licensed distributors who handle logistics and support, leveraging existing supply chains rather than building new ones.
*   **Supply Constraints:** Selling out is a demand signal, not a failure; it validates the market before scaling.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the $35 board requires proprietary adapters costing $50, negating the price advantage. However, standard HDMI and USB are ubiquitous. Failure also occurs if the charity tries to build its own support desk; the distributor model avoids this.

**Evidence & Math:** At $35, the device is 1/6th the cost of a budget laptop. Even with $20 in peripherals (often already owned), the total is <$60, unbeatable for 1:1 student deployment. The "cheap tablet" alternative offers consumption, not creation; this device targets programming specifically.

**Blockers:** None. The proposal's stated risks (peripherals, batch size, inexperience) are manageable constraints or positive signals, not factual contradictions. The lack of manufacturing experience is solved by the distributor model explicitly mentioned.

**Risks:** Supply chain delays; peripheral fragmentation confusing teachers. These become kill criteria: if distributors cannot meet initial demand by Q3, or if teacher setup time exceeds 15 minutes, pause expansion.

**Decision:** The price point creates a unique wedge into education that existing hardware cannot match. The risks are operational, not fundamental flaws in the concept.

DECISION: GO
