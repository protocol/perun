First call: GO. The data-backed hypothesis that viewers want bingeable, high-quality originals justifies bypassing traditional pilot testing to secure exclusive content and differentiate the service.

**Steelman:** Leveraging proprietary viewing data to greenlight a full-season, binge-ready prestige drama eliminates pilot costs, secures top talent with a bold commitment, and creates a unique value proposition that licensed content cannot match.

**Options:**
1.  **Proposal:** High risk, high reward; creates immediate brand identity and ownership. Irreversible cost but high strategic leverage.
2.  **Do Nothing:** Safe short-term budget, but long-term existential risk as licensing costs rise and competitors consolidate content.
3.  **Pilot First:** Lower initial cost, but signals hesitation to top talent, delays launch, and fails to differentiate the "all-at-once" viewing model.

**Fit:** Aligns with the north star of transitioning from a distributor to a primary destination. Reuses the stated asset: unique viewer data.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the data misinterpreted niche interest as mass appeal, or if the "all-at-once" model confuses users accustomed to weekly schedules. However, the cost of delay is losing the specific director/star package and ceding the "originals" narrative to future competitors.

**Evidence:** The proposal cites internal viewing data as the core justification. While external benchmarks for online-only prestige drama do not exist (it is a first-mover move), the internal metrics regarding completion rates and genre affinity are the only relevant leading indicators available. The financial commitment is large but calculated against the rising trajectory of licensing fees.

**Blockers:** NONE. The lack of a pilot is a feature of the strategy, not a flaw, given the data confidence. No existing evidence contradicts the data's implication.
**Risks:** Audience rejection of the format; production overspend. These are managed by the strategic necessity of owning IP.

**Kill Criteria:** If the first season's completion rate within 30 days of launch is below 40% of the subscriber base, halt further season production and revert to licensed-content-focused growth. Leading metric: Subscriber retention rate post-launch compared to pre-launch cohort.

DECISION: GO
