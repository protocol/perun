First call: NO-GO. A loss-making manufacturer with single-digit share proposing high-fixed-cost retail in a low-margin, recessionary environment contradicts basic unit economics.

**Steelman:** Direct retail is the only viable channel to demonstrate a complex hardware/software ecosystem and music strategy, as third-party retailers actively undermine the brand to push cheaper competitors.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Continue losing share to reseller neglect; brand erodes.
2.  **Shop-in-shop concessions:** Lower lease risk, but staff remain employed by partners; control is partial.
3.  **Proposed flagship chain:** Total brand control and experience, but massive fixed costs and inventory risk during a downturn.

**Fit:** Aligns with a "digital hub" vision but assumes the company can sustain heavy operating losses longer than competitors can sustain price wars.

**Pre-mortem:** A recession hits six months post-launch. Foot traffic drops 40%, but leases remain fixed. Inventory of fast-depreciating PCs obsolesces. Cash burn accelerates existing losses, forcing a liquidity crisis or fire sale before the strategy matures.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits "recent run of losses," "low margins," and "high lease costs." It offers no pilot data, no traffic-to-conversion modeling, and no proof that mall foot traffic converts to premium PC sales better than catalog/early-web channels. The "poor retail track record in the industry" is a stated fact, not a hypothetical risk.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Financial impossibility:** The company is already losing money. Adding high-fixed-cost real estate leases without a proven positive unit economic model creates an immediate cash-flow blocker.
2.  **Contradictory premises:** Entering a "low margin" business with "high lease costs" while currently unprofitable violates the constraint of capital preservation.

**Risks:** Channel conflict with remaining resellers; inventory obsolescence; macroeconomic downturn.

**Kill Criteria:** None proposed. A go-forward plan requires a capped pilot (e.g., 4 stores) with a strict "close if cash burn exceeds $X/month" clause. As proposed, it is an uncapped bet the company cannot afford.

The proposal fails the fundamental test of solvency. It attempts to solve a sales problem with a balance-sheet-breaking solution.

DECISION: NO-GO
