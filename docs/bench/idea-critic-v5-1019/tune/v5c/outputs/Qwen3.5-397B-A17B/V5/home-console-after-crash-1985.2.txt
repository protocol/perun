First call: GO. The market collapse is a distribution and perception failure, not a demand extinction; the proposed re-framing directly attacks the root cause.

**Steelman:** By rebranding the console as an "Entertainment System" bundled with non-game toys (robot, light gun) and assuming retail risk via stock buybacks, the company bypasses the "video game" stigma, secures shelf space, and controls software quality to prevent another market crash.

**Options:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Cede the recovering market to home computers; brand remains confined to Japan.
2.  **Direct Launch:** Sell as a game console. High probability of immediate retailer rejection due to 1983 trauma; likely total failure.
3.  **Proposed Plan:** Higher upfront cost (toys, buyback liability), but creates a unique value proposition ("toy" vs. "fad") and aligns retailer incentives. It beats "do nothing" by accessing the only available channel.

**Fit:** Assumes the goal is North American market entry. The strategy fits a "blue ocean" approach by creating a new category (entertainment system) where none currently exists in retailers' minds.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the toy bundles are perceived as gimmicks that don't justify the price, or if the lockout chip triggers a developer boycott resulting in a software drought. However, the buyback promise removes the retailer's financial risk, making refusal irrational unless the product is unsellable. The cost of delay is high; competitors (home computers) are entrenching themselves as the default gaming platform.

**Evidence:** The proposal relies on the stated fact of retailer refusal to stock "video games." The re-classification to "toy/entertainment" logically circumvents this specific blocker. The buyback promise directly addresses the stated risk of unsold stock.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (developer anger, return costs) are manageable trade-offs, not factual contradictions. The lack of track record is mitigated by the hardware's success in Japan and the risk-reversal for retailers.
**Risks:** High operational cost of returns; potential developer friction. These become kill criteria: if return rates exceed 40% in Q1 or if fewer than 5 third-party titles launch within 6 months, pivot or exit.

The plan transforms a blocked channel into an open one by changing the product's definition. It is a sound operator's move given the constraints.

DECISION: GO
