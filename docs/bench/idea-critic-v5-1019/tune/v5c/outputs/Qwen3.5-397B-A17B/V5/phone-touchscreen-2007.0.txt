First call: GO. The proposal attacks the core weakness of the music player business (erosion by phone makers) by defining a new category rather than competing in the existing one.

Steelman: Launch a premium, keyboard-less touchscreen device that integrates music, web, and email, secured by an exclusive carrier partnership that cedes design control and shares revenue, thereby bypassing carrier dominance and protecting the core music business.

Alternatives considered:
1. Do nothing: Music player sales erode as phones add music features; the company becomes a niche hardware vendor.
2. License software to multiple carriers: Loses design control, resulting in a fragmented, inferior user experience that fails to differentiate.
3. Build a physical keyboard phone: Competes directly with entrenched business rivals on their terms, ignoring the shift toward touch interfaces.

Fit: Moves the north star from "selling music players" to "owning the personal communication hub." The lack of phone experience is irrelevant if the product redefines the interface; the market is led by keyboard phones, but user friction with those devices is high.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the premium price ignores the value of the integrated experience, or if the single carrier cannot distribute effectively. However, the revenue share model aligns carrier incentives with sales volume. The lack of third-party apps is a temporary constraint that ensures stability for the v1 launch; it is not a blocker.

Evidence: The proposal correctly identifies that wireless carriers normally dictate design, which is the primary obstacle to a great user experience. The exclusive deal swaps reach for control, a necessary trade-off to deliver the promised quality. The risk of "no keyboard" is mitigated by the fact that current keyboard phones are clunky for media consumption.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (no experience, premium price, exclusivity, no apps) are execution challenges or strategic trade-offs, not factual contradictions. The proposal's own logic accounts for them: exclusivity buys control; premium price funds the innovation; no apps ensures a polished v1.

Risks: Market rejection of touch-only input; carrier underperformance; slow app ecosystem growth later. These become kill criteria: if v1 sales do not meet a specific threshold within 6 months, or if carrier churn exceeds industry average, the strategy must pivot.

DECISION: GO
