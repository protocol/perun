First call: GO. The company leverages its dominant hardware position to solve the industry's existential crisis: piracy. While free sharing is rampant, it offers no legal certainty or ease of use. A per-song model lowers the barrier to entry compared to subscriptions, matching existing consumer behavior of buying albums but with granularity.

**Steelman:** Create the only legal, convenient ecosystem where hardware and content reinforce each other, forcing labels to partner rather than litigate.

**Attack & Options:**
1.  **Margin Risk:** 99 cents leaves little profit. *Counter:* The goal is not store profitability but hardware lock-in. Losing money on songs to sell players is a valid strategy if the lifetime value of the user increases.
2.  **Platform Limitation:** Works only on own computers. *Counter:* This is a feature, not a bug, in 2003. It drives hardware sales. Expanding too early dilutes the unique value proposition.
3.  **Subscription Trend:** Rivals back subscriptions. *Counter:* In 2003, bandwidth and catalog depth make subscriptions clunky. Users prefer owning files they can burn to CD or move to portable devices. Subscriptions are a "rental" model consumers aren't ready for yet.
4.  **Label Withdrawal:** Labels may pull licenses. *Counter:* They have no better alternative. Litigation is failing; piracy is eroding all revenue. This is their only lifeline to monetize digital.

**Pre-mortem:** The store fails if the catalog is incomplete (labels hold out) or if the copy protection makes the user experience worse than piracy. However, the proposal explicitly includes licensing "all major record labels," addressing the catalog risk directly.

**Blockers:** None. The risks cited are market uncertainties, not factual contradictions. The margin issue is a strategic choice (subsidy), not a math error. The platform restriction is a strategic moat.

**Risks:** Slow adoption if price sensitivity is higher than expected; technical friction from copy protection. These become kill criteria: if song sales do not reach X million in quarter one, or if customer support tickets regarding DRM exceed Y%, pivot to a subscription model or remove restrictions.

The proposal correctly identifies that the bottleneck is not technology or price, but legal availability. By securing the licenses, the company solves the primary pain point for both consumers and labels.

DECISION: GO
