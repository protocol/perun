First call: GO. The proposal targets a massive, underserved market segment with a radically different value proposition (affordability and simplicity) that directly addresses the failure of previous high-end prototypes to gain traction with established players.

**Steelman:** By abandoning complex mapping for randomized movement, the product achieves a price point low enough to trigger impulse buys, making "good enough" cleaning accessible to millions who cannot justify premium costs, effectively creating a new category rather than competing in the existing luxury niche.

**Alternatives:** 
1. *Do nothing:* Cede the entire consumer robotics market to incumbents; high risk of obsolescence.
2. *High-end mapping:* Requires massive R&D, faces direct competition from well-funded rivals, and repeats the failure of securing partner interest.
3. *Proposed low-cost random:* Captures price-sensitive users, leverages existing simple tech, and bypasses the need for distributor buy-in by going direct-to-consumer.

**Fit:** This aligns with a "disruptive innovation" strategy, entering at the bottom of the market where incumbents are motivated to flee rather than fight.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if random cleaning is perceived as "broken" rather than "simple," or if battery life is insufficient to compensate for inefficiency. However, these are execution risks, not strategic blockers. The door is two-way; a failed launch can be pivoted or halted with limited brand damage compared to a massive high-end flop. Delay costs first-mover advantage in the budget segment.

**Evidence:** The lack of interest from floor-care companies validates that the traditional channel and high-price model are dead ends for this specific technology, reinforcing the need for a direct, low-cost approach. The risks (trust, experience) are mitigated by the low price point reducing purchase friction.

**Blockers:** NONE. No fact presented proves the low-cost model cannot work; the cited risks are standard for new consumer categories and are addressed by the pricing strategy.
**Risks:** Brand perception, coverage efficiency, lack of channel experience. These become kill criteria: if early sales do not show repeat usage or word-of-mouth growth within 6 months, pivot.

DECISION: GO
