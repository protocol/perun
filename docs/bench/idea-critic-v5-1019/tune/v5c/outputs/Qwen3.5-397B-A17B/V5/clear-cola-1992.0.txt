First call: NO-GO. The proposal ignores the fundamental constraint that cola flavor perception is intrinsically linked to color, and test markets are notorious for overstating novelty demand without proving repeat purchase rates.

**Steelman:** A clear cola can successfully decouple flavor from color perception, converting positive test market sentiment into sustainable national market share without eroding the flagship brand's core identity.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Evidence:** The proposal admits shoppers link cola flavor with color. Launching a product that contradicts this sensory expectation nationwide is a bet against established consumer psychology, not a brand extension. The "positive response" in test markets likely reflects curiosity (novelty), not intent to repeat. The proposal offers no data on repeat purchase rates, which are the only metric that matters for a CPG launch.
2.  **Better Way:** The "do nothing" option preserves margin. A cheaper alternative is a limited regional rollout to verify repeat purchase behavior before committing to a "large marketing campaign." The current plan skips the validation of the core mechanism: will people buy it twice?
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure. The product launches, initial sales spike due to curiosity, then crash as consumers realize the taste does not match the visual cue of "cola" or simply lose interest. The flagship brand suffers cannibalization during the spike, and the company is left with massive inventory and a diluted brand image. Rivals copy the gimmick instantly, splitting the novelty revenue.
4.  **Blockers:** The proposal explicitly lists "novelty buyers may not repeat" and "shoppers may link cola flavor with color" as risks but proceeds anyway without a mitigation plan or a kill criterion for repeat rates. Launching nationally based on test market data that cannot distinguish between novelty and habit is a factual failure of the business case. The cost of a national campaign is wasted if the repeat rate is below breakeven.

**Decision Logic:**
*   **Blockers:** Yes. The plan relies on unproven repeat purchase behavior and contradicts stated consumer sensory associations. The financial commitment (national launch + large campaign) is disproportionate to the evidence (test market curiosity).
*   **Risks:** Cannibalization, rival copies, and flavor-color mismatch are high but secondary to the lack of proof for sustained demand.

This is a classic "one-way door" with high irreversible cost based on weak evidence. The plan must be revised to prove repeat purchases in a controlled environment before national scaling.

DECISION: NO-GO
