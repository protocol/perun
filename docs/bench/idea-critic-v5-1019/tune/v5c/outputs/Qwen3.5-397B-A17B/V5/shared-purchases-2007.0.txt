First call: NO-GO. The core mechanism—broadcasting sensitive user purchases to friends—creates immediate, high-severity reputational and legal liability that an opt-out cannot mitigate.

**Steelman:** Sharing purchase data with explicit user notification and an opt-out balances ad revenue with user control, leveraging social proof to drive partner sales.

**Attack:**
1.  **Incentive Mismatch:** Users rarely check privacy settings before sensitive actions. An "opt-out" defaults to exposure, guaranteeing accidental leaks of sensitive items (medical, adult, financial).
2.  **Irreversibility:** As noted, once private data is in a news feed, it is copied, screenshotted, and cached. Withdrawal is technically impossible after distribution.
3.  **Partner Risk:** Partners face public embarrassment if user reactions to their products turn negative in a public feed, making them likely to withdraw from the ad network entirely.
4.  **Trust Erosion:** Treating privacy as a toggle rather than a default destroys platform trust. The cost of a single scandal (e.g., exposing a user's medical purchase) outweighs the incremental ad revenue.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Maintain current targeted ads without social broadcasting. Lower risk, lower potential viral uplift.
*   **Aggregated Data:** Share anonymized trends ("100 friends bought this") rather than individual transactions. Preserves privacy while offering social proof.
*   **Explicit Opt-In:** Require users to actively choose to share a specific purchase. Much lower adoption, but safe.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, a user's sensitive health purchase appears in their employer's feed. The resulting media frenzy and regulatory investigation force a shutdown, but the data is already archived by third parties. The platform loses major enterprise partners and faces class-action lawsuits.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** The proposal admits the feature is "hard to withdraw."
*   **Impact:** Broadcasting sensitive data is irreversible. Once leaked, the harm is permanent. This contradicts the basic requirement for user safety.
*   **Fact:** Purchases can be sensitive.
*   **Impact:** Defaulting to public sharing (even with opt-out) guarantees exposure of sensitive data for non-technical users.

**Risks:** User backlash, partner churn, regulatory fines. These are not merely risks; the blockers confirm they will materialize.

**Kill Criteria:** If any single user's sensitive data is exposed involuntarily, the program must terminate immediately. However, the design makes this event probable, not just possible.

DECISION: NO-GO
