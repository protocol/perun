First call: GO. The proposal leverages existing assets to solve a clear market gap (garage-compatible family hauler) with a reversible manufacturing strategy.

**Steelman:** Reuse the compact-car platform and a converted factory to launch a front-wheel-drive, car-like minivan that fits in home garages, targeting families currently limited to bulky station wagons or commercial-style vans.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Continue selling station wagons; risk losing families to competitors who eventually fill the gap.
2.  **New platform:** Build a dedicated chassis. Higher cost/risk; unjustifiable given the cash constraint and government loan stipulations.
3.  **Commercial van adaptation:** Modify existing truck-based vans. Fails the "drives like a car" and "fits in garage" requirements.

**Fit:** Aligns with the survival stage (cash preservation) and the north star of serving families. Reusing the compact platform is a feature, not a bug, ensuring car-like handling and low development costs.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if marketing positions the vehicle as a commercial work van, alienating families. The "no category exists" risk is actually an opportunity for first-mover advantage. The cash constraint is mitigated by using the existing platform and converted factory, minimizing CapEx.

**Evidence & Math:** The government loan guarantee provides the necessary liquidity bridge. The compact platform already exists (sunk cost). A converted factory is cheaper than new construction. The risk of rivals copying is secondary to establishing the category first. The space limitation of the compact platform is acceptable if the design prioritizes interior volume over exterior length (the "garage" constraint).

**Blockers:** NONE. The stated risks are manageable execution challenges, not factual contradictions. The lack of an existing category validates the innovation rather than prohibiting it.
**Risks:** Brand perception (commercial vs. family); competitor response. These become kill criteria: if initial focus groups confuse the vehicle with a work van, pivot marketing immediately.

**Kill Criteria:** If pre-orders from family-oriented demographics fall below 5,000 units in the first quarter, halt production expansion. Leading metric: Ratio of family vs. commercial buyers in early sales.

DECISION: GO
