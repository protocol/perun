First call: NO-GO. The proposal ignores the fundamental mismatch between the product's defining trait (clear) and the category's primary sensory cue (dark color = cola flavor), creating a high probability of market rejection despite positive test results.

Steelman: A clear cola can succeed as a premium, "purer" niche extension if the novelty sustains repeat purchases and cannibalization is offset by new category growth.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Sensory Contradiction:** The proposal admits shoppers link cola flavor with color. Launching a product that visually contradicts the expected taste profile nationwide is a direct assault on established consumer heuristics. Test markets often fail to capture this long-term cognitive dissonance.
2.  **Repeat Purchase Failure:** The risk that "novelty buyers may not repeat" is not just a risk; it is the likely outcome for a flavor-visual mismatch. Without repeat purchases, the large marketing campaign burns capital on one-time trials with negative ROI.
3.  **Cannibalization without Expansion:** If the product fails to retain users (due to point 1) but initially draws from the flagship (due to curiosity), the net result is immediate share erosion with no long-term gain.

**Risks:**
*   Rivals launching copies is secondary; the primary failure mode is self-inflicted consumer confusion.
*   Test market overstatement is highly probable given the "novelty" factor.

**Kill Criteria Check:**
The proposal offers no metric to validate *repeat* purchase rates specifically among non-novelty seekers before a nationwide spend. It relies on test market data that the proposal itself admits "can overstate demand." Proceeding without a mechanism to verify if the "purer" positioning overrides the "wrong color" instinct is financially reckless.

**Pre-mortem:**
Six months post-launch, sales have collapsed. Analysis shows initial trial was high, but repeat rates were near zero because consumers felt the taste "didn't match the look," leading to brand confusion that slightly dented the flagship's perception. The large marketing spend amplified a failed concept.

**Decision Logic:**
The core assumption—that a positive test market response translates to nationwide viability for a product contradicting deep-seated sensory expectations—is unsupported by the facts provided. The risks identified (flavor-color link, lack of repeat) are not mitigated; they are merely listed. A "go" requires evidence that the visual disconnect can be overcome, which is absent.

DECISION: NO-GO
