First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to execute a global, multi-country launch in an unproven market (online fashion, 1998) with high fixed costs (offices in several countries) before validating unit economics or demand, creating an immediate cash-burn trap that venture funding may not sustain.

**Steelman:** Simultaneous US/EU entry captures maximum first-mover advantage and brand scale before competitors react, leveraging VC capital to build insurmountable logistics moats immediately.

**Attack:**
1.  **Unproven Demand vs. Fixed Cost:** In 1998, consumer trust in online fashion payments and sizing is negligible. Launching in multiple jurisdictions multiplies legal, tax, and logistics complexity before a single unit is sold. The "unproven" risk is not mitigated by scale; it is amplified by it.
2.  **Cash Burn:** Establishing offices in several countries creates massive fixed overhead. If sales adoption is slow (highly likely for a new behavior), the runway provided by VC will evaporate on rent and salaries before product-market fit is found.
3.  **Execution Fragility:** Managing logistics across two continents simultaneously requires operational maturity the team likely lacks at inception. A failure in one region (e.g., customs, returns) cascades, threatening the entire entity.
4.  **Better Way:** A single-market beachhead (e.g., UK or Germany only) allows for iterative learning on logistics and customer acquisition cost (CAC) with limited capital exposure. Success there funds expansion. The proposed "all-in" approach lacks this reversibility.

**Pre-mortem:** The company runs out of cash in 18 months. Cause: Overhead from five international offices consumed 80% of funding while online conversion rates remained below 1% due to consumer skepticism. Logistics failures in cross-border shipments destroyed margins.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** Online fashion buying is explicitly stated as "unproven."
*   **Fact:** Multi-country launch "multiplies logistics and marketing costs."
*   **Contradiction:** The proposal relies on venture money lasting until "sales catch up," but the multiplied costs and unproven demand make the time-to-profitability unknowable and likely longer than the funding runway. The plan fails its own financial logic.

**Risks:** Delayed launch, venture dry-up, operational collapse. These are not mere risks here; the unproven nature of the core transaction combined with maximal fixed costs constitutes a fundamental flaw in the business model as proposed.

**Kill Criterion:** If a single-market pilot cannot achieve positive unit economics within 12 months, the global thesis is invalid. The proposal skips the pilot.

DECISION: NO-GO
