First call: GO. The proposal targets a massive unmet need (automated floor cleaning) by sacrificing perfect coverage for affordability and simplicity, creating a new category rather than fighting incumbents on their terms.

Steelman: A "good enough" robot that cleans frequently via randomization at a price point accessible to mass markets will outperform expensive, complex mapping robots that sit unused due to cost or friction.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Cede the home robotics market to high-end players; remain a prototype curiosity.
2. Build a mapper: Require R&D spend and time the company lacks; enter a price war against established giants.
3. Launch low-cost random bot (Proposed): Immediate market entry, lower price barrier, simpler mechanics. This beats "do nothing" by creating volume and beats "mapper" by avoiding capability traps the company cannot solve yet.

Fit: Aligns with a "disruptive innovation" strategy (assumed), prioritizing market creation over feature perfection.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if random cleaning is so ineffective that users return the unit, or if safety incidents destroy trust. However, these are risks, not blockers. The cost of delay is high; waiting for perfect mapping allows competitors to define the category standards.

Evidence: The rejection by floor-care companies supports the pivot to direct consumer sales; incumbents often miss disruptive, lower-margin opportunities. The lack of consumer experience is a risk, but the proposed low-cost, simple-mechanism approach minimizes the complexity of that leap.

Blockers: NONE. No fact presented proves the product cannot function or that the market refuses *any* robot. The risks (coverage gaps, trust) are inherent to the category, not fatal flaws in this specific approach.

Risks: Brand damage from poor cleaning performance; safety liabilities; distribution challenges. These become kill criteria: if return rates exceed 15% due to cleaning efficacy, or if any safety incident occurs, the line must be pulled.

Kill Criteria: Stop if initial batch return rate >20% citing "poor cleaning" or if any unit causes property damage. Leading metric: Units sold per month vs. return rate.

The proposal correctly identifies that a cheaper, simpler solution is the only viable entry point given the constraints. It turns weaknesses (no mapping, no experience) into a focused strategy (low cost, simplicity).

DECISION: GO
