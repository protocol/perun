First call: GO. The data-backed hypothesis that a full-season drop of prestige content can drive subscriptions outweighs the traditional pilot model's limitations for an online-only service, provided the cost is capped against clear kill criteria.

**Steelman:** Leveraging proprietary viewing data to bypass the pilot system and commission a high-profile, full-season original release creates a unique value proposition that differentiates the service from broadcasters and justifies the budget share.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Continue rising licensing costs with no owned IP; eventual margin collapse.
*   **Pilot First:** Delays market entry, signals "TV network" behavior rather than innovation, and may fail to capture the specific "binge" audience data suggests exists.
*   **Cheaper Original:** Lacks the star power/director attachment required to validate the platform's ambition and attract immediate press/subscription spikes.

**Fit:** Aligns with the north star of transitioning from a content aggregator to a primary destination. The "all-at-once" release directly exploits the online medium's advantage over broadcast scheduling.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the star/director combination fails to convert data insights into actual viewing quality, or if the "all-at-once" model cannibalizes conversation longevity without driving enough new subs to cover the massive upfront cost. This is a one-way door for this specific budget; once spent, it cannot be recovered.

**Evidence & Verification:** The proposal relies on internal viewing data (unverified here, but stated as the core asset). The risk of "no pilot" is mitigated only if the data is robust enough to predict series-level engagement, not just episode-level.

**Blockers:** NONE. The stated risks (no pilot, uncertain audience, cost) are inherent to the strategy but do not contradict the core claim that data replaces the need for a pilot. They are risks, not factual blockers.

**Kill Criteria:**
1.  **Metric:** Subscription growth attributable to the title must exceed Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) benchmarks within 90 days of release.
2.  **Threshold:** If completion rates for Episode 1 drop below 60%, halt further season production immediately.
3.  **Cap:** Total cost must not exceed 40% of the annual content budget.

**Strongest Attack Survived:** The argument that "no online service has done this" implies impossibility. However, the lack of precedent is the opportunity; the viewing data provides the specific validation that traditional broadcasters lack, making the leap calculated rather than blind.

DECISION: GO
