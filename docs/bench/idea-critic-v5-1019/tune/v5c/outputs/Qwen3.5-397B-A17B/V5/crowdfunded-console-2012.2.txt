First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on unproven developer adoption and ignores the capital intensity of hardware manufacturing relative to a "no marketing budget."

Steelman: A crowdfunded, open-source console can succeed by targeting a niche underserved by incumbents, leveraging community passion to offset marketing costs, and delivering within a year to maintain momentum.

Options:
1. **Proposal:** Crowdfund now. Cost: High execution risk. Risk: Total loss if hardware delays occur or devs don't port games. Reversibility: Low (once funds are spent/pledged).
2. **Do Nothing:** Avoid capital risk. Cost: Missed opportunity. Risk: None. Reversibility: N/A.
3. **Software-First:** Build the OS/emulator for existing PC/mobile hardware first. Cost: Low. Risk: Lower hardware margin potential initially. Reversibility: High. This reaches the same upside (proving the platform/software) cheaper and safer.

Fit: Assuming the goal is a sustainable hardware business, launching hardware without a marketing budget or confirmed developer pipeline contradicts basic market dynamics.

Pre-mortem: The project fails because component sourcing delays push shipping past 18 months, causing backer refunds and reputation collapse. Alternatively, the console ships with no games because developers refused to port to an unproven, low-install-base platform. This is a one-way door with high capital exposure. The cost of delay is negligible compared to the cost of premature failure.

Evidence: The proposal admits "no marketing budget" and "hardware delays are common" while facing "established console makers." These are not risks to be managed; they are blockers to the core value proposition of competing for players and developers. The math does not work: acquiring users and devs requires capital or existing scale, neither of which exists here.

Blockers:
1. **No Marketing Budget vs. Established Competitors:** Impossible to acquire users against incumbents without spend or a viral hook not described.
2. **Developer Chicken-and-Egg:** No evidence of developer commitment; platforms fail without content, and devs won't build without users.
3. **Hardware Delays:** High probability of missing the one-year window, destroying trust in a crowdfunding model.

Risks: Supply chain volatility, price sensitivity of backers.

Kill Criteria: N/A (Decision is No-Go).

DECISION: NO-GO
