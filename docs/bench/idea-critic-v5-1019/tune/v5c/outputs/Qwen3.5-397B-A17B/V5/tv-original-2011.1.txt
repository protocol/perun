First call: GO. The proposal leverages unique proprietary data to bypass industry-standard de-risking (pilots) in exchange for a first-mover advantage in original prestige content, addressing the structural threat of rising licensing costs.

**Steelman:** Use granular viewing data as a surrogate for pilot testing to greenlight a high-budget original series immediately, securing exclusive IP and differentiating the platform before competitors react.

**Attack & Options:**
The primary objection is the financial exposure of ordering two seasons without a pilot or proven audience for originals. However, "do nothing" guarantees continued margin erosion from licensing hikes. A traditional pilot delays market entry and incurs production costs without guaranteeing a series order. The data-driven approach substitutes empirical viewer patterns for qualitative pilot feedback. If the data accurately reflects demand for the specific genre/talent combination, the pilot becomes redundant cost.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This fits a growth-stage strategy requiring defensible moats (exclusive IP). Failure mode: Data misinterprets niche interest as mass appeal, leading to a costly flop. Yet, waiting for "perfect" certainty allows competitors to lock up talent. The cost of delay is losing the specific director/star package and ceding the "originals" narrative.

**Evidence & Blockers:**
The proposal rests on "the service's viewing data." Assuming this data shows strong correlation between the attached talent/genre and high completion rates among the core subscriber base, the lack of a pilot is a feature, not a bug. No factual blocker exists in the text; the risks (uncertain audience, cost) are inherent to all R&D, not unique failures of this method. The "all-at-once" release further differentiates the product from broadcast norms, leveraging the medium's strengths.

**Decision:**
Blockers: NONE. The stated risks are managed by the unique data asset, which acts as the de-risking mechanism.
Risks: Audience mismatch, budget overrun. These become kill criteria (e.g., specific completion rate thresholds post-launch).

The plan converts a cost center (licensing) into an asset (IP) using a competitive advantage (data) that traditional broadcasters lack. Refusing based on industry convention ignores the specific asymmetry this proposal exploits.

DECISION: GO
