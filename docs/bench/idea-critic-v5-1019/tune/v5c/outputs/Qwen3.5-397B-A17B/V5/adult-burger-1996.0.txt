First call: NO-GO. The proposal contradicts the chain's core identity and customer value proposition without a validated path to overcome them.

**Steelman:** A massive ad spend can forcibly reposition a family brand as premium by overwhelming existing perceptions with a high-quality, higher-priced product.

**Attack:**
1.  **Identity Mismatch:** Adults associate the brand with "kids" and "value." A premium burger creates cognitive dissonance; the brand equity actively works *against* the "premium" claim.
2.  **Price Sensitivity:** The core demographic expects low prices. A price increase alienates existing customers without guaranteeing new adult acquisition.
3.  **Sunk Cost Risk:** Spending the "largest budget in industry history" before validating demand violates basic capital allocation. If the positioning fails (likely due to brand perception), the loss is catastrophic and unrecoverable.
4.  **Better Way:** Test the product in adult-only markets or via limited-time offers with minimal spend to validate demand before committing to a global repositioning campaign.

**Blockers:**
*   **Contradictory Evidence:** The proposal admits customers come for "kids' meals and value" and see the chain as "fast, not premium," yet plans to sell "premium" at "higher prices." The stated facts prove the target audience does not want the proposed value proposition from this specific brand.
*   **Financial Flaw:** Committing maximum capital before proving demand is a failure of unit economics. The plan relies on buying perception rather than earning it through product-market fit.

**Risks:** Brand dilution, alienation of the core family base, massive financial loss on ad spend, failure to attract adults who distrust the brand's premium claims.

**Kill Criteria:** If a limited pilot does not show >20% repeat purchase rate from adults within 3 months at the proposed price point, the project stops.

**Decision:** The plan bets the company on an unproven hypothesis that contradicts established brand reality. The "largest budget" amplifies the likelihood of failure rather than mitigating it.

DECISION: NO-GO
