First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on unproven developer adoption and ignores the capital intensity of hardware, creating a high probability of failure before shipment.

Steelman: A crowdfunded, open-software console can succeed by targeting a niche underserved by closed ecosystems, leveraging community goodwill to offset the lack of a marketing budget.

Options:
1. **Proposed Plan:** High risk; requires simultaneous success in hardware manufacturing, developer recruitment, and logistics with zero safety net.
2. **Do Nothing:** Avoids capital loss; misses the niche opportunity but preserves resources.
3. **Software-First Alternative:** Build the open platform for existing hardware (PC/Raspberry Pi) to prove developer interest and software value before committing to custom silicon. This is cheaper, reversible, and validates the core assumption (developer adoption) without hardware risk.

Fit: Assuming the goal is market penetration, entering a hardware market dominated by incumbents with no marketing budget and no existing developer base contradicts basic market dynamics.

Pre-mortem: The project fails because hardware production delays drain the crowdfunding capital before units ship, or the device arrives with no compelling games, causing backers to demand refunds. This is a one-way door with significant financial liability.

Evidence: The proposal admits "hardware delays are common" and "developers may not build games." These are not mere risks; they are historical facts that act as blockers when the plan offers no mitigation (e.g., existing prototypes, signed developer letters, or manufacturing contracts). Relying on crowdfunding to fund initial tooling without verified supply chain capacity is a fatal flaw.

Blockers:
1. **Chicken-and-Egg Dependency:** The value proposition requires games, but developers won't build without users. The proposal offers no mechanism to break this cycle other than hope.
2. **Capital Insufficiency:** Crowdfunding pre-sales often fail to cover unexpected hardware manufacturing overruns, leading to insolvency before delivery.

Risks: Marketing reach, competitor retaliation.

Kill Criteria: None provided, but the lack of a working prototype or manufacturing partner makes the current plan untenable. The "Software-First" alternative achieves the vision's core (open platform) without the fatal hardware blocker.

DECISION: NO-GO
