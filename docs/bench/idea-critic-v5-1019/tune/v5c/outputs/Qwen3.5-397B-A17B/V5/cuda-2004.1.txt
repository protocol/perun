First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits massive capital to a non-existent market while the company fights a survival-level race in its core gaming business, violating basic capital allocation discipline for a public company.

**Steelman:** Embedding general-purpose compute capabilities in every consumer GPU now creates an unassailable ecosystem moat by the time scientific demand matures, turning a commodity hardware race into a platform monopoly.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Maintain focus on the core gaming/workstation war; let academics continue hacking existing APIs. Cost: $0. Risk: Low. Reversibility: N/A.
2.  **Targeted High-End Only:** Add programmability only to expensive workstation cards where early adopters exist. Cost: Moderate. Risk: Medium. Reversibility: High.
3.  **Proposal (Mass Market):** Subsidize R&D across all units. Cost: High per-unit margin erosion. Risk: Existential if core market share drops. Reversibility: Low (silicon is permanent).

**Fit:** This contradicts the immediate north star of winning the discrete GPU performance war against the main rival. Resources diverted to compiler toolchains and university grants are resources not spent on clock speeds or pixel pipelines, which are the only metrics current customers value.

**Pre-mortem:** The program launches, raising unit costs and reducing price competitiveness in the critical gaming sector. The rival captures market share by focusing solely on gaming performance. The scientific market fails to materialize quickly enough to offset the lost gaming revenue. The company runs out of cash or is acquired.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits "no large paying market exists yet" and relies on "academic users experimenting." There are no committed customers to underwrite the NRE (Non-Recurring Engineering) costs. The cost is borne by *every* unit sold, directly attacking the margin needed to fund the core competitive fight.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Capital Misallocation:** Funding a speculative, zero-revenue segment while engaged in a "core race" with a rival is a factual failure of priority. The proposal offers no mechanism to protect the core business's margin.
2.  **Unit Economics:** Raising unit cost on a mass-market product without immediate price power or demand guarantees destroys value per unit sold.

**Risks:** Engineering distraction, niche demand remaining niche, competitor undercutting on price.

**Kill Criteria:** If gaming market share drops by >5% within 12 months of launch due to price/performance disadvantage, the program must be immediately terminated.

DECISION: NO-GO
