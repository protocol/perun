First call: NO-GO. A loss-making manufacturer with 3–5% share entering a low-margin, high-fixed-cost business during a recession violates basic capital preservation.

**Steelman:** Direct retail is the only viable channel to demonstrate the integrated hardware-software music strategy, as third-party staff actively undermine sales by pushing cheaper competitors.

**Attack:**
1.  **Financial Suicide:** The proposal admits recent losses and low industry margins. Adding prime mall leases (high fixed costs) creates massive operating leverage. In a recession, a sales dip immediately deepens losses, threatening solvency.
2.  **Channel Warfare:** Opening stores directly competes with existing electronics retailers. These partners, already incentivized to push rivals, will likely retaliate by deprioritizing or delisting the brand, collapsing the remaining 95% of revenue to chase a tiny, unproven direct slice.
3.  **Execution Risk:** The industry has a "poor retail track record." Attempting to master complex retail logistics while fixing a broken manufacturing core splits management focus.
4.  **Alternatives:** The "digital-music strategy" demonstration could occur via dedicated kiosks inside existing partner stores (sharing cost, avoiding conflict) or online interactive tours, rather than full-scale stores.

**Pre-mortem:** Two years later, the company is bankrupt. The stores drew foot traffic but conversion was too low to cover $50M/year in lease overhead. Major retailers dropped the brand due to channel conflict. The recession reduced discretionary spending, leaving the company with empty stores and unsold inventory.

**Blockers:**
*   **Capital Mismatch:** Loss-making entities cannot sustain high-fixed-cost expansions in cyclical downturns without guaranteed volume, which is not present.
*   **Channel Dependency:** The brand relies on third parties for the vast majority of sales; antagonizing them without a proven direct-demand engine is fatal.
*   **Margin Reality:** Low hardware margins cannot subsidize high retail overheads without a premium pricing power not yet demonstrated in this context.

**Risks:** Inventory obsolescence, brand dilution if stores feel empty, management distraction.

**Kill Criteria:** If a pilot store cannot achieve positive unit economics within 6 months, or if any major retail partner threatens delisting, the program stops.

The proposal bets the company on a high-cost channel shift during a downturn without securing the core business first.

DECISION: NO-GO
