First call: GO. The proposal solves the core friction of early e-reading (need for a PC) and aligns with the company's catalog strength, despite hardware inexperience.

**Steelman:** A dedicated, cellular-enabled device removes purchase friction, unlocking latent demand for digital books that previous PC-tethered devices failed to capture, while low prices drive volume to offset margin compression.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Cedes the digital transition to competitors; print sales eventually decline without a digital replacement.
*   **Partner/License:** Avoids hardware risk but cedes control of the user experience and pricing model, likely resulting in another failed generic reader.
*   **Hardware Risk:** Valid, but the "paper-like" screen and cellular connectivity are specific differentiators addressing prior failure modes (poor readability, connectivity hassle).
*   **Cannibalization/Margins:** Low prices are a feature, not a bug, to build the ecosystem. Cannibalization is inevitable; better to cannibalize self than lose the customer entirely.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the device is too expensive, the screen is inferior to print, or publishers withhold content. The proposal addresses screen and connectivity; price and publisher relations are the critical variables.

**Blockers:** None. The risks cited (inexperience, margin squeeze, prior failures) are execution challenges, not factual contradictions. The market has not yet seen a device combining this specific screen technology with ubiquitous cellular access at a low content price point. The "prior failures" lacked these specific attributes.

**Risks:** Hardware supply chain delays; publisher resistance to pricing; slower than expected adoption. These become kill criteria: if device cost exceeds target by >20% at launch, or if <50% of top publishers sign on by pilot date, pause.

**Kill Criteria:**
1.  **Metric:** Device BOM cost must allow profitability at the proposed book price within 18 months.
2.  **Date:** Launch within 12 months.
3.  **Pivot:** If <10% of attach-rate targets are met in Q1, re-evaluate pricing or hardware specs.

The potential upside of owning the dominant digital reading platform outweighs the hardware entry risk. The specific technical choices directly address historical failure points.

DECISION: GO
