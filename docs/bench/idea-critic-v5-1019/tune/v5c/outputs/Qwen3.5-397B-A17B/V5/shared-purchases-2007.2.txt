First call: NO-GO. The core mechanism—broadcasting sensitive user purchases to friends—fundamentally conflicts with the stated risk of privacy invasion, creating an immediate trust deficit that outweighs ad revenue potential.

**Steelman:** The feature maximizes ad targeting precision by leveraging verified purchase data, assuming users prioritize social sharing over privacy when given a notification and opt-out.

**Attack:**
1.  **Incentive Mismatch:** An "option to decline" places the burden of privacy protection on the user, while the default setting exploits them. Most users will not navigate complex settings, leading to accidental exposure of sensitive data (e.g., medical supplies, gifts).
2.  **Partner Risk:** Partner sites face reputational damage if their products trigger awkward public notifications. They are likely to block this data sharing to avoid customer backlash, starving the feature of inventory.
3.  **Irreversibility:** Once users feel betrayed by exposed purchases, trust is broken permanently. The "hard to withdraw" risk is actually a guarantee of brand toxicity; you cannot "un-ring" a bell regarding a private purchase.
4.  **Alternatives:** Aggregated, anonymized ad targeting achieves revenue goals without exposing individual transactions. This proposal chooses the highest-risk path for marginal gain in transparency.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, a viral story emerges about a user's sensitive purchase being mocked in their feed. Major partners withdraw, regulators investigate, and user churn spikes. The feature is killed, but the brand damage remains.

**Blockers:**
*   **Contradictory Facts:** The proposal admits users may see this as an invasion of privacy yet proceeds with a default-on model for sensitive data. This internal contradiction proves the plan fails its own risk assessment.
*   **Partner Viability:** The stated risk that partners may be embarrassed is a blocker; rational partners will opt out of the data sharing entirely, rendering the feature empty.

**Risks:** User churn, regulatory scrutiny, permanent brand damage. These are not merely risks; given the nature of sensitive data, they are near-certainties if launched as proposed.

**Kill Criteria:** If even 1% of users opt out immediately upon notification, the premise of "social sharing" is false. However, the presence of a concrete blocker (the contradiction between privacy risks and default exposure) dictates the decision now.

DECISION: NO-GO
