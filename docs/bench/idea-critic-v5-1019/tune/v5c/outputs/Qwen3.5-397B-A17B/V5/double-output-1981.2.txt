First call: NO-GO. Doubling production while sales lag by 60% during a recession with rising interest rates destroys cash without creating demand.

Steelman: The claim assumes that flooding the market now secures future market share or forces dealer acceptance before competitors react, betting that the recession will end soon enough to sell the accumulated inventory before cash runs out.

Options:
1. **Double Production**: Maximizes cash burn, increases inventory financing costs, and risks dealer insolvency. Upside: speculative market dominance if demand instantly recovers.
2. **Do Nothing (Maintain Rate)**: Continues slow cash bleed but avoids accelerating the crisis. Upside: Buys time without immediate catastrophe.
3. **Cut Production**: Aligns output with sales to preserve cash. Upside: Extends runway, allowing survival until economic conditions improve.

Fit: A start-up's north star is survival and product-market fit. This proposal contradicts the basic economic reality stated: demand is collapsing while financing costs rise.

Pre-mortem: The company doubles output, dealers refuse further stock due to high interest costs, inventory sits unsold, cash reserves hit zero within months, and the firm enters bankruptcy before the recession ends. This is a one-way door; once cash is spent on unsold cars, it cannot be recovered.

Evidence: The proposal's own facts confirm the failure mode. Producing 7,500 units but selling only 3,000 creates a 4,500-unit overhang. Doubling monthly output widens this gap exponentially. With limited cash and rising interest rates, the cost to finance this growing inventory will exceed available capital. The stated fact that "dealers may not be able to finance more stock" is a confirmed blocker, not a risk, as high interest rates directly impair their borrowing capacity.

Blockers:
1. **Cash Flow Mismatch**: Limited cash cannot support doubled production costs plus increased inventory financing fees while revenue remains stagnant.
2. **Dealer Capacity**: Rising interest rates objectively reduce dealer borrowing power, making additional stock intake impossible regardless of volume.
3. **Demand Contradiction**: Recessionary conditions explicitly lower demand; increasing supply against falling demand guarantees inventory obsolescence.

Risks: None remain; the blockers are fatal based on current facts.

DECISION: NO-GO
