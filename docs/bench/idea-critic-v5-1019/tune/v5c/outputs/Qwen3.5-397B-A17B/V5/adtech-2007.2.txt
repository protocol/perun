First call: NO-GO. The $6 billion cash price for a fast-changing technology asset, absent verified synergies or retained revenue guarantees, represents an unjustifiable concentration of risk that could cripple the buyer's balance sheet if the market shifts or key clients defect due to conflicts.

Steelman: Acquiring the platform is the only viable path to rapidly scale display capabilities and block the search leader's consolidation, provided the technology remains relevant and major agency clients stay.

Options:
1. **Do Nothing:** Avoids immediate cash drain and client conflict but cedes the display market to the competitor, potentially relegating the buyer to a distant second place in total ad revenue.
2. **Build/Buy Smaller:** Develop internally or acquire smaller niche players; lower cost and conflict, but too slow to catch the competitor's momentum.
3. **Proposed Acquisition:** Immediate scale and competitive parity, but carries extreme financial leverage, high integration risk, and likely revenue loss from conflicted agencies.

Fit: Aligns with the goal to compete with the search leader, but the "cash-only" structure ignores the volatility of the display market.

Pre-mortem: The acquisition fails because major agency clients, fearing competition with the buyer's existing units, immediately pull contracts. The technology becomes obsolete within 18 months due to rapid industry shifts, leaving the buyer with $6 billion in sunk costs and no revenue bridge. This is a one-way door; the cash cannot be recovered.

Evidence: The proposal admits to "conflicts with existing agency customers" and a "fast-changing market" but offers no mitigation, retention contracts, or earn-outs to de-risk these known failures. The price is a "large premium" without cited valuation models proving ROI.

Blockers:
1. **Financial Overexposure:** Spending the company's largest ever cash reserve on a single, unproven (in this owner's hands) asset in a volatile market creates a single point of failure for the entire organization.
2. **Revenue Contradiction:** The core value proposition relies on agency relationships that the proposal explicitly states are now in conflict with the new ownership structure.

Risks: Technology obsolescence, culture clash, regulatory scrutiny.

The plan commits irreversible capital to an asset with active, acknowledged reasons to lose value immediately upon closing.

DECISION: NO-GO
