First call: NO-GO. The proposal explicitly states the synthetic costs more than leather while offering inferior comfort and facing manufacturer resistance; a premium price cannot be justified by worse performance.

Steelman: The material's durability and leather-like appearance could create a new high-end category if marketed aggressively to overcome inertia.

Options:
1. **Proposed:** Heavy marketing spend to force adoption. Cost: High. Risk: Total loss if comfort rejects it. Reversibility: Low (sunk marketing).
2. **Do Nothing:** Avoid launching a superior-cost, inferior-comfort product. Cost: Zero. Risk: Missing a future trend. Reversibility: N/A.
3. **Niche Pilot:** Target specific non-footwear durable goods or low-comfort footwear (e.g., rain boots) to validate durability claims without fighting the comfort barrier. Cost: Low. Risk: Limited scope. Reversibility: High.

Fit: Launching a "premium" product that is less comfortable than the incumbent contradicts the fundamental value proposition of premium footwear, which prioritizes fit and feel alongside aesthetics.

Pre-mortem: The product fails because shoe manufacturers, pressured by the marketing spend, produce a limited run. Consumers buy the shoes based on the "leather-like" marketing, experience discomfort, and return them. The brand reputation for both the chemical company and the shoe manufacturers is damaged. The high cost prevents price-based recovery. This is a one-way door with significant financial loss.

Evidence: The proposal's own facts are the evidence against it.
- Cost: Synthetic > Leather.
- Comfort: Synthetic < Leather.
- Adoption: Manufacturers reluctant.
- Market: Fashion volatility.
No external data is needed; the internal logic is broken. You cannot charge a premium for a product that is objectively worse in the primary functional metric (comfort) and more expensive to make.

Blockers:
1. **Negative Unit Economics:** Higher production cost than the incumbent (leather) with no stated efficiency gain for the manufacturer.
2. **Inferior Value Proposition:** Lower comfort at a higher price point makes the "premium" positioning indefensible.
3. **Channel Resistance:** Manufacturers are already unwilling to change factories; heavy marketing spend does not solve the retooling cost or the end-user comfort rejection.

Risks: Fashion trends shifting (mitigated by durability claim, but irrelevant if the shoe is unwearable).

Kill Criteria: None can save the current plan because the core trade-off (more money for less comfort) is fatal.

DECISION: NO-GO
