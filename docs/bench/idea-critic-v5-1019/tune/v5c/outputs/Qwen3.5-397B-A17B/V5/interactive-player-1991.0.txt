First call: NO-GO. The $1,000 price point creates an immediate market mismatch against cheaper, evolving alternatives.

**Steelman:** A dedicated $1,000 appliance bridges the gap between passive TV and complex PCs by offering superior interactive media playback in 1991.

**Attack & Options:**
The proposal fails the "cheaper alternative" test. Personal computers are rapidly gaining multimedia capabilities; a PC offers computing, productivity, and gaming for a similar or lower effective cost per function, making a single-purpose $1,000 box redundant. Game consoles provide entertainment at a fraction of the price. The "do nothing" option (waiting for PC prices to drop or standards to unify) carries less risk than forcing a proprietary hardware format.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
Positioning "between" a console and PC is a strategic void, not a niche. Consumers will not pay a premium for a device that does less than a PC and costs far more than a console. Assuming the library remains small (a stated risk), the value proposition collapses. A pre-mortem reveals failure via channel conflict (retailers pushing PCs) and consumer confusion ("Why buy this when my next PC does this?").

**Evidence & Blockers:**
The proposal admits the price is "far above game consoles" and the library "will be small." These are not mere risks; they are blockers. High hardware cost suppresses install base; a small install base discourages software developers; lack of software validates consumer hesitation. This is a negative feedback loop inherent in the stated facts. The risk of buyer confusion is compounded by the lack of a clear "must-have" application that justifies the $1,000 entry fee over existing or near-future alternatives.

**Decision:**
Blockers:
1. Price ($1,000) exceeds the perceived value relative to multi-function PCs and low-cost consoles.
2. Small content library guarantees low adoption, invalidating the platform model.
3. No defensible niche against rapidly improving general-purpose computers.

Risks: Consumer confusion, format wars, slow content ramp-up.

The stated facts confirm the unit economics and market positioning cannot sustain the platform.

DECISION: NO-GO
