First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to high-volume production and a premium price before resolving fundamental market and regulatory uncertainties.

**Steelman:** Launching a novel, well-funded personal transporter at $5,000 with 40,000-unit capacity will capture a new market segment despite current ambiguities.

**Attack:**
1.  **Regulatory Blocker:** The legal status on sidewalks and roads is explicitly "unclear." Deploying 40,000 units without defined legality creates immediate liability and potential confiscation risks. A product cannot succeed if its primary use case is illegal or undefined in target markets.
2.  **Market Mismatch:** At $5,000, the price is prohibitive for mass adoption, yet the proposal targets no specific high-value niche beyond vague "institutional buyers." The admitted lack of proven use cases beyond institutions, combined with social stigma ("conspicuous") and utility limits ("cannot carry much"), suggests demand will not meet the 40,000-unit factory capacity.
3.  **Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "pilot" alternative is superior. Instead of sizing a factory for 40,000 units, produce a limited batch (e.g., 500 units) to validate legal frameworks, refine the value proposition, and test actual consumer behavior before committing capital to mass production.

**Pre-mortem:** The company produces 40,000 units, but cities ban them from sidewalks due to safety concerns, and consumers reject the high price for a device with limited utility. The company is left with massive inventory, legal battles, and a brand associated with a failed fad.

**Evidence Check:** The proposal itself admits the critical flaws: unclear legality, unproven use cases, high price, and social friction. These are not future risks; they are current stated facts that contradict the viability of a mass launch.

**Blockers:**
*   **Regulatory Uncertainty:** Operating without legal clearance is a fatal flaw for a mass-market vehicle.
*   **Unproven Demand vs. Capacity:** Committing to 40,000 units without proven consumer use cases is financially reckless.

**Risks:** Social stigma, limited utility, high price sensitivity. These reinforce the need for a pilot, not a full launch.

**Kill Criteria:** Do not proceed until legal status is clarified in at least three major markets and a pilot program validates consumer demand at the $5,000 price point.

DECISION: NO-GO
