First call: NO-GO. Launching a premium-priced tablet with a tiny app catalog against a dominant incumbent, while the OS has negligible share and leadership is in flux, ignores the primary driver of tablet adoption: ecosystem depth.

**Steelman:** The brand's hardware prestige can temporarily offset OS weakness if developer momentum is ignited immediately through heavy investment.

**Attack:**
1.  **Ecosystem Gap:** Tablets are utility devices, not just hardware. A "small fraction" of apps means missing critical use cases (media, productivity) that define the category. Users rarely adopt a secondary ecosystem for hardware alone unless the price is disruptive, not "in line."
2.  **Developer Chicken-and-Egg:** Heavy outreach fails if the installed base is near zero. Developers prioritize platforms with existing users; advertising to consumers without apps wastes capital.
3.  **Leadership Void:** Transitioning leadership creates strategic drift. Executing a complex two-sided market launch (users + devs) requires absolute strategic continuity, which is currently absent.
4.  **Price Mismatch:** Pricing at parity with the market leader removes the only viable entry strategy for a challenger with inferior utility (fewer apps).

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Preserve capital until OS share grows or leadership stabilizes.
*   *Low-Cost Entry:* Price significantly below the leader to force adoption, accepting hardware losses to build the user base for developers.
*   *Niche Focus:* Target a specific vertical where the small app catalog is sufficient, rather than a general consumer launch.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches to strong initial sales driven by brand loyalty, but churn is massive within 90 days as users hit "app walls." Developers, seeing low engagement, abandon the platform. The heavy ad spend yields no retention. The project becomes a costly footnote during the leadership transition.

**Blockers:**
*   **Price-to-Value Disconnect:** Matching the leader's price while offering a fraction of the utility (apps) guarantees commercial failure in a mature market.
*   **Timing:** Launching during leadership transition without a stabilized OS share is operationally reckless.

**Risks:** Consumer perception of "unproven" status; developer apathy despite outreach.

The proposal demands premium pricing for a sub-par ecosystem experience during internal instability. This violates basic market dynamics for platform businesses.

DECISION: NO-GO
