First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to solve a scaling problem by acquiring a second declining asset, compounding execution risk without addressing the core revenue decay.

**Steelman:** Combining two distressed media portfolios creates the critical mass needed to negotiate better terms and build a unified mobile ad stack that neither could achieve alone, potentially stabilizing cash flow.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves capital. Investing the $4.8B directly into mobile infrastructure or acquiring a high-growth tech firm offers higher upside with lower integration drag. Buying a "declining" asset to fix "decline" is a negative momentum strategy.
2.  **Fit:** The carrier has zero history in consumer media. Integrating one legacy culture is hard; integrating two failing ones with repeated management turnover is a distractor from the core wireless business.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 24 months. Cause: The combined ad revenue continues to fall faster than cost synergies can be extracted. The $4.8B purchase price leaves no margin for error if the "decline" accelerates post-merger.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits traffic and revenue are falling *years* prior. There is no evidence provided that the carrier possesses the specific product DNA to reverse this trend, only an assumption that "scale" equals "competitiveness" against dominant platforms.
5.  **Blocker:** The core claim relies on building a business to compete with dominant platforms using assets explicitly described as "declining" and "falling." Without a proven mechanism to reverse the revenue trend (which the target's own history shows it cannot do), the math does not work. Scale amplifies a broken model; it does not fix it. The lack of carrier experience in this sector turns integration from a risk into a near-certain failure mode.

**Kill Criteria:** If the combined entity cannot show flat ad-revenue growth within two quarters of close, the thesis is broken. However, given the trajectory, the probability of hitting this is low based on current facts.

**Decision:** The proposal fails the blocker test. It spends significant capital on negative momentum without a verified engine for reversal.

DECISION: NO-GO
