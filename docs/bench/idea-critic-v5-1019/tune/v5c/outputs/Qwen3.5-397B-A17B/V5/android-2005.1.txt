First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a search-ad company with no hardware experience to spend at least $50 million on a six-person team building an OS for a market controlled by carriers and entrenched vendors, with no revenue model and a multi-year payback.

Steelman: Acquiring this team is the only way to secure a free, open mobile platform that will expand mobile web usage, thereby protecting and growing the core search-ad business against future platform lock-out.

Options:
1. **Acquire (Proposed):** High cost ($50M+), extreme risk (adoption, carrier resistance), low reversibility.
2. **Do Nothing:** Zero cost, risk of future platform dependency, but preserves capital.
3. **Partner/Invest:** Lower cost, limited control, tests adoption without full acquisition risk.
The proposal fails to demonstrate why acquisition is superior to partnership or waiting, given the lack of traction.

Fit: While expanding mobile web access aligns with the north star of increasing search volume, the method (building an OS) diverges wildly from the company's core competency (software/services) and current stage (search monopoly, not hardware vendor).

Pre-mortem: The project fails because handset makers refuse to adopt a new, unproven OS over established players, carriers block non-compliant devices, and the company burns $50M+ over five years with zero market share, distracting leadership from the core ad business. This is a one-way door with a high cost of delay only if competitors are already solving this (which is unproven).

Evidence: The proposal admits there is "no revenue model," "no hardware business," and "carriers control distribution." The $50M price tag for six people is unsupported by any current revenue or user metric.

Blockers:
1. **Competency Mismatch:** The company has no hardware business; building an OS requires capabilities it does not possess.
2. **Distribution Reality:** Carriers and handset makers have "no reason to adopt" a new platform, making the core bet (adoption) speculative without a guaranteed distribution channel.
3. **Economic Disconnect:** $50M for six people with no revenue model contradicts basic valuation logic for a pre-product entity in a hostile market.

Risks: Multi-year payback, open-source monetization failure, platform fragmentation.

The risks are not merely uncertainties; the lack of a distribution path and hardware expertise constitutes a fundamental flaw in the plan as stated. The bet relies on changing the behavior of entrenched gatekeepers (carriers/OEMs) with no leverage other than "free software," which has not worked elsewhere in this context.

DECISION: NO-GO
