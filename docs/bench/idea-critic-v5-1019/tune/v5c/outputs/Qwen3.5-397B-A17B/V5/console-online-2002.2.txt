First call: NO-GO. The proposal mandates broadband and a paid subscription in 2002, directly contradicting the stated market reality where few homes have broadband and players expect free online play.

Steelman: A unified, premium online service could create a sticky ecosystem and superior user experience if the market were ready for paid, broadband-only gaming.

However, the attack on this steelman reveals fatal blockers based strictly on the provided facts:
1.  **Infrastructure Mismatch:** The plan *requires* broadband, yet the facts state "few homes have broadband." This immediately caps the addressable market to a tiny fraction of console owners, making the business model untenable at launch.
2.  **Pricing Contradiction:** The proposal charges an annual subscription, but the facts state "players expect online play to be free" and rivals offer it for free or via publishers. There is no evidence provided that this specific console's users will break this expectation.
3.  **Operational Void:** The company has "never run" hosting or support operations. Building this from scratch while simultaneously trying to convert a skeptical, unconnected user base introduces extreme execution risk with no proven capability.

Alternatives:
*   **Do Nothing/Status Quo:** Allow publishers to handle online play (as rivals do). Cost: Low. Risk: Fragmented experience. Reversibility: High.
*   **Hybrid Approach:** Launch a free, basic service to build the user base and operational muscle, charging only for premium features later. Cost: Medium. Risk: Lower adoption friction. Reversibility: High.

The proposal fails the "fit" test against the current market constraints. It attempts to force a high-friction, high-cost solution onto a low-readiness market. The lack of broadband penetration is not a risk to be managed; it is a hard ceiling that prevents the core value proposition from functioning for the majority of users. The operational inexperience compounds this, suggesting the company cannot deliver the promised quality even if users signed up.

Blockers:
1.  Market infrastructure (broadband) is insufficient to support the mandatory requirement.
2.  User pricing expectations directly conflict with the mandatory subscription model.
3.  Zero internal experience in required operational domains (hosting/support).

Risks:
1.  Low adoption due to cost and connectivity.
2.  Service instability due to operational inexperience.

DECISION: NO-GO
