First call: GO. The pivot to fashion transforms a commodity liability (plastic) into a strategic asset (affordable style), directly addressing the reputation risk by redefining the product category rather than diluting the core brand.

Steelman: Launching twelve distinct fashion models allows rapid market testing of aesthetic preferences, turning the risk of changing tastes into a portfolio advantage where multiple bets increase the probability of a hit.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Cedes the low-cost segment to competitors and ignores the potential for volume-driven manufacturing efficiency.
2. Traditional launch: Marketing as "cheap timekeepers" invites direct comparison on durability, guaranteeing failure against established metal-watch incumbents.
3. Limited pilot: Reduces upfront risk but fails to achieve the scale necessary to validate the "fashion item" thesis or meet the one-million-unit economic model.

Fit: This aligns with a volume-based growth strategy, assuming the market accepts plastic as a deliberate fashion choice rather than a quality compromise.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the twelve models look too similar, failing to trigger distinct fashion impulses, or if distribution channels refuse to stock "toy" watches alongside luxury goods. This is a two-way door; unsold inventory can be discounted or discontinued with minimal long-term brand damage compared to a failed luxury extension.

Evidence: The proposal's own facts support the go decision. The "inexpensive" nature is a feature for fashion turnover, not a bug. The aggressive target is justified only by the fashion angle; plain timekeepers could never sustain such volume at low margins without established utility dominance.

Blockers: NONE. The stated risks (reputation, taste, competition) are inherent to the fashion strategy but are mitigated by the low unit cost and the diversity of the twelve-model collection. No fact presented proves the target users reject plastic fashion items.

Risks: Brand dilution if the fashion line visually conflicts with core luxury lines; missing the sales target due to misjudged trends. These become kill criteria: if first-quarter sell-through is below 15% of target, halt production on underperforming models.

Kill Criteria: Stop expansion if any single model fails to achieve 5% of total sales volume within six months, indicating a misread of fashion trends. Leading metric: Weekly sell-through rate per model across diverse retail partners.

DECISION: GO
