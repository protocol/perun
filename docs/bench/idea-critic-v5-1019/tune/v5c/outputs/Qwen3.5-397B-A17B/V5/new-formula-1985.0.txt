First call: NO-GO. The plan trades a functional product for an emotional asset based on incomplete data.

**Steelman:** Replacing the formula maximizes immediate taste preference scores, theoretically halting share loss to sweeter rivals.

**Attack:** The testing methodology is fatally flawed. Participants evaluated taste in isolation, unaware the original would vanish. This measures sensory preference, not purchase intent under loss aversion. The proposal ignores the "endowment effect": consumers value the original higher once told it will be removed. Blind tests cannot capture grief, nostalgia, or brand identity, which drive cola loyalty more than sweetness.

**Alternatives:**
1. **Do Nothing:** Retain current share trajectory; safe but passive.
2. **Line Extension:** Launch the sweeter formula as "New Cola" alongside the original. Captures new drinkers without alienating loyalists. Reversible.
3. **Proposed Plan:** Total replacement. Irreversible without massive backlash.

**Fit:** A century-old flagship is not just a SKU; it is cultural infrastructure. Removing it entirely contradicts the brand's heritage pillar.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume launch fails catastrophically. Why? Because the company told loyalists their favorite drink is dead. Protests, hoarding, and boycotts erupt not because the new drink tastes bad, but because the old one is gone. The silence in the focus groups regarding "withdrawal" is the smoking gun.

**Evidence & Blockers:**
*   **Blocker:** The testing explicitly withheld the consequence of removal. Therefore, the data proving the new formula "beats" the old is invalid for a replacement decision. It only proves it beats the old in a vacuum.
*   **Risk:** Emotional attachment is listed as a risk but treated as a manageable variable rather than a potential dealbreaker.
*   **Kill Criterion:** If the plan does not include a fallback or parallel sales strategy, it assumes zero elasticity in consumer emotion, which is factually unsupported.

**Decision:** The absence of a "withdrawal" variable in testing invalidates the core claim that the new formula is superior in the market context. The plan creates a unilateral conflict with the most profitable customer segment (loyalists) without a safety net. A line extension achieves the taste goal without the existential risk.

DECISION: NO-GO
