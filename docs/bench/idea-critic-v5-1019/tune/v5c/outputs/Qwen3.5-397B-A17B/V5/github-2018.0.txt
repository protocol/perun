First call: NO-GO. Buying a loss-making, leaderless asset hated by its users for $7.5B during an antitrust-sensitive era is financially reckless and strategically dangerous.

**Steelman:** The acquisition instantly neutralizes the biggest competitive threat to the buyer's cloud ecosystem and captures the global developer graph, provided the community can be won over through autonomy.

**Attack:**
1.  **Financials:** Paying a 2018 premium for a company without a CEO and negative earnings assumes the buyer can fix operations faster than the community defects. There is no evidence the target's revenue justifies $7.5B without massive, unproven monetization shifts.
2.  **Community Risk:** Developer trust is the sole asset. A historically hostile buyer acquiring a distrusted target creates a "poison pill" scenario. Vocal alternatives (free competitors) lower the switching cost, making mass defection a high-probability event, not a risk.
3.  **Regulatory:** Antitrust scrutiny in 2018 is intensifying. Consolidating the dominant host under a massive software incumbent invites immediate regulatory blocks or costly divestitures, stalling integration.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" allows the target to potentially fail or sell cheaper later. Building a competing host is slower but avoids the toxicity premium.

**Pre-mortem:** The deal closes, key maintainers migrate to free alternatives en masse within six months citing loss of neutrality, the platform becomes a "corporate graveyard," and the $7.5B stock payment dilutes shareholders for zero growth.

**Blockers:**
*   **Valuation Mismatch:** No permanent CEO and ongoing losses make the $7.5B price indefensible based on current facts.
*   **Hostility Gap:** The proposal admits user distrust and historical hostility but offers no concrete mechanism (beyond "run independently") to prevent immediate value destruction via defection.
*   **Regulatory Exposure:** High antitrust sensitivity combined with market dominance creates a tangible block to deal completion or future operation.

**Risks:** Developer exodus, failure to monetize, regulatory breakup. These are not merely risks; the stated facts (distrust, no CEO, losses) elevate them to blockers because the proposal lacks a specific turnaround plan or price adjustment to account for them.

DECISION: NO-GO
