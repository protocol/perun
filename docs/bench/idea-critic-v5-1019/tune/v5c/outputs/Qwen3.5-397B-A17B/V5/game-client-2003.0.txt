First call: GO. The core failure modes (updates, cheating) are existential; the proposed cure directly addresses them despite friction.

Steelman: A mandatory client is the only viable mechanism to secure revenue and game integrity in a digital distribution era, accepting short-term retail and user friction to prevent long-term obsolescence.

Attack: The proposal assumes broadband adoption will outpace user churn caused by mandatory online requirements. In 2003, this is a sharp bet; dial-up users cannot download large patches or the client itself, effectively cutting off a segment of the current retail base. Retail partners may indeed retaliate by delisting boxed copies if the client undermines their value proposition. However, the "do nothing" alternative guarantees continued inability to patch or ban cheaters, leading to product degradation and lost sales from piracy. The risk of competitors copying is irrelevant if the first mover secures the ecosystem lock-in. The risk of early bugs is real but manageable via the very update mechanism the client provides.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Retain retail goodwill but lose control over product integrity and monetization. High risk of slow death by piracy and fragmentation.
2. Optional client: Fails to solve cheating or update consistency as users opt-out.
3. Proposed mandatory client: High initial friction, but secures the platform future.

Fit: Aligns with the north star of controlling the distribution channel and protecting IP.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if broadband adoption stalls longer than expected or if the initial client launch is so buggy it creates a permanent reputational scar. However, waiting for perfect conditions cedes the market.

Evidence: The proposal admits the struggles are current and unsolved. No data suggests a better alternative exists.

Blockers: NONE. The lack of universal broadband is a risk, not a blocker, as the market is shifting inevitably online. The retail backlash is a cost of transition, not a fatal flaw.
Risks: User churn due to friction; retail partner retaliation; initial technical instability.

Kill criteria: If active user count drops >20% within 6 months of launch due to client requirements, or if major retailers drop >50% of shelf space within 3 months, pivot to a hybrid model. Leading metric: Weekly active users on the client vs. total units sold.

DECISION: GO
