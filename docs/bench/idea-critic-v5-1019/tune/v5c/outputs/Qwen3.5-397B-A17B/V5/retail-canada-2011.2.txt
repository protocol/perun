First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to solve three unproven, high-complexity variables (cross-border logistics, new inventory systems, and foreign regulation) simultaneously with a massive capital commitment before validating unit economics in the new market.

**Steelman:** Rapid saturation prevents competitor counter-moves and achieves scale efficiencies immediately, leveraging the retailer's domestic brand loyalty to capture the neighboring market's price-sensitive segment.

**Options:**
1.  **Proposal:** 130–150 stores in 2 years. Cost: Extreme. Risk: Catastrophic if supply chain fails. Reversibility: Low (long leases, sunk infrastructure).
2.  **Do Nothing:** Maintain domestic dominance. Cost: Opportunity cost of growth. Risk: Competitor eventually enters domestic market. Reversibility: N/A.
3.  **Pilot (Better Way):** Open 5–10 stores using existing manual processes or third-party logistics to validate cross-border unit economics and regulatory hurdles. Cost: Low. Risk: Contained. Reversibility: High.

**Fit:** Expansion aligns with growth vision, but the "north star" of profitable growth is jeopardized by untested operational assumptions.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs because the new inventory system cannot handle bilingual labeling and cross-border tax nuances, causing stockouts or compliance fines. The supply chain bottlenecks prevent replenishment, leading to empty shelves while lease obligations bleed cash. The entrenched competitor lowers prices temporarily to starve the newcomer.

**Evidence & Blockers:** The proposal admits the supply chain and inventory systems are "not yet proven." Deploying an unproven core operating system across 150 sites simultaneously is a factual contradiction to operational stability. There is no evidence that domestic loyalty translates to the new market or that the price advantage survives new logistics costs.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Unproven Core Systems:** Relying on a new, unproven inventory system for immediate mass deployment creates a single point of failure for the entire expansion.
2.  **Simultaneous Complexity:** Learning regulation, logistics, and launching a new system concurrently violates the principle of isolating variables for risk management.

**Risks:** Price perception misalignment, competitor retaliation, lease liability.

**Kill Criteria:** If a 10-store pilot cannot achieve positive same-store sales growth within 12 months using temporary logistics, the expansion halts.

DECISION: NO-GO
