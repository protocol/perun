First call: GO. The proposal targets an unserved demographic (youth) with a portable, affordable music experience, leveraging existing parts to minimize R&D risk despite high initial tooling costs.

**Steelman:** A dedicated playback-only device creates a new "personal music" category for youth, trading recording utility for portability and price, effectively bypassing dealer skepticism by creating direct consumer demand.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Maintain status quo; cede potential portable market to future competitors.
2.  **Full-featured recorder:** Higher cost, bulkier, misses the "youth price" target.
3.  **Proposal (Playback only):** Cheapest path to portability; highest risk of dealer rejection but highest upside for market creation.

**Fit:** Assumes the strategic goal is market expansion among younger users who value mobility over recording.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if dealers refuse to stock it due to "no recording" bias, or if consumers reject headphones as antisocial. However, these are adoption hurdles, not fundamental flaws. The cost of delay is high; if competitors copy the parts, first-mover brand affinity becomes the only moat.

**Evidence:** Facts provided are internal constraints (existing parts, large run needed) and external skepticism (dealer/staff doubts). No data contradicts the existence of youth demand; the lack of research is a risk, not a disproof.

**Blocker Test:**
*   *Blockers:* NONE. The skepticism regarding recording functionality and social isolation represents market education challenges, not factual impossibilities. The requirement for a large production run is a financial constraint inherent to the pricing strategy, not a contradiction. The ability for competitors to copy parts is a standard hardware risk, mitigated by speed to market.
*   *Risks:* Dealer resistance, unproven social acceptance of headphones, copycats. These become kill criteria: if initial sell-through to early adopters fails within 6 months, or if copycats launch within 3 months with better distribution, the project pivots.

The proposal survives the attack because the objections are based on current market norms, which this product intends to disrupt. The "antisocial" risk is actually the core value proposition (privacy/personalization). The lack of recording is a feature, not a bug, for cost reduction.

DECISION: GO
