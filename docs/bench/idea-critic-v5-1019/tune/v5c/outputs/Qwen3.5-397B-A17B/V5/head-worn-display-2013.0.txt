First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to launch a high-friction, privacy-invasive hardware product as a mass-market everyday item without solving core utility or social acceptance barriers.

Steelman: An invitation-only launch at $1,500 allows the company to refine the product with early adopters before a general release, mitigating initial risks through iterative feedback.

However, the stated facts reveal fatal blockers. The proposal positions the device as an "everyday consumer product" for the "general public," yet explicitly acknowledges "no clear everyday use for most people." A product cannot succeed as an everyday essential if its primary value proposition is undefined for the target mass market. Furthermore, the $1,500 price point combined with "short battery life" and active "privacy objections" creates a compounding failure mode: the cost is prohibitive for a gadget with limited utility, while the camera feature invites social hostility that undermines the "everyday" wearing requirement.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids reputational damage from a failed public launch and preserves R&D for when utility is clear.
2. Enterprise-only pivot: Target specific industrial use cases where the camera adds verified value, bypassing consumer privacy and social acceptance hurdles.
3. Proposed plan: Fails because it bets on mass adoption despite admitted lack of use cases and known social friction.

Pre-mortem: The product launches, media highlights privacy violations and social awkwardness, early adopters return the device due to lack of daily utility, and the brand becomes synonymous with intrusive, useless tech. The "invitation-only" phase fails to solve the fundamental problem that the device solves no common problem for the general public.

Blockers:
1. The proposal admits "no clear everyday use for most people" while positioning it as an "everyday consumer product." This is a direct contradiction between the product's definition and its market fit.
2. "Privacy objections" and "social acceptance" issues are stated as current facts, not hypotheticals, for a camera-wearing device in public spaces.

Risks: High price, short battery life, and execution difficulty are secondary to the lack of product-market fit.

The plan relies on hope that utility will emerge post-launch, which contradicts the operator's duty to verify demand before scaling to a general public release.

DECISION: NO-GO
