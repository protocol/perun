First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks to launch and promote widely based on test markets that have not yet demonstrated consumer acceptance, while explicit risks confirm the core user experience (taste, lighting mechanism, complexity) is likely inferior to the incumbent product.

Steelman: This heated device offers a genuinely cleaner smoking experience with reduced smoke, potentially satisfying health-conscious smokers who refuse to quit, justifying the sunk development costs if adoption occurs.

However, the attack on this claim reveals fatal flaws in the current plan:
1.  **Product-Market Fit Failure:** The risks state smokers may dislike the taste and find the lighting/usage complicated. In 1988, convenience is the primary driver of tobacco adoption. A product that is harder to use and tastes worse than a standard cigarette cannot sustain wide promotion unless the health benefit is proven and marketed as such.
2.  **Regulatory Blocker:** The proposal intends to promote it as a "cleaner way to smoke." Regulators are flagged as likely to object to health claims. Without the ability to make the only compelling claim (cleaner/safer), the product loses its differentiator against ordinary cigarettes, leaving only a worse-tasting, harder-to-use alternative.
3.  **Sunk Cost Fallacy:** The "very large sum" already spent is irrelevant to the decision to launch. Launching a product that fails due to taste and complexity compounds the loss with marketing spend and reputational damage.

The plan fails the "beat do nothing" test. Doing nothing preserves capital. Launching now, without resolving the taste/usability defects or securing a regulatory pathway for health claims, guarantees a failed rollout. The "test markets" mentioned are insufficient if the known risks (taste, complexity) remain unaddressed before a wide launch. A cheaper, safer alternative is to pause the wide launch, fix the usability/taste defects, and resolve the regulatory stance on claims before committing to promotion.

Blockers:
- Core value proposition (cleaner) cannot be advertised due to likely regulatory objection.
- User experience (taste, lighting, complexity) is explicitly noted as potentially inferior to current options, destroying retention.

Risks:
- Smoker rejection of the new ritual.
- Wasted marketing spend on a defective product.

DECISION: NO-GO
