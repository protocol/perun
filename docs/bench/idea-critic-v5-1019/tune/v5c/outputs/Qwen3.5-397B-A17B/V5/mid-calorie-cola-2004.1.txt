First call: NO-GO. The proposal targets a narrow demographic with a compromised product at a premium price during a volatile trend, facing direct competition and inherent consumer confusion.

**Steelman:** A mid-calorie option captures young men wanting taste without full sugar guilt, leveraging a premium brand to monetize the low-carb trend before it fades.

**Attack:**
1.  **Value Proposition Failure:** The core risk is fatal. Dieters choose zero-calorie; taste-seekers choose full-sugar. A "half-way" drink satisfies neither, creating a product with no loyal constituency.
2.  **Pricing Contradiction:** Charging a premium for *less* ingredient (sugar) while offering inferior taste to full-sugar and inferior dieting to zero-sugar is economically illogical. It actively deters the trial needed to overcome the "satisfies neither" perception.
3.  **Trend Volatility:** Betting a major campaign on the "low-carb" trend's longevity is speculative. If the trend is a fad (as noted), the product becomes obsolete instantly, leaving high fixed costs.
4.  **Competitive Saturation:** With a rival launching a similar drink, the market splits further. Without a distinct advantage, this becomes a price war the premium model cannot win.
5.  **Demographic Narrowness:** Limiting the initial aim to "young men" ignores the broader demographic driving low-carb diets, unnecessarily capping the potential user base for a niche product.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Protect margins on existing full-sugar and diet lines; avoid cannibalization.
*   **Reposition:** Launch as a value-priced trial to encourage switching, not a premium niche.
*   **Wait:** Let the rival test the market; enter only if the "mid-calorie" segment proves viable.

**Pre-mortem:** The campaign launches, but sales stall immediately. Consumers try once, find the taste unsatisfying compared to regular cola and the diet benefit insufficient compared to Diet Coke. The premium price creates resentment. The low-carb trend peaks and declines within 18 months. The product is discontinued with significant write-offs.

**Blockers:**
*   **Contradictory Economics:** Premium pricing for a compromised value proposition (less sugar, potentially worse taste) lacks a rational consumer incentive.
*   **Undefined Market Fit:** The product explicitly risks satisfying neither existing segment, with no evidence provided that a distinct "mid-calorie" segment exists or will pay more.

**Risks:** Trend expiration, competitor dominance, brand dilution. These are secondary to the fundamental lack of product-market fit.

DECISION: NO-GO
