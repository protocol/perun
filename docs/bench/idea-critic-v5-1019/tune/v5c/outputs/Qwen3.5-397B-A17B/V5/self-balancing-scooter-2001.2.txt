First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to high-volume production and a premium price point despite explicit, unproven demand and unresolved legal barriers.

**Steelman:** The device is a revolutionary urban transport solution that will create its own market category, justifying early scale to meet inevitable demand from early adopters and institutional buyers.

**Attack:**
1.  **Legal Blocker:** The proposal admits legal status on sidewalks and roads is "unclear." Selling 40,000 units of a vehicle that may be illegal to operate in major markets is a catastrophic liability and inventory risk. Regulatory approval must precede mass production.
2.  **Demand Mismatch:** At $5,000 (a significant premium for 2001), the device targets a niche. The proposal explicitly states uses beyond institutional buyers are "unproven" and riders may feel "conspicuous." Scaling factory capacity to 40,000 units assumes mass adoption before validating consumer comfort or utility.
3.  **Better Way:** A "do nothing" or "pilot" approach is superior. Produce a limited batch (e.g., 500 units) for institutional trials and regulatory testing. This validates the legal framework, refines the use case, and proves consumer acceptance without the sunk cost of a full-scale factory.
4.  **Fit:** The strategy ignores the "north star" of sustainable urban mobility if the product cannot legally enter the urban environment.

**Pre-mortem:** One year post-launch, the company holds 30,000 unsold units. Cities have banned the device from sidewalks due to safety concerns, and consumers refuse to pay $5,000 for a gadget they feel silly riding. The factory overhead bankrupts the venture.

**Blockers:**
*   **Legal Uncertainty:** The core utility is contingent on laws that do not yet exist or are undefined.
*   **Scale vs. Evidence:** Factory capacity (40k) vastly exceeds proven demand (institutional only).

**Risks:** High price sensitivity; social stigma of use; limited cargo utility.

**Decision Logic:** The proposal fails the "must beat do nothing" test. Building a large factory before resolving legal status and proving consumer demand is financially reckless. The plan requires a pilot phase and regulatory clarity before capital commitment.

DECISION: NO-GO
