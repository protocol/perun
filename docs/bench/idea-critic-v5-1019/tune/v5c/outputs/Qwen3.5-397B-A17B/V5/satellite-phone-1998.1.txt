First call: NO-GO. The unit economics and market trajectory described make commercial failure the base case, not a risk.

**Steelman:** This is the only viable solution for true global coverage where terrestrial networks cannot reach, capturing a high-margin niche of desperate international travelers.

**Attack:**
1.  **Substitution Threat:** Ground cellular is spreading fast. The value proposition relies on a coverage gap that is rapidly closing. A "several dollars per minute" cost structure cannot compete with roaming rates that are trending down as infrastructure expands.
2.  **Product-Market Fit:** At $3,000 per handset with bulky, indoor-incompatible hardware, the Total Addressable Market (TAM) is microscopic. International business travelers require reliability indoors (offices, hotels, airports), which this system explicitly fails to provide.
3.  **Financial Suicide:** With $5 billion in borrowed capital, debt service requires massive, immediate scale. The high price point and poor user experience prevent the necessary subscriber velocity. The sales channels are not ready, guaranteeing a slow start that the debt load cannot tolerate.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" regarding this specific launch avoids sinking further capital into a product likely to be obsolete before it achieves profitability. Waiting for handset miniaturization and better indoor penetration (or partnering with emerging GSM standards) is a safer, cheaper alternative than launching a flawed v1.

**Pre-mortem:** Two years post-launch, the company defaults. Why? Subscriber growth missed targets by 80% because the phones were too expensive and didn't work inside the very buildings where business travelers conduct deals. Competitors offered "good enough" roaming at 1/10th the cost.

**Blockers:**
*   **Unit Economics:** The combination of high debt service, low projected volume (due to price), and high churn risk (due to poor indoor performance) creates a negative cash flow loop that cannot be breached.
*   **Functional Deficit:** A mobile phone that fails indoors contradicts the core use case of the target demographic.

**Risks:** Market adoption speed, regulatory hurdles, technical reliability (all secondary to the fundamental economic mismatch).

The proposal asks to light money on fire based on a technology that is already inferior to the rapidly improving status quo, sold at a price the market will reject, funded by debt that demands impossible growth.

DECISION: NO-GO
