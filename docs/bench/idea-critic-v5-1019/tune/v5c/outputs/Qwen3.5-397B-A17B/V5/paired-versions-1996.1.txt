First call: GO. The mechanic directly solves the core engagement problem (completion) by manufacturing social necessity, turning a single-player limitation into a network effect.

**Steelman:** Releasing two distinct versions forces interdependence, making the link cable mandatory rather than optional, thereby driving double sales per social circle and creating viral word-of-mouth that marketing cannot buy.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Single version relies on traditional marketing against a declining install base. Likely failure due to low visibility.
2.  **Price Grab Perception:** Valid risk. Mitigation: Position as "companion" editions, not sequels. The value prop is the *trade*, not the duplication.
3.  **Hardware Friction:** Requires a second player and cable. This is not a bug; it is the feature. It creates high-friction, high-reward social interactions that digital downloads cannot replicate.
4.  **Publisher Hesitation:** The "declining platform" argument is inverted. A declining base needs a shockwave to reactivate dormant users. This mechanic is that shockwave.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if version differences are trivial (cosmetic only) or if the trade mechanic is buggy. If players can complete the game alone easily, the dual-version strategy collapses into pure greed.

**Fit:** Aligns with the "collect them all" vision by making it physically impossible without cooperation.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal's stated risks (popularity, price perception, hardware requirements) are inherent to the strategy's success, not contradictions of it. The "price grab" concern is neutralized if the creature exclusivity is compelling enough to warrant the second purchase. The publisher's uncertainty is addressed by the low marginal cost of duplicating assets for a second SKU versus the high potential upside of doubled unit sales per customer cluster.

**Kill Criteria:** If playtesting shows <20% of users attempt a trade within the first 3 hours, the mechanic is too opaque. If version differences are found to be purely cosmetic without gameplay impact, the value proposition fails.

**Decision:** The risk of cannibalization is lower than the risk of obscurity. The forced social interaction is the only viable path to breakout success on aging hardware.

DECISION: GO
