First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits $36 billion to acquire a network explicitly described as incompatible and data-limited, requiring the buyer to run legacy equipment while absorbing massive goodwill risk.

**Steelman:** The merger secures a sticky, high-value business niche (push-to-talk) that the buyer cannot currently serve, justifying the $36B price and integration pain as the cost of immediate market dominance in the enterprise sector.

**Attack:**
1.  **Technological Suicide:** In 2005, the industry trajectory is unequivocally toward high-speed data (3G/4G evolution). Acquiring a "data-limited" network forces the combined entity to maintain two distinct, incompatible infrastructures. This doubles operational complexity and CapEx while the acquired asset depreciates rapidly.
2.  **Financial Hazard:** The "large goodwill" implies the purchase price far exceeds the tangible value of the incompatible assets. If the integration fails or the legacy technology becomes obsolete faster than anticipated (a near certainty), the company faces a massive write-down, destroying shareholder value.
3.  **Better Way:** Building or licensing a push-to-talk application over the buyer's existing modern network is cheaper and future-proof. Buying the whole network for one feature is inefficient.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** Integration stalls due to technical friction; the business customers churn because the legacy network performance degrades during the transition; the stock collapses under the weight of dual-network maintenance costs and a subsequent goodwill impairment charge.

**Blockers:**
*   **Incompatible Core:** The target's technology is fundamentally misaligned with the future of wireless (data). Integrating it drags down the acquirer's agility.
*   **Asset Quality:** Paying a premium ($36B) for "limited" technology and "legacy equipment" violates basic acquisition hygiene unless the customer list can be migrated instantly without the hardware—which the proposal admits is not the case.

**Risks:** Integration cost overruns, customer churn during migration, regulatory scrutiny on market consolidation.

**Kill Criteria:** Proceed only if an independent audit confirms the target's customers can be migrated to the buyer's native network within 12 months without hardware replacement. Without this, the deal purchases obsolescence.

DECISION: NO-GO
