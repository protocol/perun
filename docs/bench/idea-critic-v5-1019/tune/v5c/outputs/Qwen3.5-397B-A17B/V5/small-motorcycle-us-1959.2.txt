First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on unverified assumptions about US consumer preference for small bikes and ignores critical mechanical limitations.

**Steelman:** A small, accessible motorcycle can create a new commuter segment in the US by bypassing traditional dealership channels, provided the product functions reliably on US infrastructure.

**Attack:**
1.  **Product-Market Fit:** The claim that Americans will buy 50cc bikes contradicts the stated fact that the US market favors "large machines." Without evidence of latent demand for commuters, this is a guess.
2.  **Mechanical Blocker:** The proposal admits larger models fail at US highway speeds. While the 50cc model avoids highways, US roads often require sustained higher speeds than typical Japanese urban traffic. If the 50cc engine cannot maintain safe speeds on US arterials or freeways (common in car-centric 1959 America), the product is unsafe and unsellable.
3.  **Channel Risk:** Selling via sporting-goods stores bypasses motorcycle expertise. If the bikes require specific maintenance or assembly, non-specialist retailers will fail to support them, leading to immediate brand damage.
4.  **Financial Exposure:** Tying up scarce foreign currency on an unproven hypothesis with a known product limitation (speed) creates an asymmetric downside.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do nothing:* Preserve capital; wait for market data.
*   *Pilot only:* Ship a tiny batch to test speed durability and retail reception before committing to a subsidiary.
*   *Licensing:* Partner with an existing US manufacturer to avoid currency risk and infrastructure setup.

**Pre-mortem:** The subsidiary opens, inventory sits unsold because the bikes are too slow for US roads, retailers refuse to service them, and the parent company drains its cash reserves trying to subsidize a failed education campaign.

**Blockers:**
*   **Mechanical Viability:** No evidence exists that the 50cc engine can safely sustain speeds required on US roads outside dense city centers.
*   **Demand Validation:** Zero data supports the assertion that US commuters want step-through motorcycles over cars or large bikes.

**Risks:** Brand unknown, channel mismatch, currency drain. These become kill criteria if a pilot is run, but currently, the lack of proof on speed and demand prevents approval.

**Kill Criteria:** If a limited test shows average travel speeds exceed the bike's safe capacity, or if sporting-goods stores cannot move 10 units in 30 days, stop immediately.

DECISION: NO-GO
