First call: GO. The shift in buyer demographics toward American-brand customers seeking smaller sedans indicates an unmet demand within the existing luxury segment that competitors are not addressing with compact offerings.

Steelman: Leveraging a proven mass-market platform allows rapid entry into the compact luxury segment with controlled costs, enabling a premium price point driven by exclusive trim and features rather than expensive bespoke engineering, thus capturing a new customer cohort without cannibalizing the core large-sedan base.

Options analysis:
1.  **Do nothing:** Cede the emerging compact luxury segment to rivals and lose customers who want the brand but not the size.
2.  **Build new platform:** Prohibitive cost and time-to-market; unnecessary risk when a suitable base exists.
3.  **Proposed shared platform:** Balances speed, cost, and brand differentiation through styling and features. It beats "do nothing" by capturing revenue and beats "new platform" by preserving margin.

Fit: Aligns with the north star of expanding the buyer base while maintaining luxury positioning through perceived value (trim/features) rather than underlying mechanical exclusivity.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the visual and tactile differentiation is insufficient, causing buyers to perceive it as a "badged economy car" rather than a true luxury vehicle, or if sales channels refuse to support the sub-brand identity. This is a two-way door; the model can be discontinued if prestige erosion becomes measurable.

Evidence: The proposal cites a verified trend: buyers are already shifting from American brands to this luxury maker but desire smaller sedans. The shared platform is a fact of existing corporate assets.

Blockers: NONE. The risks listed (platform visibility, price justification, salesperson behavior, prestige dilution) are execution challenges, not factual impossibilities. They are mitigated by the "added trim and features" strategy and distinct model naming. No evidence suggests the target demographic cares about platform sharing if the luxury experience is delivered.

Risks: Brand dilution and channel resistance. These become kill criteria: monitor brand sentiment scores and salesperson adherence to naming conventions.

Kill criteria: If within 12 months, brand prestige metrics drop by >5% or sales mix shows >20% cannibalization of higher-margin large sedans without net new customer acquisition, terminate the model line.

DECISION: GO
