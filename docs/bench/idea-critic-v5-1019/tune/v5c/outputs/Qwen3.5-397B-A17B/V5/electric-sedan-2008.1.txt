First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a capital-intensive path while explicitly admitting the company lacks the necessary funds for the core requirements (factory and batteries).

Steelman: Becoming a pure-play EV manufacturer by acquiring an existing large factory is the fastest route to scale, provided the capital gap can be bridged immediately.

Options:
1. **Proposal**: Buy large factory, produce only EVs. Cost: Prohibitive (admitted gap). Risk: Extreme (insolvency before production). Reversibility: Low (asset lock-in).
2. **Contract Manufacturing**: Partner with an established maker to build the initial volume. Cost: Higher unit cost, lower CapEx. Risk: Margin pressure. Reversibility: High.
3. **Do Nothing/Wait**: Retain cash. Cost: Opportunity loss. Risk: Obsolescence. Reversibility: N/A.

Fit: The strategy (pure EV) fits a visionary north star, but the tactic (buying a large factory now) contradicts the financial reality of a "young carmaker."

Pre-mortem: The company exhausts its limited capital purchasing and retrofitting the factory before resolving battery supply or charging infrastructure, leading to bankruptcy without a single unit sold. This is a one-way door; once the factory is bought, liquidity is gone. The cost of delay is lower than the cost of premature asset acquisition.

Evidence: The proposal itself states the company "has little experience" and needs "far more capital than the company has." These are internal contradictions to the execution plan.

Blockers:
1. **Capital Mismatch**: The plan requires buying a factory and securing battery packs, but the proposal admits the company lacks this capital. You cannot commit to a purchase you cannot fund.
2. **Experience Gap**: Mass production in a newly acquired large factory requires expertise the company explicitly lacks, increasing the risk of costly failures during the critical ramp-up phase.

Risks: Charging scarcity and competitor response are market risks, but the financial and operational blockers are fatal as proposed.

Kill Criteria: If the company cannot secure funding equal to the factory purchase price plus 18 months of operating capital before signing the acquisition, the plan must stop.

DECISION: NO-GO
