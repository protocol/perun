First call: NO-GO. The plan ignores the core behavior of the existing customer base without validation.

**Steelman:** Rebranding to everyday low pricing and branded shops will attract new, higher-margin shoppers faster than it alienates coupon-dependent legacy customers, replicating a successful electronics model.

**Attack:**
1.  **Customer Mismatch:** The proposal admits most sales come from discounted items and customers are "trained to wait." Abruptly removing coupons removes the primary purchase trigger for the entire revenue base. The electronics success relied on different buyer psychology (tech novelty vs. commodity apparel/home goods).
2.  **Zero Pilot:** Announcing a chain-wide change without a test in a few stores creates an irreversible binary outcome. If the hypothesis fails, the chain collapses before a pivot is possible.
3.  **Pace:** Changing pricing and physical layout simultaneously maximizes operational risk and customer confusion.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Continued sales erosion, but preserves current cash flow while a strategy is formed.
*   *Hybrid/Pilot:* Test everyday pricing in 5-10 stores while maintaining coupons elsewhere; phase in shop-in-shop concepts gradually. This isolates risk and validates the "new customer" acquisition claim before burning capital.
*   *Cheaper Path:* Retain coupons but reduce frequency/depth slightly to test price elasticity before eliminating them.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, legacy customers stop visiting due to perceived "higher prices" (loss of coupon thrill), new customers do not arrive in sufficient volume to offset the loss, and the chain faces a liquidity crisis.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** Most sales historically come from discounted items.
*   **Contradiction:** The plan eliminates discounts immediately without evidence that the target demographic (new shoppers) exists in sufficient density or will switch from competitors.
*   **Missing Evidence:** No data supports the transferability of the electronics model to department store apparel/home goods.

**Risks:** Brand alienation, inventory obsolescence, cash flow collapse. These are not mere risks; given the lack of pilot, they are probable outcomes based on stated facts.

**Kill Criteria:** If a pilot (which is missing) showed <5% drop in transaction count among legacy users while attracting new segments, proceed. Without this, the plan fails the basic test of preserving the current revenue engine while building a new one.

DECISION: NO-GO
