First call: NO-GO. The plan ignores the core behavior of the existing customer base without validation.

**Steelman:** Eliminating coupons and rebuilding stores will attract new, higher-value shoppers who prefer simplicity, offsetting the loss of discount-driven traffic before cash reserves run out.

**Attack:**
1.  **Customer Mismatch:** The proposal admits most sales come from discounted items and customers are "trained to wait." Abruptly removing this incentive treats loyal customers as liabilities rather than assets to be migrated. There is no evidence the target "new" shopper exists in sufficient density at these specific locations to replace the immediate revenue cliff.
2.  **Execution Risk:** Rebuilding the entire chain simultaneously into branded shops requires massive capital expenditure while sales are already falling. If the new model fails to gain traction immediately, the chain lacks the liquidity to revert or pivot, as all stores are committed to the new format.
3.  **Missing Pilot:** Announcing a total systemic shift without a test in a few stores removes the ability to measure price elasticity or shop-in-shop viability. The "pace" objection is critical; a chain-wide rollout assumes the electronics CEO's previous success is directly transferable to mid-market apparel/home goods without local validation.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Continued erosion, but preserves cash for a measured transition.
*   *Hybrid/Pilot:* Test everyday pricing in 5-10 stores while maintaining coupons elsewhere; phase in shop-in-shops only where metrics improve. This retains the upside potential while capping the downside.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, same-store sales drop 25% as coupon shoppers leave, new shoppers do not materialize in time, and the company runs out of cash mid-renovation, forcing bankruptcy or a fire sale.

**Blocker:** The plan relies on an unproven assumption that the customer base can be swapped instantly without a transitional mechanism, despite explicit evidence that current sales depend on the very behavior being eliminated. The lack of a pilot makes this a binary bet on an unverified hypothesis during a liquidity crunch.

**Kill Criteria:** Not applicable as the plan should not start. A viable plan would require a pilot showing neutral or positive comp sales in test stores before chain-wide commitment.

DECISION: NO-GO
