First call: NO-GO. Buying a shrinking handset maker to solve an app-ecosystem deficit contradicts the stated facts: sales are falling, and the rival already dominates developer mindshare.

Steelman: Vertical integration is the only remaining lever to guarantee a reference device exists, preventing the manufacturer's immediate defection to the rival platform and buying time to court developers.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: High risk of manufacturer defection, likely collapsing the OS share entirely.
2. Subsidize the partner: Cheaper than €5.4B, but lacks control; the partner may still defect if the rival's pressure mounts.
3. Acquire: Highest cost, highest integration risk, but secures the hardware channel.

Fit: Assumes controlling hardware directly influences developer adoption. However, the proposal admits the core problem is the rival's app dominance, not hardware availability. Developers follow users; if sales are falling, owning the factory does not reverse the trend.

Pre-mortem: The acquisition closes, but integration chaos distracts leadership. Sales continue to fall due to the app gap. The €5.4B becomes a sunk cost in a dying asset, while competitors mock the "integrated failure." The market views this as desperation, not strength.

Evidence & Calculation: The €5.4B price tag for a division with falling sales suggests a "falling knife" investment. The proposal states the rival has "most of the apps." History shows hardware ownership does not force developer support if the user base is shrinking (e.g., later industry failures). The causal link between "owning the handset maker" and "fixing the app ecosystem" is broken; the former cannot manufacture the latter without a growing user base, which the target currently lacks.

Blockers:
1. **Causal Mismatch**: The plan claims to fix an "app-ecosystem problem" via hardware ownership. The stated facts confirm sales are falling and the rival has the apps. Owning a shrinking distribution channel does not solve the supply-side (app) deficit; it merely monetizes the decline.
2. **Economic Logic**: Paying a premium (€5.4B) for a depreciating asset (falling sales) to solve a problem (apps) that the asset cannot fix is a fundamental strategic error.

Risks: Integration friction, competitor retaliation, continued developer apathy.

Kill Criteria: If smartphone sales do not stabilize within 12 months post-acquisition, the thesis is dead.

DECISION: NO-GO
