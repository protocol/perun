First call: GO. The friction of per-order fees directly suppresses repeat purchase frequency, and a fixed fee aligns retailer revenue with customer usage, unlocking volume that marginal shipping costs cannot offset if logistics are efficient.

**Steelman:** An annual prepaid fee eliminates transactional friction, locking in loyalty and increasing order frequency such that the lifetime value (LTV) gain and operational density outweigh the immediate margin dilution from heavy users.

**Attack & Options:**
The primary risk is adverse selection: only heavy buyers join, bleeding margin. However, "do nothing" guarantees continued friction and lost share to any competitor who solves this. A "pay-per-use" discount fails to remove the psychological tax of every checkout. The proposed model bets on behavioral change: members order more to "earn back" the fee, driving volume that lowers per-unit fulfillment costs.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
Fits a growth-stage strategy prioritizing market share and retention over short-term margin. Failure mode: shipping costs rise unexpectedly or fulfillment breaks under increased volume. This is a one-way door for the cohort (hard to revoke mid-year) but reversible for future sales. Cost of delay is high; first-mover advantage in loyalty is significant.

**Evidence & Blockers:**
No internal data exists to verify exact elasticity, but the logic holds: removing a variable cost (shipping) encourages consumption of the core service (shopping). The $79 price point acts as a filter; if it's too low, losses mount; too high, adoption stalls. The proposal admits economics are unproven, which is a risk, not a blocker. The "unproven" nature is inherent to innovation; waiting for proof cedes the market.

**Sort:**
*Blockers:* NONE. The risks (margin hit, irreversibility) are features of the bet, not facts proving failure. The proposal acknowledges them.
*Risks:* Heavy buyer loss, fulfillment strain. These become kill criteria: monitor loss per member and fulfillment error rates monthly.

**Decision:**
The plan addresses the stated biggest friction. The risks are manageable via the inherent cap of the annual fee and operational scaling. Waiting for perfect data is the real failure.

DECISION: GO
