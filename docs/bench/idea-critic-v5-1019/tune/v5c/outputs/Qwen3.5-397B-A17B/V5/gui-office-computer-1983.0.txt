First call: NO-GO. The stated price is an order of magnitude higher than competitors while offering inferior speed and a barren software ecosystem.

Steelman: A premium, mouse-driven interface justifies a high price by radically improving office productivity despite hardware limitations.

Options:
1. **Proposed Plan:** High cost, low performance, low software support. High risk of immediate market rejection.
2. **Do Nothing:** Wait for hardware costs to drop and software libraries to mature. Zero capital risk.
3. **Internal Alternative:** The proposal admits a cheaper, similar machine is already in development internally. This renders the $10,000 unit redundant and uncompetitive within its own company.

Fit: The goal is to serve office workers. Current facts indicate these users are price-sensitive and require robust software, neither of which this machine provides.

Pre-mortem: The product launches and sits unsold because competitors offer faster machines at one-third the price with established software libraries. The internal cheaper model cannibalizes any niche interest. This is a one-way door into inventory write-offs.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits the price is "several times" higher, performance is "slow," and software is "little." These are fatal contradictions to a successful launch.

Blockers:
1. **Price/Value Mismatch:** $10,000 for a slow machine with no software is economically indefensible against existing alternatives.
2. **Internal Cannibalization:** A cheaper, similar internal project exists, making this specific configuration strategically unnecessary.
3. **Ecosystem Void:** Business users cannot adopt a computer without third-party software; "little software" is a hard stop for enterprise adoption.

Risks: Market education costs, hardware reliability, competitor response (all secondary to the blockers).

Kill Criteria: If the internal cheaper machine reaches parity in interface quality, this project must terminate immediately.

The presence of a superior internal alternative and the extreme price disadvantage relative to performance creates an insurmountable barrier. Launching now guarantees financial loss and brand confusion.

DECISION: NO-GO
