First call: NO-GO. The proposal repeats the pattern of a recent hardware failure while introducing severe privacy friction and unproven technology in a non-existent category.

Steelman: A cylindrical speaker at $199 ($99 for members) creates a new "always-listening" category, leveraging membership pricing to overcome privacy fears and using invitations to manage risk after a poor phone launch.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Contradictory Evidence:** The proposal admits "customers may not accept an always-on microphone" and "voice recognition often fails." These are not hypothetical risks but stated negative facts that directly remove the route to utility. If the core mechanism (listening) is rejected by users and the execution (recognition) is unreliable, the product cannot function as intended.
2.  **Category & Track Record:** There is "no product category like it," requiring massive education spend exactly when the company's "last hardware launch went badly." Capital efficiency demands fixing the core mobile business before funding a speculative, category-creating device.
3.  **Alternative Superiority:** Phone-based assistants already exist. They solve the same user need (questions, lists, music) without the privacy intrusion of a dedicated always-on home device or the cost of new hardware manufacturing. The proposal fails to demonstrate why a $199 cylinder is superior to a device users already own.

**Risks:**
*   Privacy backlash could permanently damage the brand.
*   High return rates due to voice failure.
*   Inventory write-offs if the invitation model fails to generate demand.

**Kill Check:** The proposal states voice recognition "often fails" and users "may not accept" the microphone. These are not future possibilities; they are present-tense negatives cited as facts. Since the plan offers no technical breakthrough to fix recognition or a proven method to override privacy aversion, these stated facts act as blockers, not mere risks. The alternative (improving the phone assistant) reaches the same upside with zero new hardware risk.

The plan commits real money to a device where the stated facts confirm the technology is immature and the market is hostile. A capped pilot is insufficient because the fundamental value proposition is contradicted by the proposal's own evidence.

DECISION: NO-GO
