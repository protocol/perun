First call: GO. The company already possesses the core competency in silicon design for its mobile devices, creating a strategic asymmetry against competitors reliant on generic suppliers.

Steelman: Leveraging proven mobile silicon architecture for laptops will unify the ecosystem, enabling superior performance-per-watt and deeper hardware-software integration that off-the-shelf suppliers cannot match, justifying the transition costs.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Maintains status quo but cedes long-term differentiation and margin control to the supplier.
2. Hybrid approach: Slowly introduce custom chips in lower-volume models first; reduces risk but delays ecosystem benefits and splits engineering focus.
3. Full switch (Proposal): Highest upfront cost and risk, but captures maximum value and strategic control.

Fit: Aligns with a vision of vertical integration and ecosystem lock-in. The mobile success proves the design approach works; scaling it to laptops is a logical extension, not a new gamble.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if legacy software compatibility breaks or performance gains are negligible compared to the supplier's roadmap. However, the company controls the OS, allowing for a coordinated software transition (e.g., emulation layers, developer tools) that mitigates the compatibility risk. The "unproven" nature is countered by the mobile track record.

Evidence: The proposal states the company *already* designs successful phone/tablet processors. This is the critical fact. The risks (software, cost, confusion) are execution challenges, not fundamental blockers. The cost of delay is losing the window to define the next decade of computing architecture.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited are manageable execution hurdles, not facts proving the plan fails. The company's existing design capability invalidates the "unproven" claim as a blocker.
Risks: Software transition friction, short-term buyer confusion, engineering resource strain. These become kill criteria: if developer adoption lags by X% after 12 months, or if performance benchmarks fail to exceed supplier offerings by Y%, the program pivots.

The proposal leverages an existing, proven asset to solve a strategic dependency. The risks are real but do not outweigh the opportunity to own the entire stack.

DECISION: GO
