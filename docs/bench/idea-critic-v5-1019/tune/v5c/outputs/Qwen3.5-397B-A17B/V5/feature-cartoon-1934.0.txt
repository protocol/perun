First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits the studio's entire steady income to a single unproven format at 10x normal cost, violating basic capital preservation without evidence of demand.

**Steelman:** A feature film could unlock a new revenue tier and cement brand legacy, provided the market accepts long-form animation.

**Attack:**
1.  **Demand Assumption:** The claim assumes audiences will sit for 80 minutes of animation. History shows cartoons are viewed as novelty shorts; there is zero evidence of appetite for a feature. If the audience rejects the format, the studio loses its entire bankroll.
2.  **Financial Ruin:** Funding this from short-film earnings means one failure destroys the company. The "thin profits" cited cannot absorb a 10x loss. This is not a calculated risk; it is a gamble with existential stakes.
3.  **Expert Dissent:** The founder's family and industry insiders advise against it. While innovators sometimes ignore experts, doing so requires a counter-proof of concept (e.g., a test screening), which is absent.
4.  **Alternatives:** The "do nothing" option preserves the steady, albeit thin, profits. A "better way" would be a medium-length pilot or a serialized release to test endurance before committing the full budget. The proposal ignores these safer ramps.

**Blockers:**
*   **No Market Validation:** There is no data showing audiences want feature-length animation. The core revenue hypothesis is untested.
*   **Existential Financial Exposure:** The funding model (using all operating cash) creates a single point of failure that threatens the entire business.

**Risks:** Budget overruns and production delays are secondary to the primary risk of total market rejection.

**Conclusion:** The proposal fails the "survive the attack" test. It relies on an unproven assumption (audience endurance) to justify an existential financial risk. Without a pilot, a capped budget, or external financing to protect the core business, this plan is reckless. The absence of a "kill criterion" or a "stop-loss" mechanism in the proposal confirms it is not ready for approval.

DECISION: NO-GO
