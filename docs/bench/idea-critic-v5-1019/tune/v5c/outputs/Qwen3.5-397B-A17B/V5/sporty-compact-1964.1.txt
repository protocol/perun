First call: GO. The strategy targets a verified demographic shift (postwar boom entrants) with a capital-efficient execution model (reusing proven mechanicals) and a high-margin revenue structure (long options list).

Steelman: Launch a stylistically distinct, customizable sports coupe/convertible using existing compact underpinnings to capture emerging youth demand at minimal R&D risk, funded by aggressive marketing to create immediate category dominance.

Options:
1. **Proposal:** High upside, low technical risk, moderate financial risk (marketing spend). Reversible via production halt.
2. **Do nothing:** Cede the emerging youth segment to competitors; risk brand stagnation as current buyers age.
3. **All-new platform:** Maximizes differentiation but triples cost and time-to-market; likely prices out the target demographic.

Fit: Aligns with capturing market share during a demographic wave. Reusing parts lowers the break-even point, essential for an unproven segment.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the "cheap" parts perception overwhelms the styling, or if the ad spend fails to ignite cultural desire. However, the low base price mitigates the "cheap" risk by framing it as value, not deficiency. The door is two-way; production can be scaled down if initial sales miss, limiting downside to marketing sunk costs. The cost of delay is losing the first-mover advantage with this specific generation.

Evidence: The proposal relies on the stated demographic fact (young buyers entering market) and the mechanical reality (parts availability). No internal data contradicts the desire for sporty styling; the risk is volume, not existence.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (unproven demand, cheap perception, spend) are execution risks, not factual blockers. The parts-sharing risk is mitigated by the "sporty styling" differentiator. The spend is the mechanism to prove demand, not a barrier to trying.

Risks: Demand volume uncertainty; brand dilution if styling fails to mask economy roots. These become kill criteria: monitor sell-through rate vs. ad spend in Q1; if customization uptake is <20%, re-evaluate trim strategy.

The plan bets on style over engineering novelty, which is the correct allocation of capital for this demographic. The options list ensures margin protection despite the low entry price.

DECISION: GO
