First call: NO-GO. The proposal mandates a high-risk, irreversible brand change with zero validation and a communication strategy guaranteed to inflame the very risks identified.

**Steelman:** A simultaneous, unannounced rollout maximizes shock value to force immediate market repositioning from "mature" to "contemporary," betting that hesitation signals weakness.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves current revenue. A "staged pilot" (e.g., online only) tests reception with minimal downside. The proposal rejects these, demanding total exposure to untested risk.
2.  **Fit:** While the goal (contemporary look) aligns with vision, the method contradicts the core asset: trust. Loyal customers value consistency; shocking them without warning treats loyalty as a liability.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure. Why? Customers perceive the sudden change as a sale, bankruptcy, or hostile takeover. Social media amplifies confusion into outrage before the company can explain. The "no announcement" strategy removes the only mechanism to control the narrative.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits the design is untested and explicitly lists known risks (attachment, backlash, recognition) without mitigation. Proceeding despite confirmed negative indicators is negligence, not boldness.
5.  **Blocker:** The "no advance announcement" clause is a factual blocker. In a social media environment, silence during a radical change guarantees a vacuum filled by speculation and anger. Additionally, skipping customer testing on a core brand asset violates basic operational hygiene for a mature retailer.

**Kill Criteria Check:** The proposal offers no metric for success, no pilot phase, and no rollback plan. It relies entirely on the hope that the new design is inherently superior, an assumption contradicted by the stated risk of losing recognition.

**Decision Logic:**
*   **Blockers:** Unannounced rollout guarantees narrative loss; zero testing on a core asset; rejection of safer alternatives with equal upside potential.
*   **Risks:** Backlash, loss of loyal base, brand confusion. (These are elevated to blockers by the lack of mitigation).

The plan fails because it intentionally removes safety mechanisms (testing, communication) that cost nothing but prevent catastrophe. A "contemporary" brand is not built by alienating its existing base in a single day.

DECISION: NO-GO
