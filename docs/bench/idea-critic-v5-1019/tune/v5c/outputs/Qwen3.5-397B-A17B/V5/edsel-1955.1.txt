First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a massive, irreversible infrastructure build (new dealers, new brand) just as a recession risk emerges and the target segment shows signs of potential contraction.

**Steelman:** Capturing the growing mid-priced gap requires a distinct brand identity that low/premium lines cannot stretch to cover, justifying the dealer network investment to secure market share before competitors.

**Attack:**
1.  **Timing & Cyclicality:** Launching a discretionary, mid-priced product line immediately prior to a recession is historically catastrophic. Consumers trade down in downturns; a new, unproven brand lacks the loyalty to withstand this shift. The "growing segment" data is likely lagging and invalid if a recession hits in 1957.
2.  **Fixed Cost Trap:** Building a dedicated dealer network creates enormous fixed costs before a single unit is sold. If volume misses due to economic contraction, these costs become a bleed that cannot be quickly reversed, unlike using existing dealers.
3.  **Cannibalization:** Without precise pricing guards, the new line will likely eat sales from the company's own low-priced division during a downturn, diluting overall margin without gaining net new customers.
4.  **Alternatives:** A "badge-engineered" model sold through existing low-priced dealers would test the water with a fraction of the capital risk. Waiting for post-recession clarity preserves cash.

**Blockers:**
*   **Economic Mismatch:** The proposal acknowledges a recession risk yet proceeds with a high-fixed-cost launch timed for peak discretionary spending. This contradicts basic cyclical strategy.
*   **Irreversibility:** Once the dealer network contracts are signed and tooling cut, the company cannot pivot if the segment shrinks. The cost of delay (waiting 12-18 months) is lower than the cost of failure in a downturn.

**Risks:** Brand confusion, execution failure, marketing overspend. These are secondary to the structural blocker of launching a luxury-adjacent infrastructure into a contracting economy.

**Kill Criteria:** If consumer confidence indices drop or leading economic indicators signal contraction within 6 months, the program must halt before dealer signing.

The plan fails the "do nothing" or "wait" test because the downside of being wrong (bankruptcy-level losses from fixed costs) vastly outweighs the upside of being early.

DECISION: NO-GO
