First call: NO-GO. Raising prices 60% and immediately fracturing the user experience into two brands, two logins, and two bills creates a compound shock that ignores the stated risk of customer confusion.

**Steelman:** Separating a declining legacy asset (DVD) from a growth engine (streaming) allows distinct strategies and prevents the legacy cost structure from dragging down the growth valuation.

**Attack:**
1.  **Timing & Trust:** Executing a brand split and billing separation immediately after a 60% price hike is operationally tone-deaf. It signals to customers that the price increase was a prelude to service degradation, not an investment in quality. The "confusion" risk is not a possibility; it is a guaranteed outcome that will be interpreted as a hostile attempt to force cancellations.
2.  **Value Destruction:** Severing data sharing (ratings/queues) destroys the unified value proposition. If the services do not talk, the combined plan becomes two unrelated, more expensive products rather than a cohesive ecosystem.
3.  **Alternatives:** The "do nothing" or "internal separation only" options achieve the management isolation goal without imposing friction on the customer. A single login with distinct queues is technically trivial compared to a full brand bifurcation.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume the launch fails. The cause is a mass churn event triggered by the perception of "double dipping" (higher price + split service). Customers feel punished for loyalty. The stock price collapses due to subscriber loss, not gain. The cost of delay (waiting until the price hike sentiment cools) is negligible compared to the reputational damage of immediate execution.

**Blockers:**
*   **Compound Friction:** The proposal forces a change in billing, branding, and login credentials immediately after a massive price increase. This contradicts the goal of retaining customers while transitioning them to streaming.
*   **Data Silos:** Explicitly removing cross-service recommendations reduces the product's utility, making the higher price harder to justify.

**Risks:** Customer backlash, brand dilution, operational complexity of running two distinct stacks.

**Kill Criteria:** If customer churn exceeds 5% in the first month post-split, or if support tickets regarding "two bills" exceed 10% of volume, the split must be paused and re-integrated under a single account structure.

The plan as proposed guarantees a backlash by combining maximum financial pain with maximum usability friction.

DECISION: NO-GO
