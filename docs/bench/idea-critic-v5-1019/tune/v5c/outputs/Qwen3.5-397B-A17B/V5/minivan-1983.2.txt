First call: GO. The proposal leverages existing assets to address an unmet need for family transport that drives like a car but fits in a garage, using a rescued manufacturer's available platform and factory.

**Steelman:** Launch a front-wheel-drive minivan on an existing compact-car platform in a converted factory to create a new "garage-fit" family vehicle category, capitalizing on the company's recent stabilization via government loan guarantees.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Continue selling station wagons (too small) and full-size vans (too large/unwieldy); risk stagnation as family needs evolve.
2.  **New platform:** High cost, high risk, impossible given cash constraints.
3.  **Proposed plan:** Reuses compact-car mechanics for car-like handling and low cost; converts underused factory capacity; creates a new category between wagons and trucks.

**Fit:** Aligns with the north star of saving the company by utilizing constrained cash and existing hardware to serve families, not just commercial buyers.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if marketing positions the vehicle as a commercial van, alienating families. The "commercial" perception risk is mitigated by designing it to drive like a car and fit in a garage. The "no category exists" risk is the opportunity; station wagons are shrinking families, and full-size vans are impractical for homes.

**Evidence:** The company has a compact-car platform (verified asset), a convertible factory (verified asset), and government backing (verified fact). Cash is low, ruling out clean-sheet designs.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (no category, cash, perception, copying) are execution challenges, not factual contradictions. Lack of cash specifically *forces* this reuse strategy rather than blocking it. The "commercial van" perception is a marketing hurdle, not a product flaw, solvable by positioning.

**Risks:** Competitor copying (mitigated by first-mover advantage in a new segment); platform space limits (accepted trade-off for garage fit).

**Kill Criteria:** If pre-orders from family-focused demographics do not exceed commercial fleet interest by 2:1 within 6 months of reveal, pivot to commercial-only or cancel. Leading metric: Ratio of retail vs. fleet inquiries at launch.

DECISION: GO
