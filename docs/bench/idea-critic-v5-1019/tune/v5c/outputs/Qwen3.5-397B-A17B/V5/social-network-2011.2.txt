First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to solve a product-market fit problem with distribution force and financial incentives, ignoring three prior failures and entrenched network effects.

Steelman: Leveraging existing search, mail, and video dominance to bootstrap a new social layer, using unique "circles" and "hangouts" to carve a niche where broad networking failed.

Options:
1. **Proposed Plan:** High cost, high risk of user churn due to forced integration, low reversibility once bonuses and architecture are committed.
2. **Do Nothing:** Preserve core product utility; avoid alienating users with unwanted social features.
3. **Acquisition/Partnership:** Buy or integrate an existing niche player rather than building from scratch after three strikes.

Fit: Challenging a market leader with hundreds of millions of users requires organic pull, not internal mandates. A "company-wide push" contradicts the reality that social graphs are user-owned, not platform-owned.

Pre-mortem: The launch fails because users perceive the integration as spammy, degrading the core search/mail experience. The "invite-only" beta creates artificial scarcity without solving the empty room problem. Bonuses drive internal gaming of metrics rather than genuine adoption.

Evidence: The proposal admits three previous failures. Network effects favor the incumbent; switching costs are high. No evidence suggests "circles" or "hangouts" are sufficient to overcome the inertia of an established graph. The plan relies on forced distribution (integration) rather than product-led growth.

Blockers:
1. **History of Failure:** Three prior attempts failed; the proposal offers no fundamental correction to the root cause, only broader distribution and financial pressure.
2. **Incentive Misalignment:** Tying executive bonuses to adoption of an unproven product encourages manipulating metrics or forcing usage, likely accelerating user churn in core products.
3. **Forced Integration:** Integrating into search/mail risks degrading the utility of the company's actual revenue drivers to subsidize a speculative venture.

Risks: User annoyance, brand dilution, wasted engineering cycles.

Kill Criteria: If the beta does not show organic retention (users returning without email prompts) above 40% within 3 months, stop.

DECISION: NO-GO
