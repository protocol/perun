First call: GO. The proposal targets an unmet market segment with a differentiated brand strategy, leveraging existing engineering capability to undercut incumbents on price while matching quality.

Steelman: Creating a distinct luxury brand with a dedicated network and a high-quality flagship sedan allows the manufacturer to capture premium margins without diluting its economy brand equity, directly challenging European dominance through superior value.

Options:
1. **Proposed Plan:** High upfront cost and risk, but offers the highest upside by creating a new revenue stream and brand equity. Reversibility is low once the network is built.
2. **Do Nothing:** Zero cost, zero risk. Guarantees continued exclusion from the lucrative luxury segment, ceding all future growth there to Europeans.
3. **Badge Engineering:** Lower cost, faster launch. High risk of brand contamination and failure to convince luxury buyers; unlikely to match European refinement.

Fit: Aligns with the goal of expanding beyond the mid-price ceiling. Assumes the market values quality/price ratio over heritage alone.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the separate dealer network fails to attract foot traffic due to lack of brand recognition, or if the single flagship model cannot sustain showroom viability. This is a one-way door with significant sunk costs; delay allows Europeans to solidify their hold and potentially launch their own entry-luxury models.

Evidence: The proposal states the company is "known for reliable, economical small cars" and aims to "match European leaders on quality." The risk of brand dilution is mitigated by the "separate luxury brand" and "own dealer network" structure.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (brand perception, cost, prestige, thin line-up) are inherent to market entry, not factual contradictions. The "separate brand" specifically addresses the brand perception risk. The "flagship" strategy is a standard wedge for luxury entry.

Risks: Brand acceptance, high capital expenditure, competitive response. These become kill criteria: monitor initial sales velocity against forecast and brand sentiment scores within the first 12 months.

The plan accounts for the primary failure mode (brand dilution) via structural separation. The cost and thin line-up are strategic bets, not disqualifiers.

DECISION: GO
