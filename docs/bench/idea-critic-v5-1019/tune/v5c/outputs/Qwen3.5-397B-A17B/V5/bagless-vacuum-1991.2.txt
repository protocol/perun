First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks to launch a capital-intensive hardware company with no manufacturing track record, a price premium over incumbents, and no proof that consumers value the innovation enough to pay extra.

Steelman: A superior bagless cyclone technology justifies a premium price and can disrupt the vacuum market if brought to market directly by the inventor.

Attack:
1.  **Fit & Evidence:** The proposal admits buyers "may not pay extra." Without verified data showing demand for bagless units at a higher price point, the core revenue assumption is unproven. Launching manufacturing before validating willingness-to-pay is financially reckless.
2.  **Alternatives:** The "do nothing" option (licensing the technology to an existing manufacturer) avoids the massive capital risk of building a factory and supply chain from scratch. Given the lack of manufacturing record, partnering leverages existing distribution and production expertise, mitigating the two biggest stated risks (cost and distribution).
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 18 months. Cause: Cash flow collapse due to high unit costs and slow retail adoption. Incumbents copy the feature and sell it cheaper, leveraging their existing distribution channels which the new company cannot match.
4.  **Blockers:** The combination of "no manufacturing record" and "priced above conventional" creates a fatal barrier to entry. Retailers are unlikely to stock an expensive, unproven brand from an unknown manufacturer when established brands offer cheaper alternatives. The risk of incumbents copying the idea is high; without a patent moat explicitly confirmed as impenetrable, competition will erode the premium immediately.

The proposal fails because it attempts to solve a distribution and manufacturing problem with a product innovation alone. It ignores the cheaper, safer alternative of licensing. The stated risks (cost, distribution, buyer hesitation) are not just possibilities; they are structural realities that the current plan does not address. A "go" decision requires a validated path to retail shelf space and proof of premium demand, neither of which exists here.

DECISION: NO-GO
