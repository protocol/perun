First call: GO. The proposal targets an underserved segment (young people) with a focused value proposition (portability, affordability) by stripping non-essential features (recording, speaker) to hit a critical price point, leveraging existing supply chains.

**Steelman:** A dedicated, low-cost playback device unlocks a new mass market of youth who prioritize music mobility over recording utility, creating volume that offsets low margins and deters copycats through speed and scale.

**Attack & Analysis:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" cedes the emerging portable segment to competitors. A full-featured recorder misses the price-sensitive youth demographic. The proposed "playback-only" approach is the cheapest path to the specific upside of market expansion.
2.  **Fit:** Aligns with the vision of democratizing music access. The assumption is that "portability" outweighs "social sharing" for this demographic; if false, the product fails, but the bet is small enough to test.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if dealers refuse to stock it due to bias against "non-recording" devices, or if the "antisocial" perception sticks. However, these are distribution and marketing hurdles, not product flaws. The lack of market research is a risk, but in 1979, speed to market with a novel form factor often *creates* the data rather than waiting for it.
4.  **Evidence:** The use of existing parts reduces R&D risk. The constraint (large initial run) is necessary for the price target; without it, the unit cost kills the youth appeal.

**Blockers:** NONE. The objections raised (dealer doubt, no research, copycats) are risks, not factual blockers. Dealer doubt is a sales challenge, not a product impossibility. Copycats are inevitable but speed and brand dominance in a new category provide a moat. No evidence suggests the target users *reject* playback-only devices; the doubt is projected by adults/dealers.

**Risks:** Market rejection due to social stigma; competitor cloning.
**Kill Criteria:** If pre-orders from key youth-oriented retailers do not cover 40% of the first run within 60 days, halt production expansion. Leading metric: Units sold per capita in the 15–25 demographic vs. traditional hi-fi units.

The proposal survives the attack because the risks are manageable execution challenges, while the opportunity defines a new category.

DECISION: GO
