First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a massive, irreversible infrastructure build (new dealer network) and a fixed 1957 launch date despite an acknowledged recession risk and a segment that "could shrink."

**Steelman:** Capturing the growing mid-priced gap requires a distinct brand to avoid diluting existing lines, justifying the dealer investment to secure market share before competitors.

**Attack:**
1.  **Macro Mismatch:** Launching a capital-intensive, mid-priced luxury product immediately preceding or during a recession is historically fatal. Consumers trade down in downturns; the "growing" segment is the first to contract. The proposal admits this risk but proceeds with a fixed date anyway.
2.  **Sunk Cost Trap:** Building a new dealer network is a high-fixed-cost commitment. If the segment shrinks, these assets become stranded liabilities that cannot be easily repurposed, threatening the parent company's liquidity.
3.  **Cannibalization:** Without a confirmed buffer against the firm's own low-priced line, a recession will force internal competition for fewer dollars, eroding margins across the board.
4.  **Rigidity:** The plan relies on "extensive research" from a pre-recession mindset. A 1957 debut allows no time to pivot if the economy turns in 1956. The cost of delay (missing the initial teaser window) is far lower than the cost of launching into a downturn.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fixed Launch vs. Economic Volatility:** The commitment to a 1957 debut despite a known recession risk creates a high probability of launching at the cycle's peak rather than its growth phase.
*   **Infrastructure Bloat:** Initiating a new dealer network before verifying segment stability during a downturn exposes the firm to unsustainable fixed costs.

**Risks:** Brand overlap, marketing inefficacy, execution delays. These are secondary to the macro-economic blocker.

**Verdict:** The plan fails the "reversibility" and "timing" tests. A sound operator would delay the dealer build and launch until economic indicators stabilize, or pivot to using existing channels to test demand without the infrastructure capex. Proceeding as proposed ignores the primary threat to the business case.

DECISION: NO-GO
