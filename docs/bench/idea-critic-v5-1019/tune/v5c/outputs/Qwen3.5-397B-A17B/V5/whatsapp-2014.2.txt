First call: NO-GO. The price is 12x the prior valuation with no revenue, no monetization plan, and explicit refusal to adopt the buyer's core business model (ads), creating an unbridgeable gap between cost and verified value.

Steelman: Acquiring the target is the only viable defense against mobile displacement, securing hundreds of millions of users who are abandoning the buyer's legacy services, where the strategic value of preventing competitor dominance outweighs the lack of immediate financial returns.

Options:
1. **Acquire:** Costs $19B; risk of total capital loss if monetization fails; irreversible cash/stock burn.
2. **Do Nothing:** Costs $0; risk of continued user erosion to mobile messaging; fully reversible (can build or buy later).
3. **Build/Buy Smaller:** Lower cost; slower execution; avoids single-point failure of a $19B bet.

Fit: While stopping displacement aligns with survival, paying $19B for a company explicitly rejecting the buyer's monetization engine contradicts the economic reality of a public company. A strategy requiring the target to fundamentally change its core anti-revenue stance post-acquisition is an assumption, not a plan.

Pre-mortem: The deal closes, founders leave due to culture clash over monetization, regulators impose strict privacy limits, and the $19B yields zero ROI while the stock price collapses under the weight of the expenditure. This is a one-way door; the cash and stock are gone forever.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits the target has "minimal revenue," "no monetisation plan," and "refused to show advertising." The $19B price tag is 12,600x the annual fee revenue potential (assuming 100% collection on hundreds of millions of users, which is unstated). The prior $1.5B valuation was one year ago; the 12x jump lacks fundamental financial justification.

Blockers:
1. **Economic Incompatibility:** The target's explicit refusal to advertise contradicts the only known path to recouping a $19B investment for this buyer.
2. **Valuation Disconnect:** No evidence supports a 12x valuation increase in 12 months without revenue growth.

Risks: Founder retention, regulatory privacy hurdles, integration failure.

The proposal fails the basic test of economic logic: buying a non-revenue asset at a record price when the asset rejects the buyer's business model.

DECISION: NO-GO
