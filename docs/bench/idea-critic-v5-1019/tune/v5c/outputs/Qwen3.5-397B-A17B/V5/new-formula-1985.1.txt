First call: NO-GO. The plan trades a functional product for a psychological asset it does not own.

**Steelman:** Replacing the formula maximizes immediate taste satisfaction and halts share loss to sweeter rivals by offering a superior sensory experience validated by blind data.

**Attack:** The proposal conflates "preferred taste" with "preferred product." Blind tests isolate flavor from brand equity, heritage, and ownership. By withholding the fact that the original would be removed, the data measures incremental preference, not replacement tolerance. The core claim—that a better-tasting drink ensures market success—fails because cola is not sold blind; it is sold as a cultural icon. Removing the original entirely creates a single point of failure with no fallback, ignoring the explicit risk of emotional attachment. A sweeter alternative can coexist with the heritage brand; total withdrawal forces a binary choice on a segment that values tradition over sweetness. The "better way" is to launch the new formula as a distinct extension (e.g., "New Cola") while retaining the original, allowing the market to self-segment without alienating the core base.

**Blockers:**
1. **Fatal Data Gap:** The testing methodology explicitly excluded the consequence of removal. The data therefore cannot support a decision to withdraw the original, only to add the new. Proceeding on this data is acting on false premises.
2. **No Fallback:** The plan admits "no fallback product" while acknowledging "emotional attachment" as a known risk. If the attachment triggers backlash, there is no operational mechanism to revert, turning a manageable risk into a catastrophic loss of the entire flagship line.

**Risks:** Bottler revolt, permanent brand damage, inability to recover lost customers if the sweet profile fatigues.

**Kill Criteria:** If launch tracking shows >5% volume drop in week one due to boycotts (not just trial), or if social sentiment regarding "removal" exceeds negative thresholds, immediate reinstatement of the original formula is required. Since the current plan precludes reinstatement, it must not proceed as written.

DECISION: NO-GO
