First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a fixed public schedule for unproven core technologies without a demand signal or regulatory mandate, creating massive downside with no immediate upside.

**Steelman:** Committing now forces the organization to solve battery durability and transmission reliability immediately, securing a first-mover advantage before regulators force the issue.

**Attack:**
1.  **Blocker (Evidence Contradiction):** The proposal admits battery durability is "unproven" and fuel is "cheap." In the mid-1990s, nickel-metal-hydride packs in volume production faced unknown cycle-life failures. A bold public schedule combined with unproven durability guarantees a catastrophic warranty exposure that cheap fuel cannot offset for consumers. The math fails: high cost + unproven reliability + no price incentive = market rejection.
2.  **Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "limited pilot" option dominates. Since no competitor has a production hybrid and regulators haven't mandated it, there is no competitive threat requiring a 1997 launch. A quiet R&D program to validate battery life over 3–5 years removes the technical risk without exposing the brand to a failed mass launch.
3.  **Fit:** This contradicts basic operational hygiene. You do not tie a corporate-level program to a specific date when the core mechanism (battery life) is unknown. This is a one-way door with a high probability of failure.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** It is 1998. The cars are recalled due to premature battery failure. The public schedule made the failure visible globally. The company loses billions in warranty claims and brand trust, while competitors watch and learn from the mistake.

**Blockers:**
*   Unproven battery durability paired with a fixed mass-production date creates an uninsurable financial risk.
*   No demand signal and cheap fuel mean the product cannot clear its own cost barrier.

**Risks:**
*   Supply chain immaturity for NiMH packs.
*   Consumer confusion over hybrid value proposition.

**Decision:**
The plan fails the basic test of technical readiness and market fit. The "bold schedule" is a liability, not an asset, when the technology is unproven.

DECISION: NO-GO
