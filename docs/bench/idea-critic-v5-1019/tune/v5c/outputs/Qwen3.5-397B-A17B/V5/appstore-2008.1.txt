**Attack & Options:**
The proposal faces valid risks: security instability, carrier friction, and review bottlenecks. However, "do nothing" guarantees irrelevance as users migrate to rivals with native apps. Open distribution (the rival model) sacrifices the product's core differentiator: stability and polish. The 30% fee and review process are not blockers but necessary controls to ensure quality and fund the platform, directly addressing the "hurt the polish" concern. The "web apps are sufficient" stance is contradicted by the very existence of rival native ecosystems; maintaining it is a strategic error, not a safeguard.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This moves the north star from "best hardware" to "best platform." Failure would stem from a review process so slow it stifles developer interest, or security breaches that validate carrier objections. These are execution risks, not fundamental flaws. The cost of delay is total market exit; rivals are already entrenched.

**Blockers vs. Risks:**
*Blockers:* NONE. No fact provided proves the model fails financially or technically; the concerns are manageable execution challenges.
*Risks:* Review backlog slowing adoption; carrier pushback on specific app types; a high-profile security failure. These become kill criteria: if review time exceeds 7 days or a critical breach occurs within 6 months, the model must pivot.

**Decision:**
The proposal beats "do nothing" and "open distribution" on the stated facts. It preserves product integrity while entering the necessary market. The risks are operational, not existential, and the 30% cut funds the required oversight.
