First call: GO. The growth of small-computer makers signals a market shift that the current integrated model cannot capture quickly enough; a separate team with off-the-shelf parts is the only viable path to speed.

Steelman: To capture the emerging personal computer market before it consolidates, the company must bypass its slow, integrated development and direct-sales habits by deploying an autonomous team to build an open, retail-ready product using existing components within one year.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Retain high margins but cede the high-growth small-computer segment to agile rivals, risking long-term irrelevance.
2. Integrated internal build: Leverage existing supply chains but miss the market window due to slow development cycles and incompatible sales channels.
3. Acquisition: Too slow and costly given the fragmented nature of the emerging software ecosystem.

Fit: This moves the north star from "maximizing mainframe margin" to "securing computing platform dominance." It assumes the market values speed and software variety over proprietary reliability.

Pre-mortem: The project fails if the internal culture strangles the separate team's autonomy, forcing them back into slow, integrated processes. This is a two-way door; if the retail experiment fails, the team can be dissolved with limited loss compared to the cost of missing the market entirely. The cost of delay is total exclusion from the personal computing standard.

Evidence: The proposal cites the factual growth of small-computer makers. The risks (copying, supplier dependence, lower margins, no retail experience) are real but are the necessary price of entry. Copying is inevitable in open standards; winning depends on speed and ecosystem, not secrecy. Supplier dependence is mitigated by the abundance of outside software driving demand. Lower margins are acceptable for volume growth. Lack of retail experience is solved by hiring independent partners, not building internal capability.

Blockers: NONE. No fact presented shows the plan cannot work; the risks are execution challenges, not fundamental flaws.
Risks: Cultural contamination of the skunkworks team; failure to secure key retail shelf space. These become kill criteria: if the team cannot ship within 12 months or secure 500 retail outlets by launch, pivot or stop.

DECISION: GO
