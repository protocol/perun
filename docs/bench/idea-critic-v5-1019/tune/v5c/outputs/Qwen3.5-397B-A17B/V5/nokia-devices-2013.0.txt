First call: NO-GO. Buying a failing hardware division to solve a software ecosystem deficit contradicts basic market dynamics; developers follow users and momentum, not vertical integration of a shrinking base.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the handset maker secures the primary reference device, prevents immediate defection to the rival platform, and signals long-term commitment to the OS, potentially buying time to attract developers.

**Attack:**
1.  **Ecosystem Fallacy:** The core problem is the app gap caused by the rival's dominance. Ownership of a struggling hardware arm does not magically attract developers; it merely adds a low-margin, losing hardware business to a software company. The €5.4B cost drains resources needed for developer incentives or marketing.
2.  **Channel Alienation:** Other handset manufacturers running this OS will view the acquirer as a direct competitor rather than a neutral platform partner. This incentivizes them to accelerate their own exit to the rival platform, shrinking the total OS install base faster than the acquired unit can compensate.
3.  **Integration Drag:** Integrating a large, culturally different hardware workforce with falling sales diverts executive focus from the critical software and platform battles. The "falling sales" trend suggests the hardware is already losing consumer relevance; buying the decline locks in losses.
4.  **Opportunity Cost:** The capital and management bandwidth consumed by this turnaround could be better spent on simplifying the OS, subsidizing key apps, or pivoting strategy entirely.

**Pre-mortem:** Two years post-acquisition, the handset division continues to lose money, other partners have defected to the rival platform citing conflict of interest, and the app gap remains unchanged because total market share collapsed. The company is stuck with a hardware graveyard and a dead platform.

**Blockers:**
*   **Strategic Mismatch:** The proposal attempts to solve a network-effect problem (apps) with a balance-sheet solution (acquisition), which historically fails when the platform is already trailing significantly.
*   **Partner Reaction:** The move actively encourages the remaining ecosystem partners to abandon the platform, accelerating the very death spiral the deal aims to prevent.

**Risks:** Execution complexity, cultural clash, continued sales decline. These are secondary to the fundamental strategic flaw.

**Kill Criteria:** If total OS market share drops by another 2% within 12 months post-deal, the hardware anchor has failed to stabilize the platform.

DECISION: NO-GO
