First call: NO-GO. The proposal trades certain, high-margin licensing revenue for speculative subscriber growth while intentionally burning cash on an unproven technology platform, creating a fatal mismatch between guaranteed income loss and uncertain future returns.

**Steelman:** Withdrawing content to launch a subsidized, low-price streaming service will capture sufficient market share within five years to offset the immediate loss of billions in licensing fees and cover the costs of building a new tech stack from scratch.

**Attack:**
1.  **Revenue Mismatch:** Licensing revenue is immediate, high-margin, and risk-free. The proposal replaces this with a "multi-year operating loss." The math requires capturing tens of millions of subscribers at a *low price* to eventually match billions in lost licensing fees. Given the competitor already has >100M subscribers, displacing them requires massive marketing spend, deepening the burn.
2.  **Capability Gap:** The company has "never run" a technology platform of this scale. Building a global streaming infrastructure while simultaneously engaging in a price war against an entrenched leader introduces extreme execution risk. Failure here means zero revenue stream to replace the lost licensing income.
3.  **Cannibalization:** The plan explicitly risks eating into existing cable and theatrical revenue. Without a proven tech platform, the company risks losing existing high-value streams while failing to secure the new low-margin volume needed to survive.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" or a hybrid model (licensing non-core content while launching a premium, higher-priced niche service) preserves cash flow and reduces tech risk. The proposed "low price + high burn" strategy fails the test of beating "do nothing" because it guarantees immediate financial deterioration based on unproven execution capabilities.

**Blockers:**
*   **Financial Contradiction:** The plan relies on future subscriber volume to justify immediate billions in lost revenue, yet admits to a multi-year loss and lack of technical capacity. The stated facts (billions lost vs. unproven tech) show the core claim (successful transition) fails as proposed.
*   **Execution Reality:** Entering a saturated market (>100M incumbent) with no prior tech experience and a low-price strategy is a verified path to capital destruction, not growth.

**Risks:** Market saturation, tech failure, prolonged cash burn. These are not mere risks; the lack of technical history turns them into near-certainties of failure under the current constraints.

DECISION: NO-GO
