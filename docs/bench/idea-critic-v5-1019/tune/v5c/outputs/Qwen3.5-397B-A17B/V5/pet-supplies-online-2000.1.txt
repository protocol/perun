First call: NO-GO. The plan relies on selling goods below cost to acquire customers while simultaneously burning cash on TV ads, acquisitions, and infrastructure, creating a mathematical impossibility for survival without infinite capital.

Steelman: The company aims to dominate market share instantly via an IPO war chest, using predatory pricing and massive brand visibility to crush competitors before they can react.

Attack: The core mechanism—selling below cost while expanding fixed costs (warehouses) and variable costs (shipping heavy items, TV ads)—accelerates cash burn rather than delaying it. Negative gross margins mean every new customer deepens the loss hole. In early 2000, capital markets are volatile; if the IPO window closes or post-IPO sentiment sours due to mounting losses, the company faces immediate insolvency. Buying a competitor adds integration risk and debt without fixing the unit economics. The "growth at all costs" model fails here because the underlying unit economics are fundamentally broken, not just temporarily negative.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve cash, fix unit economics, and grow organically. Low risk, slower growth.
2. Niche focus: Target high-margin products only, avoiding heavy shipping costs. Moderate risk, sustainable margins.
3. Proposed plan: Maximum risk, near-certain failure if capital markets tighten or margins don't flip instantly (which they won't given the strategy).

Pre-mortem: The IPO fails or stocks crash post-launch due to unsustainable losses. The company runs out of cash within 12 months, unable to service warehouses or ad contracts, leading to bankruptcy or a fire-sale acquisition.

Blockers:
- Negative gross margins combined with increased fixed costs create a cash-flow trap that no amount of revenue volume can solve without profitable unit economics.
- Reliance on continuous capital market access in a volatile environment is a single point of failure.

Risks: Competitor retaliation, shipping cost inflation, IPO window closure.

Kill criteria: If gross margins remain negative after Q1 post-IPO, halt ad spend and acquisitions immediately. Leading metric: Cash burn rate vs. new capital raised.

DECISION: NO-GO
