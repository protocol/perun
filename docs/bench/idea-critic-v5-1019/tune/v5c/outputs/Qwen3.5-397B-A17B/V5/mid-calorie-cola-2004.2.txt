First call: NO-GO. The proposal targets a shrinking trend with a product that likely satisfies no one, at a higher price, against an imminent rival launch.

**Steelman:** A mid-calorie cola captures young men wanting taste without the full carb load of regular soda, differentiating from diet cola's artificial perception, justifying a premium before the low-carb trend fades.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Trend:** The core driver is the "low-carb" diet. If this is a fad (as risked), the window is closing. Launching a complex new SKU with a "large campaign" requires long lead times; the trend may expire before ROI is realized.
2.  **Product-Market Fit:** The "satisfy neither" risk is critical. Regular soda buyers want taste; diet buyers want zero calories. Half-sugar often tastes inferior to both, creating a "worst of both worlds" scenario. Young men, the target, are typically price-sensitive and brand-loyal to full-sugar or switch entirely to diet/energy drinks.
3.  **Pricing & Competition:** A premium price deters trial for an unproven flavor profile. With a rival launching a similar drink simultaneously, the market fragments immediately, doubling marketing costs to educate consumers on a confusing category (mid-calorie) while fighting a price war or feature war with no clear advantage.
4.  **Cannibalization:** The proposal admits diet cola is growing. This product risks cannibalizing high-margin diet sales with a lower-volume, higher-cost premium product, without proven incremental volume from regular soda users who refuse to compromise on taste.

**Blockers:**
*   **Contradictory Value Prop:** The stated risk that the drink "may satisfy neither group" is supported by the fundamental conflict between "half sugar" (compromised taste) and "premium price" (high expectation). No evidence is provided that young men specifically demand *half* sugar rather than *zero* or *full*.
*   **Timing Mismatch:** Building a large campaign for a trend explicitly identified as a potential "fad" creates a high probability of launching into a collapsed market.

**Risks:** Rival duplication, flavor rejection, trend expiration. These are severe, but the blockers regarding the core value proposition and timing are fatal based on the provided facts. The proposal asks to bet heavily on a fading trend with a compromised product at a premium price.

DECISION: NO-GO
