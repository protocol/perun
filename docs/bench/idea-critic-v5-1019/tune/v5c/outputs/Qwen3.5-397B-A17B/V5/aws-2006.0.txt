First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a capital-constrained retailer with no developer platform experience to enter an unproven market against entrenched incumbents while investors are already questioning heavy spending.

Steelman: Leveraging spiky internal infrastructure to create a new revenue stream could theoretically improve asset utilization and diversify income beyond thin-margin retail.

However, the attack reveals fatal flaws in the current context. The "do nothing" or "optimize internal use" alternative offers immediate cost savings without the massive execution risk of building a developer platform from scratch. The proposal admits the market is unproven and the team lacks necessary experience; entering a capital-intensive business with unproven demand while core investors are skeptical of capital spending is financially reckless. The risk of distraction is not theoretical; it is guaranteed given the operational complexity of running a reliable public cloud versus an internal one. The margin dilution risk is acute for a thin-margin retailer; a failure here could threaten the core business's liquidity.

The pre-mortem is clear: The company burns cash building features developers don't want, reliability slips due to lack of platform expertise, and the core retail business suffers from diverted attention and capital, leading to a loss of investor confidence and a forced shutdown of the new unit at a total loss.

Blockers:
1. Capital Contradiction: Investors are already questioning heavy capital spending; this plan requires *more* speculative capex without proven demand.
2. Experience Gap: No internal experience running a developer platform at scale creates an insurmountable execution barrier against established vendors.
3. Unproven Market: The core value proposition (hourly server rental) is explicitly stated as unproven, making the ROI calculation impossible to validate.

Risks: Distraction from core retail, margin dilution, reliability failures, and competitive displacement are not just risks but likely outcomes given the blockers.

This is a one-way door with a high cost of delay only if the market explodes immediately, which is unlikely given it is unproven. The safer, reversible path is to wait for market validation by others before committing scarce capital.

DECISION: NO-GO
