First call: NO-GO. The stated price is an order of magnitude higher than competitors while offering inferior speed and software support, guaranteeing market rejection.

**Steelman:** Launching a premium, user-friendly computer could redefine office work if the productivity gains from the graphical interface justify the cost.
**Attack:** The proposal fails basic arithmetic. At $10,000, the unit costs several times more than established business PCs that are faster and have existing software ecosystems. Office workers, the target audience, cannot justify a 10x price premium for a slower machine with "little" software. The internal threat of a cheaper similar machine further cannibalizes potential sales before launch.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Avoid sinking capital into a non-competitive product.
2.  **Cheaper Alternative:** Accelerate the internal project developing the similar but cheaper interface; this captures the innovation upside without the pricing suicide.
3.  **Proposed Plan:** Launch at $10k. This loses to both "do nothing" and the internal alternative on cost and capability.

**Fit:** A north star of "office adoption" is impossible when the product is priced out of the market and lacks the tools (software) offices need.

**Pre-mortem:** The product launches, sits unsold due to price and lack of applications, and the company loses credibility in the business sector while the internal cheaper team watches resources wasted. This is a one-way door to financial loss; delaying allows the internal cheaper option to mature.

**Evidence:** The proposal itself admits the price is "several times" competitors, the speed is "slow," and software is scarce. These are fatal blockers, not risks.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Price:** $10,000 is unsupported by value proposition compared to faster, cheaper rivals.
2.  **Capability:** Slow performance and lack of software make it unusable for serious office work.
3.  **Internal Conflict:** A cheaper, similar internal project makes this specific configuration redundant.

**Risks:** None need listing; the blockers are definitive facts provided in the text.

**Kill Criteria:** If the price cannot be reduced to within 2x of competitors immediately, or if software partnerships are not secured before manufacturing, the project must stop.

DECISION: NO-GO
