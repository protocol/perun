First call: NO-GO. The proposed $700 price point is prohibitive for a mass-market entertainment device in 1993, especially when competitors subsidize hardware to drive software sales.

**Steelman:** Licensing shifts capital risk to partners, allowing rapid market saturation if the royalty model aligns incentives.
**Attack:** The model creates a fatal misalignment. Competitors sell hardware at a loss to maximize game attach rates; this proposal forces licensees to profit on hardware *and* pay royalties. To achieve a $700 retail price while covering high-end graphics costs, manufacturing margins, licensee profit, and startup royalties, the bill of materials must be implausibly low, or the retail price must be even higher. At $700, the console is a luxury item, not a mass-market platform. Early adopters will not sustain the volume needed to attract third-party game developers, which is the true moat of a console business.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Avoid certain failure in a price-sensitive market.
2.  **Subsidize hardware:** The startup raises capital to sell hardware at a loss, recouping via game royalties (industry standard). This beats the proposal because it enables a competitive retail price (~$300-$400).
3.  **Single manufacturer partner:** Joint venture to share risk, rather than fragmenting efforts among multiple licensees who all need immediate hardware profit.

**Pre-mortem:** The console launches at $700. Sales are anemic. Game developers ignore the platform due to low install base. Licensees, stuck with unsold inventory and unable to lower prices without losing money on every unit, terminate contracts. The startup earns zero royalties and collapses.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Price vs. Market:** $700 is >2x the price of dominant competitors. The proposal admits this risk but offers no mitigation.
2.  **Incentive Clash:** Licensees cannot compete on price if they must profit on hardware while rivals do not. This structural flaw guarantees the hardware will be uncompetitive.

**Risks:** Strong incumbents, unproven demand for high-end graphics at premium prices. These are secondary to the pricing blocker.

**Kill Criteria:** If a competitive retail price (<$400) cannot be achieved while paying licensee margins and royalties, the plan fails. The current math does not support this.

DECISION: NO-GO
