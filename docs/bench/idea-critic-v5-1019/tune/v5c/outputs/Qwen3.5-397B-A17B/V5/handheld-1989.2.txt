First call: GO. The price point and battery life create a distinct category (portability/affordability) that the stronger, color rival cannot match without sacrificing margin or power.

**Steelman:** Win by dominating the "second device" and youth markets through extreme portability, durability, and a $60 price advantage over the competitor, leveraging a killer puzzle app to drive software attachment.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Monochrome vs. Color:** The rival's color screen drains battery and raises cost. A monochrome display enables 10+ hour battery life and sub-$90 pricing. The risk is not technical but perceptual; it is mitigated by the puzzle game's abstract nature, which does not require color.
2.  **Modest Hardware:** Sufficient for the target library. Over-engineering would destroy the unit economics required for the $89.95 price point.
3.  **Single-Game History:** This is a platform play, not a dedicated unit. The risk is real but addressed by the bundled software hook and future cartridge ecosystem.
4.  **Thin Margins:** Volume compensates. At $89.95 with a $20M marketing push, the goal is market share saturation, not immediate high-margin per unit. The alternative (a $150 color device) cedes the mass market to the rival.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the marketing fails to distinguish "long battery/low price" from "cheap/inferior." If the puzzle game fails to stick, hardware sales stall.

**Blockers:** None. The proposal's own facts (price gap, battery implication) support the strategy. The "risks" are features of the cost-reduction strategy, not contradictions.
**Risks:** Software library depth post-launch; consumer perception of "toy" vs. "console."

**Kill Criteria:** If initial sell-through does not reach 500k units in 90 days despite the marketing spend, pivot pricing or bundle strategy immediately. Leading metric: Weekly sell-through rate vs. marketing burn.

The proposal correctly identifies that beating the rival on specs is impossible; winning on form factor and price is the only viable path. The constraints are the strategy.

DECISION: GO
