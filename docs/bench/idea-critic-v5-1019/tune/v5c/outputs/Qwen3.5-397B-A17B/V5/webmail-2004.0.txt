First call: GO. The proposal leverages a core competency (search) to disrupt a stagnant market by solving the primary user pain point (storage limits) with a proven monetization model (text ads).

**Steelman:** Offering 1GB of storage (orders of magnitude above the megabyte standard) creates an immediate, defensible differentiation that forces incumbents into a costly arms race they are culturally unwilling to fight, while ad-scanning funds the infrastructure without upfront user fees.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
*   **Cost Risk:** Critics argue 1GB/user is financially unsustainable. *Rebuttal:* Storage costs follow Moore's Law; the marginal cost is negligible compared to the lifetime value of a locked-in user and the data asset gained.
*   **Privacy Objection:** Scanning mail for ads is invasive. *Rebuttal:* In 2004, users routinely trade privacy for free utility; clear disclosure mitigates backlash. The alternative (charging users) fails in a free-market dominated by entrenched players.
*   **Mature Market:** Email is "solved." *Rebuttal:* Maturity implies complacency. Incumbents are optimized for low storage; they cannot pivot to high storage without breaking their unit economics.
*   **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" cedes the data layer to competitors. "Smaller storage" fails to differentiate. "Paid model" limits adoption too severely to build the network effect required for ad targeting.

**Pre-mortem:** The project fails if privacy backlash becomes regulatory or if ad relevance is too low to cover server costs. However, the invitation-only launch controls growth to match infrastructure scaling, acting as a natural circuit breaker.

**Blockers vs. Risks:**
*   **Blockers:** NONE. No fact presented proves the model mathematically impossible or legally barred today.
*   **Risks:** Privacy perception, storage cost volatility, ad market fluctuation. These are managed via the invitation-only cap and kill criteria (e.g., stop expansion if cost-per-user exceeds ad-revenue-per-user by 20%).

The proposal turns a commodity into a strategic asset. The "risks" are actually the barriers to entry that protect this move from immediate copying. The cost of delay allows competitors to realize the storage arbitrage first.

DECISION: GO
