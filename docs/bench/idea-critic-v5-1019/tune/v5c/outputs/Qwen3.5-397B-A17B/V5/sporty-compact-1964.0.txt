First call: GO. The demographic shift creates a clear, underserved segment: young buyers seeking style over utility. The proposal leverages existing mechanicals to minimize development risk while targeting high-margin customization.

Steelman: By reusing proven compact components, the company can deliver a stylistically distinct vehicle at a low base price, monetizing through options and capturing a new demographic before competitors react.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Cede the emerging youth market to rivals; brand ages with current customers.
2. All-new platform: Prohibitive cost and time-to-market; misses the immediate demographic window.
3. Upgrade existing compact: Fails to shed the "economy/dull" perception; cannot command option premiums.

Fit: Directly addresses the stated influx of postwar baby boomers. Reuses strategy of volume manufacturing with premium margin extraction via options.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the "cheap parts" perception overrides the "sporty styling," causing buyers to reject the car as a disguised economy model. However, the long hood/short deck design specifically counteracts this visual cue. The door is two-way; if launch metrics miss, production can be throttled or marketing pivoted, as the tooling is shared with an existing profitable line. Delay costs the entire cohort entry window.

Evidence: The proposal relies on the stated fact of the demographic surge. No external data is available, but the logic holds: unproven demand is mitigated by the low capital risk of shared parts. The "unproven demand" risk is actually an opportunity gap; if demand were proven, competitors would already be there. The "cheap look" risk is addressed by the specific design constraints (long hood/short deck). The spending risk is acceptable given the low R&D outlay.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited are inherent to any new category creation but are structurally mitigated by the low fixed costs of the shared platform.
Risks: Demand volume uncertainty; brand dilution. These become kill criteria: monitor first-quarter take rates on high-margin options. If option attachment is low, the "sporty" value proposition has failed.

The plan beats "do nothing" by capturing a new revenue stream with minimal incremental engineering. It beats a clean-sheet design by reaching the market faster and cheaper. The heavy ad spend is the necessary catalyst to define the new category.

DECISION: GO
