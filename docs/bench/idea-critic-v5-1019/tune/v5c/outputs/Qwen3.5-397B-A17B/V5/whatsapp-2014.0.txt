First call: NO-GO. The price is 12x the last valuation with no revenue, no monetization plan, and explicit refusal to use the buyer's core monetization engine (ads), making ROI impossible to justify on stated facts.

**Steelman:** Paying a premium is necessary to stop mobile messaging from displacing the core business, acquiring hundreds of millions of users instantly that cannot be built organically fast enough.

**Attack:**
1.  **Financials:** $19B for 50 employees and $0 meaningful revenue implies a valuation based entirely on speculative future dominance. The target explicitly rejects ads, the buyer's only proven monetization scale. There is no bridge between cost and return.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" costs less than $19B. Building a competitor or licensing technology likely costs less than 1% of this price. The "cheaper alternative" (build/buy smaller) is not ruled out by the fear of displacement; fear does not validate a specific price tag.
3.  **Blocker:** The proposal states the target has "no monetisation plan" and "refused to show advertising." Since the buyer's business model relies on ads, and the target refuses them, the acquisition destroys the very mechanism required to recoup the $19B cost. This is a factual contradiction within the proposal's own terms: you cannot buy a non-monetizable asset for a record price to solve a revenue threat.
4.  **Risk vs. Blocker:** Founder retention and privacy are risks. The lack of any path to profitability at this price point, given the stated refusal to monetize via ads, is a blocker. The numbers do not work.

**Kill Criteria Check:** The proposal offers no metric, date, or pivot condition. It asks for a blind bet on displacement fear. A sound operator cannot approve a transaction where the cost is certain ($19B) and the return mechanism is explicitly denied by the target's operating principles.

**Verdict:** The plan fails the basic test of capital allocation. Paying 12x valuation for a company that refuses to make money in the way the buyer makes money is not a strategy; it is a panic response.

DECISION: NO-GO
