First call: GO. The proposal avoids a direct hardware war against a superior graphics rival by pivoting to a novel form factor (dual-screen, touch) that targets a broader, non-core audience at a lower price point. This leverages asymmetric strategy rather than head-to-head specification competition.

Steelman: By accepting lower graphical fidelity in exchange for unique interaction models and a second screen, the company creates a distinct market category where the rival's raw power is irrelevant, effectively expanding the total addressable market while maintaining a budget-friendly entry point.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Cede the high-end to the rival and slowly lose relevance as developers flock to the stronger platform.
2. Match hardware: Enter a costly arms race with lower margins, likely failing to dislodge the incumbent's momentum in the premium segment.
3. Proposed path: Differentiate on gameplay innovation and price, preserving the legacy install base while capturing new users.

Fit: This aligns with a "blue ocean" strategy, creating uncontested market space. The decision to keep the older handheld on sale mitigates the risk of alienating the core base during the transition.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the dual-screen architecture proves too difficult for third-party developers to adopt, resulting in a software drought. However, the lower price point and unique hook may attract first-party exclusives that define the system, offsetting third-party hesitation initially.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (developer confusion, unproven touch mechanics, gimmick perception) are execution challenges inherent to innovation, not factual contradictions that make the plan impossible. The proposal explicitly accepts the trade-off of lower graphics for novelty and cost, which is a coherent strategic choice. The "two screens add cost" concern is countered by the explicit goal of being cheaper than the rival, implying the overall BOM (Bill of Materials) is managed via the lower-spec components.

Risks: Developer adoption speed and consumer perception of the touch screen as a toy rather than a tool. These become kill criteria: if key software partners do not commit within the first year, or if early sales data shows no uptake beyond the core niche, the strategy must pivot.

The proposal offers a viable path to growth without requiring impossible feats of engineering or market displacement of a superior technical product. It bets on creativity over brute force.

DECISION: GO
