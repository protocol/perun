First call: NO-GO. The proposal contradicts basic market mechanics for the stated demographic.

**Steelman:** Launching a stripped-down, high-cost social device via a single carrier creates an exclusive, affordable entry point for youth, leveraging the acquired team's expertise before the main platform arrives.

**Attack:** The math fails immediately. Requiring a "full smartphone data plan" for a device costing as much as a smartphone itself destroys the value proposition for "price-sensitive" young buyers. They will choose a used full-smartphone or a cheaper feature phone over a costly, limited device. The "no app store and no games" constraint removes the primary utility drivers for this demographic, making the high data cost unjustifiable.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Preserve capital; avoid channel conflict with the upcoming main platform launch.
2.  **Cheaper path:** Release the social features as an app on existing low-cost devices or the upcoming main platform, removing hardware and exclusive carrier risks.
3.  **Current Plan:** High CAPEX, high churn risk, guaranteed internal friction between teams, and a product that costs too much for what it offers.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** This cannibalizes the main platform launching later same year. Assuming internal conflict remains unmanaged, the project will likely miss deadlines or launch with poor integration. If it launches, sales will miss targets due to price/value mismatch, forcing a write-down of the acquisition's potential. The "one carrier" limitation restricts reach precisely where volume is needed to subsidize the niche hardware.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Price/Value Mismatch:** The target demographic cannot or will not pay smartphone-level costs (hardware + data) for a device explicitly lacking smartphone core utilities (apps, games). This is a factual contradiction of the buyer persona.
2.  **Strategic Conflict:** Launching a competing hardware line months before the company's primary platform launch dilutes focus and confuses the market.

**Risks:** Internal team conflict, carrier dependency, delayed time-to-market. These are secondary to the fundamental economic blocker.

**Kill Criteria:** If a pilot cannot demonstrate willingness-to-pay exceeding the combined hardware and data cost among the target demographic within 30 days, stop. However, the current proposal lacks even a mechanism to test this without incurring full launch costs.

The proposal asks to sell a luxury price point for a budget utility to a budget-conscious audience while fighting internal wars. It fails the basic logic test.

DECISION: NO-GO
