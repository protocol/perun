First call: NO-GO. The company lacks hardware credibility following a smartphone failure, and the core value proposition relies on unproven consumer trust regarding always-on listening.

Steelman: A dedicated, always-listening speaker removes friction from music and shopping, creating a new high-margin category that leverages existing retail data to dominate home audio.

However, the attack reveals critical blockers. First, the "always-on microphone" risk is not merely a hesitation; in 2014, without a established privacy brand or prior hardware success, this is a non-starter for mass adoption. The recent smartphone failure proves the market does not yet trust this retailer with personal devices. Second, the proposal admits voice recognition "often fails." Selling a $199 device (or $99 loss-leader) that frequently misunderstands commands creates immediate negative word-of-mouth, compounding the recent brand damage. Third, the "invitation only" launch contradicts the need to prove category viability; if the product is risky, limiting supply delays the feedback loop required to fix the acknowledged recognition flaws.

Alternatives: Doing nothing preserves capital and brand equity. Partnering with an established audio manufacturer to license the software reduces hardware risk. Improving the existing phone-based assistant leverages devices users already own and trust, addressing the "voice fails" issue without new hardware overhead.

Pre-mortem: The product launches, early adopters encounter recognition errors and privacy concerns, reviews tank, and the "cylindrical speaker" becomes a symbol of the company's hardware incompetence, dragging down core retail sentiment.

The proposal's own facts confirm the blocker: the company's last hardware launch went badly, and the core mechanism (always-listening voice) faces high skepticism and technical instability. The risks listed are not future possibilities but current realities that the plan does not solve. The $99 member price suggests a margin kill that cannot be sustained if volume fails due to the very risks identified.

Blockers: 1) Recent hardware failure destroys trust required for invasive new hardware. 2) Acknowledged high failure rate of voice recognition makes the primary feature unreliable. 3) Privacy concerns in 2014 are a market barrier, not a risk to be mitigated later.

Risks: Category creation failure, competitor response, supply chain issues.

DECISION: NO-GO
