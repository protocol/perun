First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to solve three unproven, high-complexity variables (cross-border logistics, new inventory system, foreign regulation) simultaneously with maximum capital exposure (130–150 sites) before establishing product-market fit in the new region.

Steelman: Leveraging existing lease availability to achieve immediate scale and deny sites to the entrenched competitor, betting that speed outweighs the operational friction of launching a new supply chain and IT system concurrently.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Preserve capital; miss the window on available leases; cede ground to the incumbent.
2. **Pilot (Better Way):** Open 5–10 stores to validate the new supply chain, inventory system, and bilingual labeling compliance before signing remaining leases. Cost is lower; risk is contained; reversibility is high.
3. **Proposed Plan:** High cost, extreme risk, low reversibility once leases are signed and systems deployed.

Fit: Expansion aligns with growth vision, but the "north star" of profitable international presence is jeopardized by a launch strategy that guarantees operational chaos. A failure in the new inventory system across 150 stores is catastrophic, not a learning opportunity.

Pre-mortem: The new inventory system fails to handle bilingual labeling or cross-border tax nuances, causing stockouts or compliance fines across all 150 locations simultaneously. The supply chain bottlenecks prevent replenishment, emptying shelves while fixed lease costs burn cash. The entrenched competitor undercuts prices on key goods, exploiting the retailer's operational distraction. This is a one-way door; exiting 150 leases mid-term involves massive write-offs and reputational damage.

Evidence: The proposal admits the supply chain and inventory systems are "new" and "not yet proven." It acknowledges "higher prices" in the target market, threatening the core "discount" value proposition. No data proves the local market wants this specific retailer over the incumbent.

Blockers:
1. **Unproven Core Systems:** Launching 150 stores on a new, untested inventory system and supply chain violates basic operational hygiene. The system must work at small scale before large scale.
2. **Simultaneous Complexity:** Learning regulation, logistics, and labeling while deploying new IT and opening 150 stores creates a failure cascade where no single variable can be isolated or fixed.
3. **Value Proposition Mismatch:** Entering a market with "higher prices" without a proven mechanism to lower them via the new supply chain risks brand irrelevance.

Risks: Competitor retaliation, lease liability, currency fluctuation. These are secondary to the operational blockers.

Kill Criteria: If a pilot of 5 stores cannot achieve positive same-store sales and inventory accuracy >98% within 6 months, the expansion halts.

DECISION: NO-GO
