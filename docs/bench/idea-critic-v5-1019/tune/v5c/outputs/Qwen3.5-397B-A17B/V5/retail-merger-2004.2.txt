First call: NO-GO. Merging two declining, underinvested retailers with different customer bases during a rise of superior big-box rivals is a classic "diworsification" that rarely creates value.

**Steelman:** The acquisition offers immediate scale, real estate assets, and a chance to revitalize the older chain's brands through the discounter's efficient distribution before competitors extinguish them.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fundamental Momentum:** Both entities have negative sales momentum. Combining two shrinking lines does not create growth; it aggregates decline. The proposal admits cost savings are "uncertain," removing the primary financial justification for such a merger.
2.  **Customer Mismatch:** Discount shoppers and department-store appliance buyers have distinct economic profiles and shopping behaviors. Forcing a merge risks alienating the core discount base with higher-priced legacy brands while failing to attract the legacy customers who may view the discount banner as inferior.
3.  **Capital Misallocation:** The target stores are "old and underinvested." Post-restructuring, the acquirer has limited capital. Spending it to retrofit dying stores diverts resources from modernizing the core winning format or investing in e-commerce, exactly when big-box rivals are expanding.
4.  **Competitive Landscape:** Big-box rivals are growing because they offer better selection and pricing. This merger creates a distracted, debt-laden entity with an identity crisis, unable to compete on price (due to legacy costs) or experience (due to underinvestment).

**Pre-mortem:** By 2007, the integration has failed. Corporate distractions caused inventory shortages in the discount stores. The legacy stores continue to bleed cash despite conversion attempts. The combined entity faces liquidity issues, forcing a fire sale of assets or a return to bankruptcy, having wasted the post-restructuring fresh start.

**Blockers:**
*   **Negative Synergy Logic:** The plan relies on uncertain cost savings to offset two declining revenue streams. Without a proven mechanism to reverse sales declines immediately, the math fails.
*   **Strategic Dilution:** Entering a different customer segment with a weakened balance sheet while a dominant competitor grows is a fatal strategic error.

**Risks:** Integration complexity, brand cannibalization, culture clash. These are secondary to the fundamental flaw: buying a problem to solve a problem.

DECISION: NO-GO
