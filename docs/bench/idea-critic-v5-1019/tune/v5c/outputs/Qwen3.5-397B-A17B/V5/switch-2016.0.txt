First call: GO. The hybrid form factor directly addresses the eroding handheld line and differentiates from both failing home consoles and phones, creating a unique market niche despite lower raw power.

Steelman: A unique hybrid device leverages existing IP to create an uncontested market space, prioritizing versatility and first-party software over raw graphical fidelity, thereby mitigating direct competition with more powerful rivals.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Continued erosion of handheld share and irrelevance in the home market; high risk of exiting the hardware business.
2. High-power home console: Direct competition with established rivals requires massive R&D and marketing spend with low differentiation; high financial risk given recent losses.
3. Hybrid (Proposed): Moderate cost, unique value proposition, leverages strong first-party studios. Beats "do nothing" by offering a clear path to relevance.

Fit: Aligns with the necessity to stop financial bleeding and reclaim market share by avoiding a spec war they cannot win.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if third-party support remains non-existent, making the library too thin to justify the price, or if supply constraints prevent meeting holiday demand. This is a two-way door; if it fails, the company pivots to pure software or exits hardware, but the unique positioning offers the highest probability of recovery. Delaying launch cedes the hybrid concept to competitors or allows mobile phones to fully consume the handheld market.

Evidence: The proposal relies on the maker's own studios to carry the thin launch lineup. Historical data suggests strong first-party titles can drive hardware adoption even with weaker specs. The price parity with more powerful rivals is a risk, but justified by the unique form factor.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (specs, confusion, third-party support) are valid but do not constitute factual blockers based on the provided text; they are execution challenges inherent to the strategy. The "confusing positioning" risk is mitigated by the clear "hybrid" value proposition if marketed correctly.

Risks: Limited third-party support, supply constraints, weak specs relative to price. These become kill criteria: if pre-orders do not meet a specific threshold indicating consumer understanding, or if key third-party partners do not commit by a specific date, the plan must be revisited.

DECISION: GO
