First call: NO-GO. The plan attempts to solve three unproven variables (product-market fit, logistics viability, and unit economics) across two distinct, complex continents simultaneously, multiplying the surface area for failure without a learning loop.

**Steelman:** Simultaneous global launch captures maximum market share before competitors react, leveraging first-mover advantage in a nascent online fashion sector.

**Attack:**
1.  **Options:** "Do nothing" preserves capital. A single-country pilot (e.g., UK or Germany) tests the core hypothesis—will young buyers purchase fashion online?—at a fraction of the cost. The proposed "big bang" approach lacks reversibility; if online fashion fails or logistics collapse, the entire venture burns out instantly. A staged approach allows pivoting the model based on real data from one market before scaling.
2.  **Fit:** The goal is to become the "largest retailer," which requires longevity. Burning venture capital on unproven multi-continent operations threatens survival before the brand is established. Speed matters, but not at the expense of immediate insolvency.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The venture fails because logistics costs in the US differ vastly from Europe, inventory gets stranded across borders, and marketing spend is diluted. Venture capital runs out 18 months in because customer acquisition costs were underestimated in both regions, and no single market generated enough profit to sustain operations.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits online fashion buying is "unproven." Launching globally on an unproven premise is speculation, not strategy. The risks listed (logistics complexity, delayed launch, capital runway) are not mitigated; they are amplified by the scope. There is no evidence that simultaneous launch yields significantly higher success probability than a dominant regional foothold followed by expansion.

**Blockers:**
*   **Unproven Core Premise:** The fundamental assumption that consumers will buy fashion online is unverified. Testing this in two massive markets simultaneously violates the principle of cheap experimentation.
*   **Capital Efficiency:** The plan guarantees higher burn rate with no confirmed upside over a staged rollout. If the model fails, it fails everywhere at once, leaving zero salvageable value.

**Risks:** Execution complexity, currency fluctuations, cultural mismatches in fashion taste, and supply chain fragmentation. These are manageable in a single market; they are fatal in two.

**Kill Criteria:** If a single-country pilot cannot achieve positive unit economics within 12 months, the global expansion is halted.

DECISION: NO-GO
