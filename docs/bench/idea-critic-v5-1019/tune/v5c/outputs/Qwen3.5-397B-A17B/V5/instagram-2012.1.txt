First call: NO-GO. The price implies a valuation with no revenue, no business model, and extreme execution risk, violating basic capital discipline.

**Steelman:** Buying the only credible mobile threat neutralizes a strategic existential risk before it erodes the core business, acquiring talent and momentum that cannot be built internally in time.

**Options:**
1. **Do Nothing:** Risk rapid user migration to the competitor; attempt to build a mobile product from a position of weakness. High risk of irrelevance.
2. **Build/Buy Smaller:** Acquire a smaller player or accelerate internal development. Lower cost, but likely insufficient speed or scale to stop the threat.
3. **The Proposal:** Immediate neutralization of the threat. Highest cost, highest integration risk, but guarantees the competitor does not win independently.

**Fit:** Aligns with the north star of maintaining social dominance, assuming mobile is the future. If mobile is a fad, this is a catastrophic overpay.

**Pre-mortem:** Integration destroys the very culture that made the app successful; users flee due to platform changes or privacy concerns; regulators block the deal causing public failure; the $1B cash burn yields zero revenue for years, angering shareholders post-IPO. This is a two-way door in theory (can sell), but practically a one-way bet on culture preservation.

**Evidence:** Facts provided show zero revenue and "no business model." The valuation is purely speculative based on user count and threat level. The buyer's own mobile weakness confirms the threat is real, but does not justify any price.

**Blockers:**
*   **Valuation Math:** Paying $1B for zero revenue with no defined path to monetization is a factual failure of financial prudence. The numbers do not work under any standard model.
*   **Strategic Desperation:** The purchase is driven by fear of a "weak mobile product," suggesting a reactive panic buy rather than a sound strategic expansion.

**Risks:** Culture clash, regulatory block, user exodus, monetization failure. These are severe but secondary to the primary blocker: the absence of any economic rationale for the price tag.

The proposal fails the fundamental test of capital allocation. While the strategic threat is real, the price is untethered from reality. A sound operator cannot approve a billion-dollar expenditure with no revenue and no business model, regardless of the competitive landscape. The risk of destroying shareholder value via overpayment outweighs the risk of competition, which can be fought through other means.

DECISION: NO-GO
