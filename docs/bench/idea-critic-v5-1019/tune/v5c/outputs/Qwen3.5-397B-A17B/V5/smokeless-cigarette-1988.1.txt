First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks to launch and promote widely based solely on test markets that have not yet confirmed user acceptance, while explicit risks indicate the core user experience (taste, lighting mechanism, complexity) may be fundamentally inferior to the existing product.

**Steelman:** The heated cigarette offers a cleaner smoking experience with less smoke, potentially retaining smokers who want reduced harm without quitting, justifying the sunk development costs if adoption succeeds.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the blocker test. It assumes "test markets" will validate the product, yet the stated facts explicitly list "smokers may dislike the taste" and "complicated to use" as active risks. A product that tastes bad or is difficult to light compared to a standard cigarette has no market fit, regardless of smoke reduction. Promoting widely *before* resolving these specific usability and preference failures is a direct path to wasting the "very large sum" already spent on further marketing losses. Furthermore, promoting it as a "cleaner way to smoke" triggers the stated regulatory risk regarding health claims, which could halt the launch entirely post-investment.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Avoids marketing spend on a likely failed product; preserves capital.
2.  **Fix then launch:** Resolve taste and lighting complexity issues *before* any wide promotion; test specifically for these friction points.
3.  **Proposed plan:** Launch and promote widely now.

**Fit:** The strategy conflicts with the reality that a superior user experience is a prerequisite for adoption in a habit-driven category. The "cleaner" benefit does not override a bad taste or difficult ignition.

**Pre-mortem:** The launch fails because smokers try the product once, find the taste unpleasant or the lighting ritual annoying, and return to ordinary cigarettes. Regulatory bodies issue cease-and-desist orders on "cleaner" claims, resulting in fines and forced rebranding.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Unproven Core Value:** Stated risks confirm the product may be worse (taste/complexity) than the incumbent. Launching before proving these are non-issues is a failure of basic product logic.
2.  **Regulatory Trap:** The proposed messaging ("cleaner") directly invites the stated regulatory objection.

**Risks:** Sunk cost fallacy driving a bad launch; brand damage from a failed premium product.

The plan attempts to solve a business problem (sunk costs) with marketing, ignoring the product defects that will cause market rejection.

DECISION: NO-GO
