First call: NO-GO. The price ($1.65B) vastly exceeds any verifiable revenue multiple for a company with "no meaningful revenue," and the stated copyright threats represent an existential liability that could invalidate the entire asset post-acquisition.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the dominant traffic leader instantly solves distribution scale, allowing the buyer to pivot from a failing proprietary video service to the industry standard platform, betting that ad monetization and copyright licensing can be solved faster than organic growth.

**Options:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Continue growing the smaller, safer proprietary service; avoid massive debt/equity dilution and litigation risk.
2.  **Build/Buy Smaller:** Acquire a licensed, revenue-positive competitor at a fraction of the cost to gain technology without the legal poison pill.
3.  **Proposed Deal:** Massive immediate market share but assumes unlimited liability and unproven economics.

**Fit:** While dominance aligns with a "organize the world's information" vision, the specific mechanism (buying unlicensed copyrighted content) contradicts legal compliance required for a public entity.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-close, media giants win injunctions forcing the removal of 80% of popular content. Traffic collapses, bandwidth costs remain high, ad revenue fails to cover operational burn, and the $1.65B stock value evaporates, leaving the buyer with a lawsuit-ridden shell.

**Evidence & Math:** The proposal admits "no meaningful revenue." At $1.65B, the buyer is paying billions for a cost center (bandwidth) and a lawsuit magnet. There is no mathematical path to ROI based on *current* facts; the entire thesis rests on two unproven assumptions: that copyright law will bend to allow monetization of stolen content, and that ads can support high-bandwidth video costs. These are not risks; they are blockers because the proposal offers no mitigation for immediate litigation that could shut the site down entirely.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Liability:** Active threats of lawsuits over core inventory (copyrighted clips) create a scenario where the primary asset value (content) is illegal.
2.  **Valuation:** Price is disconnected from any financial metric (revenue, profit), relying entirely on speculative future ad models.

**Risks:** Bandwidth costs exceeding ad yield; failure to license content post-acquisition.

**Kill Criteria:** If media companies file suit within 90 days, the integration halts.

DECISION: NO-GO
