First call: NO-GO. The plan attempts to enter a saturated market with an unproven business model using a timeline that contradicts the scope of work, creating a high probability of financial loss and brand dilution.

Steelman: Reusing assets allows a rapid, low-cost entry into the popular last-player-standing genre to capture users via a free-to-play model, diversifying revenue beyond the initial paid sale.

Options:
1. **Proposed Plan:** High risk of failure due to inexperienced operations, likely cannibalization of the core paid product, and unrealistic development speed for a competitive multiplayer mode.
2. **Do Nothing:** Protects the reputation of the paid game and avoids sinking costs into a genre dominated by an entrenched rival.
3. **Delayed Pilot:** Extend the timeline to validate the free-to-play economy and technical stability before full release, accepting a later market entry.

Fit: While expanding the user base aligns with growth, the method contradicts the studio's current operational maturity and risks the primary revenue source (the paid game) without a verified secondary stream.

Pre-mortem: The mode launches late or buggy due to the two-month constraint, fails to monetize because the studio lacks live-ops experience, and drives existing paying customers to the free version, collapsing overall revenue. This is a one-way door for reputation; a failed free title signals low quality.

Evidence: The proposal admits the studio has "not run a free-to-play business before" yet plans a complex economy in "about two months." Industry standards suggest building a stable, competitive multiplayer infrastructure and economy takes significantly longer than eight weeks for a team without prior F2P data. The rival's dominance means the margin for error is zero; the proposed timeline guarantees errors.

Blockers:
1. **Timeline vs. Scope:** Building a stable, competitive last-player-standing mode in two months is technically implausible, especially for a studio new to the genre.
2. **Operational Inexperience:** Launching a free-to-play economy without prior experience or data is a strategic failure waiting to happen.
3. **Cannibalization:** No mechanism is proposed to prevent the free mode from devaluing the paid cooperative game.

Risks: Market saturation, asset reuse limitations, brand damage.

The lack of F2P experience combined with an impossible timeline creates a fatal flaw that cannot be mitigated by kill criteria alone; the foundation is unsound.

DECISION: NO-GO
