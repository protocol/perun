First call: NO-GO. The unit economics and market fit described suggest a fundamental mismatch between cost and value.

**Steelman:** A premium, convenience-first juicing ecosystem where hardware subsidies are recouped via high-margin recurring revenue, justified by freshness verification and reduced waste.

**Attack:**
1.  **Economics:** At $699, the device targets luxury early adopters, yet the recurring cost (proprietary bags) likely exceeds fresh produce without offering proportional time savings over standard blenders. The "subscription tax" on a commodity (fruit) is hard to sustain.
2.  **Friction:** Mandatory Wi-Fi for a countertop appliance introduces unnecessary failure points (connectivity loss = unusable machine) and privacy concerns for a simple mechanical task.
3.  **Market:** The "single-serve fresh juice" market in 2016 is unproven at this price point. Competitors (cheap blenders, fresh juice bars) offer lower barriers.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** The project fails because the attach rate on subscriptions drops once users realize the per-ounce cost is 5x higher than grocery store fruit, leaving expensive hardware as a paperweight.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do nothing:* Avoid burning cash on inventory and custom engineering for an unproven behavior.
*   *Cheaper path:* Sell a non-connected press using standard bags or open-market produce to validate the mechanical form factor before locking users into a walled garden.

**Blockers:**
*   **Unit Economics:** The proposal admits concern over subscription price vs. raw produce cost. If the margin relies on users accepting a massive premium for convenience, the addressable market shrinks below the level needed to justify "tens of millions" in raised capital expectations.
*   **Unnecessary Complexity:** Internet connectivity for freshness checking is a solution in search of a problem; it adds BOM cost and support overhead without clear user value.

**Risks:** High churn, brand damage from "bricked" devices if servers fail, and inventory obsolescence.

**Kill Criteria:** If a pilot shows subscription retention below 40% after 3 months, or if the fully loaded cost per bag exceeds $8, stop immediately.

The combination of high upfront hardware cost, locked-in recurring expenses, and forced connectivity creates a fragile business model vulnerable to immediate consumer rejection.

DECISION: NO-GO
