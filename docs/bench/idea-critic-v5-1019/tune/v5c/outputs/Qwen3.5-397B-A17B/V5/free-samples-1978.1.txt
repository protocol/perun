First call: NO-GO. The initial four-city test demonstrated little demand, and renaming a product without changing its fundamental value proposition rarely converts non-buyers.

**Steelman:** The product failed due to poor branding and lack of trial; free samples in a controlled office environment will prove utility, generating the social proof needed for a national launch.

**Attack:**
1.  **Evidence Contradiction:** The proposal admits the first test showed "little demand." Renaming addresses perception, not utility. If office workers "may not see a need" (as raised in risks), free samples will likely result in waste, not conversion. The core assumption—that usage creates desire—is unproven and contradicted by the initial failure.
2.  **Better Way:** Before spending on a city-wide sample campaign and rebranding, conduct low-cost interviews with the original test participants to understand *why* they didn't buy. If the issue is price or lack of awareness, samples help. If the issue is lack of utility, no amount of free paper will fix it.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume the city rollout fails. Why? Because recipients viewed the notes as junk mail or a novelty with no workflow integration. The cost of samples and printing new branding is sunk, delaying the inevitable pivot or cancellation.
4.  **Kill Criteria Check:** The proposal's success metric is "if most recipients say they would buy it." Stated intent is a weak proxy for actual purchase, especially for free goods. This metric invites false positives.

**Blockers:**
*   **Contradictory Facts:** The proposal cites "little demand" as a historical fact but assumes a rename + free sample overcomes this without evidence. The risk that "office workers may not see a need" is a known negative from the first test that this plan does not resolve, only hopes to bypass.
*   **Metric Flaw:** Relying on stated intent ("say they would buy") rather than actual purchase behavior in the pilot makes the go/no-go decision for the national rollout unreliable.

**Risks:** Wasted budget on rebranding and sampling; false positive signal from free users; national rollout failure due to unaddressed utility gap.

The plan ignores the primary lesson of the first test (lack of demand/need) and relies on a vanity metric for the next stage.

DECISION: NO-GO
