First call: GO. The growth of small-computer makers signals a market shift that the current integrated model cannot capture quickly enough; a separate team with off-the-shelf parts is the only viable path to speed.

**Steelman:** A dedicated, isolated team can bypass internal inertia to launch a standardized, open-architecture PC via retail channels within 12 months, capturing the emerging volume market before competitors dominate.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary objection is that open design invites copying and erodes margins. However, in a nascent mass market, speed and ecosystem adoption (via outside software) outweigh the defensibility of proprietary lock-in. Waiting to build custom chips or a direct sales force ("do nothing" or "integrated approach") guarantees missing the window entirely. The "better way" of licensing the design to others forfeits the brand opportunity and control. The risk of supplier dependence is real but necessary for the one-year timeline; building internal capacity would take too long.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This fits the survival imperative: adapt to the "small-computer" trend or face irrelevance. If we assume the project fails, it will be because the mainframe culture strangled the small team or because retail partners were mismanaged. The cost of delay is total exclusion from the personal computing era.

**Blockers vs. Risks:**
*Blockers:* NONE. The proposal explicitly accepts lower margins and lack of retail experience as trade-offs for speed, showing awareness rather than blindness.
*Risks:* Copying by rivals, supplier reliance, channel inexperience. These are inherent to the strategy but do not invalidate the core claim that this is the *only* way to enter the market fast enough. The proposal's constraints (separate team, off-the-shelf parts) are the specific mitigations for the company's structural slowness.

**Decision:**
The facts presented show that the traditional model is incompatible with the required speed and ecosystem needs of the personal computer market. The risks are manageable trade-offs, not fatal flaws. The proposal correctly identifies that a distinct operating model is required to survive the shift.

DECISION: GO
