First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to enter a saturated market with an unproven business model while threatening existing revenue, all within an unrealistic timeframe.

Steelman: Reusing assets allows a rapid, low-cost entry into the popular last-player-standing genre to capture users via a free-to-play model, diversifying revenue beyond the initial paid sale.

However, the attack on this claim reveals critical failures. First, the "two months" timeline is a blocker; building a balanced, networked competitive mode distinct enough to survive against a genre-dominating rival requires extensive iteration and tuning that cannot be compressed into eight weeks without releasing a broken product. Second, the cannibalization risk is not merely theoretical but a direct threat to the studio's only proven income stream; converting a paid cooperative experience into a free competitive one devalues the original purchase without a guaranteed replacement revenue stream. Third, the studio lacks the operational infrastructure for a free-to-play economy; launching cosmetic monetization requires live ops, anti-cheat, and community management capabilities the team explicitly does not possess.

Alternatives: Doing nothing preserves the paid game's integrity and avoids sinking costs into a likely failure. A better way would be to add a limited, paid competitive mode to the existing game to test demand without fracturing the player base or requiring a new business model.

The pre-mortem is clear: the mode launches late or buggy, the rival's entrenched player base refuses to switch, the free model cannibalizes paid sales, and the studio burns cash on servers and support with no return. The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of a failed launch that damages the brand.

Blockers: The two-month timeline is insufficient for a viable competitive product; the lack of free-to-play operational experience makes monetization unlikely to succeed; the direct cannibalization of the sole revenue source creates a negative expected value.
Risks: Market saturation, brand dilution, technical debt from rushed asset repurposing.

DECISION: NO-GO
