First call: NO-GO. The price ($1.65B) vastly exceeds any verifiable asset value given zero revenue, negative cash flow from bandwidth, and existential legal threats that could shutter the platform entirely.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the dominant network effect immediately prevents a competitor from locking up user behavior, betting that legal liabilities can be settled or monetized faster than they destroy equity.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Continue building the smaller internal service; risk losing the category war but avoid immediate massive dilution and liability.
2.  **License content:** Pay media companies directly for a library; slower growth, but legal safety and predictable costs.
3.  **Acquire (Proposal):** Instant dominance, but inherits unlimited liability and unproven economics.

**Fit:** Assumes "winning the video category" is the north star, regardless of short-term profitability. However, acquiring a potentially illegal operation contradicts sustainable growth strategies.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-close, major studios win an injunction forcing the removal of all copyrighted content. The site's value proposition vanishes overnight, leaving the buyer with $1.65B in diluted stock, soaring bandwidth bills for empty storage, and no revenue stream to offset them.

**Evidence & Math:** The proposal admits "no meaningful revenue." At $1.65B, the buyer pays ~$82M per month in value transfer for a site burning cash on bandwidth. There is no mathematical path to ROI without a fundamental change in the business model (ads) and legal status (licensing), neither of which is proven. The "largest destination" metric is a vanity number if the content is illicit.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Existential Legal Risk:** The "large volume of copyrighted TV and film clips" combined with "threatened lawsuits" represents a factual blocker. If courts rule against the site, the core asset (the library) is deleted, rendering the acquisition worthless.
2.  **Negative Unit Economics:** High bandwidth costs + zero revenue = burning cash at scale. The proposal offers no mechanism to flip this immediately.

**Risks:** Ad model failure, culture clash, retention drop post-acquisition. These are secondary to the blockers.

**Kill Criteria:** This deal fails the basic test of solvency and legality as presented. The liabilities are not "risks" to be managed; they are active threats that negate the asset's existence.

DECISION: NO-GO
