First call: NO-GO. The plan trades a dominant brand's equity for negligible royalties in a market where execution risk lies entirely with an unmotivated licensee.

**Steelman:** Licensing allows rapid, low-capital entry into the consumer console market by leveraging a toy partner's distribution, accepting low margins to establish a foothold against entrenched rivals.

**Attack:**
1.  **Incentive Misalignment:** The computer maker earns only a "small royalty," while the toy company bears all marketing costs. In a market dominated by established players, heavy marketing is the *only* way to gain share. The licensee has little incentive to spend aggressively on a product yielding them thin margins after royalty payments, leading to product failure.
2.  **Brand Dilution:** Consumers may not connect the product to the licensor. If the product fails due to poor marketing or low quality (common in toy-led tech), the computer maker's premium brand suffers without gaining any market share.
3.  **Better Way:** The "do nothing" option preserves brand equity. A superior alternative is a joint venture with shared marketing spend and brand control, or a direct, focused launch of a single high-quality unit rather than ceding control for pennies.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** The product launches, sells poorly because the toy company didn't market it against Sony/Sega, and the computer maker is remembered for a cheap plastic failure.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fatal Incentive Flaw:** The proposal admits the licensee may not market hard. In a dominant-competitor market, this guarantees failure. The plan offers no mechanism to enforce marketing spend.
*   **Negative Brand Impact:** The risk of brand disconnection or association with a failure outweighs the "small royalty" upside.

**Risks:** Market dominance by rivals; low royalty revenue.

**Kill Criteria:** If the licensee cannot contractually commit to a minimum marketing spend equal to 20% of projected revenue, the deal must not proceed.

The current proposal lacks control, incentives, and brand protection. It is a bet on a partner to fight a war they aren't paid to win.

DECISION: NO-GO
