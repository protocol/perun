First call: GO. The stated facts describe a massive, unserved market segment (families on scooters) and a price point ($2,500) that creates a new category, making the upside justify the execution risks.

Steelman: Launching the world's cheapest car at $2,500 successfully converts millions of scooter-owning families into car owners by offering unmatched value, despite safety concerns and land protests.

Options analysis:
1. **Do nothing:** Maintain status quo; families remain on scooters, missing the safety and comfort of enclosed transport.
2. **Premium launch:** Higher price reduces volume, failing the core mission of mass mobility and losing the first-mover advantage in the ultra-low-cost segment.
3. **Proposed plan:** High volume, dedicated factory, aggressive pricing. Beats "do nothing" by capturing a new market; beats premium by aligning with the target demographic's purchasing power.

Fit: Aligns perfectly with the vision of motorizing India's middle class. The "cheapest" label is a feature, not a bug, for cost-sensitive buyers.

Pre-mortem & Door: If this fails, it will be due to prolonged land protests halting production or a catastrophic safety incident validating quality doubts. This is a one-way door; building a dedicated factory creates high sunk costs. The cost of delay is high, as competitors could enter the void or inflation could erase the $2,500 price advantage.

Evidence & Blockers: The proposal admits risks (protests, safety perception, alternatives) but presents them as manageable hurdles, not factual impossibilities. No internal contradiction exists: the cost-cutting required to hit $2,500 is the mechanism, not a blocker, provided the base safety standards are met. The existence of cheaper scooters defines the target, not the competition; used cars lack the warranty and novelty appeal. No fact provided proves the target users *do not want* this; the shift from scooters implies a desire for upgrade.

Blockers: NONE. The risks are executional, not fundamental flaws in the thesis.
Risks: Land acquisition delays, brand stigma of "cheap," safety perception. These become kill criteria: if land is not secured by Q4 or initial safety ratings fall below minimum legal thresholds, pivot or stop.

The potential to redefine personal mobility for a billion people outweighs the known operational headwinds.

DECISION: GO
