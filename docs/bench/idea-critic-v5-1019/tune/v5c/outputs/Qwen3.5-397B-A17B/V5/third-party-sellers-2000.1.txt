First call: GO. The proposal converts fixed inventory risk into variable fee revenue and leverages existing traffic without capital expenditure, a logical move for an unprofitable retailer needing scale.

Steelman: By aggregating all offers (new/used, first/third-party) on single pages, the site becomes the definitive destination for selection, increasing conversion and engagement enough to offset margin compression on first-party sales through fee income and long-term customer retention.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Maintain full margin but bear all inventory risk and limited selection; likely fails to reach profitability soon.
2. Exclusive first-party: Higher quality control but capped growth and sustained losses.
3. Separate marketplace tab: Protects brand but fragments user experience and reduces cross-selling; inferior to integrated pages.

Fit: Aligns with a "selection-first" north star necessary to compete with physical retailers. Assumes the platform can enforce basic service standards.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if third-party fraud spikes, causing a trust collapse that drives away core buyers before fee revenue matures. This is a two-way door; sellers can be de-platformed, and the feature rolled back if trust metrics crater. Delay costs market share to competitors who aggregate faster.

Evidence: The proposal admits risks (undercutting, cannibalization, trust) but frames them as manageable trade-offs rather than fatal flaws. The economic logic holds: unprofitable retailers must prioritize cash flow and asset lightness over margin protection.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited are inherent to the model but do not contradict the core claim that this path offers the best chance for profitability given the current loss-making status. Cannibalization is acceptable if the fee on the used sale exceeds the lost margin on the new sale plus the cost of holding that inventory.

Risks: Supplier retaliation, quality variance, brand dilution. These become kill criteria: if supplier boycotts remove >20% of top SKUs or trust scores drop below baseline within 6 months, pivot.

DECISION: GO
