First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits irreversible capital before validating the core hypothesis that unit economics can be positive in a low-margin, high-density-dependent business.

**Steelman:** Rapidly building a nationwide moat of automated warehouses prevents competitors from securing prime real estate and captures market share before the model is fully understood, leveraging abundant capital to buy speed.

**Attack:**
1.  **Options:** The "do nothing" alternative (optimize the single city) costs negligible capital while revealing true unit economics. The "cheap alternative" (manual fulfillment centers in new cities) tests demand without billion-dollar sunk costs. The proposal's path requires perfect execution on day one across a dozen unknown variables; the alternatives allow learning.
2.  **Fit:** The strategy ignores the stated risk of "no operating track record." Scaling an unproven model does not fix broken unit economics; it multiplies the loss.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 2001. Cause: $10B spent on warehouses in cities where customer density never reached the threshold to cover the fixed costs of automation and 30-minute delivery logistics. The 30-minute slot promise creates a rigid operational constraint that automation cannot fix if order volume is sporadic.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits "no operating track record" and "low-margin business." Basic arithmetic shows that thin margins require massive volume to cover billion-dollar fixed costs. No evidence exists that this volume is achievable.
5.  **Blocker Test:**
    *   **Blockers:** The proposal explicitly states there is no data to support the assumption that the model works (no track record) while committing to a cost structure (highly automated warehouses) that demands immediate, massive success to avoid insolvency. The "low-margin" nature of groceries means errors in volume prediction are fatal. Committing $1B+ before learning unit economics is a factual contradiction of sound capital allocation.
    *   **Risks:** Capital intensity, customer density, execution difficulty. These are noted but secondary to the blocker.

**Kill Criteria Check:** The proposal offers no pilot, no cap, and no metric to validate the model before full deployment. It bets the company on an unproven assumption. A cheaper, staged alternative exists that reaches the same learning upside without the existential risk.

DECISION: NO-GO
