First call: GO. The current roadmap fails the core requirement for thin, efficient laptops, and the dominant alternative offers a clear path to competitiveness.

**Steelman:** Transitioning to the dominant architecture within two years, backed by a translation layer, is the only viable strategy to regain performance leadership and market relevance, despite short-term friction.

**Attack & Analysis:**
*   **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" guarantees product irrelevance as thermal constraints persist. Sticking with the current supplier has already failed for a decade. No cheaper alternative exists that solves the thermal/efficiency deficit.
*   **Fit:** This aligns with the survival imperative of shipping competitive hardware. Distinctiveness must shift from "unique chip" to "best user experience."
*   **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the translation layer is too slow for pro users or if the announcement cannibalizes sales before new units ship. However, these are execution risks, not strategic blockers.
*   **Evidence:** The proposal admits the current roadmap "lags" and cannot meet "thin laptop" needs. This is a factual blocker for the status quo. The risks (sales freeze, rebuilds, speed) are known variables manageable via communication strategy and engineering focus, not reasons to abort.
*   **Blockers:** NONE. The facts provided show the current path is broken.
*   **Risks:** Developer friction and performance overhead are real but acceptable trade-offs for hardware viability. The "painful earlier transition" is a lesson for planning, not a veto.

**Kill Criteria:** If the translation layer cannot run core professional applications within 20% of native speed by the midpoint of the two-year window, the strategy must pivot.

**Strongest Attack Survived:** The argument that "brand distinctiveness will weaken" was tested. Counter: Distinctiveness based on inferior hardware is a liability. The brand survives only if the product works; architecture is invisible to most users compared to battery life and heat.

**Dissent Ledger:** High risk of short-term revenue dip during announcement; requires aggressive developer relations to mitigate app gaps.

DECISION: GO
