First call: NO-GO. The stated price is several times higher than leading competitors while offering inferior speed and a barren software ecosystem; no rational office buyer chooses this over established alternatives.

**Steelman:** This machine pioneers the graphical interface for business, betting that future software demand and usability will eventually justify a massive premium over command-line rivals.

**Attack:** The proposal fails on current facts.
1.  **Price vs. Value:** At $10,000, it costs multiples of faster, proven business PCs. Office managers buy productivity, not novelty. Without immediate "killer apps," the ROI is negative.
2.  **Software Void:** "Little third-party software" is a fatal blocker for business adoption. Hardware is useless without tools to run the office.
3.  **Performance:** Running "slowly" undermines the efficiency promise of a business tool.
4.  **Internal Cannibalization:** A cheaper, similar internal project exists. Launching the expensive, slower version first destroys the company's own future market positioning and invites immediate obsolescence upon the cheaper unit's release.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Wait for the software ecosystem to mature or the internal cheaper project to finish. Cost: $0. Risk: Competitors might move first (but they face the same software hurdle).
*   *Pivot to Niche:* Target only specific high-value design roles, not general "office workers."
*   *Delay Launch:* Halt until the price drops or software exists.

**Pre-mortem:** We launch at $10k. Sales are zero because competitors offer 3x speed for 1/3 the price. The internal cheaper team feels betrayed and delayed. We are stuck with unsold inventory, and the "graphical future" narrative dies due to poor execution.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Price Disparity:** The unit is priced out of the market relative to performance and competitors.
2.  **Missing Ecosystem:** No software means no utility for the target demographic.
3.  **Internal Conflict:** The existence of a cheaper, similar internal alternative makes this specific configuration strategically redundant and financially suicidal.

These are not risks; they are stated facts that guarantee failure as proposed. The plan requires a fundamental rewrite of price, target, or timing.

DECISION: NO-GO
