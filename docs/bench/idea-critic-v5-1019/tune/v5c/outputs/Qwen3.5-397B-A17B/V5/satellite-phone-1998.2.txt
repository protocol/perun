First call: NO-GO. The unit economics and market timing fundamentally contradict the capital structure.

**Steelman:** A premium, niche service for global travelers can generate sufficient early cash flow to service debt while ground networks remain incomplete in key regions.

**Attack:**
1.  **Economics vs. Debt:** $5 billion in debt requires massive, immediate scale. A $3,000 handset priced for "international business travelers" targets a tiny niche. Even capturing 100% of this niche cannot service the interest on billions borrowed, let alone principal. The math fails: high price suppresses volume; high debt demands volume.
2.  **Substitution Risk:** Ground cellular is spreading "fast." The value proposition relies on a temporary gap in coverage that is closing rapidly. A two-way door decision to wait would likely reveal a shrinking addressable market before launch.
3.  **Product-Market Fit:** Bulky hardware with poor indoor performance severely limits utility for the target demographic, who often work indoors. At several dollars per minute, usage will be restricted to emergencies, reducing average revenue per user (ARPU) below sustainable levels.
4.  **Operational Readiness:** Unready sales channels combined with a complex, expensive product guarantee a slow ramp-up. Debt holders do not tolerate slow ramps.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoid sinking further capital into a launch destined to miss debt covenants.
*   **Pivot to Infrastructure:** Lease capacity to governments or military (B2G) rather than B2C, avoiding the handset subsidy and channel buildup.
*   **Delay:** Wait for handset miniaturization and cost reduction, though this risks obsolescence against ground networks.

**Pre-mortem:** The company launches, sells few units due to price and bulk, misses debt payments within 18 months, and enters bankruptcy while ground coverage becomes ubiquitous.

**Blockers:**
*   **Revenue/Debt Mismatch:** The stated price point and target market size cannot mathematically service $5 billion in debt.
*   **Timing:** Rapid ground network expansion undermines the core unique selling proposition before break-even.

**Risks:** Execution delays, handset defects, regulatory hurdles (all secondary to the fundamental economic blocker).

**Conclusion:** The proposal attempts to solve a infrastructure-scale debt load with a boutique consumer product strategy. The numbers do not work.

DECISION: NO-GO
