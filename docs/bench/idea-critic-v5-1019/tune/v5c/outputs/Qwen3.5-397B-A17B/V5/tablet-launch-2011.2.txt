First call: NO-GO. The proposal ignores the fatal mismatch between a "small app catalog" and a "market-leading price" in a category where software availability is the primary purchase driver.

**Steelman:** Launching now leverages first-mover hardware integration to secure early adopters before the OS transition creates uncertainty, betting that marketing can temporarily mask the app deficit.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Evidence:** In 2011, the tablet market is defined by the leader's ecosystem. Shoppers do not buy hardware; they buy access to apps. Pricing a device with a "small fraction" of the catalog at parity with the leader contradicts the stated fact that shoppers see the product as "unproven." Value perception will collapse immediately upon comparison.
2.  **Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "delay" option is superior. Wait until the app catalog reaches a critical mass (e.g., top 100 apps) or price significantly below the leader to compensate for the software gap. Heavy ad spend on an incomplete product accelerates brand damage, not adoption.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The launch fails because reviewers and consumers highlight the missing apps within 24 hours. The heavy investment in advertising drives traffic to a product that cannot convert, burning cash and cementing the "unproven" reputation. The leadership transition amplifies this, signaling internal instability to developers needed to fix the catalog gap.
4.  **Blocker:** The proposal's own facts create a mathematical blocker: Price = Leader, Value (Apps) << Leader. This guarantees negative unit economics on perceived value. No amount of outreach solves a missing inventory problem at launch. The risk is not "execution difficulty"; it is immediate market rejection based on the stated specs.

**Kill Criteria Check:** The proposal lacks a metric for minimum viable app count or a price discount strategy to offset the software gap. It asks to spend heavily on a known deficit.

**Verdict:** The plan commits to a premium price point without the premium ecosystem required to justify it, directly conflicting with the stated risk of shopper perception. This is a guaranteed capital destruction event.

DECISION: NO-GO
