First call: NO-GO. The proposal contradicts the brand's core identity and ignores explicit market risks without a validation mechanism.

**Steelman:** A massive ad spend can forcibly reposition a family brand to attract adults by associating it with a new, high-quality premium product, overcoming existing perceptions through sheer volume.

**Attack:**
1.  **Brand Incompatibility:** The chain's "child-centered image" is a structural barrier, not just a perception. Adults seeking "premium" experiences actively avoid venues associated with children's entertainment. No amount of advertising can easily decouple a deeply ingrained brand archetype without alienating the existing, profitable base.
2.  **Economic Mismatch:** The core customer base values low cost. Introducing a significantly higher-priced item creates cognitive dissonance. If regular buyers are deterred by the price, and new adult buyers are deterred by the "kids' place" image, the product falls into a value gap.
3.  **Unverified Scale:** Committing the "largest advertising budget" before proving demand violates basic capital allocation logic. The risks explicitly state demand is unproven; scaling spend before validation magnifies potential loss rather than mitigating risk.
4.  **Better Way:** A "do nothing" or "limited test" approach is superior. Launch the burger in a small, adult-demographic-heavy market with minimal marketing to test actual uptake. If adults buy it despite the brand image, *then* scale. The current proposal skips the learning phase for a high-stakes gamble.

**Blockers:**
*   **Contradictory Evidence:** The proposal admits adults see the chain as "fast, not premium" yet assumes advertising alone can invert this deeply held view immediately.
*   **Fatal Flaw:** The strategy relies on attracting a demographic (adults seeking premium) that fundamentally conflicts with the brand's primary asset (child-centered fun), while risking the core value proposition for existing customers.
*   **Missing Kill Criteria:** There is no pilot, cap, or metric to stop the bleeding if the "largest budget" fails to convert.

**Risks:** Total brand dilution, alienation of core family customers, massive financial loss on unproven demand. The proposal offers no fallback if the premium narrative fails to stick.

DECISION: NO-GO
