First call: GO. The pivot from "timekeeper" to "fashion item" fundamentally changes the value proposition, making the low cost an enabler of volume rather than a signal of poor quality.

Steelman: By positioning the plastic watch as an accessible fashion accessory, the company creates a new market segment where low price drives impulse buys, rendering traditional quality reputation metrics less relevant while mitigating brand risk through distinct positioning.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Safe, but cedes the emerging low-cost, high-volume segment to competitors.
2. Premium plastic line: Higher margin, lower volume; fails to capture the mass-market scale needed to justify tooling costs.
3. Licensing brand: Faster launch, but erodes long-term control and margin; the proposed direct launch builds internal capability.

Fit: Assumes the strategic goal is market expansion and volume growth over margin preservation. If the north star is exclusivity, this fails; if it is ubiquity, this is the optimal path.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the "fashion" narrative doesn't stick, leaving only a "cheap plastic watch" perception that cannibalizes core sales. This is a two-way door; a failed collection can be discontinued with minimal long-term damage compared to the opportunity cost of missing the trend. Cost of delay is high; fashion trends are ephemeral, and first-mover advantage in this specific price point is critical.

Evidence: The proposal relies on the assumption that "fashion" status can be conferred on plastic. The aggressive target (1M units) is the necessary bet to achieve economies of scale that make the price point viable.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (reputation, taste changes, competition) are inherent to the strategy but do not invalidate the core logic. Reputation risk is managed by the "fashion" framing; taste changes require rapid iteration (a feature of the low-cost model); competition validates the market existence.

Risks: Brand dilution if the fashion angle fails; inventory obsolescence if tastes shift mid-year. These become kill criteria: if sell-through <60% in Q1, halt production expansion.

Kill Criteria: Stop if Q1 sell-through is below 60% or if core brand sales drop >5% attributed directly to this line. Leading metric: Weekly sell-through rate by model.

DECISION: GO
