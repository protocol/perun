First call: GO. The shift in buyer demographics toward American-brand customers seeking smaller sedans indicates a market gap that current European offerings do not fill. Leveraging an existing mass-market platform reduces development time and cost, enabling a faster response to this emerging demand.

Steelman: Launching a compact luxury model on a shared platform allows the brand to capture a new customer segment with lower investment risk, provided the premium features and branding effectively differentiate it from the mass-market base.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Lose the growing segment of buyers seeking smaller luxury sedans to competitors.
2. **Develop a new platform:** High cost, long lead time, misaligned with the immediate opportunity.
3. **Shared platform with premium trim (Proposed):** Balances speed, cost, and market entry; beats doing nothing by capturing revenue now.

Fit: Aligns with the need to expand the customer base beyond traditional European luxury owners. Assumes the brand can maintain prestige through design and feature differentiation.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the shared platform is too obvious, eroding the luxury perception. This is a two-way door; the model can be discontinued if prestige damage is detected. Cost of delay is losing first-mover advantage in the compact luxury segment.

Evidence: The proposal cites a factual shift in buyer origin (American-brand customers) and preference (smaller sedan). No internal data contradicts the feasibility of adding premium trim to the shared platform.

Blockers: NONE. The risks raised are valid concerns but not factual blockers. They are execution challenges, not inherent flaws.
Risks: Platform transparency, price justification, salesforce alignment, brand dilution. These become kill criteria: monitor brand sentiment scores, sales mix by previous ownership, and discount levels required to move units.

Kill Criteria: If within 12 months, >30% of buyers cite "value over brand" as the primary driver AND brand prestige metrics drop by >10%, discontinue the model. Leading metric: Sales conversion rate from American-brand trade-ins vs. European-brand trade-ins. Review date: 12 months post-launch.

The proposal addresses a clear market shift with a cost-effective solution. The risks are manageable through monitoring and do not prevent initiation.

DECISION: GO
