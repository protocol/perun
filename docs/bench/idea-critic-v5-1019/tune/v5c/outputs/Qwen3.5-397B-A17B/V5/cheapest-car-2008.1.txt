First call: GO. The stated facts describe a massive, unserved market segment (families on scooters) and a price point that theoretically unlocks it. The risks are executional, not fundamental flaws in the thesis.

Steelman: A dedicated high-volume factory enables a ~$2,500 price point, successfully converting millions of scooter-owning families to four-wheel safety, creating a new market category despite land protests and stigma.

Options:
1. **Launch as proposed:** High capex and reputational risk, but captures the "first mover" volume needed to sustain the low margin. Beats doing nothing.
2. **Do nothing:** Cedes the emerging lower-middle class to competitors or leaves them on two wheels; misses the strategic shift.
3. **Premium entry:** Abandons the core volume thesis; fails the specific goal of replacing scooters for the mass market.

Fit: Aligns with the vision of motorizing the masses. The "cheapest" label is a double-edged sword but necessary for the stated price strategy.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if land protests halt construction indefinitely, or if safety incidents validate quality doubts, killing demand. It is a one-way door regarding factory capex; delay costs first-mover advantage in a price-sensitive market.

Evidence: The proposal cites the 2008 context and the specific price gap. No internal data contradicts the existence of the scooter-to-car migration desire.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (protests, safety perception, alternatives) are known variables, not disqualifying facts. Protests are a hurdle to manage, not a proof of impossibility. Safety concerns are addressed by the inherent safety upgrade from scooters to enclosed cars, provided minimum standards are met. Alternatives (used cars) lack the warranty and status of a new vehicle at this price.

Risks: Land acquisition delays, brand stigma ("cheap"), margin erosion. These become kill criteria: if land is not secured by Q4 2009, or if pre-orders miss 50% of capacity due to safety fears, pivot.

The plan commits real money; the cap is the dedicated factory. Kill criterion: If land acquisition is not legally resolved within 12 months, stop construction. Leading metric: Pre-order conversion rate from scooter owners.

DECISION: GO
