First call: NO-GO. The proposal contradicts the company's core strength (business email) by launching a business-oriented device without native email, calendar, or contacts unless paired with a specific phone, severely limiting its standalone value against a rival with a mature app store.

**Steelman:** A 7-inch tablet on a new OS can carve a niche by leveraging the company's phone ecosystem, accepting short-term app gaps to establish a new form factor before the rival dominates the small-tablet space.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit:** The device undermines the "business email leader" identity. Executives and professionals—the target demographic—require immediate, reliable access to communication tools. Making these features dependent on phone pairing creates friction and reduces the tablet to a secondary accessory rather than a primary productivity device.
2.  **Options:** "Do nothing" avoids brand dilution. A "cheaper alternative" is porting existing successful apps to the new OS immediately or launching with a web-based bridge, rather than shipping a hollow device. The current plan offers no upside over the rival's larger tablet except size, but charges the same price while delivering less functionality.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The device fails because business users reject a "computer" that cannot check email out of the box. Developers see low adoption due to the niche constraint and untested OS, refusing to build apps. The product becomes a costly orphan.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits the OS is untested and the app store is weak. Shipping without core utilities validates competitor claims of inferiority.
5.  **Blockers:** The absence of built-in email/calendar/contacts for a business-focused brand is a factual contradiction of the value proposition. Matching the rival's price while offering fewer core features ensures immediate market rejection.

**Risks:** Developer apathy, OS instability, brand confusion. These are exacerbated by the lack of core features.

**Kill Criteria:** If the device cannot support standalone enterprise communication within the first unit, it fails its primary purpose.

DECISION: NO-GO
