First call: NO-GO. Releasing an unfinished, unsupported game to a paying public in 2009 without marketing or a support plan invites immediate reputation destruction and refund chaos.

**Steelman:** Early access validates demand and funds development through direct user feedback, bypassing traditional publisher gatekeepers.

**Attack & Options:**
1.  **Support Capacity:** A solo developer cannot handle "large audience" support tickets while coding. In 2009, no standardized "Early Access" refund policies or community management tools exist to mitigate this. The risk of being overwhelmed is a factual blocker, not a theoretical risk.
2.  **Market Perception:** Selling a "rough" product without a clear roadmap or established trust (via a publisher) leads to negative reviews that permanently kill sales velocity. Players in 2009 expect finished goods or clear demos, not paid betas.
3.  **Alternatives:**
    *   *Do Nothing (Finish First):* Higher quality launch, but cash-flow negative.
    *   *Free Beta:* Gather feedback and build hype without the reputational risk of selling broken software.
    *   *Publisher Pitch:* Trade equity for support/marketing (matches the stated need).

**Pre-mortem:** The game launches, early buyers encounter bugs, demand support the dev cannot give, flood forums with anger, and the project dies within three months due to toxic word-of-mouth.

**Evidence & Constraints:** The proposal admits "no publisher," "no marketing," and "cannot support a large audience." These are not risks to be managed; they are missing prerequisites for a commercial launch. The 2009 market lacks the infrastructure (Steam Early Access launched later) to normalize this model for a solo dev.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Support Mismatch:** The explicit admission that the developer "cannot support a large audience" contradicts the action of opening sales to the public. This guarantees failure.
2.  **Reputation Suicide:** Selling an unfinished product without a safety net (refunds/publisher buffer) in this era creates a permanent negative brand association.

**Risks:** (Irrelevant due to blockers) Buyer fatigue, feature creep.

**Kill Criteria:** If the developer cannot answer support tickets within 24 hours, sales must halt immediately. However, since the capacity does not exist *now*, the precondition fails.

The plan requires selling before the capacity to support exists. This is a direct path to project death.

DECISION: NO-GO
