First call: NO-GO. The proposal combines maximum engineering risk with maximum business risk (no rollback) while alienating the core user base that drives traffic.

Steelman: A complete rewrite with a new algorithm and publisher focus is the only way to reverse traffic decline and modernize the platform before a rival captures the market.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Retains community trust but likely loses market share to the growing rival.
2. Phased rollout/A/B test: Allows validation of the new surfacing logic and publisher focus on a segment, preserving a rollback path and measuring community reaction before full migration.
3. Incremental UI update: Refreshes the look without the engineering risk of a full rewrite or the cultural shock of changing voting mechanics immediately.

Fit: The proposal assumes the traffic slowdown is due to outdated mechanics rather than content saturation or external competition. Shifting focus to publishers may contradict the "community values existing voting mechanics" constraint, risking a mass exodus of the loyal user base.

Pre-mortem: The launch fails because the new algorithm suppresses community-favorite content in favor of publishers, causing power users to leave. Simultaneously, a critical bug in the untested rewrite causes data loss or downtime. With no rollback plan and all users moved at once, the site remains broken or unpopular, accelerating the decline.

Evidence & Blockers:
The proposal admits the core community values current mechanics, yet the plan changes them. This is a direct contradiction.
The "all users at once" approach eliminates reversibility for a "large engineering risk."
There is no pilot, cap, or metric defined to validate the new direction before total commitment.

Blockers:
1. The plan explicitly removes the ability to rollback a high-risk rewrite.
2. The proposed changes directly conflict with stated community values without a validation step.
3. No evidence suggests the loyal community wants publisher-favored content; this is an unverified assumption that could kill the site.

Risks: Alienating the core base, engineering failure with no recovery, accelerating user loss to rivals.

Kill criteria are absent; the plan is binary (success or catastrophe). A sound operator would demand a phased approach to test the publisher hypothesis and the new code stability.

DECISION: NO-GO
