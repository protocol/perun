First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a fixed public schedule for unproven core technologies (battery durability, novel transmission) without a mandated regulatory driver, a cost advantage, or a verified demand signal, while facing low fuel prices that erase the value proposition.

**Steelman:** A bold, fixed-schedule launch in 1997 establishes an insurmountable first-mover brand moat before competitors can react, forcing regulators and customers to accept the new standard.

**Attack:**
1.  **Economic Misalignment:** With cheap fuel and no mandates, the consumer payback period is infinite. The premium price cannot be justified by operating savings, creating a product nobody needs.
2.  **Technology Risk:** Nickel-metal-hydride durability and the novel transmission are unproven at volume. A public schedule removes the ability to delay for fixes, guaranteeing a catastrophic recall or warranty crisis that could bankrupt the division.
3.  **Strategic Exposure:** Committing to mass production before validating the battery lifecycle is a one-way door with infinite downside. If the batteries fail at 3 years, the brand is destroyed.
4.  **Better Way:** The optimal path is a limited fleet pilot (taxi/government) to gather durability data and educate regulators, keeping the public schedule flexible until the technology is proven and/or mandates are imminent.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** Battery durability is unproven; **Impact:** High probability of systemic failure post-warranty or massive warranty bleed.
*   **Fact:** No demand signal + cheap fuel; **Impact:** The product has no economic rationale for the mass market.
*   **Fact:** Fixed public schedule + novel tech; **Impact:** Removes the only safety valve (time) for resolving inevitable engineering defects.

**Risks:** Competitor leapfrogging (mitigated by patents), regulatory shifts (uncertain).

**Kill Criteria:** If a 500-car pilot shows >2% powertrain failure rate within 12 months, or if fuel prices remain below $1.50/gallon by 1996, the program stops.

**Decision:** The proposal ignores the fundamental lack of market pull and the extreme technical risk of a fixed-date launch for unproven hardware. It bets the company on a solution looking for a problem.

DECISION: NO-GO
