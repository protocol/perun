First call: GO. The company leverages its dominant hardware position to solve the industry's existential piracy problem with a legal, convenient alternative, despite thin margins and platform restrictions.

**Steelman:** A proprietary ecosystem selling affordable, licensed singles converts pirates to paying customers by offering unmatched ease of use, securing label partnerships through hardware leverage, and establishing a foothold before subscription models mature.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Piracy erodes label relations and hardware value as music becomes unmonetized.
2.  **Subscription model:** High friction for 2003 dial-up/broadband limits; consumers prefer ownership.
3.  **Proposed store:** Captures immediate revenue, satisfies labels, and locks users into the hardware ecosystem.
The proposal beats "do nothing" by creating a monetizable market where none exists.

**Fit:** Aligns with the north star of selling more portable players; music is the bait, hardware is the catch.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if labels withdraw licenses due to low volume or if rivals quickly clone the model. However, being first creates a brand association with "legal music." The cost of delay is ceding the digital transition entirely to chaotic piracy.

**Evidence:** Assumed based on the prompt's facts: hardware is selling well (leverage exists), file-sharing is widespread (demand is proven), and labels are willing to license (supply is available).

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (margins, platform lock-in, rival strategies) are execution challenges, not factual contradictions. Thin margins are acceptable if they drive high-margin hardware sales. Platform restriction is a feature, not a bug, for ecosystem lock-in. Rival subscription bets are unproven in this specific era.

**Risks:** Label friction, slow adoption due to price vs. free, competitor catch-up. These become kill criteria: monitor weekly song sales and label retention rates.

**Kill Criteria:** If major labels withdraw catalog within 6 months or if attachment rate (songs per device) remains below 1 after one quarter, pivot strategy. Leading metric: Weekly active buyers.

DECISION: GO
