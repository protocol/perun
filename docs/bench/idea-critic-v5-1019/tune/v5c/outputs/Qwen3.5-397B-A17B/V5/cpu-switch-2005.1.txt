First call: GO. The current roadmap fails the core requirement for thin laptops; staying put guarantees irrelevance.

Steelman: Migrating to the dominant architecture with a binary translation layer is the only viable path to regain performance-per-watt leadership and market relevance within two years, despite transition friction.

**Skeptic**: The claim that "distinctiveness will weaken" assumes hardware architecture is the primary brand differentiator. In 2005, design, OS, and ecosystem matter more. The risk of sales freezing is real but manageable via controlled disclosure; the risk of *no* competitive product is existential. The "painful earlier transition" is a reason to improve execution, not avoid necessity.

**Better-way**: Alternatives are: (1) Stay on current chips (fails on thermal/efficiency goals); (2) Switch to the lagging supplier's future roadmap (gambles on unproven tech); (3) Hybrid line (increases complexity/cost). Doing nothing yields inferior products. The proposed switch beats stagnation.

**Pre-mortem**: Failure occurs if the translation layer performance is unacceptable for pro users or if the timeline slips beyond 24 months, leaving a gap competitors exploit. This is a two-way door technically (can revert per model), but strategically one-way (market perception shifts). Cost of delay is losing the mobile form-factor war.

**Evidence**: Premise relies on the stated fact that the current supplier lags the dominant maker in efficiency. No contradictory data provided.

**Blockers**: NONE. The risks cited (sales freeze, dev rebuilds, speed, brand dilution, past pain) are execution challenges, not factual impossibilities. None prove the plan fails as proposed; they define the kill criteria for the pilot (e.g., translation overhead >20%, sales drop >15% post-announcement).

**Risks**: Execution complexity, developer churn, temporary performance deficit in translated apps. These become kill criteria: if benchmark parity isn't met by month 18, pivot marketing to niche only.

**Kill Criteria**: Halt full rollout if translated app performance is <80% of native equivalent on key pro workflows by Q4 2006. Cap initial spend to first two product lines.

The proposal addresses the only fact that matters: the current path is dead. Risks are manageable; alternatives are worse.

DECISION: GO
