First call: NO-GO. Launching early with high price, scarce software, and alienated retailers contradicts basic market entry logic for a hardware platform dependent on ecosystem support.

**Steelman:** An early, limited launch secures mindshare and tests the channel before the competitor's full arrival, allowing course correction.
**Attack:** This fails because the console's value is entirely software-dependent. Releasing with "few games" at a $100 premium ($399 vs $299) offers consumers no reason to buy and every reason to wait. The "hard to program" constraint means the early window will not yield sufficient quality titles to justify the hardware cost. Alienating excluded retailers removes critical distribution capacity needed for a mass-market product, while the competitor gains a clear narrative of superior value and availability.

**Options:**
1.  **Proposed Early Launch:** High cost, extreme risk of brand damage, low reversibility once price/retailer trust is lost.
2.  **Do Nothing (Wait for September):** Neutral cost, aligns with developer schedules, matches competitor timing, preserves retailer relationships.
3.  **Staged Announcement Only:** Announce in May, ship in September. Captures mindshare without risking product failure or channel retaliation.

**Fit:** A hardware launch requires a robust software library. The proposal admits this is missing.
**Pre-mortem:** The console launches to empty shelves and angry partners; the competitor undercuts price by $100 with a full game lineup; the brand is permanently tagged as "overpriced and empty."
**Evidence:** The proposal itself states: few games ready, developers unprepared, difficult architecture, higher price, and potential retailer retaliation. These are not risks; they are stated facts that guarantee failure.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Value Proposition Failure:** $399 price with no games vs. competitor's $299 with games.
2.  **Channel Sabotage:** Excluding retailers invites retaliation in a channel-dependent business.
3.  **Supply Chain Misalignment:** Developers explicitly did not plan for this date; software will not exist.

**Risks:** Brand perception damage, lost developer trust.

**Kill Criteria:** If the launch cannot include at least 10 playable titles and 80% retail coverage, do not ship.

The proposal asks to execute a launch that fails on price, content, and distribution simultaneously.

DECISION: NO-GO
