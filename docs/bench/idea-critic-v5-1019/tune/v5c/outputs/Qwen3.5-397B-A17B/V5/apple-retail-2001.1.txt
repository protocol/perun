First call: NO-GO. A loss-making manufacturer with 3–5% share entering a low-margin, high-fixed-cost business during a recession contradicts basic survival logic.

**Steelman:** Direct retail is the only way to demonstrate the new digital-music strategy and control the customer experience, which third-party retailers actively undermine by pushing cheaper competitors.

**Attack:**
1.  **Financial Suicide:** The company is already losing money. Adding high mall leases and inventory costs creates massive operating leverage. In a recession, sales volatility could trigger immediate insolvency.
2.  **Channel Warfare:** Opening stores directly competes with the existing resellers who provide the bulk of current volume. Resellers will retaliate by deprioritizing the brand further or dropping it entirely, causing an immediate revenue cliff before new stores mature.
3.  **Execution Risk:** The proposal admits the industry has a "poor retail track record." Attempting a complex real-estate and retail operation while fixing core manufacturing losses splits management focus dangerously.
4.  **Alternatives:** The "digital-music strategy" demonstration could be achieved via lower-cost pop-up kiosks, dedicated sections within existing partner stores (paid for by the manufacturer), or online interactive tools, avoiding long-term lease liability.

**Pre-mortem:** Two years later, the company is bankrupt. The stores failed to generate sufficient volume to cover rent during the downturn, and angry resellers dropped the product line, destroying the remaining wholesale revenue. The core business collapsed under the weight of retail losses.

**Blockers:**
*   **Financial Mismatch:** Current losses + high fixed costs + recession = high probability of cash exhaustion.
*   **Channel Conflict:** The plan antagonizes the primary existing sales channel without a guaranteed replacement volume.
*   **Strategic Distraction:** Fixing manufacturing losses requires focus; retail expansion diffuses it.

**Risks:** Inventory obsolescence, inability to secure prime locations, continued consumer preference for cheaper alternatives.

**Kill Criteria:** If a pilot store cannot prove positive unit economics within 6 months, or if reseller revenue drops more than 10% post-announcement, the program must halt immediately.

The proposal fails the "do no harm" test for a distressed company. The risks are existential, and the alternatives are not exhausted.

DECISION: NO-GO
