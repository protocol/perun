First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks to launch and widely promote a product where the core value proposition (taste and ease of use) is explicitly flagged as likely failed in testing, and the primary marketing angle (health/cleaner) faces immediate regulatory prohibition.

Steelman: Launching a heated tobacco product could capture health-conscious smokers if the taste matches combustion and regulations permit "cleaner" claims.

Attack:
1.  **Product-Market Fit:** The proposal admits smokers may dislike the taste and lighting mechanism. If the sensory experience differs negatively from the incumbent (ordinary cigarettes), adoption will stall regardless of marketing spend. A "complicated" delivery system contradicts the frictionless nature of the category.
2.  **Regulatory Blocker:** Promoting the product as a "cleaner way to smoke" constitutes a health claim. In 1988, regulatory bodies (like the FTC and emerging FDA scrutiny) strictly prohibit implied health benefits for tobacco without rigorous, pre-approved scientific consensus, which this aerosol likely lacks. Wide promotion on this basis invites immediate injunctions and fines.
3.  **Sunk Cost Fallacy:** The "very large sum" already spent is irrelevant to the forward-looking decision. Investing further in launch and promotion amplifies losses if the product fails the two critical gates above.

Alternatives:
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoids regulatory backlash and marketing waste; preserves capital.
*   **Pivot to Niche:** Test in limited markets without health claims, focusing on convenience rather than "cleanliness," but this contradicts the "wide promotion" request.

Pre-mortem: The product launches widely. Regulators ban the "cleaner" advertising within weeks. Smokers reject the complex lighting and altered taste. Sales collapse. The company faces reputational damage for misleading health implications and writes off the launch costs.

Blockers:
*   **Regulatory Prohibition:** Marketing as "cleaner" is legally actionable without proven, accepted health benefits.
*   **Consumer Rejection:** Explicit risk that taste and usability are inferior to ordinary cigarettes.

Risks: High development costs cannot be recovered if the product sits on shelves.

The proposal demands wide promotion based on a claim regulators will likely block, for a product users may reject. The sunk cost is not a justification for launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
