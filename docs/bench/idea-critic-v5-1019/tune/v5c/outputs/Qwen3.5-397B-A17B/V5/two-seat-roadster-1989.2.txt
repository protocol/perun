First call: GO. The market void and low capital requirements for a lightweight, front-engine, rear-drive layout suggest a viable niche despite segment shrinkage.

**Steelman:** A low-cost, lightweight roadster can succeed by targeting underserved enthusiasts with a simple, affordable package that avoids the complexity and cost of modern safety-heavy convertibles, leveraging existing components to minimize heritage gaps.

**Options:**
1.  **Proposed:** High upside, low entry cost, reversible via limited run.
2.  **Do nothing:** Zero risk, zero reward; cedes niche permanently.
3.  **Safe alternative:** Hardtop coupe version. Lower risk, but loses the "roadster" appeal and unique selling proposition.
The proposal beats "do nothing" by capturing a dormant market; the coupe alternative fails to address the specific demand for open-top driving.

**Fit:** Assumes the company needs brand revitalization and volume in a low-margin segment. A sound idea moving the north star of "accessible driving pleasure."

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if safety compliance costs inflate the price beyond "affordable," or if the lack of heritage prevents enthusiast trust. It is a two-way door; production can halt after a pilot run. Cost of delay: competitors (or used market) fill the void; enthusiast interest wanes.

**Evidence:** Facts state mass-market makers deem it a "dead niche," implying low competition. The "shrinking segment" is a risk, not a blocker, if the target is specific enthusiasts rather than mass volume. No internal data contradicts the feasibility of a lightweight, front-engine, rear-drive layout.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks (shrinking segment, safety costs, no heritage) are known variables, not factual contradictions. The proposal acknowledges them; none prevent execution as described.
**Risks:** Sales fading after early adopters; safety regulations increasing costs. These become kill criteria: stop production if sales drop below X units/month after Month 6, or if compliance costs exceed Y% of the budget.

**Kill Criteria:** Pilot run capped at 5,000 units. Stop if sell-through <80% in first 12 months. Leading metric: Pre-order conversion rate. Review date: 6 months post-launch.

DECISION: GO
