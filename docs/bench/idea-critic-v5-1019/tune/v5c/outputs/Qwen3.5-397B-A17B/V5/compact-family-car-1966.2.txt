First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to high fixed costs (dedicated network, new plant utilization) while entering a segment with established low-price rivals and uncertain demand preference (cheaper vs. larger).

Steelman: A dedicated 1100cc compact leverages the new plant's capacity and captures family buyers through a controlled sales experience, differentiating from generic low-price rivals.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Avoids network cost but leaves new plant underutilized, bleeding capital.
2. Shared network: Sell through existing channels to save cost, risking brand dilution and lower focus.
3. Proposed plan: High investment in dedicated network and specific engine size; highest risk if volume targets miss.

Fit: Assumes the strategy prioritizes volume to amortize the new plant. If the north star is profitability or brand prestige, this low-margin, high-volume play conflicts.

Pre-mortem: The plan fails if the 1100cc engine is perceived as underpowered against larger rivals or too expensive against cheaper ones. The dedicated network becomes a sunk cost anchor if sales lag, preventing pivot to shared channels. The plant runs below capacity, unit costs soar, and losses compound.

Evidence: No data provided on buyer preference for 1100cc vs. competitors' offerings, nor price elasticity. The claim that a dedicated network is required is unproven; it may be a vanity metric rather than a necessity. The assumption that the plant *must* run near capacity immediately is a financial constraint, not a market reality.

Blockers:
1. Unverified demand: No evidence buyers want an 1100cc car over cheaper or larger alternatives.
2. Cost structure: The dedicated network adds massive overhead before revenue is proven, violating the "cheaper alternative" test (shared network).
3. Plant utilization pressure: Building a specific car to fill a plant is production-push, not market-pull; if the specific 1100cc niche fails, the whole plant suffers.

Risks: Rival price wars, shift in preference to larger cars, network underperformance.

Kill criteria missing: No pilot market, no cap on network rollout, no metric for "near capacity" timeline. The plan bets the company on a single engine size and distribution model without a staged entry. A cheaper path (shared network, smaller initial run) reaches the same upside (plant usage) with less downside.

DECISION: NO-GO
