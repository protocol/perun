First call: NO-GO. The proposal mandates a high-cost, irreversible brand reset while explicitly admitting zero validation of customer sentiment, which is the core asset being altered.

Steelman: The brand must shed its dated aesthetic immediately to survive, and a sudden, total rollout prevents dilution and forces market acceptance faster than a phased approach.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Retain equity but risk perceived stagnation. Low cost, zero backlash risk.
2. **Phased/Tested Rollout:** Test new mark in limited channels, measure sentiment, iterate. Moderate cost, contained risk, higher probability of long-term success.
3. **Proposal (Big Bang):** Total immediate replacement. High cost, extreme backlash risk, unverified upside.
The proposal fails to beat "do nothing" because the risk of alienating the loyal base (the only guaranteed revenue) outweighs the unproven gain of looking "contemporary."

Fit: Assuming the north star is sustainable growth, alienating the core customer base without data contradicts this goal. A contemporary look means nothing if the customer base abandons the brand.

Pre-mortem: The rollout happens; loyal customers interpret the removal of the iconic square as a betrayal or a sale to new owners. Social media amplifies this narrative before the company can explain. Sales dip sharply in Q3. Because there was no testing, the company cannot pivot back without admitting a massive error, destroying credibility. This is a one-way door; you cannot un-ring the bell.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits the design was "not tested with customers" and acknowledges "loyal customers are attached to the old logo." These are stated facts that directly contradict the strategy of "no advance announcement."

Blockers:
1. **Stated Fact Contradiction:** The proposal acknowledges high attachment to the old logo and zero testing, yet proceeds with the highest-risk implementation method (immediate, total swap). This guarantees a preventable crisis.
2. **Missing Validation:** A rebrand of this magnitude requires at least a leading metric from a pilot group before full commitment. None exists.

Risks: Backlash intensity, sales drop, loss of brand recognition. These are not mere risks; given the lack of testing, they are probable outcomes.

Kill Criteria: If the company cannot produce a single data point suggesting the new logo performs neutrally or better with core demographics, the project must stop.

DECISION: NO-GO
