First call: GO. The proposal targets a massive, underserved market segment with a radically simplified, cost-effective solution, despite valid concerns about coverage and brand trust.

**Steelman:** A low-cost, randomized robot vacuum can achieve sufficient cleaning for daily maintenance at a price point inaccessible to mapping-based competitors, creating a new mass market rather than competing in the niche premium segment.

**Attack & Analysis:**
*   **Coverage Risk:** Randomized movement is a known limitation, but for a "low-cost" daily maintainer, "good enough" coverage at 1/3rd the price of rivals is a viable trade-off. The lack of guaranteed spot cleaning is a feature of the cost structure, not a fatal flaw.
*   **Perception Risk:** While mapping seems "smarter," consumers often prioritize price and convenience over technical sophistication for entry-level appliances. Marketing can frame simplicity as reliability and affordability.
*   **Experience Gap:** The company lacks consumer experience, but this is mitigated by the product's mechanical simplicity (no complex sensors/software) and the potential to partner with existing retail distributors rather than building a direct sales force immediately.
*   **Trust Barrier:** Early adopters and price-sensitive buyers are often willing to trial novel concepts if the financial risk is low. A low price point reduces the barrier to entry significantly.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Cede the emerging low-cost robot market to future competitors; remain dependent on industrial contracts.
*   *Build Mapper:* Prohibitively expensive, high risk, direct competition with entrenched players; likely failure for a robotics firm new to consumers.
*   *Licensing:* The two uninterested floor-care companies rejected it; waiting for other licensees delays market entry and captures less value.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the random cleaning is perceived as useless noise rather than helpful maintenance, or if reliability issues plague the cheap unit. However, the low price allows for a "disposable" perception that forgives minor flaws.

**Blockers:** NONE. The lack of interest from two specific incumbents does not prove market failure; they may be protecting existing high-margin product lines. The proposal explicitly addresses the cost barrier.

**Risks:** Brand dilution, higher return rates due to unmet expectations. These are managed by clear marketing setting expectations for "daily touch-ups" vs. "deep cleans."

**Kill Criteria:** Halt if return rates exceed 15% in the first quarter or if unit economics cannot sustain the low price point after 10,000 units.

DECISION: GO
