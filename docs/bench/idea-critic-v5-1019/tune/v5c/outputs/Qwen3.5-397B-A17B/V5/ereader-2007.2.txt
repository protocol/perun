First call: GO. The proposal solves the core friction of digital books (need a computer, slow download) and aligns with the company's catalog strength, despite hardware inexperience.

**Steelman:** A dedicated, cellular-enabled device removes purchase friction and piracy fears (via controlled ecosystem), creating a new high-margin revenue stream that complements rather than replaces print.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Cedes the digital future to competitors; print sales eventually decline regardless.
2.  **Software App:** Requires users to have PCs/smartphones (rare in 2007 target demo) and lacks the "paper-like" reading experience; fails to solve the discovery/friction gap.
3.  **Partner with Hardware Makers:** Dilutes control over pricing, user experience, and the integrated cellular billing model essential for impulse buys.

**Fit:** Moves the north star of "any book, anywhere, instantly." The hardware risk is mitigated by outsourcing manufacturing while retaining design and ecosystem control.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the device is too expensive, the screen is poor, or publishers withhold content. However, the proposal addresses content via revenue share (low price/high volume) and experience via specific "paper-like" tech.

**Evidence & Economics:** The "single low price" model mimics the music industry's successful shift to $0.99 singles, suggesting volume can offset margin compression. Cellular connectivity eliminates the "computer required" barrier that killed earlier e-readers. Cannibalization is a feature, not a bug: it captures revenue that would otherwise be lost to piracy or competitors when print naturally declines.

**Blockers:** None. The risks cited (hardware inexperience, margin pressure, cannibalization) are execution challenges, not factual contradictions. The company has the capital to hire hardware talent or acquire it. The "low price" is a strategic lever to drive ecosystem lock-in, not a margin error.

**Kill Criteria:** If pilot sales do not reach 10,000 units in Q1, or if publisher participation remains below 50% of top titles by launch, halt expansion. Leading metric: Books downloaded per device per month.

The proposal transforms a bookseller into a platform. The risks are manageable; the cost of delay is losing the digital transition entirely.

DECISION: GO
