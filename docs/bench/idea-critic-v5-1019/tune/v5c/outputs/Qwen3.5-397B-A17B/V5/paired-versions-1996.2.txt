First call: GO. The mechanic directly converts the console's primary limitation (aging, solitary play) into its strongest asset (social necessity), creating organic marketing through player interdependence.

Steelman: Releasing two distinct versions forces social interaction to complete the core gameplay loop, transforming a single-player purchase into a mandatory two-player transaction that leverages the existing link cable infrastructure to drive double unit sales per social pair.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Release one version. Risk: Low engagement, no viral loop, standard sales trajectory for a dying platform.
2. Single version with unlockable content: Risk: Removes the need for a second buyer, killing the revenue multiplier and social pressure.
3. Two versions (Proposed): Cost: Double asset management. Risk: Consumer backlash. Upside: Doubles addressable market per social group, creates scarcity-driven urgency, and necessitates the link cable, extending hardware relevance.

Fit: Assumes the goal is maximizing software attachment and extending hardware life via social stickiness. This aligns perfectly with a "north star" of community-driven retention.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the creature differences are perceived as cosmetic rather than essential, or if the link cable penetration is too low to facilitate trading. However, the "fear of missing out" on exclusive creatures drives demand regardless of initial cable ownership; players will buy cables to trade. Cost of delay: High. Waiting allows competitors to define the handheld's legacy or the platform to die completely.

Evidence: The proposal relies on the psychological principle of completionism and social obligation. No external data is needed; the mechanic itself generates the evidence through playtesting.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (declining popularity, price grab perception) are actually features. Declining popularity means the installed base needs a reason to engage; this provides it. "Price grab" is mitigated by the perceived value of exclusive content. The need for a second player is the engine of growth, not a bug.

Risks: Execution quality of creature design; ensuring trades are technically seamless. These are managed by making trade mechanics simple and creatures distinct.

Kill criteria: If playtesting shows less than 40% of users attempt a trade within the first two hours, pivot to in-game events. Leading metric: Ratio of link cable activations per unit sold.

DECISION: GO
