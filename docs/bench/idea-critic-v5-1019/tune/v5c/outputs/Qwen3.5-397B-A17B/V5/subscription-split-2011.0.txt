First call: NO-GO. A 60% price hike followed immediately by a forced split into two brands, two logins, and two bills treats loyal customers as adversaries, guaranteeing mass churn and brand destruction.

**Steelman:** Separating a declining legacy unit (DVD) from a high-growth unit (Streaming) allows distinct strategies and prevents the legacy cost structure from dragging down the growth narrative.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "fit" and "pre-mortem" tests catastrophically. The context is a recent 60% price increase for combined service. Executing a split now confirms the customer's worst fear: they are being punished twice. First by higher costs, then by fractured utility (no shared queues, no shared ratings). The "risk" of customer confusion is understated; this is active hostility. A pre-mortem reveals immediate subscription cancellations, viral negative press, and a collapse in trust that no amount of "distinct management" can offset. The loss of cross-service recommendations devalues both products. The alternative—"do nothing" or a gradual, incentivized migration—beats this plan because it preserves the customer relationship while the market shifts. The stated rationale (declining DVD business) is true, but the proposed solution (abrupt separation post-hike) contradicts the goal of retaining revenue during the transition.

**Blockers:**
1. **Timing vs. Price Shock:** The proposal ignores the compounding negative effect of executing a structural split immediately after a 60% price hike. The combined friction creates an undeniable incentive to cancel entirely.
2. **Value Destruction:** Explicitly removing shared ratings and queues reduces the utility of the remaining subscriptions without offering a compensating benefit, violating the basic premise of customer retention.
3. **Contradictory Evidence:** The proposal cites "reaction to the price increase" as a risk, yet the plan amplifies that specific trigger rather than mitigating it.

**Risks:** Total brand reputational collapse, accelerated DVD decline due to forced abandonment, and streaming growth stalling due to associated backlash.

**Kill Criteria:** If customer churn exceeds 5% in the first month or net promoter score drops below -20, the split must be reversed immediately. However, given the current facts, the plan is self-defeating before launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
