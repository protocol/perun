First call: NO-GO. The plan explicitly dismantles the target's primary competitive moat to impose the acquirer's legacy model.

**Steelman:** The acquisition leverages the buyer's scale to accelerate the tea brand's growth by replacing inefficient independent distribution with mass-market reach and professional advertising.

**Attack:** The steelman fails because it assumes the brand's value derives from volume potential rather than its current "cult" positioning. The proposal admits the brand relies on a "small, quirky network" and "loyalty." Moving to "supermarket-focused distribution" and "mass-market advertising" directly attacks the exclusivity and authenticity that drive that loyalty. This is not scaling; it is repositioning a premium niche product as a commodity, inviting direct competition with the very "large soft-drink companies" cited as a risk. The buyer intends to fix what isn't broken, destroying the unique equity justifying the $1.7 billion price tag.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Avoids destroying the target's value; costs $0.
2.  **Acquire and preserve:** Keep independent distributors and niche marketing; higher integration complexity but protects the premium margin.
3.  **Proposed plan:** High risk of brand dilution, distributor revolt, and margin compression.

**Fit:** The strategy contradicts the "premium" nature of the asset. Applying a sports drink playbook to a cult tea brand ignores category differences.

**Pre-mortem:** One year post-acquisition, the brand loses its "cool" factor, independent distributors drop the line due to conflict with mass channels, and sales flatten as the product becomes just another shelf option among giant competitors. The $1.7 billion becomes a write-down.

**Blockers:** The proposal's core mechanism (mass distribution/ads) contradicts the stated source of value (cult image/quirky network). The plan guarantees the risks it identifies (loyalty loss, image erosion) rather than mitigating them.

**Risks:** Competition, price integration. These are secondary to the fundamental strategic mismatch.

**Kill Criteria:** If the brand's premium price point drops >10% within 18 months or distributor churn exceeds 20%, the integration strategy must be halted and reverted to the original model.

The plan as proposed destroys the asset it seeks to buy.

DECISION: NO-GO
