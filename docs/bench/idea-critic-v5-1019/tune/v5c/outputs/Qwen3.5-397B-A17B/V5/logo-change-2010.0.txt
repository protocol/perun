First call: NO-GO. The proposal mandates a high-risk, irreversible brand transformation without validation, ignoring explicit risks regarding customer attachment and backlash potential.

**Steelman:** A simultaneous, unannounced global rebrand maximizes shock value to signal a decisive break from the past, forcing immediate market recognition of the "contemporary" shift.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** The proposal ignores "do nothing" and cheaper, safer alternatives like phased rollouts, A/B testing in specific markets, or soft launches. A staged approach offers the same upside (modernization) with significantly lower risk of alienating the core base.
2.  **Fit:** While aiming for a contemporary look, the method contradicts the fundamental retail principle of knowing your customer. Ignoring the stated risk that "loyal customers are attached" suggests a strategy misaligned with revenue preservation.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume the launch failed. Why? Because the core demographic felt alienated by the sudden loss of the recognized symbol (the dark blue square), leading to a social media firestorm and sales drop. The "no advance announcement" clause removes any buffer to manage this narrative.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits the new design was "not tested with customers." In 2010, social media backlash was already a potent force; proceeding without data is speculative gambling, not strategy.
5.  **Blocker:** The combination of "no testing" and "no advance announcement" creates a single point of failure. If the new wordmark fails to resonate (a high probability given no validation), the brand has no fallback and has already burned the bridge to the old identity globally. This is a one-way door entered blind.

**Kill Criteria Check:** The proposal lacks any metric for success, any pilot program, and any rollback plan. The stated risks (backlash, attachment) are treated as obstacles to ignore rather than variables to measure. A sound plan must beat "do nothing"; this plan loses to a simple pilot test.

**Verdict:** The absence of customer testing and the decision to suppress announcement turns a strategic pivot into a reckless gamble. The risk of immediate, unmitigated backlash outweighs the potential speed of adoption.

DECISION: NO-GO
