First call: NO-GO. The proposal seeks to sell a premium subscription for content format (short-form) that the market currently satisfies with free, ad-supported alternatives, while ignoring stated "subscription fatigue."

**Steelman:** A high-fidelity, mobile-native premium video service can succeed by leveraging A-list talent and superior technology to differentiate from free user-generated content, creating a new category before competitors do.

**Attack:**
1.  **Value Mismatch:** The core value proposition (professional short-form) conflicts with the payment model (subscription). Users accept short-form as a low-attention, free commodity. Asking for a recurring fee for <10-minute episodes creates a high friction point against abundant free substitutes.
2.  **Market Timing:** Launching a new subscription service during peak "subscription fatigue" with no proven demand for *premium* short-form is speculative. The presence of major studio investors does not validate consumer willingness to pay; it only validates supply-side interest.
3.  **Alternative:** The "do nothing" or "wait" option is superior. Let competitors test the waters with lower-cost models (AVOD or hybrid) to prove demand for premium short-form before committing $1B+ to a pure subscription play.
4.  **Blocker:** The proposal admits "no proven demand" yet commits massive capital. The stated fact that free alternatives are "abundant" directly contradicts the assumption that users will pay a premium for the same duration format, regardless of production value. The technology feature (orientation) is a hygiene factor, not a differentiator worth a subscription.

**Risks vs. Blockers:**
*   *Risk:* Competition for attention. (Mitigation: Marketing).
*   *Blocker:* The economic model relies on converting users from a free habit to a paid one without evidence they value the specific "premium" attribute enough to overcome fatigue. The proposal's own admission of unproven demand, combined with the abundance of free substitutes, constitutes a fundamental flaw in the revenue hypothesis.

**Kill Criteria Check:** No pilot, cap, or metric is proposed to test demand before the $1B raise. The plan is an all-or-nothing bet on an unproven behavior change.

DECISION: NO-GO
