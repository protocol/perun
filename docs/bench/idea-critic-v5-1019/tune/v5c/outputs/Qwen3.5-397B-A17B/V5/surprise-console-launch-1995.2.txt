First call: NO-GO. Launching early with high price, scarce software, and alienated retailers contradicts the fundamental requirements for a successful console platform launch.

**Steelman:** An early May launch at $399 secures a four-month head start to establish market presence before the competitor arrives, leveraging exclusivity to capture early adopters despite limited inventory.

**Attack:**
1.  **Price Disadvantage:** At $100 above the rival, the console is perceived as poor value, especially with few games. Early adopters are price-sensitive to software libraries; a hardware premium without software depth is unsustainable.
2.  **Software Void:** Consoles live or die by games. Launching before developers are ready guarantees a "shovelware" or empty shelf scenario, damaging the brand's reputation for quality before the mass market arrives in September.
3.  **Channel Hostility:** Shipping only to select retailers creates immediate antagonism with major partners. In a hardware-constrained environment, alienating distribution channels ensures poor placement and lack of promotional support when volume finally arrives.
4.  **Technical Friction:** If the machine is hard to program for 3D, rushing the date exacerbates the lack of compelling launch titles, as developers need *more* time, not less, to overcome architectural hurdles.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing (Wait for September):** Aligns with developer schedules, allows price parity or competitive positioning, and ensures full retail support.
*   **Soft Launch (Limited Regions):** Test the waters without burning national retailer relationships or setting a premature price anchor.

**Pre-mortem:** The May launch fails because the high price and lack of games drive consumers to wait for the cheaper, better-supported rival in September. Retailers excluded from the May drop refuse to prioritize the console later, starving it of shelf space. The "head start" becomes a "head fake," depleting marketing budget and goodwill before the real race begins.

**Blockers:**
*   **Price:** $399 vs. rival's $299 is a decisive disadvantage with no software library to justify the premium.
*   **Supply Chain:** Alienating key retailers creates a distribution bottleneck that cannot be fixed by September.
*   **Software:** Developers explicitly unprepared; early launch guarantees a weak lineup.

**Risks:** Brand damage, developer churn, retailer retaliation.

The proposal sacrifices long-term ecosystem health for a symbolic four-month window that offers no tangible advantage given the product's current state.

DECISION: NO-GO
