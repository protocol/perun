First call: GO. A dormant, high-recognition space-fantasy franchise with a retiring founder offers a rare, consolidatable asset to feed parks, products, and film, justifying a premium despite recent mixed reviews.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the entire franchise secures full IP control, enabling a coordinated trilogy and spin-offs to revitalize the brand and maximize cross-divisional synergy, which partial licensing cannot achieve.

**Options:**
1.  **Acquire:** High upfront cost ($4B) but captures 100% of future upside and operational control. Reversible only via resale at a likely loss.
2.  **License/Partner:** Lower cost, but leaves value on the table and limits park/experience integration. Fails to solve the "feeding all businesses" goal.
3.  **Do Nothing:** Zero cost, zero risk, but forfeits the only available asset of this scale and fit.

**Fit:** Directly serves the north star of integrated franchise exploitation across parks, consumer products, and studios.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if new films flop, cementing the "mixed review" trend and devaluing the $4B entry price. This is a two-way door financially (assets can be sold), but a one-way door for reputation and opportunity cost. Delay risks the founder selling to a competitor or the IP fading further into obscurity.

**Evidence:** The proposal states the founder is retiring (creating a forced sale window) and the company needs a franchise to feed all divisions. The $4B price is high relative to current output but must be judged against the *potential* of a revitalized global brand, not just recent film performance. Merchandise licensing gaps are an opportunity for recapture, not a deficit.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks (mixed reviews, creative lead departure, high price) are execution challenges, not factual contradictions. The proposal acknowledges them; none prove the plan fails as proposed. The "high price" is a negotiation point, not a deal-breaker given the strategic necessity.

**Risks:** Creative misstep in new trilogy; fan backlash; integration friction. These become kill criteria: if the first film's opening weekend undershoots projections by >20%, halt spin-off production.

**Kill Criteria:** First film must achieve a specific box-office threshold (to be defined in due diligence) within 3 years; otherwise, pivot to low-budget streaming content only.

DECISION: GO
