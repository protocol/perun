First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a capital-intensive path while explicitly admitting the company lacks the funds for the factory and batteries required.

Steelman: Becoming a pure-play EV manufacturer by acquiring an existing large factory is the fastest route to scale, provided capital can be raised against the asset.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Survive as a design house; zero execution risk, zero upside.
2. Contract manufacturing: Build the sedan using an established partner's lines; lower capex, shared margin, proven production.
3. Proposed plan: Buy a large factory and go it alone; maximum capex, total control, highest bankruptcy risk.
The proposal fails to beat "do nothing" because it relies on capital the company admits it does not have. Contract manufacturing reaches the same upside (a produced car) with significantly less upfront cash and lower reversibility costs.

Fit: Assuming the goal is to sell cars, buying a factory before securing funding for batteries is a mismatch of stage and resources.

Pre-mortem: The company exhausts its cash purchasing the shell of a factory, leaving no liquidity for battery tooling or supply chains. Production never starts; the asset is sold at a loss. This is a one-way door; selling a specialized factory is slow and costly. The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of immediate insolvency.

Evidence: The proposal itself states the company has "far less capital than needed" for the core requirements (factory and packs). No source is cited for how this gap will be closed.

Blockers:
1. Funding Gap: The plan requires buying a factory and tooling for batteries, but the text explicitly states the company lacks this capital. Proceeding as proposed is mathematically impossible without an unproven financing miracle.
2. Superior Alternative: Contract manufacturing achieves the goal (producing the sedan) without the prohibitive fixed cost of buying a plant, directly addressing the stated capital constraint.

Risks: Charging scarcity, competitor response, and lack of mass production experience are valid risks but secondary to the immediate inability to fund the plan.

Kill criteria: If financing for the full factory purchase and battery tooling is not secured in hand before signing, the plan must stop.

DECISION: NO-GO
