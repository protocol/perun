**Steelman:** Partnering allows the company to leverage its unique animation tech and creative voice to pioneer a new genre (feature-length CG) while offloading distribution risk and financing to a major studio that explicitly covets their lead talent.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Remain a short-film/commercial shop; risk losing the lead animator to the competitor eventually; never test the feature market.
2.  **Self-fund:** Impossible given rendering costs and lack of distribution infrastructure; high probability of bankruptcy before completion.
3.  **Wait for tech:** Rendering speeds will improve, but first-mover advantage in a new medium and securing the specific creative team now is time-sensitive.

**Fit:** Aligns with the north star of proving computer animation as a viable storytelling medium for features. The "toy's point of view" concept leverages the medium's strength (synthetic worlds) while mitigating the "uncanny valley" risk of human characters.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the studio interferes creatively, causing delays that explode rendering budgets, or if the story fails to resonate because the "toy perspective" feels gimmicky. However, the studio's eagerness suggests they want the company's specific vision, not just their labor.

**Evidence & Math:** The studio's attempt to hire the lead animator proves the asset's value. Rendering costs are high but finite; the deal structure (studio as publisher) implies they absorb the downside. No data suggests the target audience rejects CG; shorts have already proven appeal.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (inexperience, cost, partner power) are inherent to the opportunity, not facts that make the plan impossible. The partner's "stronger hand" is the price of entry for distribution and capital.

**Risks:** Creative conflict, budget overruns, audience rejection of the format. These become kill criteria: monitor production milestones against budget caps and retain final creative approval on story beats.

**Kill Criteria:** If the studio mandates creative changes that dilute the core "toy perspective" concept, or if rendering costs exceed the capped budget by >15% without a corresponding increase in finished footage, pause and renegotiate.

DECISION: GO
