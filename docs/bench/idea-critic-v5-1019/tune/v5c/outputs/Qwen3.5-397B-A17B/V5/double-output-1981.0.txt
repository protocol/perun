First call: NO-GO. Doubling production while sales lag by 60% during a recession with rising interest rates destroys cash without a verified buyer.

Steelman: The claim assumes that flooding the market now secures future market share or forces dealer acceptance before competitors react, betting that demand will rebound sharply enough to absorb a massive inventory spike immediately.

Options:
1. **Double Production**: Maximizes cash burn, increases financing costs on unsold units, and risks dealer insolvency. Upside: speculative market dominance if recession ends tomorrow.
2. **Do Nothing/Reduce**: Preserves cash, aligns output with demand, prevents inventory bloating. Upside: Survival.
3. **Incentivize Sales**: Use limited cash for consumer rebates or dealer financing support rather than building more cars. Upside: Clears existing stock without adding new liability.

Fit: A start-up's north star is survival and product-market fit. This proposal contradicts the basic economic reality stated: demand is falling, and capital is expensive.

Pre-mortem: The company runs out of cash in six months. Dealers refuse further deliveries due to full lots and high borrowing costs. The company enters bankruptcy with 15,000 unsold cars depreciating in storage. This is a one-way door; once cash is spent on production, it cannot be recovered.

Evidence: The proposal's own facts show 4,500 unsold units (7,500 produced - 3,000 sold). Interest rates are rising, increasing the cost to hold this inventory. Cash is limited. There is no evidence of pent-up demand or a mechanism to force dealers to accept more stock.

Blockers:
1. **Cash Flow Mismatch**: Doubling output requires immediate cash for parts and labor, while sales revenue is shrinking. The stated "limited cash" cannot support this expansion against a backdrop of rising financing costs.
2. **Inventory Saturation**: With 60% of production unsold, the channel is blocked. Adding more supply without demand guarantees a logistics and financing crisis.

Risks: Recession deepening, dealer bankruptcies, total liquidity failure.

Kill Criteria: If existing inventory does not drop by 20% in the next quarter, halt all non-essential spending. The leading metric is "Cash Burn Rate vs. Sales Revenue."

DECISION: NO-GO
