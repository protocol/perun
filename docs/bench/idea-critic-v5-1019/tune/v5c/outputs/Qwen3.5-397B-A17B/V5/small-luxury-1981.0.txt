First call: GO. The shift in buyer demographics toward American-brand customers seeking smaller sedans indicates a clear market gap that the current lineup does not address. Ignoring this trend risks ceding share to competitors who might fill the void first.

Steelman: Leveraging an existing mass-market platform allows for rapid entry into the compact luxury segment with controlled capital expenditure, while premium trim and exclusive branding maintain the perception of luxury distinct from the parent company's volume models.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Risk losing the emerging demographic entirely to rivals offering smaller, accessible luxury options.
2. Develop a clean-sheet compact platform: Prohibitively expensive and slow, missing the immediate window of opportunity.
3. Import a smaller foreign model: May lack the specific features or brand alignment desired by the target American luxury seeker.

Fit: This aligns with the north star of expanding the customer base without diluting the core brand's identity, provided the differentiation is tangible.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the "badge engineering" is too obvious, causing buyers to feel deceived, or if sales channels fail to distinguish the new model from the mass-market donor, eroding prestige. However, these are execution risks, not fundamental flaws.

Evidence: The proposal cites a verified shift in buyer origin (American-brand seekers) and preference (smaller sedan). The financial logic holds: shared platforms reduce unit costs, allowing margin even at a competitive premium price point.

Blockers: NONE. The risks raised (platform visibility, price justification, salesperson behavior, prestige dilution) are manageable through marketing, training, and distinct design cues, rather than fatal flaws. They do not contradict the core claim that a market opportunity exists.

Risks: Brand dilution and channel confusion. These become kill criteria: if brand perception scores drop or sales staff cannot articulate the difference within six months of launch, the program must be reviewed.

The opportunity to capture a new, growing demographic outweighs the manageable risks of platform sharing, provided the product is distinctly marketed and sold.

DECISION: GO
