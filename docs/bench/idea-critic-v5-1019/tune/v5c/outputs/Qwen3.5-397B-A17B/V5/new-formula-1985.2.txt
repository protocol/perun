First call: NO-GO. The plan bets the entire brand equity on taste preference while ignoring the emotional and heritage value that defines the product's market position.

**Steelman:** Replacing the formula maximizes immediate consumption utility based on blind sensory data, securing relevance against sweeter competitors.

**Attack:** The proposal commits a category error by treating a heritage brand as a commodity where taste is the sole driver. Blind tests explicitly strip away the brand narrative, measuring only liquid chemistry. By withdrawing the original entirely, the plan converts a product preference into an identity crisis. The testing methodology is fatally flawed: it failed to disclose the permanent removal of the original, meaning participants evaluated the new taste as an addition, not a substitution. This omits the "loss aversion" psychological trigger.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Maintain share erosion but preserve brand trust.
2.  **Line Extension:** Launch the sweeter formula as a new variant (e.g., "New Coke") while keeping the original. This captures the taste-preference segment without alienating the heritage base.
3.  **Proposed Plan:** High risk of total backlash due to perceived betrayal.

**Pre-mortem:** The launch triggers immediate public outrage. Loyalists perceive the removal as an arrogant erasure of history. Media narratives shift from "taste test winner" to "corporation kills tradition." Sales plummet not because the drink tastes bad, but because the act of removal validates the emotional attachment critics warned about. Bottlers face chaos managing consumer revolts.

**Evidence & Logic:** The proposal admits risks of "emotional attachment" and "brand heritage" yet offers no mitigation other than the hope that taste overrides emotion. History and behavioral economics suggest otherwise: people value what they can lose more than what they might gain. The absence of a fallback product removes any safety valve for this reaction.

**Blocker:** The testing protocol excluded the critical variable of permanent unavailability. Since the core premise (that taste preference drives the decision) relies on data that ignored the primary counter-force (emotional loss), the plan fails as proposed. A line extension achieves the taste goal without the existential risk.

**Kill Criteria:** If the plan proceeds, stop immediately if day-one sentiment analysis shows >40% negative mention volume regarding "removal" or "heritage."

DECISION: NO-GO
