First call: NO-GO. The proposal discards the brand's primary shelf cue for an unproven aesthetic, directly threatening recognition among its loyal, older demographic.

Steelman: A modernized design will attract new buyers without alienating the core base if supported by heavy advertising.

Attack: The plan fails the "fit" and "evidence" tests. The stated risk—that the old image is the main shelf cue—is treated as a manageable variable rather than a foundational constraint. For an older, loyal base, visual continuity is the product identity; removing the orange-with-straw is not a refresh, it is a deletion of the brand's signal. The proposal offers no pilot data, no A/B test results, and no evidence that a "cap shaped like an orange" compensates for the loss of the literal fruit image.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserves shelf recognition and zero redesign cost.
2. Staged pilot: Test the new design in a limited region to measure recognition drop-off before a national rollout.
3. Hybrid evolution: Gradually introduce modern elements while retaining the iconic straw-orange until equity transfers.

The proposal demands a nationwide, simultaneous launch ("big bang"), making the door one-way and the cost of failure catastrophic. If shoppers fail to recognize the carton, the large advertising campaign cannot fix the shelf-blindness quickly enough to prevent permanent share loss to competitors. The "large advertising campaign" is a bet that awareness can be rebuilt faster than it is lost; there is no evidence this works for older demographics reliant on visual habits.

Blockers:
1. The plan explicitly removes the stated "main shelf cue" without verified proof that the new elements function as effective replacements for the specific target demographic.
2. The "all stores at once" approach eliminates the ability to learn from failure, violating basic risk management for a high-cost, high-visibility change.

Risks:
1. Alienation of the older, loyal base leading to immediate sales collapse.
2. High sunk cost of printing and advertising with no fallback.

Kill criteria (if revised): A regional pilot showing non-negative sales lift and maintained recognition scores among the core demographic before any national commitment.

DECISION: NO-GO
