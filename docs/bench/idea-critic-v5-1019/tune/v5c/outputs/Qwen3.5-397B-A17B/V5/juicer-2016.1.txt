First call: NO-GO. The unit economics and behavioral friction described suggest a business model that cannot achieve sustainable scale.

**Steelman:** A premium, closed-loop ecosystem offering unmatched convenience and freshness verification for health-conscious consumers willing to pay a significant premium.

**Attack:**
1.  **Economics:** At $699, the device price is prohibitive for a trial. The recurring cost of proprietary bags likely exceeds fresh produce by 3-5x once packaging, logistics, and margin are layered. Consumers rarely sustain such premiums for commoditized inputs (fruit).
2.  **Friction:** Requiring Wi-Fi for a basic mechanical function (pressing) introduces unnecessary failure points and privacy concerns without adding proportional value. "Freshness checking" via an app is a solution in search of a problem; visual inspection is free and instantaneous.
3.  **Market:** The "countertop appliance" graveyard is crowded. High-price hardware coupled with a mandatory subscription creates a double-barrier to entry. The addressable market for users willing to lock into a single vendor's expensive bags is likely too small to support the burn rate of a "well-funded" startup expecting venture-scale returns.
4.  **Alternatives:** A cheaper, open-system device accepting standard produce or a lower-cost subscription without hardware lock-in would test demand with less risk.

**Blockers:**
*   **Unit Economics:** The proposed pricing structure (high CapEx for user, high OpEx for consumables) contradicts mass-market adoption curves for kitchen appliances.
*   **Unnecessary Complexity:** Mandatory connectivity for a juicer creates a negative user experience differentiator versus dumb competitors.

**Risks:**
*   Supply chain complexity for perishable proprietary bags.
*   Rapid churn if subscription value proposition fails immediately after purchase.

**Kill Criteria:**
*   If CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost) exceeds $300.
*   If subscription retention drops below 40% after month 3.

The proposal relies on consumers accepting a luxury tax on a daily habit while tolerating artificial constraints. The "smart" features do not justify the price or the closed ecosystem.

DECISION: NO-GO
