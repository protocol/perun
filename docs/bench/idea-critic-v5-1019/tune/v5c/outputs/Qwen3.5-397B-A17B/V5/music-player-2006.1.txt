First call: NO-GO. The proposal enters a saturated market late, abandons a core industry standard (copy-protection) that secures partner support, and relies on unproven wireless sharing that labels may block, all without a price advantage to disrupt the incumbent's loyal ecosystem.

**Steelman:** A premium hardware ecosystem with superior screen and seamless sharing can displace the leader by offering a better user experience, leveraging marketing to overcome lateness.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Strategy:** Abandoning the copy-protection standard alienates existing partners (labels, other device makers) who rely on it. If labels restrict sharing due to piracy fears, the primary differentiator fails immediately.
2.  **Options:** "Do nothing" preserves partner relationships. A "cheaper alternative" (software-only or licensed hardware) avoids the massive capex of a new device and store infrastructure. The proposed "large marketing budget" cannot fix a broken value proposition if content is restricted.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The device launches, but labels refuse to license content for wireless sharing due to lack of DRM. The store launches with a smaller catalog than the incumbent. The high price and late entry result in negligible adoption. The company burns cash on marketing and hardware tooling with no ROI.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits "no clear price advantage" and "late to market." The leader's "enormous share" creates a network effect the proposal does not overcome with features that may be legally blocked.
5.  **Blockers:** The contradiction between dropping copy-protection and securing label cooperation for sharing is a fatal flaw. The plan assumes labels will allow sharing on a non-protected platform despite their stated restrictions; this is unsupported.

**Blockers:**
*   Dropping copy-protection while expecting label cooperation for sharing contradicts industry reality (labels restrict sharing without DRM).
*   No price advantage against a dominant incumbent with a loyal ecosystem makes entry impossible without a proven, unblockable differentiator.

**Risks:**
*   Wireless sharing functionality blocked by licensors.
*   Marketing spend wasted on a compromised value proposition.
*   Hardware inventory stranded due to low adoption.

The plan fails the kill check: the proposal's own facts (label restrictions, no price advantage) point directly to failure of the core mechanism (sharing) and market entry.

DECISION: NO-GO
