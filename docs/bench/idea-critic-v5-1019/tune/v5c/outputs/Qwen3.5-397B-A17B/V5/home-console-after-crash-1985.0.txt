First call: GO. The market collapse is a distribution and perception failure, not a demand extinction; the proposed repositioning directly attacks the root cause.

**Steelman:** By masking the console as an "Entertainment System" with non-game toys (robot, gun) and assuming retailer inventory risk, the company bypasses the "video game" stigma and retailer resistance, securing shelf space to prove demand before the category recovers.

**Alternatives:**
1.  *Do nothing:* Cede the recovering market to home computers; brand remains irrelevant in the West.
2.  *Launch as console:* Immediate rejection by retailers; certain failure due to 1983 trauma.
3.  *Wait for recovery:* Misses the first-mover advantage; recovery may never happen without a catalyst.
Only the proposed path beats "do nothing" by actively reconstructing the category.

**Fit:** Aligns with the north star of global expansion by adapting the product to the local psychological environment rather than forcing the local market to adapt to the product.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the "toy" bundle fails to distract retailers from the console's true nature, or if the lockout chip creates a software drought before critical mass is reached. However, the take-back promise removes the retailer's financial risk, making refusal irrational. The lockout ensures quality control, preventing the saturation that caused the 1983 crash.

**Evidence:** The proposal relies on the stated fact of retailer refusal to stock "video games." The rebranding is the direct counter-measure. The take-back promise addresses the specific fear of unsold inventory.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (retailer refusal, no track record, developer anger) are either solved by the proposal's mechanics (rebranding, buy-back) or are secondary to securing distribution. Developer anger is a manageable trade-off for quality control; without the lockout, the market collapses again.

**Risks:** High cost of returned units if the product fails; potential legal challenges on restraint of trade (lockout). These are financial and legal risks, not blockers to the strategic logic.

**Kill Criteria:** If retailers refuse the "Entertainment System" classification despite the toy bundle and buy-back offer within 6 months, or if software sales per unit sold drop below 1.5 titles within the first year, pivot to direct-to-consumer or exit.

DECISION: GO
