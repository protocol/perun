First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to launch a general consumer product while explicitly acknowledging that the core value proposition ("clear everyday use") is undefined for the target market.

**Steelman:** An invitation-only launch at $1,500 will validate demand and refine the product before a mass-market release, mitigating risk through staged rollout.

**Attack:** The staged rollout fails because the fundamental premise is flawed. The risks listed are not merely execution hurdles; they are existential blockers for the stated goal of an "everyday consumer product."
1.  **Value Mismatch:** A $1,500 price point requires immediate, obvious utility. The proposal admits this utility is unclear. Early adopters may tolerate bugs, but they will not pay a premium for a solution to a problem they don't have.
2.  **Social & Privacy Friction:** Unlike a smartphone, this device records others without explicit interaction. The "privacy objections" and "social acceptance" risks suggest the product creates negative externalities for non-users, leading to active hostility (bans in venues, social stigma) rather than mere indifference.
3.  **Battery Constraint:** Short battery life disqualifies it as an "everyday" replacement for existing tools.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoids reputational damage from a high-profile failure and preserves capital.
*   **Pivot to Enterprise:** Target specific industrial use cases (warehousing, field repair) where the camera adds documented value, the $1,500 cost is justifiable against labor savings, and privacy/social norms are controlled.
*   **Delay:** Wait until battery technology and use cases mature.

**Pre-mortem:** The product launches, sells initially to tech enthusiasts, then faces public backlash over privacy, gets banned from key locations, and is abandoned due to lack of daily utility. Inventory piles up, and the brand suffers long-term association with "creepy" technology.

**Blockers:**
*   The proposal states there is "no clear everyday use" while positioning it as an "everyday consumer product." This is a direct contradiction. You cannot sell a general market product without a general market use case.
*   The combination of high price, short battery, and social friction creates a failure mode that an invitation-only program cannot fix if the core utility is missing.

**Risks:** High price, privacy backlash, social rejection. These are treated as blockers here because the proposal offers no mechanism to overcome them other than hope.

DECISION: NO-GO
