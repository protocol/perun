First call: NO-GO. The $1,000 price point creates an immediate market mismatch against cheaper, evolving alternatives.

**Steelman:** A dedicated $1,000 hub uniquely bridges gaming and computing via proprietary optical media before PCs fully converge.

**Attack:**
1.  **Price vs. Value:** At $1,000, this costs ~3-4x a contemporary game console and approaches entry-level PC pricing. Consumers will not pay a premium for a "dumb" player when a PC offers creation, productivity, and similar playback potential for a comparable total cost.
2.  **Substitution Threat:** Personal computers are rapidly gaining multimedia capabilities. A PC is a superset of the proposed player's functions. As PC prices drop and multimedia becomes standard, the standalone player's value proposition evaporates.
3.  **Content Chicken-and-Egg:** A small library of interactive titles cannot justify the hardware cost. Developers will not invest in expensive proprietary formats for a niche, high-price installed base, creating a death spiral for content availability.
4.  **Positioning Confusion:** Sitting between a console and a PC without clearly outperforming either on price or utility leads to consumer confusion. It is too expensive for casual play and too limited for productivity.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Wait for PC multimedia standards to mature; avoid hardware risk.
*   **Cheaper Path:** License the optical technology to existing console makers to lower the entry price, accepting lower margins for volume.
*   **Pivot:** Focus solely on a specific high-value vertical (e.g., professional education) rather than the mass market living room.

**Pre-mortem:** The product launches, sells poorly due to price, developers abandon the format due to low install base, and the unit is discontinued within 18 months as a costly failure.

**Blockers:**
*   **Price Mismatch:** The $1,000 price exceeds the willingness-to-pay for a single-function media player when multi-function PCs are available nearby in price.
*   **Content Deficit:** The admitted small library fails to support the premium price, making the hardware a poor value proposition immediately upon launch.

**Risks:** Market confusion, rapid PC convergence. These are secondary to the fundamental economic blocker.

DECISION: NO-GO
