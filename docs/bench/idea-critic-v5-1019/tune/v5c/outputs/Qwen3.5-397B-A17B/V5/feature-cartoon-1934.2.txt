First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits the studio's entire steady revenue stream to a single, unproven format with a cost structure (10x average short) that guarantees insolvency if the primary assumption fails.

Steelman: A feature film could unlock a new, higher-margin market tier, leveraging existing IP to create a durable asset beyond the short-film cycle.

However, the attack reveals fatal flaws in the current framing:
1.  **Demand Validation:** There is zero evidence audiences will sit for a feature. The risk is not just "overrun"; it is total market rejection. Without a pilot, test screening, or serialized proof-of-concept, this is a gamble, not a strategy.
2.  **Financial Ruin:** Funding this entirely from short-film earnings removes the safety net. If the feature fails or delays, the core business collapses. There is no "go-if" cap or staged funding; it is an all-in bet.
3.  **Expert Consensus:** The founder's family and industry insiders advise against it. While innovators often ignore naysayers, doing so requires a specific counter-fact (e.g., "insiders missed the shift to sound"). No such counter-evidence is offered; the proposal asks to ignore expertise based on hope.
4.  **Alternatives Ignored:** The proposal lacks a "better way." A cheaper alternative exists: produce a medium-length special or a series of linked shorts to test appetite before committing to a full feature budget.

The pre-mortem is clear: The studio runs out of cash during production because the shorts' revenue dips, or the film releases to empty theaters because the format is unfamiliar. The cost of delay is low (competitors aren't doing it either), but the cost of failure is the company itself.

This is a one-way door with no parachute. The proposal presents risks as abstract possibilities rather than addressed hurdles. It lacks a kill criterion (e.g., "stop if test audiences leave before minute 40") and a leading metric for a pilot phase. To approve this now is to endorse financial suicide based on an unverified hypothesis. The plan must be revised to include a low-cost validation step and a capped budget before it can be considered.

DECISION: NO-GO
