First call: GO. The dual-version mechanic directly solves the core engagement problem (completion) by manufacturing social dependency, turning a single-player product into a networked experience without requiring online infrastructure.

Steelman: Releasing two distinct versions forces interpersonal trade to complete the collection, leveraging the existing link cable to create viral, peer-to-peer marketing that offsets declining hardware sales and publisher skepticism.

Options:
1. **Dual Versions (Proposed):** High perceived value per unit, drives two sales per dedicated player, creates mandatory social interaction. Risk: Consumer backlash if seen as greedy.
2. **Single Version + DLC/Updates:** Impossible on 1996 cartridge hardware; no distribution channel for digital adds.
3. **Do Nothing:** Single version leads to isolated play, no organic growth loop, higher risk of flopping given publisher doubts.

Fit: Aligns with the "collect them all" north star. The constraint (aging hardware) makes the link cable the only viable network; this idea maximizes that specific asset.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if players feel cheated rather than compelled to trade. If the version differences are cosmetic or trivial, the "price grab" narrative sticks. However, if the creature exclusivity is meaningful, the desire to complete the set overrides the friction. This is a two-way door; if one version sells poorly, bundles can be released later. Cost of delay is high; missing the holiday window or the console's remaining lifespan kills the opportunity.

Evidence: The proposal relies on the psychological principle of completionism and the physical reality of the link cable. No external data contradicts the feasibility of cartridge duplication.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (declining popularity, price grab perception, cable requirement) are mitigated by the mechanic itself: the decline necessitates a viral hook; the "price grab" is reframed as "necessary trade"; the cable requirement is the feature, not a bug.

Risks: Publisher hesitation remains, but the dual-release strategy offers a clearer path to double-unit sales per household than a single release.

Kill Criteria: If pre-orders for both versions combined do not exceed the publisher's break-even point for a single title by the manufacturing deadline, pivot to a single "definitive" edition. Leading metric: Ratio of linked trades to units sold in the first 30 days.

DECISION: GO
