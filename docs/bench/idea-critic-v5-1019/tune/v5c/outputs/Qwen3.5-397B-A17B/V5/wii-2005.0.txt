First call: GO. The proposal targets an unserved mass market (casual/non-gamers) by removing the primary barrier to entry (high cost/complexity) rather than fighting a losing battle for the saturated hardcore segment.

Steelman: By sacrificing raw power and HD fidelity, the console achieves a significantly lower price point and introduces a novel, intuitive interface that expands the total addressable market beyond traditional gamers, creating a blue ocean where rivals cannot compete on specs.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Remain a distant third in a specs-driven race dominated by entrenched leaders; likely eventual exit from hardware.
2. Compete on power: Requires massive R&D spend with high risk of failure against superior incumbents; ignores the casual demographic entirely.
3. Proposed path: Lower upfront risk via cheaper hardware, potential for higher volume software attachment rates among new users.

Fit: Aligns with a survival-and-growth strategy for a third-place player who cannot win the current game definition. It redefines the market rather than participating in it.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the "toy" perception becomes absolute, preventing any software sales, or if third-party developers completely ignore the platform due to non-HD limitations. However, the low hardware cost reduces the break-even point per unit. The "one-way door" aspect is the hardware commitment, but the low cost limits downside exposure compared to a high-end failure.

Evidence: The proposal relies on the assumption that non-gamers are priced out or intimidated by existing consoles, not that they demand HD graphics. The skepticism of hardcore gamers is irrelevant to the target demographic.

Blockers: NONE. The stated risks (limited third-party support, toy perception, no HD) are inherent to the strategy but do not invalidate the core logic of targeting a new audience. They are risks to be managed, not facts proving failure. The thin margin is offset by the volume potential of a lower price point.

Risks: Third-party adoption may be slow; brand perception may suffer among elites. These become kill criteria: if launch titles fail to engage the casual audience within 6 months, or if third-party support remains below a critical threshold by year-end, the strategy must pivot.

DECISION: GO
