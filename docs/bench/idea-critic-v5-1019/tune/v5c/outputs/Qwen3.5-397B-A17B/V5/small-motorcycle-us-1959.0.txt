First call: GO. The proposal targets an unserved segment (commuters/students) with a product fit for that use case, bypassing the entrenched "big bike" culture via non-traditional distribution.

**Steelman:** Introduce a low-cost, reliable 50cc vehicle to non-riders through accessible retail channels, creating a new market category rather than fighting for share in the existing heavy motorcycle segment.

**Attack & Options:**
The primary blocker claim is that US roads and culture demand large machines. However, the proposal explicitly targets commuters and students, a demographic likely underserved by expensive, difficult-to-ride heavy bikes. The "do nothing" option cedes this potential niche to future competitors. The alternative of launching only large models fails because the proposal admits those units may not survive US highway speeds, posing a reputational risk. The small bike avoids this mechanical failure mode entirely.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This fits a "blue ocean" strategy: making the competition irrelevant by changing the terms of engagement. If we assume failure, it would likely stem from branding confusion (selling in sporting goods stores) or underestimating the cultural stigma of "small" bikes. However, the low cost and ease of use are strong counter-weights. The risk of foreign currency expenditure is real but capped; the small scale of the initial subsidiary limits downside compared to a full-scale heavy bike launch.

**Evidence & Kill Criteria:**
The proposal relies on the assumption that a latent demand exists for easy transportation. There is no contradictory evidence provided that Americans *refuse* small vehicles for short trips. The mechanical limitation of the larger bikes acts as a natural filter, forcing the focus onto the 50cc unit which has no stated mechanical flaw for city use.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (brand unknown, preference for big bikes) are market entry hurdles, not factual contradictions. They are mitigated by the novel distribution channel and specific target demographic.
**Risks:** Cultural rejection, currency fluctuation. These are managed by the low-cost nature of the product and the phased rollout.

The plan creates a new market where none existed, avoiding direct competition with established heavy bike makers while leveraging a product perfectly suited for the identified gap.

DECISION: GO
