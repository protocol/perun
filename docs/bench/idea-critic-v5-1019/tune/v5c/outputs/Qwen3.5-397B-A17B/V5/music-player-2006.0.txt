First call: NO-GO. The proposal enters a saturated market late, abandons the company's own established copy-protection standard (alienating existing label partners), and offers no price advantage against a dominant incumbent with high switching costs.

Steelman: A superior hardware device with wireless sharing and a unified store can disrupt the market by offering a significantly better user experience that outweighs ecosystem lock-in.

Options:
1. **Proposed Plan:** High cost, high risk, low reversibility. Requires rebuilding label trust after dropping DRM standards.
2. **Do Nothing:** Avoids catastrophic partner fallout and marketing spend; preserves current licensing revenue.
3. **Licensing Only:** License the new software/client to existing device makers rather than building hardware; lower capex, faster reach.

Fit: Entering a hardware war contradicts a strategy of leveraging existing software dominance unless the user experience gap is insurmountable (unproven here).

Pre-mortem: The project fails because record labels, angered by the abandonment of the previous copy-protection standard, refuse to license content for the new store or restrict wireless sharing features, rendering the key differentiator useless. The large marketing budget burns cash against an entrenched competitor without a unique content library.

Evidence & Math: The proposal admits "no clear price advantage" and "late to market." The leader's "enormous share" implies high barriers to entry. Dropping the promoted copy-protection standard is a strategic contradiction that threatens the core supply chain (labels).

Blockers:
1. **Partner Alienation:** Abandoning the company's own promoted copy-protection standard creates an immediate conflict with existing partners who adopted it based on prior commitments. This threatens content availability, which is fatal for a music store.
2. **No Differentiation:** Without price advantage or exclusive content (due to label friction), the "better screen" and "wireless sharing" are insufficient to overcome the incumbent's ecosystem lock-in.

Risks: Label restrictions on sharing; high customer acquisition costs; inventory risk on hardware.

Kill Criteria: If labels do not guarantee full catalog availability including wireless rights before launch, stop. If customer acquisition cost exceeds 3x the incumbent's estimated cost within 6 months, pivot.

The strategic contradiction regarding copy-protection and the lack of a compelling reason for users to switch despite higher friction makes this plan untenable as proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
