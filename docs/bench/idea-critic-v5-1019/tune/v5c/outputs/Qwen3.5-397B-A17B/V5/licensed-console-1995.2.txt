First call: NO-GO. The proposal trades a strategic asset (platform control) for negligible financial return in a market where the licensor lacks leverage.

**Steelman:** Licensing converts fixed R&D costs into variable royalty revenue, allowing rapid market entry via an experienced toy partner without capital expenditure.

**Attack:**
1.  **Economics:** "Small royalty" on a "low-cost" console yields trivial revenue. Without volume guarantees, this fails to offset the opportunity cost of the platform license.
2.  **Incentive Misalignment:** The licensee bears marketing costs but shares upside. History shows licensees under-invest in marketing for low-margin hardware unless exclusivity or high margins exist. Neither is present.
3.  **Brand Dilution:** Consumers may not connect the product to the licensor, yet any product failure (common in early internet toys) damages the licensor's reputation by association.
4.  **Competitive Reality:** Entering a market dominated by established players with a "low-cost" clone via a third party ignores the network effects and content libraries required to compete. The "toy company" likely lacks the distribution or technical credibility to displace incumbents.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do nothing:* Preserves brand integrity and platform value for a future, more viable strategy.
*   *Direct Entry:* High risk/cost, but retains control and upside.
*   *Joint Venture:* Aligns incentives better than a pure royalty model, though complex.

**Pre-mortem:** The console launches, sells poorly due to weak marketing and lack of games, the licensee cuts losses after one season, and the computer maker is remembered for a cheap, failed gadget rather than innovation.

**Blockers:**
*   The economic model (small royalty × low cost) cannot sustain the marketing spend required to break incumbent dominance.
*   The licensee has no stated incentive to prioritize this product over their core high-margin toys.

**Risks:** Brand damage, market confusion, platform devaluation.

**Kill Criteria:** If the licensee cannot commit to a minimum marketing spend equal to 3x the projected royalty revenue in Year 1, the deal destroys value.

The proposal relies on a partner to solve a problem (market entry) that they are not incentivized to solve, for a reward too small to matter.

DECISION: NO-GO
