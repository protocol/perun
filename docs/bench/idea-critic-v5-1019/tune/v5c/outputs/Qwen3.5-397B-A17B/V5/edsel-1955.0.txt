First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a massive, irreversible infrastructure build (new dealer network) and a fixed 1957 launch date despite an acknowledged recession risk and a segment that "could shrink." An experienced operator does not lock in high fixed costs when the primary growth thesis is volatile and the timing is rigid.

**Steelman:** The gap between low and premium lines is a structural opportunity that, if captured with distinct styling and dedicated dealers, will secure long-term market share before competitors react.

**Attack:**
1.  **Timing vs. Commitment:** The plan admits a recession risk and potential segment shrinkage but proceeds with a "massive" upfront spend on dealers and advertising for a hard 1957 debut. If the economy turns in 1957 (a known cyclical possibility), the new division faces high fixed costs with collapsing demand, threatening the parent company's stability.
2.  **Infrastructure Risk:** Building a new dealer network from scratch is slower and costlier than utilizing existing channels or badge-engineering. This creates a "do or die" pressure to launch regardless of market conditions to justify the sunk cost.
3.  **Alternatives Ignored:** The proposal lacks a "staged" approach. A cheaper alternative exists: launch the cars through existing premium or low-priced dealers first to test volume without the capital expenditure of a standalone network. This preserves the upside while capping the downside.
4.  **Internal Cannibalization:** With "overlap" cited as a risk, launching a full line simultaneously increases the chance of stealing sales from the firm's own profitable lines rather than taking share from the leader.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fixed Cost Trap:** The commitment to a new dealer network before validating demand in a potentially shrinking/recessionary market creates a financial profile that cannot survive a downturn.
*   **Inflexible Launch:** The hard 1957 date prevents pivoting if pre-launch economic indicators worsen.

**Risks:** Market timing error, brand confusion, capital drain.

**Kill Criteria:** The plan fails because it treats a cyclical, uncertain market opportunity as a certainty worthy of maximum irreversible investment. The absence of a phased rollout or a "wait-and-see" trigger on the dealer network is a fatal flaw in the current economic context.

DECISION: NO-GO
