First call: NO-GO. The proposal targets price-sensitive teenagers with a high-cost mandatory data plan and no app store, directly contradicting the stated needs of the demographic and the competitive reality of 2010.

**Steelman:** Leveraging the acquired brand's social credibility to lock in teens via a exclusive carrier deal, using a custom OS to differentiate, despite the lack of apps at launch.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Evidence:** The target (teens on social networks) requires cheap data and rich third-party apps. The proposal offers expensive data and zero apps. Rivals already have large catalogs; this plan offers none. The "sunk cost" of two years development is a trap; continuing to burn capital on a product that fails the core user requirements (price, apps) compounds the loss.
2.  **Better Way:** The cheaper, safer alternative is to port the acquired brand's messaging features to the company's *existing* mobile platform or a proven third-party OS, immediately accessing an app ecosystem and avoiding the massive overhead of a new OS and exclusive carrier bottleneck.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The launch fails because teens refuse the high data fee and reject a phone without popular apps. The exclusive carrier deal limits reach, and the new OS lacks developer support. The sunk costs become total write-offs.
4.  **Blocker:** The proposal's own facts confirm the target users cannot afford the data plan and will not accept a device without an app store in a market where rivals already provide both. The decision to use a new OS instead of the company's existing platform ignores available assets to solve the app gap.

**Kill Criteria:** If the average teen cannot afford the monthly total cost, or if the app count is zero at launch while competitors have thousands, the venture fails.

**Decision:** The plan ignores the fundamental economic and functional constraints of its own target market. The sunk cost argument is invalid justification for a flawed go-to-market strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
