First call: NO-GO. The proposal contradicts the brand's core identity and customer value proposition without a mechanism to overcome it.

**Steelman:** A massive ad spend can temporarily override brand perception, forcing adults to trial a premium burger before the brand's "cheap/kids" image repels them.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit:** The strategy ignores the "north star" of the current brand: child-centered value. Adults visiting for kids' meals are price-sensitive; a premium price point alienates the existing base without guaranteeing new adult traffic.
2.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure. The chain spends record amounts on ads. Adults try the burger once due to curiosity but do not return because the environment (playplaces, kids' marketing) signals "not for serious dining," and the price feels unjustified for fast food. The core value customers feel priced out or ignored. Result: Massive sunk cost, diluted brand equity, no repeat usage.
3.  **Evidence/Logic:** The proposal admits the risks: customers associate the brand with kids/value, not premium quality. Heavy spending *before* demand validation violates basic unit economics. If the brand perception is the barrier, advertising alone rarely fixes a fundamental product-market fit mismatch; it often accelerates the realization that the product doesn't fit the venue.
4.  **Better Way:** Test the product in a limited market with targeted, low-cost promotion to validate repeat purchase rates among adults before committing an industry-record budget. Or, acquire/create a sub-brand distinct from the child-centered image.

**Blockers:**
*   **Contradictory Premise:** The plan relies on changing deep-seated brand perception (kids/value) solely through ad volume, despite admitting the perception is the primary barrier.
*   **Financial Risk:** Committing the "largest budget... seen" before validating demand or repeat usage is a unilateral bet with no kill criteria or pilot phase.

**Risks:** Brand confusion, alienation of core family demographic, failure to achieve repeat adult visits.

The proposal demands an irreversible, massive expenditure based on a hypothesis that directly conflicts with the stated brand reality. It lacks a pilot, a cap, or a validation step.

DECISION: NO-GO
