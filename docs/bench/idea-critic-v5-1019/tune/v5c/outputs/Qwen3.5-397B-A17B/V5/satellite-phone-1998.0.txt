First call: NO-GO. The unit economics and market trajectory described make commercial failure highly probable before scale is reached.

**Steelman:** A premium, niche service for global travelers can generate sufficient early cash flow to service debt before terrestrial networks achieve ubiquitous coverage.

**Attack:**
1.  **Market Shrinkage:** The core value proposition (global connectivity) is being erased by the rapid spread of ground cellular networks. Roaming agreements are a cheaper, faster alternative for the target demographic.
2.  **Product-Market Fit:** At $3,000 per handset and high per-minute costs, the Total Addressable Market (TAM) is tiny. Bulky hardware with poor indoor performance further restricts utility, making it a "last resort" device rather than a primary line.
3.  **Financial Mismatch:** $5 billion in debt requires massive, immediate subscriber growth. The high price point and product limitations inherently cap adoption speed. The sales channels are not ready, creating a fatal lag between launch and revenue.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" regarding this specific launch avoids sinking more capital into a deteriorating position. A cheaper alternative is waiting for hardware costs to drop and networks to mature, then offering roaming, though the debt load makes waiting difficult.

**Pre-mortem:** The company launches, fails to hit aggressive subscriber targets due to price and bulk, cannot service the massive debt load, and enters bankruptcy within two years as terrestrial coverage reaches 80% of business hubs.

**Blockers:**
*   **Unit Economics:** The stated price ($3k + high OPEX) vs. the shrinking window of opportunity creates a mathematical impossibility to service $5B debt quickly enough.
*   **Product Deficit:** Poor indoor performance is a disqualifier for a primary business tool.
*   **Channel Readiness:** Launching without ready sales channels guarantees a slow start, which the debt structure cannot tolerate.

**Risks:** Regulatory hurdles, further terrestrial expansion speed, hardware supply chain issues.

**Kill Criteria:** If subscriber growth does not hit 50% of the break-even trajectory within 6 months, or if terrestrial roaming rates drop below 20% of this service's cost, the project must be halted or restructured immediately.

The risks are not merely executional; they are structural. The market is moving away from the solution faster than the solution can be deployed.

DECISION: NO-GO
