First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a capital-intensive path while explicitly admitting the company lacks the necessary funds for the two most critical components: the factory and battery packs.

Steelman: Betting the entire company on a single BEV sedan in a purchased factory forces focus and speed, potentially capturing early EV market share before incumbents react.

However, the attack reveals immediate structural failure. The proposal's own facts state the factory and batteries require "far more capital than the company has." Committing to "only" battery-electric vehicles while lacking the means to build the powertrain or the facility creates an insolvency trap before production begins. This is not a risk; it is a mathematical impossibility based on the provided figures.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserves remaining cash, avoids immediate bankruptcy.
2. Contract manufacturing: Avoids the massive capex of buying a factory, testing the design with lower fixed costs.
3. Phased rollout: Start with a niche model or hybrid technology to generate revenue before committing to full BEV exclusivity.

The "buy a large factory" strategy contradicts the "little experience with mass production" constraint. Managing a massive, purchased industrial complex requires deep operational maturity the team admits it lacks, increasing the likelihood of costly delays that the undercapitalized balance sheet cannot survive.

Pre-mortem: The company buys the factory, drains its liquidity, fails to secure additional funding due to the 2008 credit crunch (implied by era/scarcity), and cannot produce batteries. The result is an empty factory and zero product.

Blockers:
1. Capital mismatch: The plan requires funds explicitly stated as unavailable for the core assets (factory, batteries).
2. Operational overreach: Buying a large factory contradicts the stated lack of mass production experience.

Risks:
1. Charging infrastructure scarcity (mitigated only if the car sells, which it can't without production).
2. Incumbent response (irrelevant if the company fails to launch).

The proposal demands a commitment to a path that the stated facts prove is currently unfundable and operationally mismatched. It fails the basic viability test of matching resources to requirements.

DECISION: NO-GO
