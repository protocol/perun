First call: GO. The proposal converts a philosophical stance ("web apps are sufficient") into a defensible ecosystem moat, directly addressing the risk of rival platforms outpacing product polish through third-party innovation.

**Steelman:** A curated, revenue-sharing native app store transforms the device from a static appliance into a dynamic platform, leveraging external developers to solve feature gaps while the 30% cut and review process fund quality control and align incentives.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Maintains current stability but cedes the ecosystem to rivals. Given the rival's head start, this leads to commodity status and eventual irrelevance.
*   **Open Distribution:** Maximizes developer speed but invites malware and carrier backlash, potentially getting the radio banned.
*   **Web-Only:** Preserves control but fails to match native performance, ensuring developers build for rivals first.

The proposed "walled garden" beats "do nothing" by capturing upside; it beats "open distribution" by mitigating the specific security and carrier risks cited. The 30% fee is standard for assuming distribution and payment liability; rejecting it yields no better alternative.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the review process becomes a bottleneck, starving the store of content, or if the 30% cut drives top-tier developers to sideloading despite restrictions. However, the proposal includes the mechanism (company review) to prevent stability crashes, directly answering the primary internal concern.

**Evidence & Logic:** The existence of rival ecosystems proves demand; the CEO's previous web-only stance is a sunk cost fallacy if maintained against market reality. The "polish" concern is paradoxical: without apps, the phone remains polished but useless compared to feature-rich competitors. The review workload is an operational tax, not a strategic blocker.

**Blockers:** NONE. No fact presented shows the model is financially unviable or technically impossible.
**Risks:** Review bottlenecks, developer revolt over fees, carrier friction. These are managed via the proposed review gate and revenue share, becoming kill criteria (e.g., if approval time > 7 days or developer churn > 20%) rather than reasons to stop.

The plan addresses the core threat (rival ecosystems) while containing the cited risks (security/carriers) better than any alternative.

DECISION: GO
