First call: NO-GO. The proposal requires a mandatory annual subscription and broadband in a 2002 market where broadband penetration is low and players expect free online play, creating an immediate adoption barrier that contradicts the stated facts.

Steelman: A unified, premium identity and voice network creates a sticky ecosystem and superior user experience that justifies a fee, differentiating the console from fragmented, free rivals.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Match rivals with free, publisher-hosted servers; zero new revenue, high fragmentation.
2. Hybrid model: Free basic matchmaking/identity; charge only for premium voice/hosting. Lowers entry barrier while testing willingness to pay.
3. Proposed (Paid/Broadband-only): High revenue per user potential, but severely caps total addressable market given the "few homes have broadband" constraint and "expect free" sentiment.

Fit: The strategy assumes users value unity enough to pay, but the stated facts indicate the opposite: price sensitivity and infrastructure gaps are the dominant constraints.

Pre-mortem: The service launches to a tiny fraction of the installed base due to dial-up dominance. Publishers refuse to support a paid-only ecosystem, releasing exclusive content on free rival platforms. The company bleeds cash on unused server capacity and support overhead for a service nobody buys.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits "few homes have broadband" and "players expect online play to be free." These are not risks; they are current market realities that directly contradict the requirement for mandatory broadband and subscription.

Blockers:
1. Infrastructure mismatch: Mandating broadband in 2002 excludes the vast majority of the installed base (fact stated in proposal).
2. Pricing contradiction: Charging for a service the market expects to be free, without a proven premium value proposition, will suppress adoption below viable levels (fact stated in proposal).
3. Operational gap: The company lacks the specific hosting/support operations required, introducing execution risk before product-market fit is proven.

Risks: High churn if early adopters find no one to play with; publisher revolt.

The proposal fails the kill check: its own stated facts (low broadband, free expectation) act as blockers to the core mechanism (mandatory paid access). A hybrid or free-tier entry is required to build the network effect before monetization.

DECISION: NO-GO
