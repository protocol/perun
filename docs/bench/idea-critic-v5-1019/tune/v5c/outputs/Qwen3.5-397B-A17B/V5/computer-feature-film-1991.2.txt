First call: GO. The offer solves the company's two hardest problems simultaneously: capital for unproven technology and access to a distribution network it lacks.

**Steelman:** Partnering allows the company to leverage its unique technical moat to create a new market category (all-CG feature) while offloading financial risk and distribution complexity to an established player, despite the loss of some control.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Remain a short-film/commercial shop; likely eventually outcompeted as tech commoditizes or lead talent leaves for larger studios.
2.  **Self-fund:** Impossible given rendering costs and lack of feature production experience; high probability of bankruptcy before completion.
3.  **The Deal:** Accepts dilution of control in exchange for survival, scale, and the chance to define a new medium.

**Fit:** Aligns with the vision of proving computer animation as a storytelling medium, not just a commercial tool. The "toy's point of view" is a strategically safe narrative entry point for audiences unfamiliar with the tech.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the studio micromanages the creative process, stifling the innovation that made the company attractive, or if rendering bottlenecks cause massive budget overruns that trigger contract clauses allowing the studio to cancel. However, the cost of delay is existential; waiting for tech to improve means losing the first-mover advantage to competitors or internal talent drain.

**Evidence & Blockers:** The proposal admits no prior feature experience and slow rendering. These are *risks*, not *blockers*. The blocker test asks: Do the stated facts prove failure? No. The lack of precedent is the opportunity. The studio's desire to hire the lead animator away proves the talent is the valuable asset; this deal secures that talent within the company structure rather than losing it entirely. The studio's "stronger hand" is the price of admission for a company with zero distribution leverage.

**Kill Criteria:** If the studio demands creative control over the script or character design (killing the unique voice), or if rendering tests show costs exceeding the budget cap by >20% in the first quarter, the project must pivot or stop.

Blockers: NONE. The risks are inherent to innovation but mitigated by the partnership structure.
Risks: Creative interference, budget overruns, technological failure.

DECISION: GO
