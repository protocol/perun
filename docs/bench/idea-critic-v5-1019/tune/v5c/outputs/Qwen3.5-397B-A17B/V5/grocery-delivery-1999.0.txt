First call: NO-GO. The plan commits irreversible capital before validating the core hypothesis that unit economics can work in a single market.

**Steelman:** Rapidly building a nationwide moat of automated warehouses prevents competitors from securing real estate and achieves scale before margins erode.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" beyond the first city costs near zero and preserves capital. A "cheaper alternative" involves leasing existing spaces or using manual picking to validate density requirements before automating. The proposal rejects learning in favor of guessing at scale.
2.  **Fit:** The stated goal is a viable grocery business. Groceries are low-margin; errors in volume forecasting lead to immediate, massive cash burn. Expanding to a dozen cities without data from the first violates basic operational hygiene for this sector.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 2001. Cause: The $1B fixed cost burden requires 80% utilization to break even, but actual customer density in new cities proves 40% because the 30-minute slot promise is too expensive to fulfill outside hyper-dense cores. The company cannot pivot because all cash is tied up in specialized, unused warehouses.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits "no operating track record" and "risks raised" regarding unit economics. Committing $1B based on unproven assumptions is not strategy; it is gambling with limited downside protection.
5.  **Blocker:** The plan explicitly refuses to "learn the unit economics of the first" before scaling. This is a factual contradiction: you cannot scale a model you have not verified. The low-margin nature of groceries means any error in the unverified model is fatal at this scale.

**Kill Criteria Check:** The proposal lists risks (capital intensity, thin margins) but offers no metric to validate them before spending. It treats "speed" as a substitute for "truth."

**Conclusion:** This is a one-way door decision based on unverified assumptions. The absence of a pilot, cap, or validated metric for the first city is a blocker because the proposal explicitly rules out learning before acting. A sound operator validates the unit economics in the first metro, proves a path to profitability, and *then* scales.

Blockers: Committing $1B without validated unit economics from the initial launch; explicit refusal to learn before scaling.
Risks: Customer density insufficient to support 30-minute slots; margin erosion from fixed costs.

DECISION: NO-GO
