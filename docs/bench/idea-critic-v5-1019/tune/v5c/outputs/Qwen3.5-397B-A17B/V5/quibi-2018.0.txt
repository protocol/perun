First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to solve a non-existent problem (premium short-form demand) with a costly solution (high production budgets) in a saturated market, ignoring clear signals of subscription fatigue.

**Steelman:** A premium, mobile-native studio could capture underserved high-income viewers willing to pay for quality short-form content, differentiated by seamless orientation technology.

**Attack:**
1.  **Demand Mismatch:** The proposal admits "no proven demand" for premium short-form while free, abundant alternatives exist. High production costs require high subscription fees, directly clashing with "subscription fatigue."
2.  **Feature Irrelevance:** "Video looking right" in any orientation is a baseline expectation in 2018, not a unique value proposition worthy of a billion-dollar moat.
3.  **Economic Contradiction:** Raising $1B+ implies a need for massive scale to justify A-list talent costs. However, the niche format (short-form) and barrier (paywall) inherently limit reach compared to ad-supported giants.
4.  **Competition:** The risk of "competition for phone attention" is understated; the service competes with established habits on platforms offering similar content for free.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoid sinking capital into an unproven category with negative unit economics potential.
*   **Pivot to Ad-Supported:** Leverage the content library on existing platforms or a free tier to prove demand before building a subscription wall.
*   **Licensing:** Sell the produced shorts to existing streamers lacking short-form libraries, removing the distribution risk.

**Pre-mortem:** The service launches, burns $500M on content, acquires minimal subscribers due to price sensitivity, and fails to retain them because the content format does not justify a standalone subscription. The "orientation" feature is ignored by users who simply rotate their phones.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** No proven demand for premium short-form exists.
*   **Fact:** Subscription fatigue is rising.
*   **Contradiction:** High-cost structure vs. unproven willingness to pay.

**Risks:** Execution difficulty, talent availability (noted but secondary to the fundamental market gap).

**Kill Criteria:** If a pilot with $10M content fails to achieve 5% conversion from free trials within 3 months, stop.

The proposal relies on hope rather than evidence, asking to bet $1B on a behavior change that contradicts current market data.

DECISION: NO-GO
