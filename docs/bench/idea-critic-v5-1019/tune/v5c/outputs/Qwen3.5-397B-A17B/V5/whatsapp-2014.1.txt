First call: NO-GO. The price is 12x the prior valuation with no revenue path, violating basic capital discipline.

**Steelman:** Paying a historic premium is the only way to neutralize an existential mobile threat that organic efforts cannot stop.

**Options:**
1. **Acquire:** Costs $19B immediately; risk of total loss if founders leave or regulation strikes; irreversible.
2. **Do Nothing:** Saves $19B; risk of slow displacement by the competitor; reversible via later build/buy.
3. **Partner/Clone:** Lower cost; slower execution; likely insufficient against network effects.

**Fit:** Assumes the buyer's core service is dying without this specific app. If the threat is real, fit is high; if exaggerated, this is empire-building.

**Pre-mortem:** Deal closes, founders quit within six months due to culture clash, users flee over privacy fears triggered by integration, regulators block data merging. The $19B becomes a goodwill write-down. This is a one-way door; the cash is gone forever.

**Evidence:** The target's refusal to run ads and minimal revenue prove no monetization model exists. The $1.5B valuation one year ago suggests the $19B price is speculative frenzy, not fundamental value. No fact supports the claim that *only* this acquisition saves the company.

**Blockers:**
1. **Valuation Mismatch:** A 1200% increase in value in 12 months without revenue growth is mathematically indefensible based on provided facts.
2. **No Monetization:** Buying a company with a stated anti-revenue philosophy ($1 fee, no ads) at a growth multiple is a contradiction in terms.
3. **Founder Risk:** High price increases pressure to integrate, directly conflicting with the retention of founders who value independence.

**Risks:** Regulatory backlash, user churn, cultural failure. These are severe but secondary to the financial impossibility of the deal structure.

**Kill Criteria:** If the target cannot present a credible path to $1B annual revenue within 3 years, the thesis fails. The current facts show zero path.

The proposal asks to bet the company's largest ever sum on an asset with no income, a hostile stance to monetization, and a price detached from reality. The fear of displacement does not justify financial suicide.

DECISION: NO-GO
