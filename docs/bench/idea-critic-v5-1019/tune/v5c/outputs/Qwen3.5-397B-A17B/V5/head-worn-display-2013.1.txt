First call: NO-GO. The proposal ignores stated, fundamental blockers that prevent the product from functioning as an "everyday consumer product" at the proposed price and timeline.

**Steelman:** An invitation-only launch at $1,500 validates demand among tech enthusiasts to fund iterations for a mass-market everyday device.

**Attack:**
The proposal fails the blocker test on four specific facts provided in the text:
1.  **Contradictory Positioning:** The plan targets the "general public" as an "everyday consumer product," yet the stated risks include "no clear everyday use for most people." You cannot sell an everyday solution to a market that perceives no daily problem.
2.  **Social Blocker:** "Social acceptance" is listed as a risk. For a wearable camera, social rejection is not a future risk but an immediate functional failure; if users are barred from wearing it in public spaces due to backlash, the product cannot function as advertised.
3.  **Privacy as a Hard Stop:** "Privacy objections to a wearable camera" are cited. In 2013, without established legal frameworks or social norms for constant recording, this creates a regulatory and reputational minefield that an invitation-only program cannot insulate against once scaling to the general public.
4.  **Unit Economics vs. Utility:** A $1,500 price point combined with "short battery life" and limited utility creates a negative value proposition that early adopters may tolerate for novelty, but which cannot sustain a transition to the general public.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoids brand damage from a failed consumer push.
*   **Enterprise Pivot:** Target specific industrial use cases (logistics, medical) where the camera and voice control solve defined problems, bypassing social acceptance and privacy hurdles.
*   **Delay:** Wait for battery technology and social norms to mature.

**Conclusion:**
The risks listed are not uncertainties to be managed; they are confirmed negatives that directly contradict the core claim of mass-market viability. The proposal admits the market has no use for it, society may reject it, and the technology is insufficient (battery), yet proceeds with a general public launch plan. This is a guaranteed failure of adoption and brand reputation.

DECISION: NO-GO
