First call: NO-GO. The $1,000 price point creates an immediate market mismatch against established game consoles and rapidly evolving personal computers, while the value proposition ("interactive multimedia") lacks a clear consumer definition.

**Steelman:** A dedicated $1,000 appliance bridges the gap between passive TV viewing and complex PC setup, offering superior interactive media performance before PCs mature.

**Attack:**
1.  **Price vs. Value:** At $1,000, this device costs significantly more than game consoles and approaches entry-level PC pricing. Unlike a PC, it cannot perform general productivity tasks; unlike a console, it lacks a massive, cheap software library. The "interactive disc" format is unproven with a tiny title library, offering insufficient ROI for the hardware cost.
2.  **Substitution Threat:** Personal computers are explicitly gaining multimedia capabilities. A consumer spending $1,000 will likely prefer a versatile PC that plays discs *and* works, rather than a single-function player. The window for a dedicated appliance is closing, not opening.
3.  **Consumer Confusion:** The proposal admits buyers may not understand the product. Launching a high-cost device with an ambiguous identity ("between console and PC") and limited software guarantees poor adoption. Marketing cannot fix a fundamental category definition problem at this price tier.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Wait for PC multimedia standards to solidify or console makers to add disc drives (cheaper, lower risk).
*   **Licensing Only:** License the disc technology to existing console/PC manufacturers rather than building a standalone $1,000 box.
*   **Lower-Cost Entry:** Launch a cheaper player focused solely on one media type (e.g., music/photos) to build a user base before attempting interactive video.

**Pre-mortem:** The product launches to confusion. Early adopters buy it, find only five interactive titles, and realize their new $1,500 PC does the same thing plus word processing. Sales stall; the format war is lost to VHS or emerging digital distribution. The company burns capital on hardware inventory and fails to secure third-party developer support due to low install base.

**Blockers:**
*   **Price/Value Mismatch:** $1,000 is unjustifiable for a single-function device with a tiny library when multi-function PCs are available at similar price points.
*   **Category Ambiguity:** The stated risk that "buyers may not understand what the product is for" is a fatal flaw for a mass-market consumer electronics launch, not a mitigatable risk.

DECISION: NO-GO
