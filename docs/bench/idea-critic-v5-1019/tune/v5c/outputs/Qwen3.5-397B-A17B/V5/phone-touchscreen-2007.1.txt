First call: GO. The proposal leverages existing core competencies (music, design, software) to defend against convergence threats while flipping the carrier power dynamic through revenue sharing.

**Steelman:** A premium, keyboard-less device integrating a proven music ecosystem and full web browser can redefine the market if a carrier partner cedes design control for exclusivity and revenue share, offsetting initial reach limits.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Cede the mobile frontier to competitors; music player sales erode as phones absorb functionality. High strategic risk.
2.  **License OS to multiple carriers:** Faster reach but surrenders design control and user experience integrity; commoditizes the product.
3.  **Proposed path:** High upfront friction (single carrier, no keyboard) but secures the integrated experience required to justify a premium price and protect the core brand.

**Fit:** Aligns with the vision of seamless integration across devices. The "no keyboard" constraint forces the innovation necessary to leapfrog existing business phones.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the single carrier cannot market a high-priced, unproven form factor, or if the lack of third-party apps makes the device a "walled garden" toy rather than a tool. However, the integrated music and web browsing provide immediate unique value.

**Evidence:** The company's dominance in digital music and reputation for design are stated facts. Carrier willingness is the variable, but the revenue-share model directly addresses their incentive to differentiate.

**Blockers:** NONE. The lack of phone experience is a risk, not a factual blocker, given the transferable skills in miniaturization and UI. The premium price is justified by the unique value proposition (music + web + design) unavailable elsewhere.

**Risks:** Limited initial reach due to exclusivity; consumer resistance to no physical keyboard; lack of apps. These are mitigated by the kill criteria: monitor activation rates and churn against the single carrier base. If adoption lags behind the music player's historical launch curves within two quarters, pivot to multi-carrier or revise pricing.

The proposal correctly identifies that controlling the user experience is more valuable than immediate ubiquity. The revenue-share model turns a potential blocker (carrier control) into a partnership aligned with long-term value.

DECISION: GO
