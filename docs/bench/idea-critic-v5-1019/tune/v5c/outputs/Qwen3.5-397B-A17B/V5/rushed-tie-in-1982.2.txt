First call: NO-GO. The timeline and production volume create an asymmetric downside where failure is catastrophic and success is merely profitable.

**Steelman:** A single elite programmer can distill the film's core appeal into a functional product within five weeks, and the brand's strength guarantees sufficient sales to clear a massive inventory run before the holiday demand cliff.

**Attack:**
1.  **Capacity vs. Scope:** Five weeks is insufficient for debugging, balancing, and polish on a "hit" title expected to meet high consumer standards. Normal development cycles exist for a reason; compressing them by 80% with one person guarantees technical debt and likely unplayable states.
2.  **Inventory Risk:** Ordering a "very large" run based on a rushed, untested product is financial negligence. If quality is poor (highly probable), the company is left with high fixed costs (license + manufacturing) and zero revenue, potentially bankrupting the division.
3.  **Single Point of Failure:** Relying on one programmer creates a critical bottleneck. Illness, burnout, or technical blockers immediately miss the Christmas window, rendering the inventory worthless.
4.  **Market Reality:** A high license fee increases the break-even point. A bad game damages the brand, reducing future value. The market punishes rushed cash-grabs, especially in the growing video game sector where word-of-mouth spreads quickly.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing/Wait:** Skip Christmas, develop properly for next year. Lower risk, missed Q4 revenue.
*   **Small Run Pilot:** Produce a limited batch to test market reception and quality before committing to mass production. This caps downside while retaining upside potential.
*   **Extended Team:** Add resources to meet a realistic quality bar, even if it means missing the absolute peak of the season.

**Pre-mortem:** The game ships with game-breaking bugs. Retailers return unsold cartridges. The company cannot recover the license fee or manufacturing costs. The brand is tarnished.

**Blocker:** The combination of an unrealistically short timeline (5 weeks) for a high-stakes product and a "very large" unconditional production run creates a verified path to catastrophic financial loss that outweighs the potential seasonal gain. The proposal lacks a quality gate or inventory cap.

**Decision:** The risk profile is unacceptable. The plan must be revised to include a smaller initial run and a realistic development timeline or additional resources.

DECISION: NO-GO
