First call: GO. The market position in running is secure, but the basketball segment requires a discontinuous jump to gain share; incremental spending will not dislodge incumbents.

**Steelman:** Commit disproportionately to a single, high-upside asset to manufacture cultural relevance and bypass traditional adoption curves, using regulatory conflict as a marketing accelerant.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Maintain status quo; likely continued share erosion in basketball.
2.  **Spread budget:** Sign multiple mid-tier players; low risk, low differentiation, high noise.
3.  **Proposed Plan:** High cost, high risk, but the only path to category leadership via brand transformation. It beats "do nothing" because the current trajectory is loss of relevance.

**Fit:** Assumes the goal is category leadership, not just participation. A sound idea must move the needle; safe plays do not move needles in crowded markets.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the player suffers a career-ending injury immediately or if the league bans the shoes permanently before cultural traction is gained. This is a two-way door financially (loss of capital) but a one-way door for brand reputation if it flops visibly. The cost of delay is ceding the "rebel" narrative to competitors.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits the player is unproven and risks are high. However, the "unproven" status is precisely why the asset is acquireable; proven stars command prices that preclude this level of creative control and budget concentration. The uniform rule violation is not a bug but a feature: fines generate press coverage that money cannot buy.

**Blockers:** NONE. No fact presented proves the plan fails as proposed. The risks (injury, fines, budget share) are explicit in the proposal and accepted as the cost of the strategy. They are risks, not blockers, because the proposal does not claim they are impossible, only that they are threats to be managed. The lack of prior success for signature shoes is a market gap, not a prohibition.

**Risks:** Career-ending injury, league ban, consumer rejection of the style. These become the kill criteria: if the player plays 20 games injury-free or the league allows a probationary period, the bet stands. If the first colorway is banned outright without wear, pivot.

DECISION: GO
