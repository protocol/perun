First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to enter a saturated market with a dominant incumbent using an unproven business model for the studio, while risking the core revenue source.

Steelman: Leverage existing assets to rapidly capture market share in a trending genre via a free-to-play model, monetizing through cosmetics without altering the paid core.

The "better way" analysis reveals a critical flaw: "do nothing" preserves the profitable paid cooperative niche. Entering a "last-player-standing" market where a rival already "owns the genre" suggests a low probability of success unless the offering is significantly superior, which is unlikely given the two-month constraint and lack of F2P experience. The cheapest alternative is to add cooperative features to the rival's model or wait for the trend to stabilize, rather than burning resources on a speculative pivot.

Fit is poor. The studio's north star appears to be paid cooperative experiences; this proposal diverts focus to a competitive, service-based model they have never operated. The pre-mortem is straightforward: the mode launches, fails to retain players against the incumbent, generates negligible cosmetic revenue due to low engagement, and confuses the existing user base, causing a dip in paid sales (cannibalization). The cost of delay is minimal; the trend may fade, or the studio can learn from the rival's mistakes over the next quarter.

Evidence check: The proposal admits the studio has "not run a free-to-play business before." This is a factual blocker for a plan relying entirely on that specific expertise to generate revenue. Furthermore, the "two months" timeline for a net-new multiplayer mode, even with asset reuse, contradicts industry standards for balancing and anti-cheat implementation required in a competitive shooter.

Blockers:
1. Lack of operational experience in F2P monetization and live services creates a high probability of financial failure.
2. The timeline is insufficient to build a competitive product in a genre owned by a rival.
3. Direct risk to the existing paid revenue stream without a validated hedge.

Risks: Cannibalization of paid sales; failure to attract users from the incumbent; technical debt from rushed development.

DECISION: NO-GO
