First call: NO-GO. The proposal admits the synthetic costs more than leather while offering no proven premium value to offset the price, creating an immediate margin and adoption failure.

Steelman: Launching now could secure first-mover advantage in a new category if manufacturers accept the cost for durability. However, the stated facts contradict this path.

Options:
1. **Proposed Plan:** High spend, premium pitch. Cost: High. Risk: Extreme (price disadvantage, comfort unknown). Reversibility: Low (marketing spend sunk).
2. **Do Nothing:** Wait for cost parity or comfort data. Cost: Zero. Risk: Competitor entry. Reversibility: High.
3. **Niche Pilot:** Target specific industrial or high-wear segments where durability outweighs comfort/cost. Cost: Low. Risk: Moderate. Reversibility: High.

Fit: A premium fashion play requires perceived value exceeding cost. The proposal states the material costs *more* than leather but risks being *less* comfortable. Without a verified comfort advantage or brand cachet, the "premium" claim is unsupported.

Pre-mortem: The launch fails because shoe manufacturers refuse to retool factories for a material that increases their COGS and risks consumer returns due to comfort issues. Fashion trends shift away from "plastic" looks. This is a one-way door for the marketing budget; once spent, it cannot be recovered if the market rejects the premise.

Evidence: The proposal itself provides the blocker. It explicitly states: "the synthetic costs more than leather" and "wearers may find it less comfortable." In footwear, comfort is a primary driver of repeat purchase; cost is a primary driver of manufacturer adoption. The plan asks manufacturers to accept higher costs for a product that might be inferior in use. No evidence suggests consumers will pay the necessary premium to cover this gap.

Blockers:
1. **Negative Unit Economics:** Higher cost than incumbent (leather) with no stated premium pricing power or performance advantage to justify it.
2. **Adoption Friction:** Manufacturers must change factories for a material that offers them no clear economic benefit.

Risks: Fashion volatility, comfort rejection. These are secondary to the fundamental economic mismatch.

Kill Check: The proposal admits the cost disadvantage. Unless the "premium" positioning can command a price increase greater than the cost differential *and* overcome comfort concerns, the core business case fails. The facts provided indicate the opposite.

DECISION: NO-GO
