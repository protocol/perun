First call: NO-GO. Doubling production while sales lag by 60% during a recession with rising interest rates destroys cash without a verified buyer.

Steelman: The claim assumes that flooding the market now secures future market share or forces dealer acceptance before competitors react, betting that demand will rebound sharply enough to absorb a massive inventory spike immediately.

Options:
1. **Double Production**: Maximizes cash burn, increases financing costs on unsold units, and risks dealer insolvency. High risk, low reversibility once cash is spent.
2. **Do Nothing/Reduce**: Preserves cash, aligns output with current demand, minimizes storage and financing costs. Lower risk, fully reversible.
3. **Incentivize Sales**: Use limited cash for customer rebates or dealer financing support to move existing stock. Targets the bottleneck (demand) rather than the symptom (supply).

Fit: A start-up's north star is survival and product-market fit. This proposal contradicts the basic economic reality stated: demand is collapsing while capital costs rise.

Pre-mortem: The company doubles output, cash reserves vanish within months paying for parts and storage. Dealers refuse further stock due to credit constraints. Interest payments on the swollen inventory become unsustainable. The company faces insolvency before the economy recovers. This is a one-way door; spent cash cannot be recovered.

Evidence & Calculation:
- Current Stock: 4,500 unsold units (7,500 produced - 3,000 sold).
- Trend: Economy sliding, rates rising (demand down, cost of capital up).
- Constraint: Limited cash.
- Logic: Increasing supply when demand is falling and financing is expensive accelerates the burn rate. There is no evidence provided that demand will spike imminently to justify building ahead.

Blockers:
1. **Cash Flow Mismatch**: The proposal requires significant upfront cash for production while revenue is stagnant and financing costs are rising. The stated "limited cash" makes this mathematically impossible to sustain.
2. **Dealer Capacity**: Dealers cannot finance more stock in a high-interest recession; pushing more inventory breaks the distribution channel.
3. **Inventory Bloat**: Adding to an already growing surplus of 4,500 units creates a liability, not an asset, under current conditions.

Risks: Economic recovery timing, competitor actions. These are secondary to the immediate liquidity crisis this plan guarantees.

Kill Criteria: If cash reserves cannot cover 6 months of operations at the new production rate without new equity (not stated as available), the plan fails.

DECISION: NO-GO
