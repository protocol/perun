First call: NO-GO. The proposal admits unproven economics and a large short-term margin hit while risking irreversible customer expectations.

**Steelman:** An annual fee locks in loyalty and increases order frequency, offsetting shipping losses through volume and upfront cash flow.

**Attack:**
1.  **Economics:** The proposal explicitly states economics are "unproven." Without data showing that increased order frequency covers the marginal cost of free two-day shipping plus the fee discount, this is a bet on hope, not math. Heavy buyers could drain margin faster than the $79 fee replenishes it.
2.  **Irreversibility:** Once customers pay upfront and adjust behavior, removing the benefit destroys trust and triggers churn. The "hard to reverse" nature combined with unproven unit economics creates a one-way door to potential losses.
3.  **Alternatives:** The proposal ignores cheaper tests. A pilot program (limited geography or category), a higher price point to ensure margin safety, or a minimum order threshold for free shipping would validate demand without exposing the entire retailer to unchecked liability.
4.  **Short-term Hit:** Accepting a "large short-term margin hit" without a guaranteed path to profitability violates basic capital preservation.

**Blockers:**
*   **Unproven Economics:** The core financial premise is admitted as unknown. Launching a structural pricing change without verified unit economics is a factual failure of the plan.
*   **Margin Risk:** The admission of a "large short-term margin hit" with no cap or pilot constitutes a financial blocker.

**Risks:**
*   Heavy buyers exploiting the system.
*   Inability to reverse the program.

**Kill Criteria Check:** The proposal lacks a pilot, a cap on exposure, and specific metrics to prove the model works before full rollout. It asks for immediate, total commitment based on unverified assumptions.

**Conclusion:** You cannot approve a pricing structure where the costs may exceed revenue and the economics are explicitly unproven. The risk of permanent brand damage from a failed reversal outweighs the potential upside of an untested hypothesis. Test small first; do not launch globally.

DECISION: NO-GO
