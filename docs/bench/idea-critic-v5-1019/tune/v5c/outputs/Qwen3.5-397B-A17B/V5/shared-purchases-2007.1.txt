First call: NO-GO. The proposal trades core user trust for incremental ad revenue by exposing sensitive behavioral data without explicit, granular consent.

**Steelman:** The feature leverages existing social graphs to create viral marketing loops, increasing ad value through verified purchase signals while offering a notification-based opt-out to respect user agency.

**Attack:**
1.  **Incentive Misalignment:** A notification step is not consent; it is a friction point designed to be ignored. Users cannot effectively "decline" social pressure once a purchase is broadcast to friends.
2.  **Sensitivity Blindness:** "What users buy" includes medical supplies, gifts, and addictive substances. Publicizing these creates immediate reputational harm for users and embarrassment for partners, driving churn rather than engagement.
3.  **Irreversibility:** As noted in the risks, this is a one-way door. Once private behavior becomes public norm, retracting the feature admits prior wrongdoing, shattering trust permanently.
4.  **Alternative:** The "do nothing" or "explicit opt-in" approach preserves trust. An opt-in model captures only users eager to share, yielding higher quality data without the backlash of forced exposure.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, major partners withdraw due to brand safety concerns after users mock their products in feed comments. A class-action lawsuit forms around "sensitive" purchases (e.g., health conditions) being exposed. The platform is forced to kill the feature, but the perception of being a "surveillance engine" remains, stalling user growth and inviting regulatory scrutiny.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** The proposal relies on "notification" rather than "prior explicit consent" for sensitive data.
*   **Contradiction:** The stated risk ("invasion of privacy") is confirmed by the mechanism (broadcasting private acts), yet the plan proceeds anyway.
*   **Evidence:** The proposal admits the feature is "hard to withdraw," violating the safety requirement for high-risk experiments involving personal data.

**Risks:** Partner revolt, user exodus, regulatory fines. These are not merely possible; they are probable given the mechanism.

**Kill Criteria:** If any sensitive category (health, finance) is exposed without explicit prior opt-in, the project fails. The current proposal lacks this guardrail.

The cost of delay is negligible compared to the cost of a trust collapse. The cheaper, safer alternative (explicit opt-in) achieves the same upside without the existential risk.

DECISION: NO-GO
