First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on unproven core technology (handwriting recognition) reported as unreliable, paired with a high price point and unverified market demand, creating a high probability of commercial failure.

**Steelman:** Launching a $700 pen-based device creates a new market category by leveraging CEO publicity to overcome early technical limitations and software scarcity.

**Attack:**
1.  **Core Mechanism Failure:** The proposal admits handwriting recognition is "unreliable in testing." For a device where this is the *main* input, unreliability is a functional blocker, not a risk. If the primary interface fails, the product fails regardless of publicity.
2.  **Economic Mismatch:** A $700 price tag (1993 dollars) is prohibitive for an unproven category with "little software." Users will not pay a premium for a device that cannot reliably perform its primary function.
3.  **Form Factor Friction:** Being "too big for a pocket" contradicts the "handheld" value proposition, reducing utility compared to existing notebooks or emerging smaller devices.
4.  **Publicity vs. Product:** Heavy CEO publicity cannot compensate for a broken core feature. It may drive initial sales but will accelerate negative word-of-mouth if the technology does not work.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do nothing:* Avoids sunk costs in a likely failed category.
*   *Delay/Refine:* Wait until recognition reliability is proven and form factor is pocket-sized before committing to mass production.
*   *Software First:* Develop the software ecosystem on existing hardware platforms to validate demand before building custom hardware.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches to fanfare, users immediately encounter input errors, returns surge due to the high price and poor utility, and the brand becomes synonymous with overhyped, broken technology. The "unproven category" remains unproven because the entry product was defective.

**Blockers:**
*   Unreliable core technology (handwriting recognition) presented as the main input.
*   High price ($700) for a device with known functional defects and no software ecosystem.

**Risks:** Market rejection, brand damage, inventory write-offs. These are secondary to the functional blockers.

The proposal asks to launch a product with a known, critical defect in its primary function at a premium price. This is not a calculated bet; it is a guaranteed failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
