First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to solve a distribution problem with forced integration, ignoring three prior failures and entrenched network effects.

Steelman: Leveraging existing search, mail, and video dominance to bootstrap a new social layer, using unique "circles" and "hangouts" to differentiate where generic profiles failed.

Options:
1. **Proposed Plan:** High cost, high risk of user churn from forced integration, low reversibility once bonuses and architecture are committed.
2. **Do Nothing:** Preserve current product satisfaction; avoid repeating a pattern of three failed social attempts.
3. **Acquisition/Incubation:** Buy a rising competitor or build a standalone app without risking core search/mail utility.

Fit: Challenging a market leader with hundreds of millions of users requires organic pull, not internal mandates. A strategy relying on forced integration contradicts the need for genuine network effects.

Pre-mortem: The launch fails because users resent the intrusion into email and search, causing churn in core revenue products. The "invite-only" beta creates artificial scarcity but no real utility without the incumbent's network. Bonuses drive internal gaming of metrics rather than genuine adoption.

Evidence: The proposal admits three previous social failures. It acknowledges network effects favor the incumbent but offers no mechanism to overcome them other than "integration." Tying executive bonuses to adoption of a unproven product incentivizes short-term manipulation over long-term value.

Blockers:
1. **Historical Precedent:** Three prior failures in the exact same domain suggest a fundamental mismatch between the company's core competency (search/utility) and social networking, which the proposal does not address.
2. **Incentive Misalignment:** Tying executive compensation to the adoption of a beta product creates a conflict of interest that risks distorting product decisions and harming core products.
3. **Forced Integration Risk:** The plan explicitly risks annoying existing users of profitable products (mail, search) to feed a loss-leading social experiment, violating the principle of protecting core revenue streams.

Risks: Users refusing to switch despite integration; video hangouts failing to gain traction; circles being a minor feature rather than a platform shift.

The proposal repeats a failed pattern, introduces dangerous incentive structures, and threatens core products without a viable mechanism to break the incumbent's network effects.

DECISION: NO-GO
