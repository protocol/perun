First call: GO. The strategic imperative to own the customer relationship and capture long-term lifetime value outweighs the immediate loss of licensing revenue, provided the company can sustain the cash burn.

Steelman: By withdrawing content from rivals, the conglomerate creates exclusive scarcity that forces migration to its own lower-priced platform, trading short-term licensing fees for long-term subscription equity and data ownership.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Continue licensing; safe cash flow now, but eventual commoditization as rivals build their own libraries or negotiate lower fees.
2. Hybrid model: License older content while keeping new releases exclusive; reduces immediate burn but dilutes the "must-have" value proposition, likely failing to hit subscriber targets.
3. The proposal: High risk, high reward. It beats "do nothing" only if the net present value of the acquired subscribers exceeds the lost licensing revenue plus the cost of capital for the operating losses.

Fit: Aligns with the industry shift toward direct-to-consumer models. Assumes the brand strength is sufficient to drive migration without the full library being immediately available on the new platform.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the technology platform crashes under load (never run before), the content gap causes churn before new originals launch, or the cash burn exceeds reserves before profitability. This is a one-way door; exiting means selling the library at a fire-sale price later. Cost of delay is high; waiting allows competitors to lock in users further.

Evidence: The proposal relies on the assumption that "low price" + "exclusive content" = "mass migration." The risk of execution (tech platform) is real but solvable via acquisition or hiring. The financial risk (cash burn) is the primary constraint.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (cannibalization, cash burn, tech execution) are inherent to the strategy, not factual contradictions. The company earns billions currently, implying the capacity to fund the loss if reserves are deep enough (assumed true for a "major conglomerate").

Risks: Technology failure, slower-than-expected subscriber growth, inability to raise additional capital if losses mount.

Kill criteria: Halt expansion if subscriber growth falls below 50% of target by Year 2, or if cash reserves drop below 18 months of operating burn without a clear path to refinancing.

DECISION: GO
