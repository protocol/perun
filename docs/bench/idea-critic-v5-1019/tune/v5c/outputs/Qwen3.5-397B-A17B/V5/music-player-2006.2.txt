First call: NO-GO. The proposal enters a saturated market late, abandons a strategic standard (copy-protection) that secured partner alignment, and relies on uncertain label cooperation for its key differentiator (sharing) without a price advantage to disrupt the incumbent's loyalty.

Steelman: A superior hardware form factor (larger screen) combined with seamless wireless sharing and a unified store can fracture the incumbent's ecosystem if marketing overcomes late-entry inertia.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve partner trust and avoid CAPEX burn in a low-margin hardware battle.
2. License software only: Become the "Windows of music" without hardware risk or store logistics.
3. Acquire a niche player: Gain immediate share and tech without building from scratch.
The proposal beats "do nothing" only if sharing works; it loses to licensing on risk/capital.

Fit: Assumes the goal is total ecosystem ownership. If the north star is profit or reach, this misaligns by alienating existing partners (labels/device makers) who rely on the abandoned copy-protection standard.

Pre-mortem: Labels block wireless sharing due to piracy fears, rendering the main feature dead on arrival. The device becomes a costly commodity with no content advantage. One-way door: Abandoning the copy-protection standard burns bridges with partners; re-adopting it later signals instability. Cost of delay: High, as the incumbent locks in users monthly, but rushing a broken ecosystem is worse.

Evidence: The proposal admits "no clear price advantage," "late to market," and "labels may restrict sharing." These are not risks; they are stated facts that dismantle the value proposition. The "enormous share" of the leader is a factual barrier not addressed by marketing spend alone.

Blockers:
1. The core differentiator (sharing) depends on label approval which is explicitly flagged as likely restricted.
2. Abandoning the industry copy-protection standard alienates the supply chain (labels/partners) required to stock the store.
3. No price advantage exists to incentivize switching costs for users in a loyal ecosystem.

Risks: Marketing burn without product-market fit; hardware inventory obsolescence.

The plan fails on its own stated facts: it removes the industry standard without a guaranteed replacement feature (sharing) or a price incentive.

DECISION: NO-GO
