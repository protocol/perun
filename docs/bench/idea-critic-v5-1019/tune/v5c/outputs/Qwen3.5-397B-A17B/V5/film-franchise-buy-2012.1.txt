First call: GO. The strategic fit of a space-fantasy franchise for a company with theme parks, consumer products, and a film studio is undeniable, offering a decades-long content pipeline despite the high upfront cost.

**Steelman:** Acquiring a dormant but culturally massive space-fantasy IP for $4B secures exclusive rights to drive theme park attendance, merchandise sales, and film revenue for a generation, outweighing the risks of recent creative stagnation.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Maintains current cash flow but cedes the "space fantasy" category to competitors, leaving theme parks and product lines without a modern tentpole.
2.  **Organic Creation:** Building a new franchise from scratch carries higher failure risk and takes 10+ years to reach comparable cultural saturation.
3.  **Licensing Only:** Insufficient for theme park integration and yields lower margins than full ownership.

**Pre-mortem & Risks:**
Assume failure: The new trilogy alienates the core fanbase due to the founder's absence, and box office returns fail to cover the $4B premium. The "mixed reviews" of recent entries suggest eroding brand equity. However, these are *risks*, not *blockers*. The proposal acknowledges the creative void; the solution is hiring top-tier talent, which the acquirer can afford. The high price is a function of the asset's scarcity and cross-business utility, not just film ROI.

**Evidence & Kill Criteria:**
The valuation relies on the assumption that the franchise's "famous" status translates to immediate park and product lift. If the first new film underperforms significantly against projections, the thesis breaks.
*   **Blocker:** None found. The risks are manageable execution challenges, not fundamental flaws in the acquisition logic.
*   **Kill Criterion:** If the first installment of the new trilogy fails to gross >$800M globally or receives a <70% positive sentiment score from legacy fans, halt further spin-off production and re-evaluate the IP strategy.

**Decision:**
The strategic necessity of a multi-generational franchise for a diversified entertainment conglomerate justifies the premium. The risks are real but mitigated by the company's existing infrastructure in parks and products, which can monetize the IP even if film performance is merely adequate.

DECISION: GO
