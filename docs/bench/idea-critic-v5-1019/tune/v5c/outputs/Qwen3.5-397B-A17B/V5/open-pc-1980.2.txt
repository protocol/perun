First call: GO. The growth of small-computer makers signals a market shift that internal capabilities cannot address quickly enough; a separate team with off-the-shelf parts is the only viable path to speed.

Steelman: A dedicated, autonomous team using commodity hardware and external software can penetrate the emerging personal computer market within a year, bypassing internal bottlenecks and leveraging existing retail channels to capture growth before incumbents dominate.

The "do nothing" option cedes the high-growth personal segment to agile competitors, risking long-term irrelevance as the market bifurcates. Building internally using standard processes would take too long, missing the window entirely. The proposed "open design" and "outside software" trade margin and control for speed and ecosystem adoption, which is critical when standards are still forming.

Risks identified are real but manageable. Competitors copying the design is inevitable in a commodity market; the defense is speed to market and brand trust, not secrecy. Dependence on outside software is actually a strength here, as it shifts development burden to the ecosystem (e.g., Microsoft), accelerating time-to-value. Lower margins are acceptable for a new, high-volume segment where unit share drives future service revenue. Lack of retail experience is mitigated by using *independent* stores already serving this demographic, rather than building a new sales force.

The pre-mortem failure scenario is that the mainframe culture strangles the separate team, slowing decisions or forcing integration too early. The kill criterion is clear: if the separate team cannot ship a prototype within 6 months or secure key software partnerships within 3 months, the initiative must be re-evaluated. However, the proposal explicitly mandates a "small separate team," directly addressing this cultural risk.

Blockers: NONE. The facts presented show a clear market opportunity that internal processes cannot capture. The risks are inherent to the new market structure, not flaws in the approach.
Risks: Margin compression, ecosystem dependence, execution speed. These become the metrics to watch, not reasons to stop.

DECISION: GO
