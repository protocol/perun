First call: NO-GO. A $6 billion cash outlay at a large premium in 2007 for a fast-changing display ad market, while lagging in search, concentrates excessive risk on an unproven pivot rather than fixing the core search deficit.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the platform instantly provides the scale and technology to block the search leader's recent move, bypassing years of internal R&D in a winner-take-all market.

**Attack:**
1.  **Financial Blocker:** The price represents a massive premium in cash during a period of impending credit tightening (2007 context). If the display market shifts or the integration stumbles, the sunk cost cripples the balance sheet without guaranteeing search parity.
2.  **Strategic Conflict:** Buying an agency creates an immediate adversarial relationship with the buyer's existing agency customers, who may flee to competitors to avoid funding a rival's infrastructure. This erodes the current revenue base before the new one matures.
3.  **Market Timing:** The "fast-changing" nature of display technology suggests today's premium asset could be obsolete quickly. Paying a peak price for a shifting target violates the principle of buying stability when behind.
4.  **Alternative:** The cheaper, safer path is to license technology or build incrementally, preserving cash to fix the primary search gap which remains the core business driver.

**Pre-mortem:** In 2010, the company holds a bloated, conflicting agency arm, depleted cash reserves, and still trails the search leader because the acquisition distracted management from the core algorithm war. The "large premium" becomes a permanent write-down.

**Blockers:**
*   **Cash/Valuation Mismatch:** $6B cash at a large premium in a volatile 2007 market creates unacceptable downside risk without a guaranteed path to overtaking the search leader.
*   **Channel Conflict:** The model inherently alienates existing agency customers, threatening current revenue streams.

**Risks:** Technology obsolescence; integration failure; regulatory scrutiny.

The proposal fails the blocker test: the financial exposure and channel conflict are factual flaws in the current plan, not mere risks. The cost of delay is lower than the cost of a botched $6B integration.

DECISION: NO-GO
