First call: NO-GO. The stated facts describe a device with severe usability flaws, a limited visual palette, and a high risk of physical discomfort, all while cannibalizing attention from an imminent home console launch.

Steelman: A low-cost ($180) bridge product leveraging existing tech to capture market share between generations via unique stereoscopic 3D.

**Skeptic**: The proposal admits the core experience causes headaches and eye strain. In gaming, physical discomfort is a fatal flaw, not a trade-off. Red monochrome graphics are a significant downgrade from contemporary color standards, reducing software appeal. The "eyepiece" form factor contradicts the "tabletop" usage, creating an awkward hybrid that is neither truly portable nor comfortable for home use.

**Better-way**: "Do nothing" is superior. Waiting two years for the next home console avoids brand damage from a flawed intermediate product. Alternatively, a standard handheld with full color and no eyepiece would offer better value, even at a slightly higher cost or later date. The proposed device beats neither alternative on user experience or strategic timing.

**Pre-mortem**: The product launches to curiosity but returns surge due to nausea and poor graphics. Developers ignore the platform due to the small install base and technical constraints (red-only), resulting in the "few games" risk becoming a reality. The brand becomes associated with failed gimmicks rather than innovation.

**Blockers**:
1. **User Safety/Comfort**: The explicit admission that users may get eye strain or headaches is a blocker for a consumer entertainment device.
2. **Value Proposition**: Red monochrome visuals at $180 (1995) is a poor value compared to existing color handhelds and upcoming home consoles.
3. **Strategic Conflict**: Launching a weak product shortly before a major home console release dilutes marketing focus and confuses consumers.

**Risks**: Limited software library, brand reputation damage, inventory obsolescence within two years.

The stated facts confirm the device fails on comfort, visual fidelity, and strategic timing. No kill criteria or pilot can fix the fundamental ergonomic and visual deficits described.

DECISION: NO-GO
