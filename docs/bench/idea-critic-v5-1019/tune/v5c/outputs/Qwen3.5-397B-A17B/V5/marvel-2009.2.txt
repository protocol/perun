First call: NO-GO. The proposal bets $4 billion on a short, unproven film slate while the target's most valuable assets are locked in rival deals.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the publisher secures long-term IP ownership and vertical integration for a boy-focused expansion, leveraging the target's nascent film success to bypass licensing fees.

**Attack:**
1.  **Asset Encumbrance:** The "most famous characters" are licensed to rivals. Buying the parent company yields immediate revenue only from secondary tiers, delaying the core strategic goal of reaching boys with top-tier heroes.
2.  **Unproven Production:** The target has only one self-financed hit. A "short slate" offers insufficient data to validate a $4 billion valuation based on in-house production potential. The cyclical risk is amplified by lack of diversification.
3.  **Integration Friction:** Creative-control disputes with target leadership threaten the very content engine being purchased. In creative industries, losing key talent post-acquisition often renders the IP worthless.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" or license specific boy-focused characters individually avoids the $4 billion upfront risk and integration chaos. A cheaper, staged partnership to co-produce films tests the production capability without full acquisition.

**Pre-mortem:** Two years post-acquisition, the first self-produced films flop, the rival licenses remain in place blocking major character use, and target leaders depart due to control disputes. The conglomerate is left with $4 billion in debt and unused IP.

**Blockers:**
*   **Valuation Mismatch:** Paying a premium for production capability that rests on a single hit and a short slate contradicts the "unproven" status admitted in the proposal.
*   **Strategic Delay:** The primary assets needed to fix the "weak reach with boys" are contractually unavailable due to existing rival deals.

**Risks:** Creative exodus, box-office cyclicality, culture clash.

**Kill Criteria:** If the target cannot demonstrate a viable path to reclaim top-tier character rights within 12 months or greenlight three additional profitable films independently, the acquisition thesis collapses.

The proposal asks to pay for control of assets that cannot be fully utilized immediately and a production engine that is statistically unproven, violating basic capital allocation discipline.

DECISION: NO-GO
