First call: NO-GO. The proposal ignores the core contradiction between "cola flavor" and "clear appearance," risking brand confusion without a proven repeat-purchase mechanism.

**Steelman:** A clear cola can capture novelty seekers and health-conscious drinkers as a distinct sub-brand, expanding total portfolio share before rivals act.

**Attack:**
1.  **Flavor-Color Association:** Consumers strongly associate dark color with cola taste. A clear liquid signals "lemon-lime" or "water." If the taste is traditional cola, the visual cue creates cognitive dissonance, leading to immediate rejection after the first sip. The proposal admits shoppers link flavor with color but offers no solution.
2.  **Novelty vs. Repeat:** Test markets measure initial trial (novelty), not long-term retention. The "positive response" likely reflects curiosity about the visual gimmick, not product love. Without evidence of repeat purchase rates exceeding the category average, a nationwide launch is a burn of marketing capital on one-time buyers.
3.  **Cannibalization:** As a "purer alternative" from the same maker, it directly threatens the flagship's perception. If the clear version is perceived as "better" (purer), it devalues the dark original. If it is perceived as "different," it fragments shelf presence. The proposal lists cannibalization as a risk but provides no mitigation or net-gain calculation.
4.  **Competitor Response:** A large campaign telegraphs the strategy to rivals, who can quickly launch copycats with their own distribution leverage, diluting the first-mover advantage in a low-barrier novelty segment.

**Blockers:**
*   **Flavor-Color Mismatch:** The fundamental product premise conflicts with established consumer sensory expectations. No evidence is provided that this specific barrier can be overcome.
*   **Unproven Retention:** The decision relies on test market trial data, which the proposal itself admits may overstate demand. There is no data on repeat purchases, the only metric that sustains a CPG brand.

**Risks:** High cannibalization of the core cash cow; rapid competitor copying; marketing spend wasted on non-repeat buyers.

**Kill Criteria:** If repeat purchase rate in extended test markets does not exceed 25% within 90 days, or if flagship sales drop more than 3% in test zones, the launch must be halted.

The proposal asks for a nationwide commitment based on trial data while explicitly acknowledging the primary reasons the product might fail (flavor confusion, lack of repeat, cannibalization). These are not just risks; they are unaddressed flaws in the core logic.

DECISION: NO-GO
