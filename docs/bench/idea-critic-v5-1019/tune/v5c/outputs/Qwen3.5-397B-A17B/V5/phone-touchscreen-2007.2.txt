First call: GO. The proposal trades initial scale for unprecedented control over the user experience and a new revenue model, directly countering the erosion of the music player business by integrating it into a superior device.

**Steelman:** Launching a premium, keyboard-less touchscreen phone via an exclusive carrier partnership allows the company to define the smartphone category, protect its music ecosystem, and capture service revenue, rather than ceding design control to carriers or competitors.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Music player sales erode as phone makers add media features; the company remains a peripheral accessory maker.
2.  **License software:** Carriers dictate design, diluting the brand and user experience; revenue is limited to licensing fees.
3.  **Proposed path:** High risk due to lack of phone experience and premium pricing, but offers the highest upside by owning the entire stack and disrupting carrier dominance.

**Fit:** This moves the north star from selling standalone devices to owning the primary digital lifestyle interface. It assumes the market values a superior integrated experience over physical keyboards and lower prices.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the touchscreen interface proves unusable without a keyboard, or if the exclusive carrier cannot support the premium branding. However, the cost of delay is catastrophic: waiting allows competitors to lock users into inferior but entrenched ecosystems.

**Evidence & Blockers:** The proposal admits no phone experience and no third-party apps initially. These are risks, not blockers. The core claim—that carriers will concede design control for exclusivity and revenue share—is the hinge. If a carrier agrees (as stated in the proposal's premise), the lack of experience is an execution challenge, not a fatal flaw. The "no keyboard" risk is mitigated by the promise of a "full web browser" and music integration, which keyboards hinder. The premium price is justified by the unique value proposition.

**Kill Criteria:** If the exclusive carrier partner fails to meet marketing commitments or if early user testing shows the touchscreen interface is fundamentally unusable for core tasks (typing, navigation) compared to keyboards, the project must pivot or stop.

Blockers: NONE (The proposal states the carrier agrees to terms; lack of experience is an execution risk).
Risks: Usability of touchscreen, limited initial reach due to exclusivity, lack of apps.

DECISION: GO
