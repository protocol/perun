First call: NO-GO. The proposal targets a shrinking demographic (low-carb dieters) with a compromised product (half-sugar) at a premium price, while ignoring the established growth in diet cola and the risk of the trend being a fad.

**Steelman:** A mid-calorie option captures young men who want taste without the full sugar load of regular cola but distrust artificial sweeteners in diet versions, leveraging a first-mover advantage before a rival launches.

**Attack:**
1.  **Market Fit:** The core assumption is that "half-sugar" satisfies a distinct need. Evidence suggests the opposite: dieters want zero calories (diet), and taste-seekers want full sugar. A hybrid risks satisfying neither, creating a "worst of both worlds" perception.
2.  **Pricing & Trial:** Launching a speculative product at a premium during a potential "fad" creates a high barrier to trial. If the low-carb trend collapses, the brand is stuck with expensive inventory and a confused identity.
3.  **Competition:** With a rival already launching a similar drink, the first-mover advantage is negligible. Entering a crowded, niche space with a higher price point is strategically unsound without proof of unique value.
4.  **Trend Volatility:** Betting a major campaign on a trend explicitly identified as a potential "fad" violates basic portfolio hygiene. The cost of delay (waiting to see if the trend sticks) is lower than the cost of a failed national launch.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Continue growing diet cola (proven growth) and regular cola (core cash cow). Cost: $0. Risk: Low. Reversibility: N/A.
*   *Small Pilot:* Test the mid-calorie concept in a limited region without a premium price or large campaign. Cost: Low. Risk: Medium. Reversibility: High.

**Blockers:**
*   The proposal admits the target audience (low-carb dieters) already has a preferred solution (diet cola) and that the product may satisfy neither segment. This internal contradiction destroys the value proposition.
*   Pricing a speculative, fad-dependent product at a premium contradicts the need for rapid trial adoption.

**Risks:**
*   Low-carb trend collapses (high probability per proposal).
*   Brand dilution by confusing the portfolio.

The proposal fails because its own stated risks confirm the product lacks a clear market fit and relies on a volatile trend. The premium pricing exacerbates the risk of failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
