First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits $36 billion to acquire a network explicitly described as incompatible and data-limited, requiring the buyer to run legacy equipment while bearing massive goodwill risk.

**Steelman:** The merger secures a high-value business niche (push-to-talk) that the buyer cannot organically replicate quickly, justifying the premium to lock out competitors from this sticky segment.

**Attack:** The core value (push-to-talk) relies on the very technology deemed "incompatible" and "limited for data." Integrating two disparate networks in 2005 is not merely costly; it threatens service continuity for the acquired base during migration. If the technology is obsolete for data, the $36 billion valuation likely prices a dying asset. The "large goodwill" creates a future balance sheet bomb if the integration fails or the customer base churns due to service degradation.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Avoid $36B outlay and integration chaos; lose the niche to a competitor who might buy them or build a compatible solution.
2.  **Partner/MVNO:** Lease the target's push-to-talk capacity without buying the broken network or the goodwill burden.
3.  **Asset-only acquisition:** Buy only the customer contracts and spectrum, abandoning the incompatible legacy hardware.

**Fit:** Assuming the goal is data growth (standard for 2005 carriers), acquiring a data-limited network contradicts the north star. If the goal is purely voice niche dominance, the integration risk outweighs the reward given the technology mismatch.

**Pre-mortem:** One year post-merger, integration costs have doubled, the push-to-talk service suffers outages during migration, business customers flee to specialized rivals, and the buyer writes down the $36B goodwill as the "legacy" technology proves unsalvageable.

**Blocker:** The proposal admits the target's technology is "incompatible" and "limited for data" yet offers no credible technical path to migrate these high-value users to the buyer's modern network without churn. Paying a premium for an asset that actively hinders the buyer's primary technological trajectory (data) constitutes a fundamental strategic mismatch. The risk is not theoretical; the incompatibility is a stated fact.

**Risks:** Integration cost overruns, culture clash, goodwill impairment.

DECISION: NO-GO
