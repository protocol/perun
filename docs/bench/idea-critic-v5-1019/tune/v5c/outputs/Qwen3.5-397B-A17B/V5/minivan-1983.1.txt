First call: GO. The proposal leverages existing assets (compact platform, converted factory) to address a clear market gap (garage-friendly family hauler) created by the shift away from bulky vans, offering a capital-efficient path for a cash-strapped manufacturer.

Steelman: By adapting a proven compact-car platform for a front-wheel-drive minivan, the company can rapidly produce a vehicle that offers car-like handling and garage compatibility for families, minimizing upfront investment while creating a new category before rivals react.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Continue selling declining station wagons and large vans; high risk of renewed insolvency as consumer preferences shift.
2. Full-size redesign: Prohibitively expensive given cash constraints; slow time-to-market.
3. Commercial van pivot: Ignores the specific "family" need and garage constraint; lower margin potential.
The proposed path beats "do nothing" by creating revenue with minimal capex and beats redesigns by utilizing existing tooling.

Fit: Aligns with the survival imperative (north star) following the government rescue. It assumes families prioritize garage fit and driving dynamics over raw cargo volume, a logical inference given the decline of full-size vans.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if buyers strictly perceive it as a commercial vehicle due to styling or branding. This is a two-way door; styling can be updated in subsequent model years. The cost of delay is catastrophic, as competitors could seize the emerging niche immediately.

Evidence: The premise relies on the stated fact that families are currently buying station wagons or full-size vans, implying dissatisfaction with both extremes. No external data contradicts the feasibility of the platform conversion within the proposal's scope.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (no existing category, cash limits, copycats) are actually arguments *for* the plan: the lack of a category indicates a blue-ocean opportunity; cash limits necessitate this specific low-capex approach; and being first provides a temporary moat. The platform limitation is a feature, not a bug, ensuring the "car-like" drive.

Risks: Brand perception (commercial vs. family). Mitigation: Marketing must explicitly target families and emphasize "garage fit."

Kill criteria: If pre-orders from family demographics fall below 5,000 units in the first quarter, halt expansion and reassess positioning. Leading metric: Ratio of family vs. commercial buyers in early sales.

DECISION: GO
