First call: NO-GO. The proposal discards the brand's primary shelf cue for an untested aesthetic, risking immediate sales collapse among a loyal, older demographic without a safety net.

Steelman: A modernized package will attract new buyers and signal premium quality, outweighing temporary confusion among existing customers.

Attack: The "shelf cue" risk is not hypothetical; it is the mechanism of purchase for impulse and habitual buyers. Removing the orange image eliminates the visual anchor that distinguishes this brand from competitors in a split-second decision. The proposal admits the buyer base is "loyal and older," a segment statistically resistant to abstract design changes. Launching "nationwide... at once" creates a single point of failure: if recognition fails, there is no market left to recover from, as the entire inventory is simultaneously compromised. The "large advertising campaign" assumes it can retrain millions of habits instantly, which is an unproven assumption contradicting the stated risk of shopper confusion.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserves current revenue; zero execution risk.
2. Regional pilot: Test the new design in a limited market to measure recognition failure before national rollout.
3. Hybrid design: Retain the orange image but modernize the surrounding elements, balancing freshness with recognition.

Pre-mortem: Six months post-launch, sales have dropped 20%. Investigation reveals older shoppers walked past the cartons, unable to locate the brand quickly. The company is forced to revert to old packaging, incurring double production costs and massive waste from unsold new cartons.

Blockers:
1. The plan explicitly removes the stated "main shelf cue" while targeting a demographic prone to recognition friction, creating a direct contradiction between the action and the goal of maintaining sales.
2. The "all stores at once" approach eliminates the ability to learn from failure, turning a manageable risk into a catastrophic bet.

Risks: Advertising may fail to overcome visual inertia; production costs may exceed projections.

Kill criteria: If a regional pilot shows a >5% drop in repeat purchase rate within 4 weeks, halt the redesign.

DECISION: NO-GO
