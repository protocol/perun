First call: NO-GO. The plan to phase out the only successful product before the successor proves reliable or adopted creates an immediate revenue cliff with no bridge.

Steelman: A feature-complete business machine is essential to capture the enterprise market, but replacing the current revenue engine prematurely risks total collapse if the new complex design fails or the ecosystem refuses to migrate.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Retain current market share but cede the business sector to competitors; safe but limits growth.
2. Parallel launch: Release the new machine as a premium tier while maintaining the existing product for education/home users; higher cost but preserves cash flow during transition.
3. Proposed plan: Immediate replacement; highest risk, potential for total failure if adoption lags.

Fit: Assuming the goal is market expansion, alienating the existing user base contradicts sustainable growth.

Pre-mortem: The new machine ships late with bugs due to complexity. Software makers ignore it due to small install base. The company halts the old machine, revenue drops 90%, and bankruptcy follows within six months. This is a one-way door; once the old tooling is scrapped, it cannot be quickly restarted.

Evidence: The proposal admits hardware reliability is "unproven" and the schedule is "tight." It offers no data showing existing owners *want* to migrate, only that business users want features. Phasing out the current product is an assumption, not a strategy backed by migration guarantees.

Blockers:
1. The plan mandates ending the current product despite unproven reliability of the successor. This creates a single point of failure for company revenue.
2. No mechanism exists to force software makers or existing owners to migrate; the "phase out" strategy assumes compliance that is not evidenced.

Risks: Complex design leads to delays; ecosystem fragmentation; brand damage if the new machine fails.

Kill criteria: If a working prototype with 99% uptime is not demonstrated in 90 days, or if pre-orders from major software vendors do not cover 50% of development costs by month 4, the project must pivot to a parallel launch strategy rather than replacement.

DECISION: NO-GO
