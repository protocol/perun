First call: NO-GO. The proposal launches a business-focused device without its core utility (email) unless paired with another product, directly contradicting the company's primary market strength and value proposition.

Steelman: The company aims to leverage its new OS to create a portable, lower-cost companion device that drives ecosystem lock-in, betting that business users will tolerate initial app gaps for the form factor and brand trust.

However, the attack reveals fatal flaws in the current plan:
1.  **Core Value Contradiction:** A "business email leader" releasing a tablet without native email, calendar, or contacts destroys its competitive advantage. Business users cannot rely on a phone pairing for critical daily functions; this is not a feature gap, it is a category failure.
2.  **Pricing Mismatch:** Charging the same price as a rival's larger, established tablet with a mature app store offers zero value. The smaller screen and missing apps make the price point indefensible.
3.  **Untested OS Risk:** Launching on an untested OS without the safety net of core productivity apps amplifies the risk of developer abandonment. If the flagship business apps are missing, third-party developers have no incentive to commit.
4.  **Better Way:** The viable alternative is to delay launch until core business apps are native, or price significantly lower to account for the reduced utility and ecosystem risk. "Do nothing" is superior to launching a product that actively damages the brand's reputation for business reliability.

The pre-mortem is clear: The device launches, business reviewers pan the lack of native email, sales stall due to the high price relative to competitors, and the new OS is labeled a failure before gaining traction. The cost of delay is negligible compared to the cost of a brand-damaging flop.

Blockers:
- Launching a business device without native email contradicts the company's core identity and user needs.
- Price parity with a superior, larger competitor ensures immediate market rejection.

Risks:
- Developer apathy due to the untested OS and lack of flagship apps.
- Consumer confusion over the device's standalone utility.

This plan fails the basic test of offering value to its target audience. It attempts to sell a handicap at a premium price.

DECISION: NO-GO
