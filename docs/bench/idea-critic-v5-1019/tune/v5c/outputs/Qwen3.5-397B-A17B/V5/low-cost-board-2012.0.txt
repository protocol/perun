First call: GO. A $35 price point creates a wholly new category for education, distinct from shared labs or expensive personal laptops, directly addressing the charity's mission despite execution risks.

**Steelman:** A ultra-low-cost, credit-card-sized computer can democratize access to programming hardware for students globally, relying on existing user peripherals to minimize unit cost and focusing charity resources on curriculum rather than full-system support.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Status quo maintains high barriers; students lack personal hardware.
2.  **Subsidized laptops:** Higher cost ($200+) limits scale; harder to repair/modify.
3.  **Proposed SBC:** Lowest entry cost; encourages tinkering; shifts peripheral burden to users.
The proposal beats "do nothing" by enabling 1:1 ownership at a previously impossible price.

**Fit:** Aligns perfectly with the north star of rekindling student interest through accessible, hands-on experimentation.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the supply chain cannot scale past the initial batch, leaving demand unmet, or if the "bring your own peripheral" requirement proves too complex for target schools, rendering the board useless. However, these are execution risks, not fundamental flaws. The low price creates its own demand pull.

**Evidence:** The $35 target is the critical fact. If achieved, it undercuts all alternatives by an order of magnitude. The lack of manufacturing experience is a valid concern but solvable via licensing distributors, as proposed.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (peripherals, batch size, inexperience, competition) are manageable execution challenges, not facts proving the plan fails. Laptops are not "cheap" enough for 1:1 student deployment in most contexts; tablets are consumption-only.

**Risks:** Supply chain bottlenecks; user friction regarding peripherals; lack of support infrastructure. These become kill criteria: if the first batch sells out without restock capability within 3 months, or if >40% of buyers fail to boot due to missing peripherals, the model requires revision.

**Decision:** The core hypothesis—that price is the primary barrier—is sound. The plan addresses the biggest hurdle (cost) while outsourcing the rest. Proceed with the capped first batch to validate supply and demand.

DECISION: GO
