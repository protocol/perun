First call: GO. The proposal targets a distinct market segment (portability, battery life, low price) that the stronger, color-screen rival ignores, leveraging a bundled puzzle game to drive adoption despite hardware limitations.

**Steelman:** A sub-$90, long-battery device with a killer app can dominate the mass market by prioritizing playability and affordability over graphical fidelity, creating a software ecosystem where none existed for the company previously.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Cede the portable market entirely to the rival; rely solely on home consoles.
2.  **High-end handheld:** Match rival specs; likely results in high cost, poor battery, and delayed launch, failing to differentiate.
3.  **Proposed path:** Low cost, long battery, strong IP bundle. Beats "do nothing" by capturing a new user base; beats high-end by ensuring margin volume and instant software hook.

**Fit:** Aligns with expanding the total addressable market beyond the living room. Assumes the "puzzle game" has broad appeal beyond core gamers.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if third-party developers ignore the monochrome screen, leaving the device with only the bundled title. However, the low entry price mitigates consumer risk even with a small library initially. This is a two-way door; failure limits loss to the $20M marketing spend and initial tooling, while success creates a decades-long revenue stream. Cost of delay is high: the rival is already establishing user lock-in.

**Evidence:** The proposal's own figures show a clear price gap ($89.95 vs. implied higher rival cost) and a specific differentiator (battery life). The "single-game" history is a constraint to be broken by the new bundled model, not a predictor of future failure.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks (monochrome, modest specs) are features of the cost strategy, not contradictions. The thin margin risk is offset by the volume potential of the low price point and software attach rate.

**Risks:** Developer adoption; screen perception. These become kill criteria: if developer sign-ups do not reach a critical mass within 6 months, or if initial sales miss targets by >20%, pivot marketing or hardware revision.

**Kill Criteria:** Stop if software attach rate <1.5 units per device after 90 days. Leading metric: Weekly sell-through rate vs. rival.

DECISION: GO
