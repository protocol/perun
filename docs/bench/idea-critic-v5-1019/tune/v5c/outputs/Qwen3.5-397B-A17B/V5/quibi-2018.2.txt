First call: NO-GO. The proposal bets $1B+ on unproven demand for premium short-form subscriptions amid acknowledged fatigue and abundant free alternatives.

Steelman: A high-fidelity, mobile-native premium video service could capture high-value users if orientation-agnostic tech and A-list talent overcome subscription fatigue.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Avoid $1B loss; status quo remains.
2. **Ad-supported model:** Leverages existing user behavior for short-form; lower price barrier.
3. **Proposed Plan:** High capex, high price, unproven demand.
The proposal fails to beat "do nothing" or the cheaper ad-supported alternative on stated facts.

Fit: Assumes users want to *pay* for short content when free options exist. This contradicts the stated "subscription fatigue" and "abundant free" landscape.

Pre-mortem: The service launches, users try one month due to talent, then churn because the content format (short-form) does not justify a recurring premium fee compared to free competitors. The $1B burn rate becomes unsustainable before product-market fit is found. It is a two-way door experiment executed with one-way door capital intensity.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits "no proven demand," "subscription fatigue," and "abundant free" competition. These are not risks to be mitigated later; they are current facts that invalidate the core revenue hypothesis.

Blockers:
1. **Contradictory Premise:** The plan requires users to pay a premium for a format (short-form) explicitly stated as having "no proven demand" in a paid context, while free substitutes are abundant.
2. **Capital Mismatch:** Deploying $1B+ (irreversible scale) to test a hypothesis the proposers admit is unproven violates basic capital allocation logic. A pilot or capped test is mandatory before this scale.
3. **Superior Alternative:** An ad-supported model or a smaller, capped pilot reaches the same learning upside with drastically lower risk and cost.

Risks: Execution difficulty, talent retention, orientation tech failure. These are secondary to the fundamental demand blocker.

The proposal's own facts confirm the target users have not demonstrated willingness to pay for this specific value proposition. Proceeding as proposed ignores the "no proven demand" fact by treating it as a risk rather than a blocker.

DECISION: NO-GO
