First call: GO. The request comes from the dominant market leader's largest customer, addressing immediate capacity constraints during rapid industry growth. The design features (wide-body, raised cockpit for freight conversion) directly target the stated goal of lowering cost per seat while creating a durable secondary market value.

Steelman: Building a massively larger, convertible freighter/passenger jet with new engines is the only viable path to maintain market dominance and meet the specific efficiency demands of the largest customer during a travel boom.

Alternatives considered:
1. Do nothing: Cedes the high-capacity segment to competitors and fails the largest customer, risking their future loyalty.
2. Stretch existing narrow-body: Physically impractical to achieve 2.5x capacity; compromises safety and efficiency.
3. Wait for supersonic: Speculative timeline; does not solve the immediate subsonic capacity crunch.

Fit: Aligns with the vision of market dominance and serving the primary customer's growth. The freighter capability hedges against the risk of supersonic displacement by ensuring utility even if passenger demand shifts.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the new engine fails certification or if the single customer cancels before completion. However, the raised cockpit design mitigates the supersonic risk by guaranteeing freight utility. The cost of delay is losing the defining contract of the era to a rival.

Evidence: The proposal rests on the factual premise of rapid travel growth and a specific, committed request from the largest airline. The "unproven" engine is a necessary evolution for efficiency at this scale; high-bypass technology is the logical next step. The single order represents a massive baseline commitment given the customer's size.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (cost, engine, supersonic threat, single customer) are inherent to pioneering a new category but do not invalidate the core logic. The cost is large but justified by the market position; the engine risk is manageable with a supplier partner; the supersonic threat is uncertain and distant; the single customer is the market leader.

Risks: High capital exposure, engine development delays, supersonic disruption. These become kill criteria: monitor engine test milestones monthly; secure a second launch customer within 12 months to diversify risk; halt if supersonic regulatory approval accelerates unexpectedly.

DECISION: GO
