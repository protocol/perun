First call: GO. The proposal secures the franchise's survival against an inevitable market shift, despite immediate profitability risks.

**Steelman:** Offering free streaming now, even with a limited catalog, retains subscribers by establishing the brand as the primary destination for online video before competitors lock up exclusive rights and user habits.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Protects short-term DVD margins but cedes the online market to tech giants, likely rendering the DVD business obsolete faster as broadband adoption accelerates.
2.  **Paid/Better Streaming later:** High risk of failure; competitors will capture the market first, and licensing costs will only increase as they gain leverage.
3.  **Proposed Plan:** Uses the existing seven-million-subscriber base as a captive testing ground. The "no extra charge" model removes adoption friction, gathering crucial data on streaming behavior while the DVD business still funds operations.

**Fit:** Aligns with the long-term vision of video-on-demand. The current DVD profit is the fuel for the transition, not the end state.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if licensing costs balloon before scale is achieved or if the poor quality drives users to pirated or competitor sites immediately. However, waiting guarantees irrelevance. This is a one-way door; delaying entry is effectively closing the door forever. The cost of delay is total market exclusion.

**Evidence & Blockers:** The facts state the company is currently profitable with 7M subscribers. This cash flow is the specific asset required to absorb the high licensing costs and technology investment. The risks (cannibalization, cost) are real but are features of the transition, not blockers. Cannibalization is preferable to self-disruption by a third party. The small catalog is a constraint, but starting small allows for iterative expansion funded by the core business. No fact presented proves the plan fails as proposed; rather, the facts prove that *inaction* fails.

**Decision:** The plan converts a looming existential threat into a managed transition using existing strengths.

Blockers: NONE.
Risks: High licensing costs, potential short-term margin compression, technology scaling issues. These are managed via the existing profit engine and kill criteria (e.g., stop if subscriber churn exceeds X% without streaming uptake).

DECISION: GO
