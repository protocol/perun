First call: NO-GO. The proposal mandates a paid subscription and broadband requirement in a 2002 market where rivals offer free online play and broadband penetration is critically low, creating an immediate barrier to entry that contradicts stated user expectations.

**Steelman:** A unified, premium identity and voice ecosystem creates superior user retention and a recurring revenue stream, differentiating the console as a high-end service platform rather than a commodity hardware box.

**Attack:** The claim fails on three verified facts from the proposal itself. First, "few homes have broadband" means the mandatory requirement excludes the vast majority of the potential install base, capping adoption before launch. Second, competitors leaving online play "free or up to each publisher" establishes a market norm of zero marginal cost for the user; introducing a mandatory annual fee creates a price disadvantage with no proven willingness to pay. Third, the company "has never run" hosting or support operations, introducing massive execution risk for a service that requires 100% uptime to justify its cost.

**Alternatives:** 
1. *Do nothing:* Match rivals by letting publishers handle online play; preserves full install base but forfeits service revenue.
2. *Hybrid model:* Offer basic matchmaking/free identity for free, charging only for premium voice/features; lowers barrier while testing willingness to pay.
3. *Publisher partnership:* Subsidize infrastructure costs by sharing revenue with publishers rather than charging users directly.

**Pre-mortem:** The service launches, but low broadband adoption and subscriber resistance result in a tiny user base. The high fixed cost of unused hosting capacity and support staff drains capital, forcing a shutdown within 18 months.

**Blockers:** 
1. Mandatory broadband requirement vs. "few homes have broadband" (Market size mismatch).
2. Mandatory subscription vs. "players expect online play to be free" (Price resistance).
3. Zero internal experience vs. complex operational requirements (Execution risk).

These are not risks; they are factual contradictions between the proposal's requirements and the market reality described. No kill criteria can fix a business model where the addressable market is near-zero and the price point opposes established norms.

DECISION: NO-GO
