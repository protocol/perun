First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a hardware manufacturer with zero platform experience to enter a duopoly dominated by entrenched competitors with loyal user bases and iconic first-party software, while simultaneously launching against a rival's next-generation 3D system.

Steelman: A CD-based console could disrupt the market by offering significantly lower manufacturing costs and easier development tools than cartridges, attracting third-party developers ignored by incumbents focused on first-party titles.

However, the attack reveals fatal structural flaws. The "better way" analysis shows that "doing nothing" or continuing as a peripheral partner avoids the massive capital burn of platform ownership. The proposal admits no experience as a platform holder; building an ecosystem from scratch requires subsidizing hardware losses and securing hit games simultaneously—a coordination problem the company is ill-equipped to solve. The risk of an incumbent launching a superior 3D console at the same time is not a mere risk but a near-certain blocker given the competitor's established pipeline.

The pre-mortem is clear: the console launches with a weak game library because top developers stick with the incumbents' guaranteed install bases. Hardware sells at a loss, unable to recoup costs through software royalties due to low adoption. The company bleeds cash on inventory and marketing, failing to reach critical mass before the incumbent's superior technology renders the offering obsolete.

The evidence provided highlights that consoles sell at thin or negative margins. Without an existing library of famous games to drive hardware adoption, the company must spend heavily on developer incentives and consumer subsidies with no guarantee of return. The "cheaper disc manufacturing" advantage is irrelevant if no one buys the player. The proposal relies on outside developers taking a massive risk on an unproven platform, which contradicts rational economic behavior in a market defined by hit-driven dynamics.

Blockers:
1. Zero platform experience combined with immediate competition from an incumbent's 3D launch creates an insurmountable barrier to developer adoption.
2. Negative hardware margins require a software ecosystem that does not exist and cannot be guaranteed against entrenched rivals.

Risks:
1. Failure to secure key third-party titles.
2. Inability to subsidize hardware losses long enough to gain traction.

DECISION: NO-GO
