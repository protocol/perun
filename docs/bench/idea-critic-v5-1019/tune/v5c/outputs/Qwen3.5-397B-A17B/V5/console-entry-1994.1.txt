First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a hardware manufacturer with zero platform experience to enter a duopoly dominated by incumbents with loyal user bases and iconic first-party software, while simultaneously fighting a direct 3D competitor and relying on unproven third-party developer support in a low-margin hardware environment.

Steelman: A CD-based console can disrupt the market by offering significantly cheaper manufacturing and easier development tools than cartridges, attracting third-party developers ignored by incumbents focused on first-party hits.

Attack: The "cheaper disc" advantage is nullified if the hardware sells at a loss and cannot reach critical mass without first-party system sellers. The proposal admits no experience as a platform holder; courting outside developers is speculative when incumbents already lock up talent with famous series. The simultaneous launch of a rival 3D console creates a direct feature war where the proposer has no differentiation beyond media format, which consumers may not value over game quality.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Avoids massive R&D sinkhole and brand damage from a likely failed platform entry.
2. Partner with existing software houses: Secure exclusive titles before announcing hardware to de-risk the ecosystem gap.
3. License the OS to other hardware makers: Become the "Windows" of consoles rather than bearing hardware margin risk alone.

Fit: This contradicts a prudent North Star of leveraging core competencies; it assumes a shift to platform management without evidence of capability.

Pre-mortem: The console launches, developers hesitate due to small install base, hardware subsidies drain cash reserves, and the incumbent's superior first-party lineup drives consumers to the rival 3D machine. The project is cancelled after two years with significant financial loss.

Evidence: The proposal itself lists "no experience," "loyal customers" for rivals, and "thin margins" as facts. These are not risks to be managed later; they are structural blockers to immediate approval. The absence of a secured "killer app" or a committed developer partner makes the "easier tools" argument theoretical.

Blockers: 
1. No proven ability to operate a platform ecosystem (stated fact).
2. Direct competition from an incumbent's 3D launch with established IP (stated fact).
3. Reliance on uncommitted third-party developers to succeed against entrenched first-party studios (stated fact).

Risks: Consumer rejection of CD format over cartridges; inability to subsidize hardware sufficiently.

DECISION: NO-GO
