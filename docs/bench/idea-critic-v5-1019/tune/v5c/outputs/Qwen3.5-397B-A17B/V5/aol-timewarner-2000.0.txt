First call: NO-GO. The proposal exchanges stable, cash-generating media assets for a volatile dial-up business whose core value proposition is being erased by the very broadband transition the deal claims to leverage.

Steelman: The merger creates an unbeatable ecosystem by marrying premium content with dominant digital distribution, accelerating broadband rollout via cable infrastructure to monetize cross-promotion.

Attack:
1.  **Fundamental Mismatch:** The internet provider's valuation relies on dial-up growth, yet the strategy requires shifting users to broadband. As dial-up declines, the "majority" equity held by its shareholders collapses in value, while the media conglomerate's tangible assets are permanently diluted.
2.  **Dependency Risk:** The plan hinges on delivering high-speed access over the conglomerate's cable network. However, cable partners are distinct entities; access is not guaranteed, and upgrading infrastructure requires massive capital expenditure not accounted for in the all-stock premium.
3.  **Cultural & Operational Friction:** Combining a fast-moving, stock-option-driven internet culture with a traditional, process-heavy media bureaucracy historically leads to paralysis, not synergy. Cross-promotion sounds logical but often fails in execution due to conflicting incentives.
4.  **Regulatory & Market Timing:** At peak valuation (January 2000), the internet provider is priced for perfection. Any regulatory delay or immediate slowdown in dial-up revenue triggers a catastrophic loss of confidence, dragging down the combined entity.

Alternatives:
*   **Do Nothing:** Preserve the media conglomerate's cash flow and wait to acquire digital assets at post-bubble valuations.
*   **Strategic Partnerships:** License content to various ISPs without merging balance sheets or cultures.
*   **Organic Broadband Build:** Invest internally in cable upgrades without taking on dilutive equity risk.

Pre-mortem: The deal closes at the market peak. Dial-up subscribers churn rapidly to broadband providers who are not tied to the legacy cable partners. The stock price collapses as the "synergies" fail to materialize against cultural gridlock, leaving the original media shareholders with worthless paper.

Blockers:
*   The internet provider's valuation is based on a dying technology (dial-up) while the strategy requires a different technology (broadband) with uncertain access.
*   Exchange of permanent equity for a transient, volatile asset class at its historical peak.

Risks: Regulatory rejection, culture clash, execution failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
