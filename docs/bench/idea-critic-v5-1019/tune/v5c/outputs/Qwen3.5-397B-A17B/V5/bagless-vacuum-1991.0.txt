First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks to launch a capital-intensive hardware company with no manufacturing track record, a price premium over incumbents, and no proven demand for the core value proposition (bagless convenience vs. cost).

**Steelman:** A superior cyclone technology justifies a premium price and can overcome distribution barriers through direct marketing or niche positioning, eventually forcing incumbents to react too late.

**Attack:**
1.  **Economic Viability:** Pricing "noticeably more" than established brands without a brand halo is a classic adoption killer. In 1991, consumers are highly price-sensitive regarding appliances. The margin required to fund new manufacturing lines while undercutting incumbent distribution costs is likely negative.
2.  **Incumbent Response:** The claim that incumbents "could copy" understates the threat. If the technology works, giants with existing supply chains and retail shelf space can reverse-engineer or license similar cyclone tech faster than a startup can build a factory, then undercut the startup's price immediately.
3.  **Execution Risk:** "No manufacturing record" is a fatal blocker for hardware. Prototyping thousands of units is not scaling to hundreds of thousands. The capital burn to fix production bugs will bankrupt the venture before market fit is proven.
4.  **Demand Assumption:** Buyers "may not pay extra" is treated as a risk, but without data showing willingness to pay, it is a blocker. The proposal assumes the technical benefit translates to perceived value without evidence.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Preserve capital.
*   **Licensing:** Sell the patent to an incumbent. This captures value without manufacturing risk or distribution battles.
*   **Pilot:** Build a tiny batch for direct sale to validate the price point before founding a full company.

**Blockers:**
*   No evidence of willingness to pay the premium.
*   No manufacturing capability or partner secured.
*   No strategy to survive incumbent price wars if they copy the tech.

**Risks:**
*   High capital burn.
*   Retail distribution rejection.

The proposal fails the "beat do nothing" test because the licensing alternative captures the upside of the invention while removing the fatal manufacturing and distribution blockers. Founding a company now is premature.

DECISION: NO-GO
