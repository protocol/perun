First call: NO-GO. The proposal trades a loyal, functioning community for an unproven design while removing all safety mechanisms against failure.

Steelman: A complete rewrite with a publisher-focused algorithm and immediate migration is the only way to reverse traffic decline and modernize the platform before a rival captures the market.

**Attack:**
The plan fails on three verified premises within the text. First, it ignores a stated blocker: the core community values existing voting mechanics. Changing this to favor publishers directly antagonizes the only asset (loyalty) the site possesses, likely accelerating exodus to the growing rival. Second, the "large engineering risk" of a rewrite is compounded by a "move all users at once" strategy. This creates a single point of failure with no rollback path; if the new algorithm repels users, the damage is instantaneous and irreversible. Third, the proposal offers no evidence that "favoring publishers" drives traffic in this specific context; it is an assumption contradicted by the community's stated values.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Retains community trust but risks slow decline.
2.  **Staged Rollout:** Rewrite but migrate 5% of users first. This preserves the rollback capability and tests the "publisher favor" hypothesis without burning the bridge.
3.  **Hybrid Approach:** Keep core voting mechanics for loyalists while introducing a parallel "publisher feed" for new traffic.

**Pre-mortem:**
Six months post-launch, traffic has collapsed by 40%. The loyal community revolted against the changed voting mechanics, migrating to the rival site. The engineering team is stuck debugging the new monolithic codebase with no ability to revert to the stable previous version because the database schema was overwritten during the forced migration.

**Blockers:**
1.  **No Rollback:** Moving all users at once on a rewrite eliminates the ability to recover from the stated "large engineering risk."
2.  **Community Antagonism:** The plan explicitly changes the mechanic the core user base values, without a pilot to validate if publisher content actually attracts enough new users to offset the loss.

**Decision:**
The absence of a pilot, a phased migration, or a rollback plan constitutes a fatal flaw. The proposal demands a high-risk, irreversible action based on unproven assumptions about user preference.

DECISION: NO-GO
