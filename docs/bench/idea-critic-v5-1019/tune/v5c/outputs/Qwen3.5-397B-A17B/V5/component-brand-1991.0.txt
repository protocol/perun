First call: GO. The proposal addresses a critical vulnerability: commodity pricing driven by buyer ignorance. If buyers cannot distinguish the component, they will not pay a premium, and clone makers will erode margins. Making the invisible visible is the only path to pricing power.

**Steelman:** Transform the microprocessor from an unknown specification into the primary purchasing criterion for end-users, forcing computer makers to adopt the brand to remain competitive, thereby securing margin and market share against clones.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Continue as an anonymous supplier; margins collapse as clones proliferate and buyers choose solely on computer price.
2.  **Trade-only marketing:** Advertise only to computer makers; fails to stop clones or influence end-user demand.
3.  **Consumer brand campaign (Proposed):** High cost and channel friction, but creates unique demand pull that computer makers cannot ignore. It beats "do nothing" because the risk of commoditization is existential, while the cost of advertising is finite.

**Fit:** Assumes the goal is long-term margin protection and market dominance rather than short-term budget conservation. This moves the north star from "units shipped to OEMs" to "end-user preference."

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if computer makers collectively refuse the co-op funds, viewing them as controlling. However, the first mover among OEMs will gain a sales advantage, breaking any boycott. The cost of delay is immediate share loss to clones using similar part numbers.

**Evidence:** The proposal facts state buyers are currently ignorant and clones are confusing the market. The logic holds: if buyers ask by name, OEMs must supply it.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (unprecedented nature, OEM resentment, budget share) are execution challenges, not factual contradictions. Unprecedented actions often yield monopoly rents. OEM resentment is mitigated by the financial incentive (paid advertising).

**Risks:** Budget overrun; slow consumer adoption. These become kill criteria: halt if brand awareness does not rise by a measurable percentage within 12 months, or if OEM participation drops below a critical threshold.

**Kill Criteria:** Stop if, after two quarters, consumer "pull" requests do not increase OEM orders specifically for the branded chip versus generic equivalents.

DECISION: GO
