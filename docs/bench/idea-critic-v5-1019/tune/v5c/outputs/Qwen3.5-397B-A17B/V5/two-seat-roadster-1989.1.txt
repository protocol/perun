First call: NO-GO. The proposal admits the segment has shrunk for years, safety rules raise costs, and the maker lacks heritage, while relying on a finite pool of enthusiasts.

Steelman: A low-volume, lightweight roadster can succeed by targeting underserved enthusiasts with an affordable, pure-driving experience that mass-market makers ignore.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Zero risk, zero reward; confirms the niche is dead.
2. **Shared platform derivative:** Adapt an existing chassis to lower tooling costs; reduces financial exposure but compromises the "lightweight" claim.
3. **The Proposal:** High capital intensity for a shrinking market with no proven demand beyond early adopters.

Fit: Assumes a strategic pivot to niche heritage without evidence of margin sustainability. A sound idea must move a north star; here, the north star (volume/profit) is threatened by the admitted segment shrinkage.

Pre-mortem: The car launches to critical acclaim from journalists but fails to meet volume targets. Early enthusiasts buy out the first run, but repeat sales collapse because the total addressable market was overestimated. Safety compliance costs erode margins, making the "affordable" price point unsustainable. The company is stuck with a costly, low-volume line that distracts from core business.

Evidence: The proposal itself provides the blockers: "segment has shrunk," "safety rules make convertibles costly," and "sales may fade once early enthusiasts have bought." These are not hypothetical risks; they are stated facts within the premise. The plan offers no counter-evidence, such as a waiting list, pre-orders, or a cost structure that remains profitable at low volumes.

Blockers:
1. **Negative Unit Economics:** Stated high safety costs + "affordable" price target + shrinking volume = likely losses per unit.
2. **Market Ceiling:** The admission that sales will fade after early enthusiasts implies a one-time revenue spike, not a sustainable business.

Risks: Brand dilution, distraction from core models, regulatory changes.

Kill Criteria Check: The proposal lacks a specific metric for minimum viable volume or a cap on development spend. Without a confirmed path to profitability despite the shrinking segment and high costs, the plan fails as proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
