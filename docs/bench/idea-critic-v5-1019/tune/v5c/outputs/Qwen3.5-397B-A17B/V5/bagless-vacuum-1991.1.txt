First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks to commit capital to manufacturing and retail distribution without evidence that buyers will pay a premium for an unproven mechanism, while facing well-funded incumbents who can copy the feature instantly.

Steelman: A superior, bagless technology justifies a premium price and can survive incumbent retaliation if early adopters value the innovation enough to create a defensible niche.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Avoids capital loss; incumbents may eventually launch similar tech anyway.
2. **Licensing:** Sell the patent to an existing manufacturer. Lower risk, lower upside, avoids building a factory from scratch.
3. **Proposed Plan:** Build company, factory, and brand. Highest cost, highest risk, total exposure to execution failure and market rejection.

Fit: Assumes a market desire for "bagless" convenience over cost, which is unverified. No stated strategy to overcome the lack of retail distribution against entrenched competitors.

Pre-mortem: The company runs out of cash trying to establish manufacturing quality and retail shelf space. Incumbents release a cheaper cyclone model using their existing supply chains, rendering the startup's premium price unjustifiable. Buyers, unfamiliar with the technology, stick to known brands. This is a one-way door with high irreversible costs (tooling, inventory).

Evidence: The proposal admits buyers "may not pay extra" and incumbents "could copy the idea." There is no data showing willingness to pay, no pre-orders, and no pilot sales. The "thousands of prototypes" prove technical feasibility, not commercial viability.

Blockers:
1. **Unverified Demand:** The core assumption that users will pay a premium for bagless technology is explicitly listed as a risk ("buyers may not pay extra") with no evidence provided to counter it.
2. **Competitive Moat:** The plan relies on a mechanical feature that incumbents can replicate, yet offers no strategy (brand, patent enforcement, speed) to prevent them from crushing the new entrant once the market is proven.
3. **Execution Gap:** No manufacturing record exists, yet the plan requires immediate full-scale production rather than a contracted pilot.

Risks: High unit cost, retail rejection, rapid imitation.

The proposal fails because it treats a technical prototype as a business case. Without proof of willingness to pay or a defense against copying, launching a full manufacturing operation is speculative gambling, not operations.

DECISION: NO-GO
