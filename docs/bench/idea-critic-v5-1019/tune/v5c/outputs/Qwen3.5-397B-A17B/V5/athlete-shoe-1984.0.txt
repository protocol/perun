First call: GO. The market position in running is secure, but the basketball segment requires a discontinuous jump to gain share; incremental spending will not dislodge incumbents.

**Steelman:** Commit massive resources to an unproven asset to create a cultural phenomenon that transcends sports performance, using controversy (rule-breaking colorways) as a forced distribution and marketing mechanism.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Slowly lose basketball relevance; safe but guarantees mediocrity.
2.  **Sign established star:** High cost, low differentiation, limited upside as the player is already associated with other brands.
3.  **Proposed Rookie Deal:** Highest financial risk, but only option offering category redefinition and total brand ownership. It beats "do nothing" because the status quo is loss of ground.

**Fit:** Assumes the goal is market leadership, not just participation. A leader in running can afford one high-velocity bet to dominate a second category.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the player gets injured immediately *and* the brand lacks the courage to market the shoe without him playing. However, the "rule-breaking" element creates a narrative independent of game stats. The league fines become free advertising. The door is two-way regarding the contract length (can be managed), but the spend is sunk. Cost of delay is ceding the cultural moment to competitors.

**Evidence & Blockers:**
*   *Blockers:* None found in the text. The risks (injury, unproven status, fines) are explicitly acknowledged in the proposal.
*   *Risks:* High financial concentration.
*   *Kill Criteria:* The proposal implicitly accepts the financial risk. The "fines" are a feature, not a bug, generating press that money cannot buy. The rarity of multi-season signature shoes is the very market gap this exploits.

**Decision Logic:**
The proposal identifies the exact constraints needed for a breakout: exclusivity (rookie), narrative (banning/colorways), and commitment (5 years). The risks are real but are the price of admission for a category-defining move. A safe play fails the objective of stopping the loss of ground. The potential upside (global icon status) dwarfs the capped downside (lost marketing budget).

Blockers: NONE
Risks: Player injury, league fines, budget concentration (all accepted as strategic costs).

DECISION: GO
