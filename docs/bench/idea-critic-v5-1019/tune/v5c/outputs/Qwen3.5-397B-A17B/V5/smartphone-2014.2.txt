First call: NO-GO. The proposal combines a premium price with a fragmented ecosystem and a niche hardware feature in a saturated two-platform market, creating a fatal mismatch between cost and value.

Steelman: A device leveraging unique 3D navigation and instant commerce identification could create a high-margin niche for loyal shoppers, bypassing app store dependency via a focused utility model.

Attack:
1.  **Price vs. Platform:** Charging flagship prices for a forked OS lacking mainstream apps is economically unsound. Users pay premiums for ecosystem completeness, not just hardware specs. Without the leading apps, the device's perceived value collapses below its price point.
2.  **Feature Mismatch:** Head-tracking 3D displays are a novel gimmick, not a primary purchase driver for mass-market smartphone buyers who prioritize battery, camera, and app compatibility. This differentiates the phone away from mainstream needs.
3.  **Distribution Bottleneck:** Exclusivity with one carrier severely limits reach. In a saturated market, growth requires maximum distribution, not constrained channels, especially for a new entrant without brand gravity in phones.
4.  **App Gap:** The "smaller app store" is a critical failure point. Commerce identification cannot compensate for the inability to run banking, social, or communication apps users expect daily.

Alternatives:
*   **Do nothing:** Preserve capital and brand equity; avoid a high-visibility failure.
*   **Low-cost hardware:** Launch a budget device to expand the ecosystem footprint, subsidizing hardware to drive retail sales (the successful tablet strategy).
*   **Software-only:** Deploy the commerce identification feature as an app on existing dominant platforms to capture value without hardware risk.

Pre-mortem: The device launches to poor reviews citing app absence and high price. Carrier sales teams fail to push it against established flagships. Inventory piles up, requiring massive write-downs, damaging retailer margins and brand reputation.

Blockers:
*   **Price/Ecosystem Contradiction:** The proposal explicitly states "unsubsidised-equivalent price in line with leading flagship phones" while admitting "without the leading platform's own mainstream apps." This guarantees non-competitiveness.
*   **Market Saturation:** Entering a dominated, saturated market with a inferior software ecosystem at a premium price has no historical precedent for success.

Risks: Execution complexity, carrier relationship dependence, 3D feature adoption failure.

The core economic model is broken; no kill criteria can fix a product that offers less value at a higher price than incumbents.

DECISION: NO-GO
