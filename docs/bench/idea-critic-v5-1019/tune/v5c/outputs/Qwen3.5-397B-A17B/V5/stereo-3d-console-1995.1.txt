First call: NO-GO. The proposal describes a device with severe usability flaws (discomfort, eye strain), a crippled visual experience (red monochrome only), and a weak value proposition (neither portable nor a home console) at a premium price point, all while cannibalizing attention from an imminent next-generation home console.

Steelman: A low-cost, novel stereoscopic 3D experience can bridge the hardware gap and establish a new niche before the next home console arrives.

Attack: The steelman fails because the execution contradicts the value. "Neither portable nor comfortable" destroys the handheld appeal; "red monochrome" and "eye strain" degrade the core 3D promise. The $180 price is unjustified for a single-feature, uncomfortable novelty. The risk of "few games" is compounding: developers will not support a hardware base likely to be rejected by users due to physical discomfort. The proximity of the next home console (2 years) makes this a dangerous distraction; if it fails, it wastes resources; if it succeeds modestly, it confuses the brand before the real flagship launches.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve brand reputation and focus resources on the upcoming home console. Cost: $0. Risk: Gap in portfolio. Reversibility: N/A.
2. Software focus: Develop 3D software for existing handhelds or wait for the new home console. Cost: Low. Risk: No new hardware revenue. Reversibility: High.
3. Proposed hardware: High cost, high reputational risk, low reversibility once manufactured.

Pre-mortem: The product launches, reviewers cite headaches and poor visuals, sales stall, and the company is forced to slash prices within months, damaging the brand's premium perception just before the critical home console release.

Blockers:
1. Usability failure: The proposal admits the device is "neither portable nor comfortable" and causes "eye strain." A core consumer electronic failing basic comfort is a non-starter.
2. Value mismatch: $180 for a red-only, stationary, uncomfortable viewer with no software library cannot compete with full handhelds or home consoles.

Risks: Software shortage, brand confusion, opportunity cost against the next-gen console.

The admitted physical flaws and lack of comfort are not mere risks; they are fundamental product failures that no amount of marketing can overcome.

DECISION: NO-GO
