First call: NO-GO. Releasing an unfinished, unsupported product to a paying public without marketing or a support plan invites immediate reputational damage and refund churn that a solo developer cannot survive.

**Steelman:** Early revenue funds development while real user data prioritizes the most valuable features, validating demand before full completion.

**Options:**
1. **Proposed Plan:** High risk of negative reviews killing long-term sales; high support burden; zero marketing reach.
2. **Do Nothing:** Finish the game first, then launch. Zero early revenue, but protects reputation and allows a polished debut.
3. **Free Technical Demo:** Release a limited, stable slice for free to gather feedback without financial friction or support expectations.

**Fit:** Assumes "survival" is the north star. However, the stated facts (no support capacity, rough state) directly contradict the requirement for a successful paid early access launch.

**Pre-mortem:** The game launches; players encounter bugs and lack of support; Steam/forum reviews turn negative within 48 hours; the "unfinished" tag becomes permanent; sales cease; the developer burns out trying to answer tickets instead of coding. This is a one-way door for reputation.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits the game is "rough," the developer is "solo" (implying limited bandwidth), and there is "no marketing." Historically, paid alphas require a strong community or existing brand to overcome quality deficits, neither of which exists here.

**Blockers:**
1. **Support Mismatch:** A solo developer explicitly cannot support a large audience, yet a public sale invites maximum traffic and support demands immediately.
2. **Value Proposition:** Buyers pay for completion or stability; the proposal offers neither, relying on "future features" which is a weak sell without an established trust loop.
3. **Discovery Void:** With no marketing budget and a rough product, organic discovery will fail, leaving only early adopters who are often the harshest critics of bugs.

**Risks:** Running out of funds before completion; alienating the niche building-game community permanently.

**Kill Criteria:** If a free demo cannot retain 20% of players for 1 hour within 30 days, do not sell.

The lack of a support plan and the certainty of negative first impressions constitute a fatal flaw in the current strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
