First call: GO. The company already possesses the core competency in silicon design for its mobile devices, suggesting the technical capability exists to extend this to laptops. The primary strategic upside is controlling the roadmap, margins, and integration depth, which aligns with long-term platform dominance.

Steelman: Leveraging proven mobile silicon architecture for laptops will unlock superior performance-per-watt and tighter hardware-software integration, creating a defensible moat that off-the-shelf supplier parts cannot match.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Maintains status quo but cedes long-term differentiation and margin control to a supplier.
2. Hybrid approach: Slowly introduce custom chips in lower-volume models first. This reduces risk but delays the full strategic benefit and splits engineering focus.
3. Full transition (Proposed): Highest immediate cost and risk, but fastest path to total architectural control.

Fit: Assuming the company's north star is vertical integration and premium user experience, this move is essential. Staying on supplier chips limits innovation to the supplier's pace.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if legacy software compatibility breaks the user experience or if the new chips underperform in sustained laptop workloads compared to the supplier's high-end offerings. This is a one-way door; once the supplier relationship is severed, returning is costly and reputationally damaging. The cost of delay is losing the next cycle of architectural innovation to competitors who integrate deeper.

Evidence: The proposal states the company already designs phone/tablet processors. This validates the core engineering capability. The risks (software compatibility, unproven performance) are execution challenges, not fundamental blockers, provided the transition includes robust emulation or translation layers (a known technique).

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited are manageable execution hurdles, not facts that prove the plan fails as proposed. The company has the capital and engineering depth to absorb the transition cost.
Risks: Software compatibility glitches, initial performance gaps, supply chain ramp-up issues. These become kill criteria: if beta testing shows >5% performance regression on key pro workflows or critical app breakage >1%, the launch must pause.

Kill criteria: Launch is contingent on passing internal benchmarks matching 90% of supplier performance on sustained loads and 100% compatibility with top 1000 enterprise apps by Q4 2021. Leading metric: Developer sentiment and app native adoption rate post-launch.

DECISION: GO
