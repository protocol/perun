First call: GO. The proposal targets an underserved demographic (young people) with a portable, affordable music solution using existing technology, despite dealer skepticism.

**Steelman:** A dedicated, portable playback device creates a new "personal music" category for youth, leveraging existing components to achieve a price point that unlocks volume, rendering recording functionality (unused for music consumption) an unnecessary cost.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Maintain status quo; competitors may eventually launch a similar device, capturing the youth market first.
2.  **Full-featured portable:** Add recording/speaker; higher cost, higher price, likely misses the youth price-sensitive segment.
3.  **Proposed (Playback only):** Lowest cost, highest portability, risky due to unproven demand but highest upside if the "personal" angle resonates.

**Fit:** Aligns with a vision of democratizing music access; assumes youth prioritize portability and price over social sharing or recording capabilities.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the "antisocial" perception sticks or if dealers refuse to stock it, starving the launch. However, the "antisocial" trait could be reframed as "personal freedom." The lack of market research is a risk, but the low unit cost (via existing parts) mitigates financial exposure if the first run stalls.

**Evidence:** No external data exists; the proposal relies on the logical deduction that existing parts can meet a specific price target. The risk of competitor copying is real but secondary to establishing the brand in this new niche first.

**Blockers:** NONE. The concerns raised (dealer doubt, lack of research, copycats) are risks, not factual blockers. The proposal explicitly addresses the cost structure via existing parts. The "no recording" feature is a strategic constraint to hit the price, not an oversight.

**Risks:** Market rejection due to perceived antisocial nature; dealer resistance; rapid commoditization. These become kill criteria: if initial sales to youth do not materialize within the first quarter, or if dealers categorically refuse shelf space, the project halts.

**Kill Criteria:** Stop if first-run sell-through is <60% in 90 days. Leading metric: Units sold per store per week among customers under 25.

The potential to create a new market segment outweighs the conservative fears of dealers accustomed to traditional hi-fi behaviors.

DECISION: GO
