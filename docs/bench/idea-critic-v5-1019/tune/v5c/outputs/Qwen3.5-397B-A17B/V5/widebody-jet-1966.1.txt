First call: NO-GO. The financial exposure exceeds all prior precedents with a single customer anchor, unproven propulsion, and an existential threat from emerging supersonic technology.

Steelman: Building a massively scalable, convertible freighter now secures long-term dominance if subsonic demand persists and supersonic fails to penetrate the mass market.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Retain current margins but risk losing the key customer to competitors or future obsolescence.
2. Incremental stretch: Lower cost and risk, but fails to meet the specific "2.5x size" efficiency demand.
3. Wait for engine maturity: Reduces technical risk but cedes first-mover advantage and strains customer relations.
The proposal beats "do nothing" only if the supersonic threat is overblown, which is currently unverified.

Fit: Assumes subsonic travel remains the volume driver. If supersonic becomes the standard for long-haul, this factory becomes a stranded asset.

Pre-mortem: The project fails because the new engine delivers poor reliability, grounding the fleet, or because supersonic tickets drop in price, making subsonic jumbo jets economically irrelevant. The massive fixed costs of the new factory bankrupt the firm during a demand dip. This is a one-way door; the factory cannot be easily repurposed if the market shifts.

Evidence: The proposal admits the engine is unproven and the financial outlay is unprecedented. Only one airline has ordered; relying on a single customer for such scale is a critical fragility. The "supersonic replacement" risk is stated as an expectation, not a dismissed possibility.

Blockers:
1. Unproven engine dependency: The core performance relies on a supplier's untested technology.
2. Single-customer anchor: The financial model collapses if this one airline cancels or reduces orders.
3. Supersonic uncertainty: No evidence proves subsonic will remain the volume leader against the expected supersonic shift.

Risks: Cost overruns, conversion complexity, market timing errors. These are secondary to the blockers.

The plan commits irreversible capital based on unverified technical and market premises. The supersonic threat alone warrants a "go-if" with proof of subsonic longevity, not a blind "go." As proposed, the downside is existential.

DECISION: NO-GO
