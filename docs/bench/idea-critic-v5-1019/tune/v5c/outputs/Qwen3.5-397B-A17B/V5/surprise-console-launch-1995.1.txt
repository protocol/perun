First call: NO-GO. Launching early with high price, scarce software, and alienated retailers contradicts the fundamental requirements for a successful console debut.

**Steelman:** An early May launch at $399 secures a four-month head start to establish brand presence before the competitor arrives, leveraging exclusivity to capture early adopters despite limited inventory.

**Attack:**
1.  **Price vs. Value:** At $100 above the rival, the console demands superior performance or content. The proposal admits "few games ready" and the machine is "hard to program for 3D." Without a strong software library demonstrating the 32-bit advantage, the price premium is unjustifiable, driving customers to the cheaper, better-supported rival.
2.  **Retailer Relations:** Shipping only to "a few retailers" guarantees retaliation from excluded major chains. In the mass-market console business, shelf space is critical. Alienating key distribution partners before the main launch ensures they will prioritize the competitor in September, effectively blocking market access when volume matters most.
3.  **Developer Ecosystem:** Surprising developers with an early date when they "did not plan" exacerbates the software shortage. A console without games fails regardless of hardware specs. This move fractures developer trust, delaying the very titles needed to justify the $399 price point.
4.  **Strategic Mismatch:** The "head start" is illusory. Releasing a high-priced, under-supported product creates a negative first impression that is difficult to reverse. It allows the competitor to observe the misstep, adjust their strategy, and launch in September with a superior price, broader retail support, and a fuller game lineup, framing the early entrant as a failed premium experiment.

**Blockers:**
*   **Price/Content Mismatch:** $399 price with "few games" and difficult development environment ensures low adoption.
*   **Channel Sabotage:** Excluding major retailers invites active obstruction of the subsequent full launch.
*   **Developer Alienation:** Unexpected timeline reduces software availability, the primary driver of console sales.

**Risks:** Brand damage from a botched launch; competitor capitalizing on the vacuum; permanent loss of retail shelf space.

The proposal sacrifices long-term market viability for a symbolic four-month window it cannot effectively utilize.

DECISION: NO-GO
