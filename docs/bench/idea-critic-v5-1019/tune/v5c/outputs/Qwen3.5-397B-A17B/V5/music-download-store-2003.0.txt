First call: GO. The company leverages its dominant hardware position to solve the legal and usability friction of piracy, creating a sustainable ecosystem where rivals see only a retail margin game.

**Steelman:** By integrating a legal, affordable, and seamless music store directly into the best-selling portable player, the company converts pirates into paying customers, securing label cooperation through volume and DRM control while competitors distract themselves with complex subscription models users aren't ready for.

**Attack & Analysis:**
*   **Free Sharing:** Price and convenience beat "free" when the alternative involves legal risk, viruses, and poor curation. 99 cents is an impulse buy; downloading is labor.
*   **Label Risk:** Labels are desperate for revenue and control. Offering a managed pipeline with DRM is their only lifeline, making withdrawal unlikely compared to total loss.
*   **Margin:** Low per-song margin is irrelevant; the store is a loss leader to drive high-margin hardware sales. The value is ecosystem lock-in, not retail profit.
*   **Platform Limitation:** Restricting the store to own computers initially strengthens hardware differentiation. It forces the ecosystem flywheel: buy the player, get the music.
*   **Subscription Rivals:** In 2003, bandwidth and mobile data make streaming/subscriptions clunky. Ownership (per-song) matches current user behavior better than rental models.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs only if labels collude to withhold content or if the DRM makes the user experience worse than piracy. However, the urgency of the piracy crisis aligns label incentives with this proposal.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited are market conditions that this specific strategy turns into advantages (e.g., using label fear to secure licenses, using low margins to undercut piracy friction).

**Risks:** Slow adoption if price perception fails; competitor catch-up once the model proves viable. These are execution risks, not structural blockers.

**Kill Criteria:** If major labels refuse licensing within 6 months, or if attachment rates (songs bought per device) remain below 10% after one year, pivot to a subscription model or renegotiate pricing.

**Decision:** The proposal correctly identifies that the bottleneck is not technology or price, but legal availability and ease of use. It monetizes the hardware install base immediately.

DECISION: GO
