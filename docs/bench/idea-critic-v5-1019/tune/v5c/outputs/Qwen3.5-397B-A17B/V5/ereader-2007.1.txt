First call: GO. The proposal solves the core friction of early e-reading (need for a PC) and aligns with the company's catalog strength, despite hardware inexperience.

**Steelman:** A dedicated, cellular-enabled device removes the primary adoption barrier (complexity) and creates a closed, publisher-friendly ecosystem that mitigates piracy fears through controlled access and simplified pricing.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Cedes the digital future to competitors; print sales will eventually decline regardless.
2.  **Software Only:** Requires users to own PCs/mobile devices (rare in 2007 for reading) and offers no piracy protection publishers demand.
3.  **Hardware Risk:** While the company lacks hardware experience, the "paper-like" screen and cellular connectivity are differentiating features that justify the investment. The risk of cannibalization is acceptable if the margin on ebooks and increased volume offset lower print sales.

**Fit:** Moves the north star from "selling print books" to "selling reading," leveraging the massive catalog as the content moat.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the device is too expensive, the screen is poor, or publishers refuse content. However, the "free cellular" and "low price" model directly addresses adoption and publisher concerns. The one-way door is the hardware investment, but the cost of delay is losing the entire digital market.

**Blockers:** None. The risks cited (inexperience, margin squeeze, cannibalization) are execution challenges, not factual contradictions. The proposal's own logic (closed system, low price) addresses the piracy and adoption hurdles that killed earlier attempts.

**Risks:** Hardware execution failure; publisher pushback on pricing. These become kill criteria: halt if prototype costs exceed $200 or if <50% of top publishers sign on within 6 months.

**Decision:** The plan beats "do nothing" and software-only alternatives by solving the specific 2007 constraints of connectivity and piracy. The hardware risk is the price of entry.

DECISION: GO
