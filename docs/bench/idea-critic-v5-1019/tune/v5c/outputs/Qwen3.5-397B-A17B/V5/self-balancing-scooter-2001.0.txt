First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to massive fixed costs (40k unit factory) and a premium price point before resolving fundamental regulatory and market-fit uncertainties.

**Steelman:** A breakthrough personal transporter with strong backing can create a new urban mobility category if launched decisively to capture first-mover advantage.

**Attack:**
1.  **Regulatory Blocker:** The legal status is "unclear." Launching a vehicle without defined operating zones invites immediate bans, seizures, or liability suits. A $5,000 consumer product cannot survive a regulatory vacuum.
2.  **Demand Mismatch:** At $5,000, the addressable market shrinks drastically, yet the factory is sized for 40,000 units. If sales miss by 50% due to price or "conspicuous" stigma, the company faces catastrophic inventory bloat and cash burn.
3.  **Utility Gap:** The inability to carry loads and unproven use cases beyond institutions suggest the product is a niche novelty, not a mass-market necessity justifying industrial-scale production.
4.  **Better Way:** A staged launch targeting institutional buyers (security, tours) with limited manual assembly validates demand and regulatory pathways before committing to a dedicated factory.

**Pre-mortem:** One year post-launch, cities ban the device from sidewalks, sales are 2,000 units/year, and the company collapses under the weight of an idle factory and unsold inventory.

**Blockers:**
*   **Regulatory Void:** No cleared path for legal operation exists; launching now risks immediate market exclusion.
*   **Capacity vs. Demand:** Fixed infrastructure (40k factory) vastly exceeds proven demand for an unproven, high-cost novelty.

**Risks:** Social stigma, limited utility, high price elasticity. These become kill criteria if a pilot fails, but the blockers prevent even a safe pilot at this scale.

**Decision Logic:** The proposal conflates "having a working prototype" with "having a viable business." Building a factory before solving the legal and product-market fit questions is a classic "build it and they will come" failure mode. The risks are not merely executional; they are existential to the business model. The plan must be revised to a low-capital pilot before any approval.

DECISION: NO-GO
