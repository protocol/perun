First call: GO. The market gap for an affordable, lightweight roadster exists precisely because mass-makers abandoned the niche, creating a low-competition entry point for a mid-size maker.

**Steelman:** A focused, low-overhead vehicle targeting unmet demand for simple, rear-drive convertibles can succeed by avoiding the complexity and cost that killed previous competitors, rather than competing on heritage or volume.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Cede the emerging "affordable enthusiast" segment to potential future entrants; safe but yields zero growth.
2.  **Heavy heritage model:** High cost, high risk, requires brand reinvention; likely fails due to price.
3.  **Proposed lightweight approach:** Lowest capital expenditure, fastest time-to-market, directly addresses the "costly safety rules" risk by minimizing weight to offset compliance costs. It beats "do nothing" by capturing a distinct, underserved demographic.

**Fit:** Assumes the company's north star includes diversifying into high-margin niche segments to stabilize against mass-market volatility. This fits a strategy of leveraging existing front-engine/rear-drive components to minimize new tooling.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the car is priced too high due to safety compliance, making it uncompetitive against used classics. However, the "lightweight" mandate directly mitigates this. It is a two-way door; production can be capped or stopped after a limited run if early sales stall, minimizing long-term liability.

**Evidence:** The proposal states mass-makers view the segment as "dead," which ironically lowers barriers to entry (less competition). The risks (shrinking segment, safety costs) are acknowledged but addressed by the specific "affordable, lightweight" constraint. No internal data contradicts the existence of latent demand.

**Blockers:** NONE. The stated risks are market realities, not factual contradictions. The lack of heritage is irrelevant if the product delivers on driving dynamics at the right price.
**Risks:** Sales fading after early adopters; mitigated by limiting initial production volume (a built-in kill criterion).

**Decision:** The plan turns market abandonment into an opportunity. The specific constraints (lightweight, affordable) directly neutralize the primary risks (cost, complexity).

DECISION: GO
