First call: NO-GO. The proposal combines a premium price with a severely restricted compatible base (small-share computers only) in a market where cheaper alternatives have already failed to gain traction.

Steelman: A high-fidelity, high-capacity device could create a new premium category if the ecosystem lock-in drives computer sales. However, the facts provided contradict this path. The addressable market is explicitly tiny, and the music industry has not licensed legal downloads, meaning the primary use case (loading 5 GB of music) relies on piracy or tedious CD ripping, neither of which supports a $400 price point for non-owners of the computer.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids capital loss in a failing segment.
2. License software to Windows: Expands the base but requires industry partnerships not yet present.
3. Cheaper, smaller flash player: Lower risk, but margins are thin and competition is high.

Fit: This does not move a stated north star because the ecosystem is too small to sustain the hardware economics. The "walled garden" is a liability, not an asset, when the garden contains few users and no legal content source.

Pre-mortem: The device launches, sells poorly to the tiny installed base, and becomes a costly inventory write-off because the broader market cannot use it without buying a new computer just for music. The supply chain misses the timeline due to component sourcing risks, delaying launch past the holiday season.

Evidence: The proposal admits the computer maker has a "small market share" and "no consumer-electronics experience." Cheaper players have "not sold well," indicating weak demand or poor product-market fit in the current environment. Pricing at $400 (a premium) above failing competitors without a differentiated content ecosystem is mathematically unsound.

Blockers:
1. The compatible user base is too small to justify the development and sourcing costs for a niche hardware project.
2. No legal content pipeline exists to justify the 5 GB capacity advantage over cheaper units.
3. Lack of consumer electronics experience increases the probability of missing the "under a year" timeline, leading to missed market windows.

Risks: Component sourcing delays; continued industry resistance to digital licensing.

The plan fails as proposed because the economic engine (selling hardware to a tiny base at a premium) does not work without first solving the content or compatibility constraint.

DECISION: NO-GO
