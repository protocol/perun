First call: GO. The cost is trivial relative to the strategic optionality of owning the mobile gateway before the market hardens.

**Steelman:** Acquiring this team secures a defensive and offensive position to ensure search accessibility on the inevitable mobile web, using open source to bypass carrier/handset gatekeepers who otherwise would favor incumbents.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Cedes the mobile interface to competitors (Microsoft, Nokia, Palm). If mobile web grows without our presence, ad revenue stagnates.
*   **Build Internally:** Too slow; misses the window. Six people cannot be hired fast enough to replicate secretive, specialized progress.
*   **Partner/License:** Carriers and handset makers have no incentive to adopt a neutral platform from a search company without equity control. They will block it.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
The strategy assumes mobile search volume will explode. If the bet fails, it is because mobile data remains niche or carriers successfully wall off the internet. However, the cost of being wrong ($50M) is less than 1% of annual cash reserves, while the cost of missing the shift is existential. The "open source" angle is the only viable wedge against closed ecosystems; trying to sell a proprietary OS would be immediate suicide.

**Blockers vs. Risks:**
*   **Blockers:** NONE. No fact presented proves the plan fails as proposed. The lack of a direct revenue model is intentional (infrastructure play), not a flaw.
*   **Risks:** Adoption friction, long payback, carrier hostility. These are inherent to platform shifts, not specific defects in this acquisition. The proposal explicitly acknowledges them.

**Kill Criteria:**
Since this is an option purchase, the kill criterion is not immediate revenue. It is: If, within 3 years, the OS fails to secure a top-3 handset partner or achieve >5% market share in a key region, pivot the tech to embedded licensing or shut down the project.

The price is low enough to treat as R&D insurance. The alternative—waiting for clarity—guarantees losing the board.

DECISION: GO
