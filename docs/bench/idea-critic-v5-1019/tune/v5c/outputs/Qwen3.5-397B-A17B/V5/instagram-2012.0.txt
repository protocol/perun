First call: NO-GO. The price implies a valuation disconnected from any verified revenue or proven business model, violating basic capital discipline.

Steelman: Acquiring the sole mobile-native threat neutralizes a strategic risk and captures tens of millions of engaged users instantly, buying time to fix internal mobile weaknesses.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Risk rapid user migration to the threat; internal product likely too weak to catch up before the IPO locks in the competitor's status.
2. Build/Buy smaller: No other target has comparable scale or growth; organic build is too slow given the "dominant" threat status.
3. Partnership: Insufficient to stop the competitive bleed or secure exclusive mobile dominance.

Fit: Assumes the North Star is maintaining social graph dominance against mobile disruption. If the threat is real, this fits; if the threat is overblown, it fails.

Pre-mortem: Integration destroys the unique culture that drove growth, turning the asset into a feature graveyard. Regulatory bodies block the deal as anti-competitive. The $1B spend yields zero revenue for years, dragging stock price post-IPO.

Evidence Check: The proposal admits "no revenue" and "no business model." The $1B price is purely speculative based on "tens of millions of users" and "growth." There is no financial data to support a return on investment. The buyer's own "weak mobile product" confirms desperation, not strategic strength.

Blockers:
1. **Valuation without fundamentals:** Paying $1B for zero revenue and no monetization path is a factual failure of financial logic. The cost per user is unjustifiable without a clear path to monetization.
2. **Cultural destruction risk:** The proposal explicitly flags the risk of destroying the app's culture, which is its primary asset. Without a guaranteed isolation plan, the acquisition value drops to zero.

Risks: Regulatory blocking, platform dependency (one phone dominant), monetization failure.

Kill Criteria: If the acquired team's retention drops below 90% in 6 months, or if no revenue stream is pilot-tested within 12 months, the integration is a failure.

The absence of a business model and the extreme premium relative to any financial metric constitute a blocker. Desperation to fix a weak internal product does not justify a blind billion-dollar bet.

DECISION: NO-GO
