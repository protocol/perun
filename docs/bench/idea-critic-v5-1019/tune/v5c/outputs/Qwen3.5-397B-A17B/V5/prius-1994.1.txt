First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a fixed public schedule for unproven core technologies without a demand signal or regulatory mandate, creating asymmetric downside.

**Steelman:** A bold, fixed-date commitment forces the organization to solve novel battery and transmission challenges rapidly, securing first-mover status before regulators or competitors act.

**Attack:**
1.  **Blocker (Technical & Financial):** The proposal admits battery durability is "unproven" and the transmission is "novel." Committing to mass production by 1997 implies tooling and supply chain locks before validating these components. If the battery fails cycle testing at 18 months (a likely scenario for new chemistry), the public schedule forces a choice between shipping a defective product (massive warranty exposure) or missing the date (reputational collapse).
2.  **Blocker (Market):** With cheap fuel and no mandates, there is no near-term demand signal. Hybrids carry a cost premium; without regulatory push or fuel pain, volume targets will miss, turning a niche R&D project into a financial drain.
3.  **Better Way:** The "do nothing" alternative (continue R&D without public commitment) or a "technology demonstrator" approach allows solving durability issues privately. A staged pilot (limited fleet, no public hard date) reduces warranty risk while gathering real-world data. The current plan beats neither alternative on risk-adjusted return.
4.  **Fit:** This contradicts basic product development hygiene: you do not fix the launch date for a volume product before the core mechanism is proven reliable.

**Pre-mortem:** It is 1998. The car launched late, the NiMH packs degraded prematurely in hot climates, and the warranty reserve wiped out the division's annual profit. The public "bold schedule" made the delay a headline rather than a standard engineering slip.

**Decision Logic:**
*   **Blockers:** Unproven battery durability + fixed mass-production date = unacceptable warranty risk. No demand signal + cost premium = high probability of commercial failure.
*   **Risks:** Execution difficulty, competitor response (irrelevant if demand is zero).

The absence of a pilot, a durability validation gate, or a demand trigger makes this a gamble, not a plan. A "go-if" is impossible because the proposal demands a full commit now.

DECISION: NO-GO
