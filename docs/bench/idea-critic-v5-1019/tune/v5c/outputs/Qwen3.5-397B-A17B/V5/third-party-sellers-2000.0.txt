First call: GO. The proposal trades short-term margin erosion for long-term liquidity and selection, a standard pivot for scaling marketplaces facing profitability walls.

**Steelman:** Aggregating third-party supply on existing pages maximizes conversion by offering immediate price/condition variety, turning the site into a destination rather than a mere retailer, while shifting inventory risk to sellers.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Retain control but cap growth at inventory capital limits; likely path to continued unprofitability.
2.  **Separate marketplace:** Dilutes traffic and confuses users; higher engineering cost.
3.  **Proposed integration:** Immediate selection boost, monetizes traffic via fees, but risks brand dilution and channel conflict.
The proposal beats "do nothing" because the current model cannot scale without massive capital expenditure it cannot afford.

**Fit:** Aligns with the "growing range of goods" goal without requiring capital for inventory. Assumes the north star is total gross merchandise value (GMV) and ecosystem dominance over immediate per-unit margin.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if low-quality sellers drive away core customers faster than new buyers arrive, or if major suppliers boycott the platform. This is a two-way door; sellers can be de-platformed if metrics dip. Cost of delay is high: competitors capturing the long-tail inventory first creates a defensible moat we cannot breach later.

**Evidence:** The premise rests on the fact that the retailer is "not yet profitable" and needs asset-light growth. No external data contradicts the feasibility of fee-based models in 2000.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (undercutting, quality, cannibalization) are execution challenges, not factual impossibilities. Cannibalization is acceptable if the fee revenue exceeds the lost margin on new items. Supplier objection is a risk, not a blocker, manageable via segmented policies.

**Risks:** Brand trust erosion, supplier retaliation, internal channel conflict.
**Kill Criteria:** If customer return rate increases by >5% or seller defect rate exceeds 2% within 6 months, pause new seller onboarding. Leading metric: % of total sales from third-party sellers.

DECISION: GO
