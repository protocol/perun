First call: NO-GO. The proposal combines a premium price with a fragmented ecosystem and a gimmicky core feature in a saturated two-horse race, ignoring the primary driver of smartphone adoption: app availability.

**Steelman:** A differentiated device leveraging unique 3D and shopping features can carve a niche among loyal customers despite a smaller app store, provided the hardware experience is compelling enough to offset software limitations.

**Attack:**
1.  **App Gap vs. Price:** Users pay flagship prices for flagship utility. A forked OS lacking mainstream apps is a non-starter for a primary device, regardless of 3D capabilities. The "shopping" feature is a single-use case, not an OS-level replacement for missing social, banking, or communication apps.
2.  **Carrier Exclusivity:** Limiting sales to one carrier while charging unsubsidized flagship prices severely caps the addressable market, contradicting the need for massive scale to sustain a third ecosystem.
3.  **Differentiation Mismatch:** 3D displays driven by head tracking are a novelty, not a sustained value prop. History shows such features fail to drive upgrades when core utility (apps) is compromised.
4.  **Market Reality:** In a duopoly, the cost of switching is high. Without a complete app ecosystem, the device becomes a secondary toy, unable to justify a $600+ price point.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do nothing:* Preserve brand equity and focus on successful e-reader/tablet lines.
*   *Low-cost entry:* Launch a budget phone with the shopping feature as a differentiator, acknowledging the app gap, rather than competing at the high end.
*   *Software first:* Release the shopping/3D features as an app on existing dominant platforms to capture value without hardware risk.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fatal Flaw:** The proposal explicitly admits to lacking mainstream apps while targeting a premium price point. This contradicts the fundamental value proposition of a smartphone in 2014.
*   **Evidence:** Market dominance of two platforms implies network effects that a forked OS cannot easily break, especially with a reduced feature set (no apps) and high price.

**Risks:** Massive inventory write-downs, brand dilution, carrier relationship strain.

**Kill Criteria:** If pre-orders do not exceed 100k units in the first month (indicating niche appeal only), halt production immediately.

DECISION: NO-GO
