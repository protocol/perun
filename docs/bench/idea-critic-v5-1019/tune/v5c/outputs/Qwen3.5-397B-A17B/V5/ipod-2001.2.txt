First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on a tiny installed base, an unproven legal music supply, and a premium price in a market where cheaper devices have already failed to gain traction.

Steelman: A tightly integrated, high-capacity device could create a superior user experience that justifies a premium, eventually forcing the music industry to license content and expanding the market beyond the current small computer user base.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Avoid capital risk but cede the emerging digital music category to competitors.
2. License software to Windows: Immediately expands the addressable market but dilutes the hardware/software integration strategy and requires partnerships not yet in place.
3. Wait for legal downloads: Reduces product risk but misses the first-mover advantage and delays revenue.
The proposal must beat "do nothing" on current facts; it currently relies on future market creation.

Fit: Assumes the company's north star is disrupting adjacent markets via premium integration. However, the stated lack of consumer electronics experience and the small computer share directly contradict the requirement for mass-market adoption to sustain a $400 price point.

Pre-mortem: The project fails because the music industry refuses to license catalogs without a proven distribution model, leaving users with a device that can only rip CDs they already own. The high price alienates the small computer user base, and component sourcing delays push launch past the holiday window. This is a one-way door with high sunk costs; delay costs include losing the window to define the category standard.

Evidence: The proposal admits the addressable base is tiny, competitors are cheaper, and legal downloads are non-existent. These are not risks; they are current facts that block the core value proposition.

Blockers:
1. The addressable market (owners of specific computers) is too small to support the economics of a custom 5GB drive supply chain at a $400 price point without immediate Windows compatibility or legal music sales, neither of which are secured.
2. The value proposition collapses without legal digital music, which the proposal admits does not exist at scale.

Risks: Execution difficulty, brand dilution, component shortages.

The facts show the target users cannot justify the cost, and the necessary ecosystem (legal music) is absent. This is a blocker, not a risk.

DECISION: NO-GO
