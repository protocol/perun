First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits $36 billion to acquire a network explicitly described as incompatible and limited, requiring the buyer to run legacy equipment while bearing massive goodwill risk.

Steelman: The acquisition is justified solely to capture the target's high-value business customers who rely on its unique push-to-talk service, assuming these revenues outweigh the integration nightmare.

Options:
1. **Proposed Merge:** Highest cost ($36B), highest risk (incompatible tech, legacy burden), low reversibility.
2. **Do Nothing:** Zero integration cost, no goodwill risk, but forfeits the specific business segment.
3. **Roaming/Partnership:** Contractually access the target's push-to-talk network for business customers without buying the incompatible infrastructure or assuming the debt.

Fit: A sound strategy moves the north star (profitable growth); forcing the integration of limited, incompatible technology actively drags the network backward, contradicting the goal of modernization.

Pre-mortem: The integration fails because the technologies cannot be unified without replacing the entire target network, rendering the "limited" assets worthless while the $36 billion price tag creates a debt burden that crushes cash flow. The goodwill becomes an immediate write-down. This is a one-way door; once the networks are tangled, unwinding is impossible.

Evidence: The proposal itself states the technologies are "different, incompatible" and the target network is "limited for data." It admits the buyer must "run one on legacy equipment." These are not future risks; they are stated facts that contradict the claim of a synergistic merger.

Blockers:
1. **Incompatible Core:** The proposal admits the networks cannot natively integrate, forcing the operation of a separate, limited legacy system indefinitely.
2. **Value Mismatch:** Paying $36 billion for "limited" technology and "goodwill" rather than tangible, compatible assets violates basic capital allocation principles.
3. **Superior Alternative:** A commercial partnership achieves the customer access goal without the $36 billion outlay or the technical debt.

Risks: Integration cost overruns, customer churn during migration, goodwill impairment.

The stated facts confirm the core asset is technically obsolete and incompatible. Buying it at a premium to run it as a legacy silo is not a merger; it is an expensive bailout of incompatible infrastructure.

DECISION: NO-GO
