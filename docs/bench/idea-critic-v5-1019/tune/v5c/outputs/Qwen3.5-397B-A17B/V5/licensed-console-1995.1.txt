First call: NO-GO. The proposal trades a high-leverage brand asset for negligible, uncapped downside risk in a winner-take-all market.

**Steelman:** Licensing converts fixed R&D costs into variable royalty revenue, allowing rapid market entry without capital expenditure or operational distraction.

**Attack:**
1.  **Economic Misalignment:** "Small royalty" on a "low-cost" device yields trivial revenue. If the licensee fails to market aggressively (a stated risk), volume stays low, compounding the revenue failure. The licensor bears the brand risk of a cheap, potentially low-quality product while capturing almost none of the upside.
2.  **Strategic Dilution:** Entering a market dominated by established players with a "low-cost" generic console via a third party guarantees a race to the bottom. If the product fails, it tarnishes the licensor's core brand reputation for quality without building any direct consumer relationship or platform loyalty.
3.  **Incentive Fracture:** The licensee controls marketing and manufacturing. Their incentive is to maximize their own margin, potentially by cutting corners that hurt the licensor's brand, or by under-investing in promotion if early sales are soft. The licensor has no lever to fix this post-signing.
4.  **Opportunity Cost:** This locks the platform into a "toy" perception, making future premium consumer entries harder. It forecloses the option of building a proprietary ecosystem where the hardware maker captures full value.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Preserves brand equity and avoids distraction.
*   *Proprietary Pilot:* Build a limited-run, high-quality unit internally to test waters and retain control, even if slower.

**Pre-mortem:** The console launches, sells poorly due to weak marketing and stiff competition. The licensee cuts losses and discontinues the line. The computer maker is left with a "failed toy" association and zero recurring revenue, having wasted strategic focus.

**Blockers:**
*   **Value Capture:** The economic model (small royalty × low cost × uncertain volume) cannot justify the brand risk.
*   **Control:** The structure cedes critical success factors (marketing, quality) to a party with misaligned incentives.

**Risks:** Market dominance by incumbents; brand dilution. These are severe but secondary to the fundamental structural flaw.

**Kill Criteria:** If the projected royalty per unit does not cover the fully loaded cost of brand risk exposure plus a significant premium, the deal destroys value.

DECISION: NO-GO
