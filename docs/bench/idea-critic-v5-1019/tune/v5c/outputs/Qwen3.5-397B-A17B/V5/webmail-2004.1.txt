First call: GO. The proposal leverages a structural cost advantage (falling storage prices) to solve a verified user pain point (deleting mail) while creating a unique data asset for ad targeting, differentiating it from entrenched incumbents who treat storage as a liability.

**Steelman:** By offering 1,000x the standard storage, the service makes "delete" obsolete, locking users into an ecosystem where deep search becomes essential. This engagement justifies content-matched ads, turning high storage costs into a high-margin data moat that competitors cannot match without rewriting their architecture.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
*   *Cost Risk:* Critics cite gigabyte costs as prohibitive. However, hardware trends favor the entrant; competitors are optimized for scarcity. If storage costs drop faster than adoption, the unit economics improve over time, unlike fixed-cost models.
*   *Privacy Objection:* Scanning mail for ads is invasive. Counter: Users already trade privacy for free services; the value of never deleting mail outweighs abstract privacy concerns for the mass market. Explicit opt-in and clear terms mitigate backlash.
*   *Mature Market:* Incumbents are vulnerable because their business models rely on storage scarcity. They cannot pivot to "unlimited" without cannibalizing existing tiers or incurring massive retrofit costs.
*   *Alternative (Do Nothing/Small Storage):* Maintaining the status quo leaves the "delete" friction unresolved, offering no wedge to displace incumbents. A smaller storage increase (e.g., 100MB) fails to change user behavior fundamentally.

**Pre-mortem:** The venture fails if ad relevance is too low to cover server costs before scale is reached, or if privacy backlash triggers regulatory bans. However, the invitation-only launch controls growth to match infrastructure capacity, mitigating the burn rate risk.

**Blockers vs. Risks:**
*   *Blockers:* NONE. The math holds if storage costs continue their historical decline and ad click-through rates meet industry averages. No factual contradiction exists in the proposal.
*   *Risks:* Privacy backlash, slower-than-expected hardware cost declines, low ad relevance. These are managed via the invitation-only rollout (cap) and kill criteria (ad revenue per user vs. storage cost per user).

The proposal correctly identifies that a 1000x improvement in a core utility (storage) disrupts mature markets regardless of incumbency. The funding model aligns incentives: more usage equals more data equals better ads.

DECISION: GO
