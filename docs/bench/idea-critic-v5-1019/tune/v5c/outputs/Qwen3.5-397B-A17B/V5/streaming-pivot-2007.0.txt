First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits a profitable monopoly to a low-quality, high-cost adjunct with a small catalogue during a period of limited broadband adoption, inviting immediate cannibalization without a clear path to offset licensing expenses.

**Steelman:** The company must seed streaming now to retain relevance against incoming tech giants, using the existing subscriber base as a zero-marginal-cost testing ground despite current quality deficits.

**Attack:**
1.  **Unit Economics Failure:** Licensing "high prices" for a "small, older" catalogue creates negative marginal value. Unlike DVDs, streaming has no physical scarcity to manage demand, yet the content offered is inferior. Paying premium rights fees for library titles that subscribers already access via DVD (or free TV) destroys margin.
2.  **Cannibalization without Migration:** Offering this at "no extra charge" incentivizes users to switch from high-margin physical rentals (postage + late fees + inventory leverage) to a high-cost digital delivery with no revenue uplift. This accelerates revenue decay before the digital model can scale.
3.  **Strategic Misalignment:** Competing with "big technology and retail companies" on their turf (streaming) with inferior tech ("poor quality") and a weak catalogue is a losing battle. The company's moat is logistics and deep catalog ownership, not bandwidth delivery. Entering now locks them into a cost structure they cannot win against capital-rich entrants.
4.  **Market Readiness:** With "broadband not universal," the addressable market for this specific low-quality offering is fragmented. The cost to serve the early adopters exceeds the lifetime value extracted from a degraded experience.

**Blocker:** The proposal explicitly states the streaming option is "no extra charge" while incurring "high prices" for rights and technology. This guarantees immediate margin compression on every active user who switches, with no compensatory revenue mechanism. The math fails on day one.

**Risks:** The "strategic risk" of missing the trend is real, but the proposed execution guarantees financial bleeding before product-market fit is achieved. A "go-if" requiring a separate paid tier or a strictly limited pilot would be viable; the current "free addition" is fatal.

DECISION: NO-GO
