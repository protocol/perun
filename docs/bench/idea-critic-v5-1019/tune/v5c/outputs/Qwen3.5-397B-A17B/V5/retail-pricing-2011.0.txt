First call: NO-GO. The plan bets the company on unproven assumptions about a customer base with entrenched behaviors, without a safety net.

**Steelman:** Eliminating coupons and rebuilding stores will modernize the brand, attract new shoppers, and restore margins by ending the discount spiral, leveraging the CEO's prior electronics success.

**Attack:**
1.  **Customer Mismatch:** The proposal ignores that "most sales" come from discounted items. The core demographic is explicitly "coupon-driven." Removing coupons removes their primary purchase trigger. Unlike electronics (high involvement, feature-driven), department store goods for this segment are price-sensitive.
2.  **Category Error:** The CEO's success was in branded electronics, a category where consumers expect fixed MSRPs. Department stores compete on perceived value via promotions. Applying an electronics model to a discount-trained apparel/home goods base is a category failure.
3.  **Execution Risk:** Rolling out to the "entire chain at once" with "no pilot" creates a single point of failure. If the hypothesis is wrong (likely, given the customer data), the chain loses its entire revenue engine immediately with no off-ramp.
4.  **Door Type:** This is a one-way door. Once coupons stop and stores are rebuilt, regaining trust and reversing capital expenditure is slow and costly.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Continue declining sales but preserve cash flow from the existing loyal base.
*   **Pilot:** Test the new pricing in 5-10 stores for 6 months to measure churn vs. new acquisition before full rollout.
*   **Hybrid:** Reduce coupon frequency gradually while introducing everyday low price on key items, monitoring elasticity.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** Most sales historically come from discounted items.
*   **Fact:** Customers are trained to wait for coupons.
*   **Contradiction:** The plan removes the specific mechanism (discounts) that currently drives the majority of revenue, without evidence the new model works for *this* specific demographic.

**Risks:** Total revenue collapse, alienation of the only remaining loyal segment, inability to attract new customers fast enough to offset losses.

**Kill Criteria:** Since the plan lacks them, the absence of a pilot for a one-way door decision affecting 100% of revenue is itself a blocker. The cost of delay (waiting for a pilot) is negligible compared to the cost of immediate chain-wide failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
