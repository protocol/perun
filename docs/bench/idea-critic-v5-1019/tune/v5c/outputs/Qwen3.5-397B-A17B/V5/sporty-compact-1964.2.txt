First call: GO. The demographic shift creates a clear, underserved segment seeking style over utility, and the proposed architecture leverages existing assets to minimize capital risk while maximizing margin potential through options.

**Steelman:** Launch a visually distinct, customizable sporty coupe/convertible using proven economy underpinnings to capture the emerging youth market at a low entry price, funded by aggressive marketing to define the category.

**Attack & Options:**
The primary objection is that sharing parts with a "dull" economy car will taint the sporty image. However, styling (long hood, short deck) and marketing can psychologically decouple the product from its donor. The alternative—building a new chassis—is prohibitively expensive and slow, missing the demographic window. "Doing nothing" cedes this growing segment to competitors who may eventually solve the styling puzzle. The risk of unproven demand is mitigated by the low base price (low barrier to trial) and the high-margin options list (profitability even at moderate volume).

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This fits a growth strategy targeting a specific demographic inversion (youth over family). If it fails, it will likely be because the marketing fails to create the "cool" halo, not because the mechanicals are shared. The cost of delay is losing the first-mover advantage with the baby boom cohort.

**Blockers vs. Risks:**
*Blockers:* NONE. The proposal's own facts (low base price, existing parts) neutralize the cited risks. Shared parts reduce cost, enabling the low price that drives volume; they do not force a "cheap" look if design is disciplined. Unproven demand is a risk, not a blocker, addressed by the low entry price.
*Risks:* Marketing overspend before sales validation; brand dilution if styling fails to distinguish. These become kill criteria: halt ad spend if early sales mix skews heavily to base model (no option uptake); pivot styling if initial focus groups reject the "sporty" claim.

**Decision:**
The plan turns a potential weakness (economy parts) into a strength (price/margin) and targets a verified demographic wave. The risks are manageable via the proposed options strategy and marketing focus.

DECISION: GO
