First call: GO. The shift from pure utility to affordable fashion leverages low-cost manufacturing to access a mass market, a logical expansion for a movement maker facing commodity pressure.

**Steelman:** Launching twelve distinct plastic models as fashion accessories allows the company to capture volume and brand relevance among younger demographics without cannibalizing high-end lines, treating the watch as an interchangeable accessory rather than a lifetime investment.

**Attack & Options:**
The primary objection is reputational harm. However, segmenting these as "fashion items" explicitly decouples them from the core "quality timekeeper" brand promise, mitigating dilution. Doing nothing preserves reputation but cedes the emerging low-cost, high-volume segment to competitors. A cheaper alternative (licensing the movement) yields lower margins and less brand control. The proposed path beats inaction by owning the narrative.

**Pre-mortem & Door:**
If this fails, it will be because the designs feel cheap rather than trendy, or inventory mismatches occur due to rapid taste shifts. This is a two-way door; unsuccessful models can be discontinued quickly with minimal sunk cost compared to metal movements. The cost of delay is losing the "first mover" advantage in the plastic fashion niche to established brands who may soon cut prices.

**Evidence & Math:**
The proposal cites a 1M unit target. Without current capacity data, we assume the "inexpensive" nature implies high-margin scalability. The risk of aggressive targets is managed by the low unit cost; overproduction is less financially catastrophic than under-production in a fashion launch.

**Blockers vs. Risks:**
*Blockers:* NONE. No fact provided proves the target users reject plastic or that the company lacks the design capability.
*Risks:* Reputation damage (mitigated by positioning), fashion volatility (mitigated by 12-model diversification), price wars (mitigated by low cost basis).

**Kill Criteria:**
If first-quarter sell-through is below 15% of allocated inventory per model, halt production on underperforming SKUs and pivot marketing. Leading metric: Weekly sell-through rate by model.

The potential upside of defining a new category outweighs the manageable risk of temporary brand confusion, provided the marketing clearly distinguishes this line.

DECISION: GO
