First call: NO-GO. The plan trades high-certainty trust capital for low-certainty social engagement, violating the core utility of an inbox.

**Steelman:** Leveraging the existing user base via automatic enrollment creates immediate network density, overcoming the cold-start problem faster than any opt-in alternative, while default visibility encourages organic discovery.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Incentives:** An inbox is a private utility, not a public square. Users expect confidentiality. Making connections visible by default fundamentally alters the product's value proposition from "secure communication" to "social performance." This misalignment risks mass churn of power users who rely on the tool for sensitive work.
2.  **Regulatory & Reversibility:** Automatic enrollment without explicit consent invites immediate regulatory scrutiny (antitrust and privacy). The proposal admits the launch is "hard to reverse." Once private data is exposed or social graphs are publicized, you cannot "un-ring the bell." The damage to brand trust is irreversible even if the feature is deleted.
3.  **Alternatives:** The "do nothing" option preserves the core asset (trust). A strictly opt-in pilot with invisible-by-default settings tests the social hypothesis without betting the franchise. The proposed "automatic" approach offers no unique upside that justifies the existential risk; it merely speeds up a potential failure.
4.  **Evidence Gap:** The proposal assumes users *want* social features in their email. There is no evidence cited that inbox users seek social discovery there. History shows that forcing social layers onto utility products often degrades the core experience without gaining traction.

**Blockers:**
*   **Irreversible Trust Loss:** The plan explicitly accepts a "hard to reverse" exposure of user connections. This is a factual blocker; once privacy is breached, the brand utility is permanently degraded.
*   **Regulatory Trigger:** Automatic enrollment with visible data is a known trigger for privacy investigations, creating a legal liability before product-market fit is proven.

**Risks:** Low adoption (wasted engineering), user backlash. These are secondary to the blockers.

**Kill Criteria:** If any user data is exposed without explicit, granular consent, the project must stop immediately. The leading metric must be "opt-in rate," not "total enrolled."

The cost of delay is negligible compared to the cost of a trust catastrophe. The plan fails the "do no harm" baseline required for a utility product.

DECISION: NO-GO
