First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to solve three hard problems simultaneously (new country, unproven supply chain, new inventory system) at maximum speed, creating a single point of failure for the entire expansion.

Steelman: Rapidly securing 130–150 sites creates an immediate scale advantage against the entrenched competitor, leveraging the retailer's domestic loyalty before prices normalize.

Options:
1. **Proposed:** High cost, extreme risk, low reversibility (long-term leases).
2. **Do nothing:** Zero cost, zero risk, cedes market to incumbent.
3. **Pilot (Better Way):** Open 10–15 stores using existing manual logistics or a third-party partner to validate the inventory system and cross-border regulation before signing more leases. This reaches the same learning upside with 90% less capital exposure.

Fit: Expansion aligns with growth vision, but the "learn by doing" approach on a critical new supply chain contradicts operational stability.

Pre-mortem: The new inventory system fails to handle bilingual labeling or cross-border tax nuances during the holiday rush. With 130 stores opening simultaneously, the glitch causes stockouts across the entire network. The company is locked into expensive leases with empty shelves, destroying brand reputation in the new market before it gains traction. The cost of delay (waiting 6 months for a pilot) is negligible compared to the cost of a systemic collapse.

Evidence: The proposal admits the supply chain and inventory systems are "not yet proven." Deploying unproven core infrastructure to 150 locations simultaneously is a factual contradiction of operational safety.

Blockers:
1. **Unproven Core Systems:** The plan relies on a new inventory system and supply chain that have no track record. Scaling unproven technology to 150 sites immediately guarantees systemic failure modes that cannot be isolated or fixed quickly.
2. **Irreversible Commitment:** Signing 130–150 leases before validating the operating model removes the ability to pivot or stop without massive financial loss.

Risks: Price perception, competitor retaliation, regulatory hurdles. These are manageable only if the core operations work, which is not guaranteed.

Kill Criteria: If a pilot of 10 stores cannot achieve 95% inventory accuracy within 90 days using the new system, the expansion halts.

DECISION: NO-GO
