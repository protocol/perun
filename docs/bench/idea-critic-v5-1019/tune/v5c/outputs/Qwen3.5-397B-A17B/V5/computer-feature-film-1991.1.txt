First call: GO. The offer solves the company's critical constraints: capital for rendering, distribution access, and validation for a feature-length leap, despite the risks.

**Steelman:** Partnering allows the company to leverage its unique animation talent to create a groundbreaking toy-perspective film, using the studio's resources to mitigate technical and financial risks while retaining creative identity.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Remain a short-film/commercial shop; risk losing lead animator to competitors; no path to features.
2.  **Self-fund feature:** Impossible given rendering costs and lack of capital; high probability of bankruptcy before completion.
3.  **Accept deal:** Gains funding, distribution, and survival; trades some leverage and margin for existence as a feature studio.

**Fit:** Aligns with the north star of becoming a feature animation studio. Reuses existing core competency (short-form CG) but scales it.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the studio micromanages creative decisions, ruining the "toy's point of view" authenticity, or if rendering timelines slip so badly the budget exhausts before completion. This is a two-way door regarding future projects, but a one-way door for this specific production slot. Cost of delay is losing the lead animator and the window of novelty for all-CG features.

**Evidence:** The offer exists (fact). The company lacks feature experience (fact). Rendering is slow/costly (fact). No counter-evidence suggests the studio is predatory beyond standard contract leverage.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (inexperience, cost, partner leverage) are inherent to the opportunity, not facts proving failure. Inexperience is solved by the partner's resources; cost is covered by the deal; leverage is the price of entry.

**Risks:** Creative interference, budget overruns, technological bottlenecks. These become kill criteria: if the studio mandates script changes that dilute the core concept, or if rendering tests exceed 150% of projected time/cost, the project must pivot or halt.

**Kill Criteria:** Stop if pre-production rendering tests show costs exceeding the studio's advance by 20% without a contingency plan. Leading metric: Completion of a 5-minute proof-of-concept sequence within 6 months meeting quality benchmarks.

DECISION: GO
