First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to enter a dominant market with a device that compromises the core utility of a phone while failing to match the competitor's price or library.

**Steelman:** A hybrid device leverages existing mobile distribution to disrupt the handheld market by offering dual utility at a premium price point.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fundamental Usability Failure:** The requirement to remove the battery to change games is a catastrophic design flaw. It renders the device unusable for its primary function (communication) during gameplay interruptions and introduces high friction for the secondary function. No amount of game library depth compensates for breaking the core "phone" promise.
2.  **Economic Mismatch:** At $300, the device is priced significantly above the market leader's dedicated console. Consumers are asked to pay a premium for a compromised experience (awkward ergonomics, battery removal) rather than a superior one.
3.  **Content Moat:** The market leader's huge library and publisher relationships create a barrier that a new entrant with "uncommitted" publishers cannot breach, especially on inferior hardware.
4.  **Form Factor Conflict:** A device awkward to hold for calls will fail in the primary mobile market, while its gaming capabilities are inferior to the dedicated rival. It satisfies neither user need effectively.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoid brand damage and inventory risk in a losing segment.
*   **Software Only:** Partner with the existing market leader to put games on phones, leveraging the install base without hardware risk.
*   **Dedicated Hardware Later:** Enter the gaming market only when a dedicated, ergonomic device can be built that matches or beats the leader's price and library.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches, reviewers ridicule the battery-swap mechanic, gamers reject the high price for a smaller library, and phone users reject the awkward form factor. Sales flatline; the project is cancelled within 18 months, damaging the brand's reputation for usability.

**Blockers:**
*   **Usability:** Removing the battery to swap games is a functional disqualifier.
*   **Price/Value:** Higher price than the leader for inferior utility and content.
*   **Ergonomics:** Awkward for primary use case (calls).

**Risks:** Publisher apathy, market rejection. These are secondary to the hard blockers.

DECISION: NO-GO
