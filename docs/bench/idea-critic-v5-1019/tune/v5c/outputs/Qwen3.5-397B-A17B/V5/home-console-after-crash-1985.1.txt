First call: GO. The market collapse was driven by retailer distrust and product saturation, not a lack of consumer demand. This proposal directly addresses the root causes: rebranding bypasses the "video game" stigma, the take-back promise removes retailer financial risk, and the lockout chip prevents the quality collapse that ruined the previous cycle.

Steelman: By positioning the hardware as a toy/entertainment system with guaranteed buybacks, the company secures shelf space in a hostile market while the lockout chip ensures software quality control, creating a closed ecosystem that rebuilds consumer confidence.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Cede the North American market to home computers and wait years for sentiment to shift naturally. High opportunity cost.
2. Launch as a standard console: Immediate rejection by retailers due to the 1983 crash stigma; high probability of total failure.
3. Partner with an existing US toy maker: Dilutes control and margins; slower execution. The proposed direct approach with risk reversal is superior.

Fit: Aligns with the goal of global expansion by adapting to local market trauma rather than ignoring it.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the "toy" positioning confuses consumers or if the lockout chip stifles developer interest too severely. However, the bundle (robot/gun) reinforces the toy narrative, and the closed market is a necessary trade-off for quality assurance given recent history. The cost of delay is losing the first-mover advantage in the recovery phase.

Evidence: The proposal cites specific 1983 market conditions (retailer refusal, stock clearing) which validate the need for the take-back clause and rebranding. The Japanese market success proves the hardware's viability.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (retailer refusal, no track record, developer anger) are precisely what the proposed mechanisms (buyback, toy branding, controlled launch) are designed to mitigate. These are risks managed by the plan, not facts that invalidate it.

Risks: Developer relations may strain initially; high capital exposure from buybacks. These are acceptable costs for market entry and become kill criteria if buyback rates exceed 40% or if fewer than 5 key titles launch within year one.

The plan turns market weaknesses into structured safeguards.

DECISION: GO
