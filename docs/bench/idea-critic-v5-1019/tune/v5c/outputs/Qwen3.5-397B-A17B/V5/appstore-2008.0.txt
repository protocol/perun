First call: GO. The proposal converts a philosophical stance ("web apps are sufficient") into a defensible ecosystem moat, directly addressing the risk of rivals locking in developers while preserving product control.

**Steelman:** A curated, revenue-sharing native app store is the only viable mechanism to scale functionality without sacrificing the device's signature stability or alienating carrier partners, effectively turning third-party innovation into a proprietary asset.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Retain web-only approach. Risk: Developers migrate to rival open platforms; device becomes a "dumb terminal" relative to competitors.
2.  **Open sideloading:** Match rival openness. Risk: Unchecked security failures and carrier revolts destroy brand reputation for polish.
3.  **Proposed Store:** Balances openness with control. Cost: High operational overhead for review; Risk: Slower initial app volume.

**Fit:** Aligns with the north star of "premium user experience." Open distribution contradicts the CEO's stated priority on polish; a walled garden enforces it.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the 30% fee or review latency drives top-tier developers to rivals, or if a malicious app slips through, validating carrier fears. However, the "one-way door" of opening the platform is irreversible; delaying cedes the developer mindshare permanently. The cost of delay (losing the ecosystem war) outweighs the operational friction of reviews.

**Evidence & Blockers:**
*   **Blockers:** NONE. The concerns (security, carrier objection, workload) are operational hurdles inherent to the strategy, not factual contradictions. The proposal explicitly includes a review process to mitigate security and carrier risks.
*   **Risks:** Review bottleneck slowing growth; 30% fee perceived as greedy. These are managed via the proposed "company reviews every app" mechanism and market positioning.

**Kill Criteria:** If, within 12 months, the review process averages >7 days causing top developers to abandon the platform, or if a single approved app causes a widespread security breach requiring a recall, the model must pivot to a more open or restricted state.

**Strongest Attack Survived:** The argument that "web apps are sufficient" was tested against the 2008 reality of rival native ecosystems. The attack failed because web apps of that era could not match native performance or access hardware features, making the "web-only" stance a strategic liability despite the CEO's previous rhetoric. The store model corrects this without abandoning the core value of stability.

DECISION: GO
