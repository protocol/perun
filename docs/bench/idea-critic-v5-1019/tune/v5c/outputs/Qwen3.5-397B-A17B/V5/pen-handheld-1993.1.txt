First call: NO-GO. The core value proposition (reliable handwriting recognition) is explicitly stated as unreliable in testing, and the form factor fails the primary utility constraint (pocketability) for a "handheld" device.

**Steelman:** A first-mover advantage in a new category justifies launching an imperfect, expensive device to define the market standard before competitors emerge.

**Attack:**
1.  **Blocker (Core Function):** The proposal admits the primary input mechanism is unreliable. For a device marketed on this feature, shipping known broken functionality is not a risk; it is a product failure. No amount of publicity fixes a tool that cannot read user input.
2.  **Blocker (Form Factor):** A "handheld" computer that does not fit in a pocket contradicts the portability requirement of the category. At $700 (a premium price), users will not tolerate a device that is both cumbersome and functionally defective.
3.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" avoids brand damage from a high-profile flop. A "cheaper alternative" would be to license the OS to other manufacturers or delay launch until recognition accuracy meets a viable threshold.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** The device launches with fanfare, reviewers demonstrate failed handwriting tests, the bulk prevents daily carry, and the lack of software creates a ghost town. The category is declared dead for a decade because the first major entrant failed to deliver on its promise.
5.  **Evidence:** The proposal's own facts state recognition is unreliable and the size is excessive. These are not hypothetical risks; they are current realities of the prototype.

**Decision Logic:**
*   **Blockers:** Yes. The core mechanism fails testing, and the physical design violates the category definition.
*   **Risks:** High price, unproven category, lack of software. These are secondary to the fact that the product does not work as advertised.
*   **Kill Criteria:** The proposal lacks a metric for "acceptable recognition rate" or a plan to shrink the device.

Launching a premium product with a known, fatal flaw in its primary feature, compounded by a design that negates its portability, guarantees market rejection. The "first mover" benefit is nullified if the first product establishes the category as "broken."

DECISION: NO-GO
