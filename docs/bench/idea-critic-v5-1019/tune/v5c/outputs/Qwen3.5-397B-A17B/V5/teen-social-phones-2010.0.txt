First call: NO-GO. The proposal forces price-sensitive youth to pay premium data costs for a device explicitly stripped of the primary value drivers for that demographic (apps and games), while cannibalizing the company's own upcoming flagship launch.

Steelman: A carrier-subsidized, walled-garden social phone can succeed by offering exclusive, lightweight social features that do not require a full app ecosystem, leveraging the acquisition team's niche expertise to capture a specific youth segment before the main platform arrives.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve brand focus for the main platform; avoid channel conflict and margin erosion on a likely failed product.
2. Delay launch: Align with the main platform to share the app store and reduce hardware fragmentation.
3. Low-cost feature phone: Offer the social tools on a cheaper device with a limited data plan, matching the audience's budget.

Fit: This contradicts the north star of platform adoption by fragmenting the user base and delaying the primary launch. It assumes youth will prioritize a single social function over cost and utility, an assumption contradicted by the stated price sensitivity.

Pre-mortem: The product launches, but sales flatline because parents refuse the high data plan for a "toy" phone, and teens reject a device without games or popular third-party apps. Inventory piles up, and the internal conflict distracts leadership from the critical main platform launch.

Evidence & Blockers: The proposal's own facts create insurmountable blockers.
1. **Economic Mismatch:** Target buyers are "price-sensitive," yet the cost structure (full smartphone data plan) is high. The value proposition (no apps, no games) is lower than the cost.
2. **Cannibalization:** Launching a niche device immediately before the "main new phone platform" confuses the market and splits engineering focus during a critical transition.
3. **Value Deficit:** Removing the app store and games from a smartphone for young people removes the primary utility, leaving no justification for the required data plan cost.

These are not risks; they are factual contradictions within the proposal that guarantee failure. No kill criteria can fix a product where the cost exceeds the value for the target user by design.

DECISION: NO-GO
