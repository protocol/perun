First call: GO. The friction of per-order fees directly suppresses repeat purchase frequency, which is the primary lever for lifetime value in retail. Removing this friction via a fixed upfront fee aligns customer incentives with volume, even if per-unit margins dip.

**Steelman:** An annual $79 membership eliminates the psychological and financial barrier of shipping fees, driving a significant increase in order frequency and basket size that offsets the cost of expedited shipping and the upfront margin hit, while locking in customer loyalty for a year.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary objection is that heavy buyers will exploit the program, causing losses. However, "heavy buyers" are already the most profitable segment if their frequency increases; the risk is only real if the marginal cost of shipping exceeds the marginal profit of the *additional* goods sold. Given the proposal targets "repeat customers," the baseline behavior suggests high elasticity.
*Alternative 1 (Do Nothing):* Retain friction, capping growth and encouraging cart abandonment among loyalists.
*Alternative 2 (Free Shipping Threshold):* Requires customers to hit a spend cap per order. This boosts basket size but fails to solve the frequency problem for small, urgent needs, leaving the "repeat" friction intact.
*Alternative 3 (Flat Fee):* The proposed membership is superior as it decouples shipping cost from individual transaction decisions, maximizing psychological freedom to buy.

**Pre-mortem & Reversibility:**
Failure mode: Shipping costs skyrocket due to abuse by a small cohort of extreme users who do not increase basket size proportionally.
Door Type: Two-way door. While refunding memberships is messy, the program can be paused for new sign-ups or terms adjusted for future renewals immediately if metrics breach thresholds. It is not irreversible.
Cost of Delay: Every quarter delayed is a quarter of compounding loyalty and data loss to competitors who remove friction first.

**Blockers & Risks:**
Blockers: NONE. The economics are "unproven" by definition for a new product, but the unit logic holds: fixed revenue vs. variable cost driven by volume. The short-term margin hit is a feature, not a bug, of customer acquisition.
Risks: Extreme outlier abuse; operational strain on logistics. These are managed via kill criteria (e.g., halt if shipping cost > 15% of incremental revenue).

The proposal correctly identifies the core friction and offers a mechanism to monetize loyalty rather than logistics. The risks are manageable operational variables, not fundamental flaws.

DECISION: GO
