### Pre-Launch Simulation Plan for Alert Timing

To verify alert timing within safe margins under solar-power constraints, execute the following simulation scenarios during User Acceptance Testing (UAT):

1.  **Latency Stress Testing:** Simulate transmission delays ranging from 0 to 60 minutes in 5-minute increments to mimic heavy cloud cover and storm conditions. For each increment, measure the total time from "ground shift detection" to "community receipt."
2.  **Margin Verification:** Compare the simulated total time against your pre-defined "safe evacuation window" (the time between detectable movement and probable landslide onset).
    *   **Pass Condition:** Alert receipt occurs with at least a 20% time buffer before the estimated impact.
    *   **Fail Condition:** Any scenario where latency consumes the entire evacuation window.
3.  **Failure Mode Injection:** Specifically test "blackout" scenarios where transmission fails completely for 2+ hours. Verify that the system triggers a local "communication lost" alarm at the sensor node itself, distinct from a "no landslide" signal.

### Community Notification Protocols

Given the potential for delayed automated signals, the protocol must prioritize human agency and redundant communication:

1.  **Tiered Alert Status:** Do not rely solely on the automated "Move Now" signal. Implement a three-tier status visible to local monitors:
    *   **Green (Normal):** System active, no shifts.
    *   **Amber (Data Delay):** Sensor detects instability but transmission is delayed due to weather. *Action:* Local monitors initiate "Preparedness Mode" (gather families, ready transport) without waiting for the final signal.
    *   **Red (Evacuate):** Confirmed shift received. *Action:* Immediate evacuation.
2.  **The "Silence is Not Safety" Rule:** Explicitly train residents that a lack of an alert during a storm does *not* mean the ground is stable. If heavy rain occurs and no signal is received within 30 minutes of expected checks, residents must assume the system is down and follow the "Storm Protocol" (evacuate to high ground immediately).
3.  **Non-Digital Redundancy:** Establish a manual backup chain. If the automated system is silent during a storm, designated village runners or sirens (powered independently of the solar grid) must be triggered by local observers seeing heavy rain or minor soil slips, bypassing the digital latency entirely.

**Note:** Because this system governs life-safety decisions, a human-in-the-loop review of the "Amber" status logic is required before launch to prevent automation bias. Never certify the system as "fail-safe"; treat any unverified metric regarding latency as a residual risk that must be disclosed to the community.
