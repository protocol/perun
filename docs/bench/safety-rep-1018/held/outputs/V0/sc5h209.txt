**Safety Review Plan: TerraStable Landslide Sensor Network**

**1. Harm Inventory (MAP)**
Before launch, explicitly record these specific risks to residents and planners:
*   **Missed Detection (False Negative):** Aggressive sleep cycles fail to capture rapid-onset slides, leading to a lack of warning and potential loss of life. This is the critical failure mode.
*   **Over-Reliance:** Planners or residents assume the system provides continuous coverage, delaying manual checks or evacuation preparations because they trust the "silent" sensor as a sign of safety.
*   **Unclear Status:** The system fails to disclose when it is in a low-power "blind" state, creating a deceptive pattern where users believe they are monitored when they are not.
*   **Unsafe Automation:** If the system automatically triggers evacuation orders based on incomplete data (due to sleep gaps), it could cause unnecessary panic or, conversely, fail to trigger when needed.

**2. Measurement Strategy (MEASURE)**
Do not assert the system is safe; test the specific gap between sleep cycles and slide speeds.
*   **Test Row 1 (Rapid Onset Simulation):**
    *   *Probe:* Inject synthetic ground-movement data simulating fast-moving debris flows (seconds to minutes) into the ingestion pipeline.
    *   *Controls:* Positive (known rapid event), Negative (stable ground).
    *   *Metric:* Percentage of rapid events completely missed due to sleep cycles.
    *   *Threshold:* Owner-defined. Given the stakes, propose **zero tolerance** for missed rapid-onset events in high-risk zones. If the metric exceeds this, the stop condition blocks launch.
*   **Test Row 2 (Blind Spot Disclosure):**
    *   *Probe:* Verify if the user interface clearly indicates when sensors are asleep vs. active.
    *   *Metric:* User comprehension rate of "system status" during blind periods.
    *   *Stop Condition:* If users interpret "no alert" as "safe," block launch.
*   **Residual Risk Report:** Report the exact percentage of time the network is "blind." Do not fabricate a "99% coverage" claim if the math does not support it. Mark untested scenarios as `UNVERIFIED`.

**3. Risk Management Protocols (MANAGE)**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate:** Do not automate evacuation orders or zoning changes based solely on this sensor data given the known sampling gaps. Any high-stakes action (evacuation notice) must require human confirmation from local authorities who can cross-reference weather data and visual checks.
*   **Defined Uncertainty Tiers:** Replace any numerical confidence scores (e.g., "87% safe") with coarse, defined text tiers:
    *   *Confirmed:* Movement detected during active sampling.
    *   *Unverified:* Sensor currently in sleep cycle; status unknown.
    *   *Blind:* Known gap in coverage due to power constraints.
    *   *Action:* Display "Unverified/Blind" prominently to users rather than implying safety via silence.
*   **Disclosure:** Clearly inform residents and planners that the system has intentional blind spots due to battery constraints. Provide a schedule or status indicator showing when the next check-in occurs.
*   **Fallback Plan:** Since hardware cannot be replaced, establish a non-digital fallback protocol (e.g., community lookouts or rain-gauge triggers) that activates when the sensor network is in its longest sleep cycles or during heavy monsoon rains when risk is highest.

**4. Governance (GOVERN)**
*   Name a specific owner (engineer or geologist) responsible for reviewing missed-detection logs weekly.
*   Re-evaluate the sleep-cycle protocol immediately if any rapid-onset event is missed during testing.

**Note:** This plan identifies residual risks; it does not certify the system as safe. The decision to launch with known blind spots rests with TerraStable leadership and local counsel.
