**Immediate Recommendation: Do Not Go Live with Automated Escalation to a Closed Queue**

Shipping a feature that automatically routes discrimination complaints to a queue with no human review creates an unacceptable risk of ignoring critical, time-sensitive issues. This constitutes an unsafe automation of a high-stakes action where the "worst realistic case" is that a valid discrimination claim sits unopened indefinitely, causing reputational damage and potential legal liability for Northwind Support.

Since your constraint (closed specialized queue) cannot be changed immediately, you must implement a **Human-in-the-Loop Gate** before the automation executes the final routing. You cannot certify this system as "safe" without this control.

**Action Plan for Final Testing (Starting Now):**

1.  **Change the Automation Logic (The Gate):**
    Modify the bot's behavior immediately. Instead of moving tickets directly to the closed specialized queue, the bot must:
    *   Tag the ticket internally as "Potential Discrimination."
    *   Keep the ticket in the *general* queue (which is staffed).
    *   Add a high-visibility internal note or flag for human agents to see: "AI Flag: Potential Discrimination – Requires Manual Review."
    *   **Result:** The ticket remains visible to your current staff, ensuring no complaint is lost, while still identifying the sensitivity.

2.  **Define the Harm Inventory & Measurement (MAP & MEASURE):**
    Before Monday, execute these specific tests on the modified logic:
    *   **Harm:** False Negatives (Missing real discrimination).
        *   *Test:* Run 20 known discrimination complaints through the bot.
        *   *Pass Condition:* 100% must receive the "Potential Discrimination" flag. If even one fails, the keyword logic is insufficient.
    *   **Harm:** False Positives (Over-flagging normal issues).
        *   *Test:* Run 50 normal complaints containing words like "unfair" or "wrong" but no actual discrimination.
        *   *Pass Condition:* Flag rate should be low enough not to overwhelm general staff. Define an acceptable threshold (e.g., <10% false positive rate) with your team lead.
    *   **Harm:** Over-reliance.
        *   *Test:* Verify agents see the flag clearly. Ensure the UI does not hide the AI's suggestion behind a click.

3.  **Transparency Artifact (Model Card Lite):**
    Create a one-page document for your team stating:
    *   **Intended Use:** Triage only; does not make final decisions.
    *   **Limitations:** Keyword-based; may miss context or slang.
    *   **Current Status:** Flags only; no auto-routing due to queue capacity.
    *   **Owner:** Name the specific team member responsible for reviewing flagged tickets daily.

4.  **Disclosure:**
    Ensure customers are not misled. If the bot replies automatically, it must disclose that an AI assisted in tagging their request, though full legal disclosure requirements should be routed to counsel.

**Summary of Constraints:**
You cannot fix the staffing shortage by Monday. Therefore, you must not automate the *action* of moving the ticket. You can only automate the *suggestion* (flagging). Any plan that moves tickets to an unmonitored queue is a failure of safety governance.

**Notes:**
*   This plan treats the "closed queue" as a hard stop for automation, forcing a fallback to human review in the general queue.
*   Do not invent accuracy metrics; run the specific test rows above and report the actual pass/fail results to your team lead before launch.
