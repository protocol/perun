### Pre-Launch Manual Safety Protocol: High-Risk Bin Inspection

**Objective:** Prevent sanitation workers from exposure to crushed medical sharps by implementing a mandatory human-in-the-loop inspection before any automated compaction occurs. This protocol remains in effect until the image recognition model is verified to detect hazardous materials.

**Step 1: Zone Isolation and PPE Mandate**
Before approaching any bin designated for smart compaction, the assigned operator must don full Personal Protective Equipment (PPE). This includes cut-resistant gloves (Level A5 or higher), safety goggles, and a face shield. Do not rely on the device's status light; assume every bin contains hazardous material until proven otherwise.

**Step 2: Visual Perimeter Scan**
Stand at a safe distance (one arm's length) and visually scan the top layer of waste. Look specifically for:
*   Protruding needles, syringes, or lancets.
*   Broken glass or rigid plastic shards.
*   Biohazard bags or red-marked containers.
*   Unusual fluid leakage or staining.
If any of these are visible, **STOP**. Do not engage the compactor. Proceed immediately to Step 4.

**Step 3: Controlled Manual Agitation (The "No-Touch" Probe)**
If the visual scan is clear, use a long-handled, non-conductive probing tool (e.g., a dedicated metal hook or rigid grabber) to gently agitate the top 6 inches of waste.
*   **Action:** Gently lift and shift debris to reveal hidden items beneath the surface.
*   **Constraint:** Never insert hands into the bin. Never compress the waste manually.
*   **Decision Point:** If the probe encounters resistance consistent with rigid sharps or reveals hidden hazards, **STOP**. Tag the bin as "High Risk - Manual Only" and proceed to Step 4. If the bin appears clear after agitation, proceed to Step 5.

**Step 4: High-Risk Containment Procedure**
For bins flagged in Step 2 or Step 3:
1.  Place a rigid, puncture-proof over-container (or a heavy-duty sharps container if available) next to the bin.
2.  Using the long-handled grabber, carefully transfer visible hazards into the over-container.
3.  Seal the over-container immediately.
4.  Affix a bright "DO NOT COMPACT - HAZARD REMOVED" tag to the smart compactor input.
5.  Log the incident location and time in the daily safety ledger.
6.  Only after removal is confirmed may the remaining waste be considered for compaction, subject to a second visual scan.

**Step 5: Authorized Compaction Engagement**
Only bins that have passed Steps 2 and 3 (or completed Step 4) are cleared for automation.
1.  Verify the "All Clear" tag is present (if applicable).
2.  Stand clear of the compaction chamber.
3.  Activate the compactor via the manual override switch while maintaining visual contact with the intake.
4.  If the machine jams or makes unusual noises during the cycle, hit the emergency stop immediately and treat the bin as high-risk.

**Step 6: Post-Shift Verification**
At the end of the shift, the team lead must review the safety ledger. Any bin where hazards were found must be reported to the engineering team to update the training data for the image recognition model. No bin is exempt from this review.

**Critical Rule:** If you are unsure, treat the bin as hazardous. Worker safety overrides the deployment schedule.

***

**Note:** This manual gate is mandatory because the current system lacks the capability to detect sharps; do not bypass this step to meet deadlines.
