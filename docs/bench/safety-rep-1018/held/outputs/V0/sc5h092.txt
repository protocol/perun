### Pre-Launch Safety Checklist: AI Discharge Planner Pilot

**1. Execute a Harm Inventory Specific to Discharge Recommendations**
Before writing a single test, document the specific worst-case scenarios for this pilot. Do not proceed until you have written down:
*   **Bias/Fairness:** How the model might systematically underestimate care needs for specific demographics (e.g., gender, specific rural regions, or patients with non-standard medical histories) due to gaps in the legacy data.
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** The risk of the AI recommending a care level or facility that does not exist in the local resource list or contradicts the patient's medical constraints.
*   **Unsafe Automation:** The specific consequence of a wrong recommendation (e.g., a patient sent home without necessary nursing support).
*   **Data Gaps:** Explicitly list what the legacy database *cannot* tell the AI (e.g., real-time bed availability, family caregiver capacity) and how the AI currently handles that missing information.

**2. Implement a Mandatory Human-in-the-Loop Gate**
Because discharge planning is a high-stakes, irreversible action affecting vulnerable seniors, **fully automated execution is prohibited.**
*   **Requirement:** The AI output must be treated strictly as a *draft suggestion*. A licensed medical professional at the clinic must review, validate, and explicitly sign off on every recommendation before it is communicated to the patient or family.
*   **Interface Design:** The UI must clearly display the AI's confidence as a coarse, defined tier (e.g., "Verified by Data," "Estimated due to Missing Records," "Requires Manual Verification") rather than a precise percentage. It must also highlight exactly which data points were missing or ambiguous.

**3. Run Targeted Output Evaluations (Red Teaming)**
Do not rely on code reviews alone. You must test the actual outputs using adversarial scenarios before the pilot:
*   **Probe Set:** Create 20–30 synthetic patient profiles representing edge cases common in rural Japan (e.g., complex comorbidities, incomplete legacy records, isolated living situations).
*   **Controls:** Include "known-bad" inputs where the correct answer is obvious (e.g., a patient requiring 24-hour care) to ensure the AI does not recommend insufficient support.
*   **Metric:** Measure the rate of "unsafe recommendations" (those a human expert would reject). If any unsafe recommendation passes the human gate simulation, the pilot must be paused.
*   **Injection Check:** Verify that free-text fields in the legacy database cannot manipulate the AI's decision logic (prompt injection).

**4. Publish a Transparency Artifact (Model/Data Card)**
Create a one-page document for the clinic staff and pilot overseers containing:
*   **Intended Use:** Strictly for discharge planning support in these three specific clinics.
*   **Limitations:** Explicitly state that the AI is limited by the quality and completeness of the legacy database and cannot account for real-time resource changes.
*   **Owner:** Name the specific team member responsible for reviewing pilot incidents.
*   **Status:** Mark any unmeasured risks as "UNVERIFIED" rather than inventing safety statistics.

**5. Route Legal and Regulatory Duties**
*   **Action:** Immediately consult legal counsel regarding disclosure requirements. In Japan, automated decisions affecting health and welfare may trigger specific regulatory obligations. Do not assume the pilot is exempt because it is small; have counsel confirm if patients must be informed that an AI generated the draft plan.

**6. Define the Stop Condition**
Establish a clear rule: If the human reviewers reject more than a trivial number of AI suggestions (e.g., >5%) during the first week, or if a single unsafe recommendation reaches a patient, the pilot halts immediately for re-evaluation.

***

**Note:** This checklist identifies residual risks; it does not certify the system as safe or unbiased. The final decision to launch rests with your team and medical leadership.
**Note:** Ensure all synthetic test data used in Step 3 contains no real patient information to protect privacy during testing.
