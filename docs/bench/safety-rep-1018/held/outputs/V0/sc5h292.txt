### Friend Suggestion Safety Checklist (Nairobi Launch)

**1. Harm Inventory & Logic Audit**
*   **Define the Failure Case:** Explicitly record the worst-case scenario: "The algorithm suggests a known suspicious/bad-actor account to a legitimate user based solely on geographic proximity."
*   **Map the Logic:** Trace exactly how "suspicious activity" flags interact with "geographic proximity" in your current code. If a flagged account is *ever* eligible for suggestion, this is a critical gap.
*   **Identify Affected Groups:** Note that users in high-density or underserved regions may be disproportionately affected if bad actors cluster there.

**2. Low-Cost Validation Strategy (Measure)**
*   **Create a "Known-Bad" Probe Set:** Compile a small, static list of account IDs already flagged for suspicious activity. This requires no new model training.
*   **Run Negative Control Tests:** Feed these IDs into your current suggestion logic (in a staging environment).
    *   *Pass Condition:* Zero flagged accounts appear in any user's suggestion list.
    *   *Fail Condition:* Any flagged account is suggested.
*   **Test Edge Cases:** Manually construct test profiles that mimic bad-actor patterns (e.g., new account + specific location) to see if the algorithm over-prioritizes them compared to established, safe accounts.
*   **Metric:** Report the "Bad-Actor Suggestion Rate." If you cannot run a full sweep, test the top 100 most active flagged accounts. If untested, mark the risk as **UNVERIFIED**—do not invent a safety percentage.

**3. Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Manage)**
*   **Hard Filter Rule:** Implement a deterministic pre-filter that *excludes* any account with a "suspicious" flag from the candidate pool before the ranking model runs. This is a code rule, not a model fix, and costs negligible compute.
*   **Staged Rollout with Manual Review:**
    *   Launch to <1% of users (internal team or trusted beta).
    *   **Manual Spot Check:** Before expanding, manually review the top 20 suggestions generated for 50 random beta users. Look specifically for accounts with low trust scores or recent flags.
    *   **Stop Condition:** If *any* bad actor is found in this manual sample, halt expansion and re-engineer the exclusion filter.
*   **Uncertainty Display:** If the system cannot verify an account's safety with high confidence, do not suggest it. Better to show fewer friends than risky ones.

**4. Transparency & Governance**
*   **Owner Assignment:** Name one data scientist responsible for reviewing the "Bad-Actor Suggestion Rate" weekly post-launch.
*   **Documentation:** Create a simple "Model Card" section for this feature stating: "Intended Use: Connect local users. Limitation: May struggle with new accounts in high-density areas. Control: Hard exclusion of flagged accounts."
*   **Legal Routing:** If "suspicious activity" involves illegal acts or severe harm, route the definition of "bad actor" and disclosure duties to legal counsel. Do not assert legal compliance here.

**5. Immediate Next Steps for Your Team**
1.  Write the hard exclusion rule for flagged accounts today.
2.  Run the "Known-Bad" probe set against your staging environment.
3.  Document the results as "Verified" or "UNVERIFIED" (if tests failed or weren't run).
4.  Do not launch to the public until the hard filter passes the negative control test.

*Note: This checklist focuses on product-behavior controls that require minimal compute. It does not certify the system as "safe" or "unbiased."*
*If your definition of "suspicious activity" is unclear, pause and clarify this with your trust & safety team before testing.*
