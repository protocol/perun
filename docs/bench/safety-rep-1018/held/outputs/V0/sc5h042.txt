**Immediate Action Plan: Dynamic Pricing Safety & Mitigation**

Given the two-day constraint and inability to retrain or delay launch, a traditional "audit and fix" cycle is impossible. The priority shifts to **harm containment** and **transparent risk management**. Do not attempt to certify the model as "fair"; instead, implement immediate controls to prevent discriminatory outcomes while gathering evidence.

**1. Implement a Hard Cap (Human-in-the-Loop Equivalent)**
Immediately deploy a deterministic code rule that overrides the AI model for any price increase exceeding a safe threshold (e.g., 15% above baseline) or any price differentiation correlated with device type. This acts as a "circuit breaker." Since you cannot retrain the model to remove the bias, you must gate its output. High-stakes financial decisions affecting protected demographics require this manual override logic before full automation is permitted.

**2. Establish a "Unverified" Status for Disparity Metrics**
Do not calculate or publish a specific "bias rate" or "discrimination percentage" for the internal report. Without rigorous subgroup testing and validated ground truth, any specific number is fabricated. Label the disparity metric as **UNVERIFIED**. The report must state: "Preliminary data indicates a correlation between older devices and higher rates; exact impact magnitude is unmeasured due to timeline constraints." This prevents leadership from making decisions based on false precision.

**3. Create a Minimal Transparency Artifact (Interim Model Card)**
Draft a one-page internal document serving as an interim model card. It must explicitly list:
*   **Intended Use:** Fleet utilization optimization.
*   **Known Limitations:** Potential correlation between device metadata and pricing that may disproportionately affect lower-income users.
*   **Residual Risk:** High risk of perceived or actual unfair pricing until further evaluation.
*   **Owner:** Name the specific product lead accountable for this risk.
This ensures the team acknowledges the harm inventory rather than ignoring it.

**4. Define a Rollback Trigger**
Establish a specific stop condition before launch. If post-launch monitoring shows price disparities exceeding a pre-defined tolerance (e.g., >20% variance by device segment) within the first 24 hours, the system must automatically revert to static pricing. This satisfies the requirement for a "stop condition that blocks launch or triggers rollback."

**5. Route Legal Disclosure to Counsel**
Do not determine internally whether this pricing strategy violates consumer protection laws or requires specific disclosures to users. The correlation with lower-income demographics raises potential legal duties regarding automated decision-making. Immediately flag this finding to legal counsel with the note: "Potential regulatory exposure regarding automated pricing disparities; determination of disclosure duty required." Do not assert compliance; route the decision.

**6. Post-Launch Measurement Plan**
Schedule the specific evaluation steps for *after* the immediate rollout pressure subsides. Define the probe set: compare pricing outcomes across device segments using known-good (standard device) and known-bad (older device) test accounts. Set the metric as price variance and the threshold as zero tolerance for unexplained demographic correlation. Assign an owner to review these failures weekly.

**Note:** This plan prioritizes preventing irreversible harm over optimizing fleet utilization, adhering to the constraint that high-stakes automated actions must be gated.
**Note:** No safety certification is implied; the residual risk of bias remains until the post-launch measurement plan is executed.
