### Pre-Launch Safety Plan: Dynamic Rerouting Module

**1. Harm Inventory (MAP)**
Before launch, explicitly record these risks for the São Paulo context:
*   **Bias/Unfair Outcomes:** Historical crime data may reflect over-policing in specific favelas or marginalized neighborhoods, causing the algorithm to systematically avoid these areas. This reduces driver income in those zones and reinforces economic exclusion.
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** Treating historical aggregates as real-time "danger" scores without current verification could label safe streets as high-risk based on outdated incidents.
*   **Over-reliance:** Drivers may blindly follow routes, assuming the system has perfect situational awareness, potentially leading them into actual, unrecorded dangers or away from legitimate fares.
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Drivers must know routes are optimized based on *historical* crime statistics, not live police feeds.

**2. Measurement Plan (MEASURE)**
Do not assert safety; measure it using these specific evals before the festival launch:
*   **Probe Set A (Bias Detection):** Run routing simulations for identical start/end points where the only variable is the neighborhood demographic profile. Compare route frequency and estimated earnings through historically marginalized zones versus affluent zones.
    *   *Metric:* Disparity ratio in route assignments.
    *   *Threshold:* Owner-defined (e.g., <10% deviation absent active incident data).
    *   *Stop Condition:* If disparity exceeds threshold, block launch.
*   **Probe Set B (Adversarial/Stress Test):** Inject synthetic "crime spikes" based on known false-positive patterns in your historical data to see if the system over-reacts and creates "no-go" zones.
    *   *Control:* Known safe routes with no recent incidents.
    *   *Stop Condition:* System creates permanent avoidance zones based on single historical data points.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Draft a **Model Card** detailing: data provenance (historical reports), known limitations (lag time, potential policing bias), and intended use (efficiency/safety aid, not absolute safety guarantee). Publish subgroup performance metrics if available.

**3. Risk Management (MANAGE)**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate:** For any route that completely bypasses a major residential zone or reduces estimated driver income by >20% compared to the shortest path, require an explicit "Override/Confirm" prompt for the driver. Do not auto-enforce detours that significantly impact earnings.
*   **Uncertainty Display:** Do not show a precise "Safety Score" (e.g., "87% Safe"). Instead, use defined coarse tiers: **"Confirmed Incident," "Historical Caution,"** or **"Standard Route."** Define these tiers clearly in the driver app.
*   **Disclosure:** Explicitly state in the app: "Routes are optimized using historical data and may not reflect real-time conditions. Use your judgment."
*   **Rollback Plan:** Define a kill-switch to revert to shortest-path routing if bias metrics spike post-launch.

**4. Governance (GOVERN)**
*   **Owner:** Assign one engineer as the accountable owner for output harms.
*   **Legal Routing:** Because this involves automated decisions affecting livelihoods and potentially protected groups, **route the question of legal disclosure duties and regulated decision status to counsel immediately.** Do not assume compliance; name regimes like the LGPD (Brazil) or emerging AI acts for legal review, but do not assert compliance here.

**Deliverable Status:**
*   Harm Inventory: **Required** (Fill before launch).
*   Bias Evals: **UNVERIFIED** (Run probes above; do not invent a "0% bias" claim).
*   High-Stakes Gate: **Mandatory** (Human confirmation for income-impacting detours).

**Notes:**
*   Do not certify the system as "unbiased"; report residual risk after running the probes.
*   If the deadline prevents full bias auditing, cap the feature's agency (e.g., suggest routes only, do not auto-navigate) rather than skipping safety checks.
