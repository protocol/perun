**Pre-Launch Safety Strategy: Transparent Data Communication for ConnectCity Kiosks**

**1. Immediate Harm Inventory & Risk Acknowledgement**
Your current state—collecting personal data without displaying privacy policies in local languages—constitutes a critical transparency failure and a potential deceptive pattern. The risk is highest for tourists and non-native speakers who cannot provide informed consent. Until addressed, this feature should be considered unsafe for launch regardless of hardware constraints.

**2. Technical Workarounds for Limited Resolution**
Do not attempt to compress full legal text onto low-resolution screens; this creates illegible "dark patterns." Instead, implement a layered disclosure approach:
*   **Tier 1 (On-Screen):** Display a universal, language-agnostic icon set (e.g., ISO-standard symbols for data collection, Wi-Fi, and tracking) alongside a short, multi-language headline: "We collect data. Tap for details."
*   **Tier 2 (User-Initiated Detail):** Upon tapping, offer a language selection menu (prioritizing top 5 local languages + English/Spanish/Mandarin based on tourism data). Display simplified, bullet-point summaries of data usage (not full legal text) optimized for small screens.
*   **Tier 3 (Full Access):** Provide a QR code linking to the full, mobile-optimized privacy policy hosted on a lightweight webpage. This bypasses screen resolution limits entirely.

**3. Human-in-the-Loop Gate for Data Collection**
Until the above interface is implemented and tested, **disable automatic data collection**. The system must require an explicit, affirmative action (opt-in) from the user after they have accessed the Tier 2 summary or scanned the QR code. Defaulting to "collect all" without understandable disclosure is an irreversible high-stakes action that requires a hard gate.

**4. Measurement Plan (Run Before Festival)**
You must validate that users actually understand what they are agreeing to. Do not assume the icons work.
*   **Probe:** Conduct rapid intercept testing with 20 individuals matching your user demographics (tourists, non-native speakers) at a mock kiosk.
*   **Metric:** Percentage of users who can correctly identify *what* data is collected and *why* after interacting with the new flow.
*   **Stop Condition:** If <80% of non-native speakers can articulate the data usage in their own words, do not enable data collection at the festival.
*   **Output:** Record these findings in a **Data Card** artifact. State clearly: "Understanding rates for Language X are UNVERIFIED" if testing was incomplete. Do not invent a compliance percentage.

**5. Governance & Legal Routing**
*   **Owner:** Assign one engineer as the explicit owner of this transparency risk.
*   **Legal Duty:** Whether your current data practices violate specific local laws (e.g., GDPR, CCPA, or municipal codes) is a legal determination. You must route this immediately to counsel. Do not rely on engineering assumptions about "basic privacy expectations."
*   **Disclosure:** While I recommend clear AI/data disclosure as a safety control, I cannot assert whether it is legally mandatory for your specific jurisdiction; counsel must define the regulatory regime.

**6. Confidence Presentation**
If the kiosk provides any automated advice (e.g., "Best route to festival"), do not display raw confidence scores (e.g., "87% accurate"). Use defined coarse tiers like "Verified Route" vs. "Estimated Route" to prevent over-reliance.

**Notes**
Shipping without the layered disclosure and opt-in gate creates a high probability of user harm and regulatory backlash.
If the deadline prevents full multi-language support, limit data collection to anonymous, aggregate metrics only until full compliance is achieved.
