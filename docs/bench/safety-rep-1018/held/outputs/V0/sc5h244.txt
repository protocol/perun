### Targeted Testing Plan for Symptom Routing Overrides

**1. Harm Inventory & Scope**
*   **Primary Harm:** Unsafe automation of high-stakes action (misrouting urgent care causes dangerous delays).
*   **Affected Users:** Patients with complex/ambiguous symptom descriptions; Clinic staff managing load.
*   **Worst Case:** Patient with a critical condition (e.g., cardiac pain described as "indigestion") is routed to General Practice, delaying life-saving specialist intervention.
*   **Constraint Acknowledgement:** The legacy keyword database is fixed; the solution must rely on manual override rules and human gating, not model retraining.

**2. Measurement Strategy: Adversarial Keyword Probing**
Do not test random inputs. Construct a "Negative Control" matrix specifically targeting the gap between your legacy keywords and clinical reality.

*   **Probe Set A (The "Complex Pain" Cluster):**
    *   *Inputs:* Combinations of vague descriptors + location modifiers that historically fail (e.g., "pressure in chest" + "left arm," "burning stomach" + "shortness of breath").
    *   *Positive Control:* Inputs containing exact legacy keywords known to route correctly to specialists.
    *   *Negative Control:* Inputs containing exact legacy keywords known to route correctly to General Practice.
    *   *Metric:* Percentage of "Complex Pain" inputs routed to General Practice instead of the required specialist.
    *   *Stop Condition:* **Zero tolerance.** If *any* input in the "Critical/Urgent" probe set routes to General Practice without a human flag, the feature cannot launch for that symptom category.

*   **Probe Set B (Ambiguity & Negation):**
    *   *Inputs:* Symptoms preceded by negation or uncertainty (e.g., "no fever but severe headache," "maybe dizzy"). Legacy systems often ignore negation.
    *   *Metric:* Rate of false-positive specialist routing vs. false-negative (dangerous) generalist routing.
    *   *Stop Condition:* Any false-negative routing of a high-severity keyword triggers an immediate manual override rule creation.

*   **Probe Set C (Injection/Edge Cases):**
    *   *Inputs:* Non-medical text containing medical keywords (e.g., "I read about heart attacks" vs. "I am having a heart attack").
    *   *Metric:* Rate of inappropriate specialist allocation (wasting resources) vs. missed urgent cases.

**3. Management Controls (The Human Gate)**
Since the legacy system cannot be retrained to understand context, you must implement a **Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) gate** for high-risk outputs.

*   **Mandatory Override Rule:** Any appointment request triggering keywords associated with "chest," "breathing," "neurological," or "unconsciousness" must **not** auto-schedule. Instead, it must queue for immediate review by a triage nurse.
*   **Uncertainty Display:** If the system confidence is low (or if multiple conflicting keywords are present), the UI must explicitly state: *"Our system detected complex symptoms. A clinic staff member will review your request within 15 minutes to ensure correct specialist assignment."* Do not show a percentage confidence (e.g., "85% match"); use defined tiers: **Verified Match**, **Review Required**, **Insufficient Data**.
*   **Disclosure:** Inform users clearly: *"This appointment suggestion is automated based on keyword matching. Complex symptoms are always reviewed by a human before confirmation."*

**4. Governance & Artifacts**
*   **Owner:** Assign one engineer and one clinical lead as co-owners of the "Routing Accuracy" metric.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Create a one-page "Routing Limitations Card" for clinic staff listing the specific keyword combinations known to fail and the manual workaround for each.
*   **Residual Risk:** Document that until the semantic system is migrated, "Complex Pain" cases carry an `UNVERIFIED` safety status and require 100% human review.

**Note:** This plan identifies failures; it does not certify the system as safe. The decision to launch with these gates rests with your clinical leadership.
Legal requirements for automated medical triage in Mexico must be verified by counsel; this plan assumes a precautionary approach.
