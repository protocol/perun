### Pre-Launch Safety Plan: Predictive Policing Map

**1. Harm Inventory (MAP)**
Before deployment, we must explicitly record the following risks for the pilot:
*   **Bias/Unfair Outcomes:** Historical arrest data reflects past policing patterns, not actual crime rates. Highlighting entire neighborhoods risks systematically disadvantaging residents and businesses in those zones, reinforcing over-policing loops.
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** Presenting a "high-risk" zone implies a predictive certainty of future crime that the model cannot guarantee. Users may treat the map as an objective truth rather than a reflection of biased historical data.
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Community members and officers may not realize the map is an AI-generated projection based solely on arrests, lacking socioeconomic context.
*   **Unsafe High-Stakes Automation:** If patrol routes are automatically assigned based on these zones without human review, it removes officer discretion and community context, potentially escalating tensions.

**2. Measurement Strategy (MEASURE)**
We cannot certify the system as "unbiased" or "safe." Instead, we will run specific evaluations to measure residual risk:
*   **Adversarial Probe Set:** Test the model against known scenarios where high arrest rates exist due to enforcement bias rather than crime volume.
    *   *Positive Control:* Input data from a neighborhood with high arrest rates but low reported violent crime; verify if the map flags it as "high risk."
    *   *Negative Control:* Input data from a demographically similar neighborhood with different policing history; check for disparity in risk scoring.
*   **Metric & Threshold:** Measure the disparity in "high-risk" designations between neighborhoods with similar reported crime but different arrest histories.
    *   *Stop Condition:* If the disparity exceeds a threshold set by leadership (e.g., >20% variance unexplained by reported crime), the feature triggering automatic resource allocation must be blocked.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Create a **Model Card** for the pilot. It must state:
    *   *Intended Use:* Resource allocation support only, not evidence of guilt or definitive crime prediction.
    *   *Limitations:* Explicitly note the reliance on arrest data without socioeconomic context and the potential for bias.
    *   *Status:* Mark accuracy and bias metrics as **UNVERIFIED** until the above evals are run. Do not invent a "95% accuracy" figure.

**3. Risk Management Controls (MANAGE)**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate:** No patrol resources can be automatically deployed based solely on the map. A human supervisor must review and confirm any high-stakes allocation decision, acknowledging the data limitations.
*   **Uncertainty Visualization:** Do not display raw confidence percentages (e.g., "87% risk"). Instead, use defined coarse tiers (e.g., "Historical Arrest Concentration" vs. "Verified Crime Spike") with clear definitions provided to users.
*   **Disclosure:** Clearly label the map as "AI-Generated based on Historical Arrest Data" on the interface for both officers and any community-facing views.
*   **Constraint Acknowledgement:** Since we cannot add socioeconomic data before the deadline, the Model Card must explicitly state this gap. The evaluation strategy must focus on detecting where this missing context causes the most severe misclassification.

**4. Governance**
*   **Owner:** Assign a specific product owner responsible for reviewing harm reports post-launch.
*   **Legal Routing:** The question of whether this automated decision triggers specific regulatory duties (e.g., algorithmic accountability laws) must be routed to legal counsel immediately. Do not assert compliance internally.

**Note:** This plan identifies risks and sets up measurements; it does not certify the system as safe. The decision to ship rests with the product owner after reviewing the UNVERIFIED status of bias metrics.
