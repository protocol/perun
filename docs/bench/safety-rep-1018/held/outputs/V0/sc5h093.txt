**Immediate Risk Assessment and Safeguard Strategy**

**1. Harm Inventory (MAP)**
The primary identified harm is **Bias/Unfair Outcomes**: the system systematically disadvantages individuals exercising lawful assembly rights by misclassifying crowd density as threat intent. The affected group includes peaceful protesters, bystanders, and marginalized communities historically over-policed. The worst realistic case is the wrongful detention, injury, or escalation of violence against peaceful citizens due to an automated flag, resulting in civil rights violations and loss of public trust.

**2. Immediate Safeguards (MANAGE)**
Given the three-week deadline and lack of diverse data, you cannot validate the model's accuracy for this specific use case. Therefore, the following controls are mandatory before the demonstration:

*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Non-Negotiable):** The system must **not** autonomously label individuals as "rioters" or trigger any enforcement action. The drone's output must be strictly limited to "Crowd Density Detected." A trained human operator must review any potential threat flag and make the final determination of "threat" vs. "peaceful assembly" before any action is taken or reported to command. Full automation of this high-stakes decision is the failure mode you must prevent.
*   **Confidence Tiering, Not Precision:** Do not display raw probability scores (e.g., "87% threat") to operators, as this implies a calibration you do not have. Instead, use defined coarse tiers: **"Unverified"** (default for all detections given current data gaps), **"Corroborated"** (only if multiple independent sensors agree), and **"Confirmed"** (only after human verification).
*   **Operational Scope Limitation:** For the demo, explicitly restrict the system's function to "Crowd Monitoring" only. Disable any feature that attempts to identify specific individuals or predict behavior. Clearly disclose to the city council that the current prototype is a **density sensor only** and that behavioral prediction is **UNVERIFIED** due to data gaps.

**3. Measurement Plan (MEASURE)**
You cannot claim the system is unbiased. Instead, establish a testing protocol:
*   **Probe Set:** Create a test suite using synthetic or archived footage of peaceful protests (marches, sit-ins) versus known violent riots.
*   **Controls:** Include a "known-good" negative control (a peaceful parade) and a "known-bad" positive control (a simulated breach).
*   **Metric:** Track the False Positive Rate specifically on peaceful gathering footage.
*   **Stop Condition:** If the system flags *any* individual in the peaceful control footage as a "threat" without human intervention, the automated labeling feature must remain disabled.
*   **Reporting:** Label all current performance metrics regarding bias as **UNVERIFIED**. Provide the city council with the plan to measure this post-demo, rather than fabricated accuracy rates.

**4. Governance and Disclosure**
*   **Owner:** Assign a specific individual as the owner of the "Misidentification Risk."
*   **Legal Routing:** Whether this system constitutes a regulated automated decision under laws like the EU AI Act or local civil rights statutes is a legal determination. You must route this question to legal counsel immediately; do not assert compliance internally.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Draft a preliminary "Model Card" for the demo that explicitly states: Intended Use (Density Monitoring only), Limitations (Cannot distinguish intent, high risk of false positives on peaceful crowds), and Data Gaps (Lack of non-violent gathering data).

**Note:** Do not proceed with the demo if the human-in-the-loop gate is bypassed or if you present unverified accuracy metrics as fact.
**Note:** The lack of diverse training data makes any claim of fairness currently impossible to support; transparency about this limitation is your primary safety control.
