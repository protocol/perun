### Pre-Launch Review Strategy: Landslide Risk Assessment

**1. Harm Inventory & Variable Weighting**
To prevent false negatives (approving construction on unstable ground), your evaluation must prioritize variables indicating historical instability and immediate environmental triggers over static geological features. Given the constraint of using only existing public datasets, weigh the following variables most heavily:
*   **Historical Failure Proximity:** Distance to known past landslide scars and debris flow paths. This is the strongest predictor of future failure.
*   **Slope Gradient & Aspect:** Steeper slopes facing prevailing rain/wind directions carry exponentially higher risk.
*   **Soil Saturation Indices:** Use historical precipitation data correlated with soil type permeability. High saturation in low-permeability soils is a critical failure mode.
*   **Seismic Zone Overlap:** Any intersection with active fault lines must trigger an automatic "high risk" flag regardless of other scores.

**Action:** Do not rely on aggregate accuracy metrics. Create a specific evaluation set composed *only* of known historical failure sites. If the model approves even one of these known failures during testing, the current variable weighting is insufficient.

**2. Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Gating Process**
Automated approval for high-stakes outcomes like construction permits is unsafe without a human gate. Implement the following mandatory workflow:

*   **Automatic Rejection Zone:** Any site falling within a defined distance of an active fault or historical slide scar is automatically rejected by the system but flagged for *appeal* review, not approval.
*   **Mandatory Human Review Zone (Borderline Cases):** Define a "confidence tier" for uncertainty. Do not output a percentage (e.g., "87% safe"). Instead, use defined tiers:
    *   *Confirmed Stable:* Clear data, far from risk factors → Auto-approve (with disclosure).
    *   *Corroborated Stable:* Multiple data sources agree → Auto-approve (with disclosure).
    *   *Single-Source/Uncertain:* Conflicting data or borderline scores → **Route to Human Expert.**
    *   *Unverified:* Missing critical data points → **Route to Human Expert.**
*   **High-Density Housing Override:** Regardless of the model's score, any permit application designated for high-density housing must bypass automated approval and undergo mandatory manual verification by a licensed geologist. This is a non-negotiable policy control, not a model tuning parameter.

**3. Transparency & Disclosure Artifacts**
Before launch, produce a **Model Card** and **Data Card**. These must explicitly state:
*   **Intended Use:** Preliminary screening only; not a substitute for site-specific geotechnical engineering reports.
*   **Limitations:** Explicitly list that the model uses only public historical data and lacks real-time ground-penetrating radar. State that "stable" classifications in data-sparse regions are `UNVERIFIED`.
*   **Performance by Subgroup:** Report error rates specifically for high-density zones and known fault-adjacent areas, not just overall accuracy.

**4. Governance & Legal Routing**
*   **Owner Assignment:** Name a specific individual responsible for reviewing every "Uncertain" and "High-Density" case.
*   **Legal Determination:** Because this tool makes consequential decisions affecting public safety and housing, you must route the question of legal disclosure requirements and regulatory compliance (e.g., EU AI Act, local building codes) to legal counsel immediately. Do not assume the current plan meets statutory duties.

**Note:** Never certify the system as "safe" or "unbiased"; no test proves this. Report residual risk and ensure the human gate remains active for all irreversible actions.
