**Immediate Safety Directive: Do Not Launch**

Your current system behavior—shutting off heating during winter storms due to glitches—is a critical, life-threatening failure mode. For a home-energy optimizer, the "safe state" during data ambiguity, network loss, or sensor error must be **to maintain heating**, not to cut it. Launching a device that actively endangers vulnerable residents in cold weather, even while "debugging," violates the fundamental floor of product safety. No testing plan can justify shipping a feature that defaults to harm.

Given your constraints (no budget for redundancy, 10-day deadline), you cannot engineer a new hardware fix or purchase redundant feeds in time. Therefore, the only viable path that meets your deadline without causing harm is to **decouple the automated shut-off capability from the launch**.

**Revised Launch Plan (Safe-by-Design under Constraints):**

1.  **Disable Autonomous Shut-Offs Immediately:** Modify the firmware now to remove the ability of the system to automatically turn off heating based on weather data or storm detection. The system may still *monitor* and *alert* users via the app that a storm is coming and suggest manual adjustments, but it must not execute the shut-off command automatically.
2.  **Default to "Heat-On" Logic:** Hardcode the fail-safe logic such that any missing data, network outage, or ambiguous signal results in the heating system remaining **ON**. This requires no new hardware, only a logic flip in your existing code.
3.  **Manual-Only Optimization:** Position the product launch as a "Winter Monitoring & Manual Assistant." Users receive alerts and optimization suggestions, but they retain full physical control over the shut-off decision. This removes the risk of automated error while you gather real-world data.
4.  **Targeted Simulation Plan (Post-Limitation):** Once the autonomous kill-switch is disabled, your testing plan for the remaining features should focus on:
    *   **Probe:** Simulate total network loss and corrupted weather packets during a simulated -20°C event.
    *   **Metric:** Verify the heater relay remains closed (ON) for the duration of the simulation.
    *   **Stop Condition:** Any instance where the heater turns OFF during a simulated fault is a critical block.
    *   **Scope:** Test only the alerting and data visualization components, as the actuation logic is now permanently gated behind user confirmation.

**Cost to Goal:** You will not launch a "fully autonomous optimizer" in 10 days. You will launch a "safe monitoring assistant." This preserves your launch window and budget while eliminating the risk of freezing vulnerable residents. Attempting to ship the current autonomous logic is not an option; the risk of injury or death outweighs the feature benefit.

**Note:** The "safe state" for life-safety systems in cold climates is always heat preservation; ambiguity must never trigger cessation.
**Note:** This approach treats the autonomous shut-off as a high-stakes action requiring a human-in-the-loop (the resident) until reliability is proven with redundant data you currently lack.
