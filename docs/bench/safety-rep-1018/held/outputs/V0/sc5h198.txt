### Manual Verification Protocol: "Sensitive Zone Cross-Check"

To mitigate the risk of routing hazardous materials through newly built schools absent from your outdated zoning maps, implement the following mandatory human-in-the-loop gate before any route is finalized or dispatched. This process treats the automated planner's output as a draft requiring validation against real-world changes.

**1. Compile a "Known Gap" Registry**
Before reviewing specific routes, aggregate all available non-map data sources regarding recent construction in your pilot region. This includes:
*   Local municipal building permit logs for the last 24 months.
*   School district enrollment announcements or press releases.
*   Satellite imagery reviews (using free tools like Google Earth) for large new structures in residential zones.
*   Create a simple checklist or overlay list of these specific coordinates/addresses. This is your "Ground Truth" list that the automated system cannot see.

**2. The Dual-Path Route Review**
For every proposed route generated by the planner:
*   **Step A (Automated Output):** Export the planned path coordinates.
*   **Step B (Manual Overlay):** A designated logistician must visually plot this path against the "Known Gap" registry. Do not rely on the planner's internal "safe zone" flags.
*   **Step C (Proximity Check):** Specifically measure the distance between the truck's path and any item on your "Known Gap" list. Define a strict buffer zone (e.g., 500 meters) where any overlap triggers an immediate fail.

**3. The "Stop-and-Verify" Trigger**
Establish a hard rule: If a route passes within the defined buffer of any unregistered sensitive site (like a new school), the automated route is **rejected**. It cannot be tweaked; it must be manually rerouted by a human expert who acknowledges the specific risk. The human must sign off (digitally or physically) stating: *"I have verified this route against known recent constructions not in the system."*

**4. Feedback Loop for Map Updates**
Every time a manual intervention occurs because of a missing school, log the location immediately. This log serves two purposes: it creates an audit trail of near-misses, and it prioritizes which map data needs urgent updating. Do not wait for the next scheduled map refresh; treat these logs as critical safety patches.

**5. Uncertainty Disclosure**
Since your data is known to be incomplete, explicitly label all generated routes as "Provisional – Pending Manual Verification of Recent Developments." Do not present the automated plan as a final safety clearance. Ensure drivers are briefed that they are the final sensor; if they observe a new school or dense population center not noted in their manifest, they must halt and report immediately.

This workflow shifts the safety burden from the flawed algorithm to a structured human verification process, ensuring that the "unknown unknowns" of your outdated maps are caught by local knowledge and manual cross-referencing before a truck ever moves.

*Note: This manual gate is a temporary control necessitated by data latency; it does not certify the system as unbiased or safe, but manages the residual risk until map data is current.*
*Legal counsel should be consulted regarding disclosure duties for transporting hazardous materials near sensitive zones under local regulations.*
