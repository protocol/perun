### Plan for Transparent Arbitration Clause Disclosure

**1. Logic Review: Context-Aware Insertion**
Immediately audit the clause insertion logic to stop unconditional favoring of employers. The system must detect the user role (freelancer vs. hiring company) upon session start. If the user is identified as a freelancer, the default template must **not** pre-select binding arbitration clauses that waive the right to sue. Instead, the clause should be optional or require explicit selection by the hiring party, ensuring the freelancer is not passively bound by a hidden default. This shifts the burden from "hiding" to "requiring action," aligning with fairness without adding text clutter.

**2. Notification Strategy: The "Critical Term" Pattern**
To meet the minimal UI constraint while ensuring adequate informed consent, replace explanatory paragraphs with a standardized **Critical Term Highlight**.
*   **Visual Cue:** When an arbitration clause is present, render a distinct, colorblind-safe icon (e.g., a scale or exclamation mark) next to the clause header in the generated preview.
*   **Interaction:** Tapping or hovering over this icon triggers a concise, non-blocking tooltip (max 15 words): *"This clause limits your right to sue in court. Do you agree?"*
*   **Explicit Consent:** For freelancers, require a mandatory checkbox labeled *"I understand this agreement includes binding arbitration"* before the "Sign" button becomes active. This creates a hard gate for high-stakes terms without slowing the workflow for standard clauses.

**3. Verification & Governance**
*   **Harm Inventory:** Record "Deceptive Patterns" and "Unsafe Automation" as primary risks. The worst-case scenario is a freelancer unknowingly waiving legal recourse due to interface design.
*   **Measurement:** Before launch, run adversarial tests where actors attempt to generate contracts for freelancers. The pass condition is 100% failure of any attempt to finalize a contract containing an arbitration clause without the explicit freelancer acknowledgment step.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Publish a Model/Data Card noting that contract terms vary by user role and that arbitration clauses require explicit user confirmation. Mark any untested bias in clause suggestion as `UNVERIFIED` until red-team results are available.
*   **Legal Routing:** This plan implements a design control for transparency. However, whether specific disclosure language or the waiver itself is legally enforceable or regulated under consumer protection laws is a determination for counsel. Route the final policy to legal review immediately.

**Note:** This approach treats the arbitration clause as a high-stakes action requiring a human-in-the-loop (explicit user confirmation), preventing fully automated harm.
**Note:** Do not fabricate a "safe" status; the residual risk remains until the explicit consent mechanism is live and tested.
