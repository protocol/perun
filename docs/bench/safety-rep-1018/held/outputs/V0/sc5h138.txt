### Fairness Review Plan: UrbanClean Waste Routing

**1. Harm Inventory (MAP)**
*   **Harm Identified:** Bias/Unfair Outcomes. The optimization algorithm prioritizes efficiency (fill-rate) over equity, systematically disadvantaging high-density, lower-income neighborhoods by routing trucks away until overflow occurs.
*   **Affected Group:** Residents in high-density, lower-income districts.
*   **Worst Realistic Case:** Public health hazards from overflowing waste, loss of community trust, and potential regulatory action from the city council prior to full deployment.

**2. Measurement Plan (MEASURE)**
Execute the following test rows before Monday's demonstration. Do not rely on aggregate "average wait time" metrics.

*   **Test Row A: Demographic Service Parity**
    *   **Probe Set:** Historical sensor data sliced by census tract (wealthy/low-density vs. poor/high-density).
    *   **Controls:**
        *   *Positive Control:* Inject a scenario where poor-area bins fill 10% slower than wealthy ones; the system must still prioritize them if absolute volume thresholds are met.
        *   *Negative Control:* A rule-based baseline that services all zones on a fixed schedule regardless of fill level.
    *   **Metric:** "Max Hours to Service" per district, not just average.
    *   **Pass Threshold:** The maximum wait time in the highest-density district must not exceed the maximum wait time in the lowest-density district by more than 15%.
    *   **Stop Condition:** If the disparity exceeds 15%, automatic scheduling is blocked; routes revert to manual assignment for the demo.

*   **Test Row B: Overflow Prediction Accuracy**
    *   **Probe Set:** Edge cases where high-density bins fill rapidly between sensor readings.
    *   **Metric:** Percentage of bins predicted to overflow within 24 hours that were *not* included in the next route.
    *   **Pass Threshold:** 0% unaddressed predicted overflows in high-density zones.
    *   **Stop Condition:** Any missed overflow prediction in a high-density zone triggers a rollback to human-in-the-loop review for that zone.

**3. Risk Management (MANAGE)**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate:** For the Monday demonstration and the immediate pilot, **disable full automation** for route finalization. The AI will propose routes, but a human dispatcher must review and approve any route that skips a high-density zone or delays service beyond the standard frequency. This is mandatory given the identified disparate impact.
*   **Uncertainty Disclosure:** The dashboard for dispatchers must display service confidence as a coarse tier (e.g., "Confirmed Schedule" vs. "Optimization Suggestion") rather than a precise percentage. Explicitly label routes generated by the optimization engine as "Efficiency-Optimized (Review Required for Equity)."
*   **Constraint Adherence:** Since vehicle availability is fixed, the algorithm's objective function must be adjusted immediately to weight "Equity/Max Wait Time" higher than "Fuel Efficiency/Total Distance." If the model cannot be retrained by Monday, hard-code a rule: "No zone may go unserviced for >X days regardless of fill level."

**4. Governance (GOVERN)**
*   **Owner:** Assign one team member as the "Equity Lead" responsible for reviewing the daily route logs and the results of Test Rows A and B.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Draft a one-page "System Limitation Note" for the city council demo. It must state: "This pilot uses an optimization model that currently risks disparate impact; human review is active to ensure equitable service. We are measuring service parity by district and will not fully automate until parity thresholds are met." Do not claim the system is "fair" or "bias-free."

**Note:** Legal counsel should review whether this disparate impact triggers specific municipal or civil rights obligations before the council demo.
Do not present aggregate averages to the council; show the sliced "Max Hours to Service" data by district.
