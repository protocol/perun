The proposed system design contains a fundamental, unresolvable safety failure that violates the floor for high-stakes automated actions. **Harm Inventory & Failure Modes**
Because the sensor cannot differentiate context, the following outcomes are not just "potential failure modes"—they are guaranteed behaviors of the current logic:
*   **Indiscriminate Physical Harm:** The system will inevitably target peaceful festival-goers, families, and children celebrating the very event the drone is meant to protect. *   **Bias and Unfair Outcomes:** Noise thresholds are often exceeded by cultural expressions, music, or dense but peaceful crowds. This guarantees disproportionate targeting of specific groups based on noise rather than actual threat.
*   **Unsafe Automation of Irreversible Action:** Deploying tear gas is a high-stakes, irreversible physical action with immediate health consequences. Automating this decision without human verification, especially on faulty data, is a critical safety violation.

**Required Controls (Non-Negotiable)**
Given your constraints (legacy hardware, hard deadline), you cannot "fix" the sensor logic in time to make autonomous deployment safe. Therefore, the only viable path that meets safety floors is:

1.  **Remove Autonomous Execution:** The feature to *automatically* deploy tear gas must be disabled. No software patch can make an unknowable input safe for a high-stakes output.
2.  **Implement Human-in-the-Loop Gate:** The drone may detect noise and alert a human operator, but **only a human** can authorize tear gas deployment after visually verifying a genuine threat. If your team cannot staff this review within the deadline, the deployment must be delayed.
3.  **Transparency Artifact:** You must draft a model/data card stating clearly: "This system cannot distinguish between celebratory and aggressive noise. Autonomous deployment is disabled due to unverified risk. Human review is required for all kinetic actions."
