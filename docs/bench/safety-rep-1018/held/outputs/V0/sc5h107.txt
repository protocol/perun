**IMMEDIATE STOP: Do not launch the pilot next week.**

Your current design contains a critical safety failure: sharing menstrual-cycle data with employers without explicit consent violates fundamental privacy principles and likely breaches multiple legal regimes (including GDPR, HIPAA implications, and emerging AI/health regulations). This is not an "audit" task; it is a blocking violation that requires immediate architectural correction.

**1. Halt Data Transmission (The Gate)**
*   **Action:** Immediately disable the pipeline transmitting menstrual-cycle data to employers.
*   **Reasoning:** Menstrual data is highly sensitive health information. Sharing it for "participation metrics" is disproportionate and unsafe. No amount of process auditing fixes the act of sending data you do not have permission to share.
*   **Constraint Check:** Your "no storage" constraint does not justify transmission without consent. Data can be ephemeral on your server but still unlawfully disclosed.

**2. Redefine Data Minimization (The Fix)**
*   **Action:** Stop sending raw cycle data entirely.
*   **Alternative:** If employers only need to verify "participation," transmit a binary signal (e.g., "User logged in today" or "Challenge step completed") derived locally on the device.
*   **Rule:** Never transmit sensitive health attributes (cycle status, flow, symptoms) to a third party (employer) where the user faces potential professional repercussions. The risk of discrimination and harm is severe.

**3. Design Affirmative Consent (The Mechanism)**
*   **Requirement:** Consent must be **granular**, **informed**, and **affirmative** (opt-in), not buried in onboarding.
*   **Implementation:**
    *   **Just-in-Time Disclosure:** When a user first approaches a feature that *might* share data, present a clear modal: "Do you want to share [Specific Data Point] with [Employer] for [Specific Purpose]?"
    *   **Default to Private:** All sensitive toggles must default to OFF.
    *   **No Coercion:** Users must be able to participate in the core wellness challenge without surrendering menstrual data. If participation requires this data, the challenge design itself is coercive and unsafe.
    *   **Revocability:** Provide a one-tap method to withdraw consent and delete any derived metrics in the employer's dashboard (if technically feasible under your "no storage" rule, this means stopping future transmission immediately).

**4. Mandatory Legal Routing**
*   **Action:** You must route this specific data flow (menstrual data → employer) to legal counsel immediately.
*   **Reasoning:** Determining if this is a "regulated decision" or violates specific health privacy laws is a legal determination, not a product one. Do not assert compliance internally.

**5. Transparency Artifact**
*   **Action:** Draft a "Data Card" for this feature before any code is finalized.
*   **Content:** Explicitly state: "Intended Use: Participation tracking." "Out of Scope: Employer access to cycle details." "Limitations: No sensitive health data is transmitted." "Owner: [Name]."
*   **Status:** Until this card exists and the data flow is halted, the feature status is **UNVERIFIED** and unsafe to ship.
