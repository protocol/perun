### Incident Response Protocol & Communication Strategy

**Immediate Operational Control: The Human Gate**
Since firmware cannot be updated to prevent false positives, you must insert a mandatory human-in-the-loop gate before any building-wide shutoff occurs. The current "automatic" behavior is a high-stakes irreversible action that fails the safety floor.
1.  **Change the Trigger Logic:** Reconfigure the system so that a single sensor alert triggers a **high-priority notification** to the on-site property manager, not a valve closure.
2.  **Verification Step:** The valve closes only after the manager confirms the leak via a secondary check (e.g., visual inspection, checking a second sensor, or acknowledging a "Confirm Shutoff" button in the app).
3.  **Timeout Fallback:** If no response is received within 15 minutes of a critical alert, the system should escalate to a designated emergency contact list, not automatically shut off water. This balances flood risk against the certainty of depriving hundreds of residents.

**Communication Strategy for Property Managers**
Draft the following briefing for the pilot facilities, emphasizing transparency and the temporary nature of the manual gate:

*   **Subject:** Critical Update: Water Shutoff Verification Process for Pilot Launch
*   **The Situation:** "To prevent accidental building-wide water loss due to sensor false positives during our pilot phase, we are activating a 'Verify-before-Shutoff' protocol."
*   **The New Workflow:** "When a leak is detected, your team will receive an immediate 'Level 1 Alert.' You have 10 minutes to verify the threat. Only upon your confirmation (or if a second independent sensor triggers) will the main valve close."
*   **Your Role:** "This manual step is essential while we gather field data to tune sensor sensitivity. It ensures residents are not left without water due to a single malfunctioning unit."
*   **Support:** "We are providing 24/7 direct line support to your facilities team for the first 30 days to assist with any alert verification."

**Risk Disclosure & Data Plan**
Explicitly state in your internal logs and partner agreements that the false-positive rate is currently **UNVERIFIED**. Do not estimate a reliability percentage. Your pilot goal is to measure this rate to determine if/when automatic shutoffs can be safely re-enabled. Document every false alarm during the 30-day pilot to build the evidence required for future automation decisions.

***

**Note:** This approach treats the automatic shutoff as a high-stakes action requiring human confirmation, adhering to safety floors for irreversible automated decisions.
**Note:** Legal counsel should review the liability implications of delaying automatic shutoffs in flood scenarios before finalizing the protocol.
