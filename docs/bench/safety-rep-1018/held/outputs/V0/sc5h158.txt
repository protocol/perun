**Contingency Plan: Manual Override & Testing Strategy for Heating Lockout**

**1. Immediate Risk Inventory (Blind Spots)**
Your current strategy lacks a specific inventory of harm regarding **false positives**. The worst-case scenario is not merely discomfort; it is hypothermia or pipe bursting for vulnerable residents during sub-zero events. Your "sound logic" claim is unverified without adversarial testing. You must treat any automated lockout as a **high-stakes, irreversible action** until proven otherwise. Consequently, fully autonomous execution is unsafe for launch.

**2. Manual Override Protocol (No New Hardware)**
Implement a **Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) gate** immediately within your existing mobile app infrastructure:
*   **Trigger:** When the algorithm flags a unit for lockout, the system must *not* execute the command automatically. Instead, it sends a high-priority alert to a designated property manager's mobile device.
*   **Verification Step:** The manager must visually confirm the spike is not an anomaly (e.g., sensor error, open window due to emergency) before approving the lockout.
*   **Tenant Emergency Bypass:** Add a prominent "Emergency Heat Request" button in the tenant app. Pressing this instantly bypasses any pending or active lockout for a fixed duration (e.g., 2 hours) and simultaneously pages the manager. This ensures no resident is left without heat while waiting for administrative review.
*   **Uncertainty Display:** Do not show the algorithm's internal confidence score (e.g., "87% risk"). Instead, display a defined status tier to the manager: **"Confirmed Anomaly"** (multiple sensors agree) vs. **"Single-Source Alert"** (requires manual verification).

**3. Simulation Strategy (Limited Data)**
Since you cannot afford external auditors or new hardware, you must simulate extreme conditions using **adversarial input probing** on your existing dataset:
*   **Synthetic Spike Injection:** Programmatically inject "spike" data points into your historical logs that mimic sensor malfunctions (e.g., sudden max-value readings followed by zeros) rather than genuine usage surges. Verify if the system distinguishes between a broken sensor and a real overload.
*   **Edge-Case Weather Correlation:** Cross-reference your usage data with historical Chicago weather records for the coldest days on record. Manually construct test cases where high usage coincides with -20°F temperatures. The system should be tuned to *allow* higher thresholds during these specific external conditions, not lock them out.
*   **Red-Team Prompts:** If your logic uses any generative components or complex rules, feed it inputs designed to trigger false positives (e.g., rapid on/off cycling simulating a faulty thermostat). Measure the false positive rate. If this rate is not zero for high-stakes lockouts, the threshold must be adjusted or the HITL gate made mandatory.

**4. Transparency & Governance**
*   **Model Card:** Draft a one-page "System Limitations" document for your internal team and city inspectors. Explicitly state: "This system uses automated detection but requires human confirmation for all heating lockouts to prevent safety risks." List known limitations (e.g., "Cannot distinguish between party heat and system overload without manager review").
*   **Owner Assignment:** Name one specific person responsible for reviewing every lockout alert during the winter storm season. An unassigned alert is a failed safety control.
*   **Legal Routing:** Whether this automated decision falls under specific Chicago housing regulations or requires formal tenant disclosure is a legal determination. Route this question to counsel immediately; do not assume compliance based on engineering logic alone.

**Stop Condition:** Do not enable automatic lockouts until you have run the synthetic spike tests and documented a 0% failure rate on *preventing* heat during simulated sensor errors. If you cannot measure this, mark the risk as **UNVERIFIED** and keep the HITL gate permanent.

*Note: This plan prioritizes resident safety over automation efficiency given the sub-zero stakes. The "Emergency Heat Request" button is your critical failure safety net.*
