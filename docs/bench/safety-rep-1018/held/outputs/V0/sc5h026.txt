**Validation Plan for Automated Power-Cut System**

**1. Harm Inventory & Scope**
Before testing, explicitly record the worst-case harm: unnecessary blackouts in vulnerable neighborhoods during heatwaves leading to health risks. Acknowledge that no test can certify the system as "safe" or "unbiased"; the goal is to measure residual risk and implement gates.

**2. Measurement Strategy (Local Simulation)**
Since budget and infrastructure are fixed, utilize existing local servers for targeted adversarial testing rather than broad simulation.
*   **Probe Set Construction:** Create a manual dataset of historical extreme weather events (heatwaves) paired with consumption data from specific demographic segments (e.g., elderly-heavy blocks, low-income areas).
*   **Control Cases:**
    *   *Positive Control:* Inputs known to trigger a cut due to genuine grid overload.
    *   *Negative Control:* Inputs from vulnerable blocks during high stress that should *not* trigger a cut if reserves exist.
*   **Execution:** Run these specific scenarios locally. Do not attempt to simulate the entire grid; focus strictly on the edge cases where prediction errors cause disproportionate harm.
*   **Metric & Threshold:** Measure the "False Positive Cut Rate" per neighborhood segment. Set a stop condition: If any vulnerable segment shows a false positive rate above 0% in these specific stress tests, the automated execution for that segment must be blocked.
*   **Result Reporting:** Report findings as "UNVERIFIED" for any segment not explicitly tested. Do not invent a global accuracy percentage.

**3. Managing Residual Risk (The Human Gate)**
Given the high-stakes and irreversible nature of cutting power during a heatwave:
*   **Mandatory Human-in-the-Loop:** You cannot fully automate this action by Monday. Implement a hard gate where any predicted cut affecting a residential block requires explicit human confirmation from an engineer before execution. The system should propose the action; a human must authorize it.
*   **Uncertainty Display:** If the model's confidence in a prediction is low, the system must surface this as a defined tier (e.g., "Unverified/Single-source") rather than a precise percentage, prompting mandatory human review.
*   **Override Mechanism:** Ensure operators can instantly override and reverse a cut decision if real-time data contradicts the prediction.

**4. Transparency Artifact**
Draft a one-page "System Limitation Card" for the regulators and internal team. This must include:
*   **Intended Use:** Supply-demand balancing during extreme events.
*   **Known Limitations:** Explicitly state which neighborhoods or weather conditions were *not* covered in the local stress tests.
*   **Measured Results:** List the specific scenarios run and the observed false positive rates.
*   **Owner:** Name the specific engineer responsible for monitoring the system during the initial rollout.

**5. Regulatory & Legal Routing**
Do not assert whether this system meets specific legal disclosure duties or regulated decision standards. Note that the determination of whether automated blackouts constitute a regulated consequential decision requiring specific legal disclosures is a matter for counsel. Route this question immediately to your legal team while proceeding with the technical controls above.

**Deliverable Status:** This plan provides a runnable stress-test framework and a mandatory human gate to prevent irreversible harm within your current constraints.

*Note: This plan identifies risks and controls but does not certify the system as safe; the final activation decision rests with GreenGrid leadership.*
*Ensure the "System Limitation Card" is versioned and updated if the model or data changes post-launch.*
