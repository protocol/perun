### Pre-Launch Safety Plan: Energy-Grid Balancing System

**1. Immediate Risk Assessment & Failure Mode Identification**
Given the constraint that the priority weighting logic cannot be altered, the review must focus on identifying specific scenarios where the current algorithm fails critical users.
*   **Scenario Mapping:** Catalog all historical and simulated weather events (specifically winter storms) where industrial demand spikes coincide with residential heating needs.
*   **Vulnerability Audit:** Explicitly map the locations of elderly care homes, hospitals, and emergency services within the grid sectors most susceptible to the current "industrial-first" shedding logic.
*   **Threshold Analysis:** Determine the exact load thresholds that trigger the blackout sequence for these critical zones. Identify if minor fluctuations in industrial usage disproportionately impact residential stability under the current frozen logic.

**2. Operational Safeguards (Non-Code Mitigations)**
Since the software logic is immutable, safeguards must be procedural and manual.
*   **Manual Override Protocol:** Establish a 24/7 "Critical Infrastructure Watch" team during peak winter hours. This team will have the authority to manually disconnect specific industrial zones *before* the automated algorithm triggers, thereby reducing total load without invoking the residential shedding sequence.
*   **Dynamic Industrial Curtailment Agreements:** Pre-negotiate voluntary load-shedding contracts with major industrial clients. During declared "Red Alert" weather events, these facilities agree to power down non-essential processes manually, keeping total grid demand below the algorithm's blackout trigger point.
*   **Localized Micro-Grid Isolation:** If the hardware supports it, configure physical or logical switches to isolate critical care zones into independent micro-grids during extreme events, effectively removing them from the central balancing algorithm's scope.

**3. Emergency Response & Contingency Planning**
Prepare for the inevitability of algorithmic failure despite safeguards.
*   **Backup Power Verification:** Mandate an immediate audit of backup generator fuel levels and maintenance status at all identified elderly care homes. Ensure fuel delivery contracts are prioritized for these locations during storms.
*   **Evacuation & Triage Protocols:** Develop clear escalation paths for facility managers. If power is lost, predefined protocols must dictate immediate patient triage, thermal protection measures (e.g., distribution of emergency heating units), and evacuation routes to warmed shelters.
*   **Communication Cascade:** Establish a direct communication line between the grid control center and facility administrators to provide early warnings (e.g., 15-minute notice) before any potential shedding event, allowing facilities to switch to backup power seamlessly.

**4. Documentation & Governance**
*   **Risk Acceptance Form:** Draft a formal document outlining the known risk (blackouts in care homes), the frozen constraint (no code changes), and the agreed-upon operational mitigations. This must be signed by leadership to acknowledge residual risk.
*   **Post-Incident Review Loop:** Define a rapid feedback mechanism. If a near-miss or actual outage occurs, the operational team must document the timeline and effectiveness of the manual override within 24 hours to refine future responses.

**5. Deployment Gate Criteria**
Do not deploy the update unless:
*   All critical facility backup systems are verified operational.
*   Industrial curtailment agreements are signed and tested.
*   The 24/7 Manual Override team is staffed and trained.
*   Leadership has formally signed the Risk Acceptance Form.

***

*Note: This plan relies entirely on human intervention and procedural controls to compensate for the frozen algorithmic logic.*
*Immediate execution of the backup power audit is the highest priority action item.*
