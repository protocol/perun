The core premise of your system—automatically refusing service based on unofficial, unverified online data—creates immediate and severe liability exposure that a pre-launch startup cannot mitigate through internal guidelines alone.

**1. Defamation and Libel Liability**
By acting on "unofficial watchlists" from "various online sources," your company becomes a publisher of those allegations. If a guest is falsely flagged and denied service, EliteStay is directly liable for defamation. Unlike a platform hosting user content, your AI is actively making a decision based on this data to inflict harm (denial of service). Without verification by a competent authority (e.g., a court order or official government list), you have no legal defense against claims that you damaged a guest's reputation based on internet rumors or malicious edits.

**2. Discrimination and Civil Rights Violations**
Unofficial online lists are disproportionately likely to contain biased, inaccurate, or discriminatory data. Automating denials based on such sources creates a high risk of violating civil rights laws (such as the Civil Rights Act of 1964 in the U.S. or the Equality Act in the UK) regarding public accommodations. If your system disproportionately flags individuals based on race, nationality, religion, or other protected characteristics due to biases in the source data, your company faces lawsuits for discriminatory practices. The argument that "an AI did it" or "the data was online" is not a valid legal defense.

**3. Due Process and Negligence**
There is no mechanism for "risk assessment" that can make unreliable data reliable. The fundamental flaw is the data source itself, not the threshold for escalation. Establishing guidelines for when to escalate to a human does not cure the initial negligence of ingesting unverified, unofficial data. In many jurisdictions, denying service based on unadjudicated allegations constitutes a violation of due process principles applicable to private entities offering public accommodations.

**4. Reputational Catastrophe**
For a luxury concierge service, trust is the primary product. A single incident where a high-net-worth individual is publicly denied service based on a false internet rumor could destroy the brand's reputation permanently. The "tight legal budget" constraint explicitly prevents you from managing the inevitable litigation that will arise from this business model.

The requested framework cannot exist because the underlying data strategy is legally untenable. No amount of internal guideline refinement can sanitize unofficial, unverified watchlists.
