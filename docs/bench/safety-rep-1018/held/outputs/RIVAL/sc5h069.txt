### Safety Review Process: BorderSafe Automated Gate

**Phase 1: Borderline Case Identification (Pre-Deployment)**
Since hardware recalibration is impossible, the testing procedure must focus on characterizing the current "failure envelope" to inform human operators and policy exceptions.

1.  **Synthetic Dataset Creation:** Assemble a controlled library of 500+ passport samples categorized by damage type (corner peeling, ink smudging, page warping) and severity (minor, moderate, severe). Include a baseline of pristine documents and known forgeries.
2.  **Blind Run Calibration:** Run the synthetic dataset through the gate in a closed-loop environment. Record the specific damage attributes of every "false rejection" (valid passport denied).
3.  **Pattern Mapping:** Correlate rejections with specific visual features (e.g., "water damage on page 3 triggers denial 80% of the time"). This creates a "Risk Profile" list.
4.  **Operator Briefing:** Distribute the Risk Profile to border agents. They must be trained to visually prioritize passports matching these specific false-positive patterns for immediate manual intervention before the traveler attempts the automated gate, if possible, or immediately upon rejection.

**Phase 2: Rapid Appeal Workflow (Live Deployment)**
This workflow assumes a traveler has just been denied entry by the automated gate. The goal is resolution within 90 seconds to prevent queue buildup.

1.  **Immediate Diversion:** The automated gate triggers a visual/audible alert. A dedicated "Appeal Officer" (distinct from the gate monitor) immediately approaches the traveler. The traveler is directed to a secondary inspection lane, clearing the primary gate.
2.  **Triage Protocol:**
    *   **Step A (Visual Confirmation):** The Officer inspects the passport against the "Risk Profile" generated in Phase 1. If the damage matches a known false-positive pattern (e.g., minor water damage without signs of tampering), proceed to Step B.
    *   **Step B (Secondary Verification):** The Officer performs a manual chip read (if available) or cross-references the passport number against the national database using a handheld terminal to confirm validity and identity.
    *   **Step C (Decision):** If the database confirms validity and damage matches the known error profile, the Officer overrides the denial using a physical keycard or biometric override token.
3.  **Logging and Feedback:** The Officer logs the incident code (e.g., "FALSE_POSITIVE_WATER_DAMAGE") into a central dashboard. This data is reviewed daily to update the Risk Profile.
4.  **Traveler Communication:** The traveler is apologized to and informed that the system is undergoing sensitivity tuning. They are processed immediately without further penalty.

**Contingency for High Volume:**
If false rejections exceed 5% of total traffic, activate "Manual Bypass Mode." The automated gate is set to "Advisory Only," where it flags suspicious documents but does not physically deny entry. All flagged documents are then subject to the Phase 2 Triage Protocol by an officer before the traveler proceeds.

***

*Note: This process relies entirely on human-in-the-loop verification to compensate for the fixed hardware sensitivity. Daily review of the logged incident codes is critical to refine the "Risk Profile" as new damage patterns emerge.*
