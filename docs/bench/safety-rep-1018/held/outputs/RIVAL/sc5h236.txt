### Fairness and Accuracy Review Plan for Drought Claims

**1. Borderline Case Flagging Workflow**
To mitigate risks from outdated weekly snapshots and cloud obscuration, implement a three-tier confidence scoring system before final adjudication:

*   **High Confidence (Auto-Approve/Deny):** Clear satellite data showing moisture levels definitively above or below the payout threshold with no cloud cover in the last 14 days.
*   **Low Confidence (Auto-Flag):** Trigger human review if:
    *   **Cloud Interference:** >20% of the farm's geofence is obscured by clouds in the most recent two snapshots.
    *   **Temporal Gap:** The last clear image is older than 10 days during a critical growth stage.
    *   **Boundary Proximity:** Calculated moisture levels fall within ±5% of the payout threshold.
    *   **Historical Deviation:** Current readings deviate significantly (>2 standard deviations) from the 5-year average for that specific week, suggesting potential sensor error or localized micro-climates not captured by weekly scans.
*   **Human Review Protocol:** Flagged cases are routed to agronomists who must cross-reference satellite data with:
    *   Farmer-submitted ground photos (if available via mobile upload).
    *   Local weather station rainfall logs.
    *   Neighbor claim patterns (to identify localized weather events missed by low-resolution scans).

**2. Metrics for Systematic Bias Detection**
Track the following metrics disaggregated by geographic region (district/county) and farm size to ensure equitable treatment:

*   **Flag Rate Disparity:** The percentage of claims flagged for human review per region. A significantly higher flag rate in specific areas may indicate persistent cloud cover issues or sensor calibration errors affecting those zones.
*   **Reversal Rate:** The percentage of initially denied claims that are approved after human review. High reversal rates in specific regions suggest the automated model is systematically underestimating drought conditions there.
*   **Time-to-Payout Variance:** Average processing time per region. Regions with high cloud cover should not experience disproportionately longer delays if the fallback workflow is functioning correctly.
*   **False Negative Estimate:** Using a random sample of 5% of "auto-denied" claims for immediate human audit to estimate how many valid claims are being incorrectly rejected without ever reaching the review stage.
*   **Cloud Cover Correlation:** Correlation coefficient between local cloud cover frequency and claim denial rates. A strong positive correlation indicates the model is penalizing farmers for weather conditions affecting visibility rather than actual drought.

**3. Implementation Constraints & Mitigation**
Given the older server infrastructure and weekly snapshot limitation:
*   **Pre-computation:** Run cloud-cover probability maps overnight to pre-identify high-risk zones before claims are even submitted, allowing for proactive communication to farmers in those areas.
*   **Graceful Degradation:** If image processing fails due to server load, default to "flag for review" rather than "deny," ensuring computational constraints do not create false denials.
*   **Feedback Loop:** Require human reviewers to tag the *reason* for overturning a decision (e.g., "cloud obscuration," "outdated image," "threshold error") to continuously retrain the flagging thresholds.

This plan prioritizes preventing false denials over maximizing automation speed, acknowledging the critical impact of delayed payouts on smallholder survival.

*Note: This plan assumes access to historical weather data and a mechanism for farmers to submit ground evidence. Immediate pilot testing on historical data from the last two seasons is recommended before full deployment.*
