### Pre-Launch Safety Checklist: AI Discharge Planner Pilot

**1. Legacy Data Integrity & Gap Analysis**
*   **Field-Level Completeness Audit:** Manually verify that 100% of pilot patient records in the legacy database contain the specific fields required for your algorithm (e.g., mobility score, cognitive status, caregiver availability). Flag any records with missing critical data as "Manual Review Required" rather than allowing the AI to infer values.
*   **Historical Bias Spot-Check:** Randomly sample 50 historical discharge cases from the legacy system where outcomes were known (successful vs. failed). Run these through the planner to see if the AI recommends care levels that contradict the known safe outcomes, specifically checking for systematic underestimation of needs for specific demographics (e.g., elderly women living alone).
*   **Resource Inventory Validation:** Physically confirm that the "local resource availability" data hardcoded or pulled from the legacy system matches reality *today*. Verify bed counts, home-visit nurse schedules, and equipment stock for the three specific clinics. Do not rely on database entries that may be months old.

**2. Algorithmic Guardrails & Logic Testing**
*   **The "Null Input" Test:** Feed the system incomplete or contradictory data (e.g., "high medical need" but "no local resources") to ensure it outputs a clear "Refer to Human" flag rather than forcing a potentially unsafe low-level recommendation.
*   **Boundary Condition Review:** Test patients at the exact threshold between care levels (e.g., needing 1 hour vs. 2 hours of care). Ensure the system does not flip-flop wildly based on minor data noise.
*   **Hard Constraint Enforcement:** Verify that the system strictly adheres to non-negotiable safety rules (e.g., "Patient with fall risk > X cannot be discharged to home without railing installation") regardless of resource scarcity. These must be hard-coded overrides, not probabilistic suggestions.

**3. Human-in-the-Loop Protocol Definition**
*   **Mandatory Co-Signature Rule:** Establish a policy that *no* AI-generated discharge plan is executed without a licensed clinician's digital signature. The interface must clearly display the AI's confidence score and the specific data points used for the recommendation.
*   **Escalation Pathway:** Define a clear, immediate workflow for when a clinician disagrees with the AI. This includes a "Report Error" button that logs the discrepancy without slowing down the discharge process.
*   **Fallback Procedure:** Create a non-digital backup protocol. If the legacy database is slow or the AI service hangs, clinicians must have a printed checklist of standard care levels to rely on so discharge is not delayed.

**4. Pilot-Specific Monitoring Plan**
*   **Daily Safety Huddle:** For the first two weeks of the pilot, mandate a 15-minute daily meeting between the AI team and lead nurses from the three clinics to review every discharge recommendation made that day.
*   **Adverse Event Trigger:** Define specific "stop conditions" for the pilot (e.g., one readmission within 48 hours linked to an AI recommendation) that would immediately pause the system for investigation.
*   **Informed Consent Script:** Prepare a simple explanation for patients and families stating that an AI tool is assisting the doctor, ensuring transparency about how the care level was determined.

**5. Legal & Ethical Compliance**
*   **Liability Clarification:** Ensure hospital administration has documented that the final decision authority rests solely with the human clinician, not the software.
*   **Data Privacy Check:** Confirm that no patient data leaves the clinic's local network or is stored in temporary caches outside the legacy system's security perimeter.

*Note: Given the lack of new integrations, all data validation must be performed via direct SQL queries or exported CSV analysis of the legacy systems.*
*Ensure all six team members are trained on the "Fallback Procedure" before day one of the pilot.*
