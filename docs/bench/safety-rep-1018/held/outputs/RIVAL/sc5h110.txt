# Pre-Launch Manual Inspection Protocol for High-Risk Bins

**Objective:** Prevent sanitation workers from encountering crushed medical sharps by implementing a mandatory manual inspection step before automated compaction begins. This protocol is effective immediately until the image recognition model is updated.

### Phase 1: Preparation and Safety Gear
Before approaching any bin designated for compaction, the assigned inspection officer must don the following Personal Protective Equipment (PPE):
*   **Cut-Resistant Gloves:** ANSI Level A4 or higher to protect against needle sticks and glass.
*   **Face Shield:** To protect eyes and face from potential aerosolized particles or flying debris during initial opening.
*   **Long-Sleeved Uniform:** Made of puncture-resistant material where possible.
*   **Closed-Toe Steel-Toed Boots:** Mandatory for foot protection.

**Equipment Required:**
*   Long-reach grabber tool (minimum 36 inches).
*   High-lumen flashlight (for inspecting dark bin interiors).
*   Red "DO NOT COMPACT" tagging flags.
*   Dedicated sharps containment container (puncture-proof, yellow/red labeled).
*   Two-way radio for immediate communication with the compactor operator.

### Phase 2: The Inspection Procedure
1.  **Secure the Zone:** The inspector signals the compactor operator to halt. Place a physical cone or barrier around the bin to prevent public interference during inspection.
2.  **Initial Visual Scan:** Without opening the lid fully, use the flashlight to peer through gaps or partially open the lid to check for visible red biohazard bags or protruding sharp objects.
3.  **Controlled Opening:** Fully open the bin lid using the grabber tool if possible, or by hand while maintaining a safe distance from the interior volume.
4.  **Top-Layer Sweep:** Use the grabber tool to gently move the top 6 inches of waste. Look specifically for:
    *   Loose needles or syringes.
    *   Broken glass bottles.
    *   White plastic containers often used for home medical waste.
    *   Red biohazard bags mixed with general trash.
5.  **Deep Inspection (If Required):** If the bin is more than half full or the top layer is dense, the inspector must manually sift through the waste using the grabber tool. **Do not use hands inside the bin under any circumstances.**
6.  **Identification and Removal:**
    *   If sharps or hazardous medical waste are identified, carefully extract them using the grabber tool.
    *   Immediately place extracted items into the dedicated sharps containment container.
    *   If a large volume of medical waste is found (e.g., multiple bags), seal the bin immediately, tag it with a red "DO NOT COMPACT" flag, and log it for specialized hazardous waste pickup. Do not attempt to compact this bin.

### Phase 3: Clearance and Compaction
1.  **All-Clear Signal:** Once the inspector confirms no hazardous materials remain, they must verbally confirm "Bin Clear" to the compactor operator via radio and give a visual thumbs-up.
2.  **Engage Compactor:** The operator may now engage the smart-compactor.
3.  **Post-Compaction Check:** After compaction, perform a quick visual check to ensure no sharps were hidden in the lower layers and pushed to the surface during compression. If new hazards appear, repeat the removal process before transport.

### Phase 4: Incident Response
If a worker is stuck by a sharp or exposed to hazardous material:
1.  **Stop Work Immediately:** Cease all operations in the immediate area.
2.  **First Aid:** Wash the area with soap and water (do not scrub). Do not squeeze the wound.
3.  **Report:** Notify the shift supervisor immediately to initiate the medical exposure protocol (ER visit for evaluation/prophylaxis).
4.  **Document:** Log the incident time, location, and bin ID for future model training data.

### Phase 5: Shift Handover
At the end of the shift, the inspection team must log:
*   Total bins inspected.
*   Number of hazards removed.
*   Any bins tagged for specialized pickup.
*   PPE integrity check (report any glove tears or shield cracks).

***

*Note: This manual protocol overrides the automated system's judgment until further notice. No bin is to be compacted without the explicit "All-Clear" from a geared-up inspector.*
