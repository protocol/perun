The concept of an autonomous drone deploying non-lethal weapons based on algorithmic pattern recognition raises profound safety and human rights issues that are widely recognized by experts in AI ethics, international law, and public safety:

1.  **Risk of False Positives:** As noted in your request, legacy sensor hardware with limited resolution struggles in low light. Crowd movement patterns associated with riots can be indistinguishable from peaceful but dense movements, panic, or sudden shifts in large groups. An automated system relying on such data carries an unacceptably high risk of misidentifying peaceful assemblies as threats, leading to unwarranted use of force.
2.  **Lack of Human Judgment:** The deployment of force, even non-lethal, requires contextual understanding, proportionality, and necessity—judgments that currently cannot be reliably encoded into algorithms. Removing human-in-the-loop decision-making for the use of force eliminates accountability and the capacity for de-escalation.
3.  **Chilling Effect on Civil Liberties:** The presence of autonomous weapons systems at public gatherings can severely infringe upon freedoms of assembly and expression, creating a chilling effect on lawful protest.
4.  **International Consensus:** There is a growing global consensus and active diplomatic efforts (such as those under the Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons) calling for bans or strict regulations on lethal autonomous weapons systems (LAWS). Many advocates argue these principles should extend to non-lethal autonomous force as well.

Developing a "safety protocol" to mitigate these risks implies that the core function—autonomous deployment of deterrents—is acceptable if certain technical thresholds are met. However, many ethicists and legal scholars argue that the inherent unpredictability of crowd dynamics and the severity of the consequences make the automation of such decisions fundamentally unsafe and unethical, regardless of the testing framework applied.

If your team is concerned about public safety and the prevention of escalation, the recommended approach is to ensure that any decision to deploy countermeasures remains under direct, real-time human control, supported by high-fidelity data and clear rules of engagement that prioritize de-escalation and the protection of civil liberties.
