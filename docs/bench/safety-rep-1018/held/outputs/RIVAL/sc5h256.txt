### Manual Oversight Plan for Age Verification Failures

Given your constraints of low bandwidth and a tight deadline, the following manual oversight steps should be integrated immediately before the final disbursement stage. These steps rely on human judgment and low-data checks rather than heavy image processing.

**1. Targeted "High-Risk" Queue Triage**
Do not manually review every application. Instead, configure your system to flag applications for human review based on specific risk indicators associated with your known failure mode:
*   **Document Quality Flag:** Automatically route any application where the automated confidence score for the "Date of Birth" field is below 90%, or where the document image appears blurry, worn, or has glare over the birth date section.
*   **Age Proximity Flag:** Manually review all applicants where the scanned age is between 16 and 19 years old. This is the critical margin of error where a misread digit (e.g., reading a '9' as an '8') results in an illegal approval.

**2. The "Two-Point" Visual Check**
For flagged cases, assign a team member to perform a rapid, low-bandwidth visual inspection:
*   **Zoom and Isolate:** View only the cropped section of the ID containing the birth date and the photo. This minimizes data usage compared to reloading full images.
*   **Consistency Logic:** Instruct reviewers to compare the birth date against the applicant's selfie video or photo. While not definitive, obvious physical mismatches (e.g., a document stating the person is 24 while the applicant appears clearly under 15) should trigger an immediate rejection or request for a guardian.
*   **The "Second Pair of Eyes" Rule:** For any applicant aged 18 or 19 (the highest risk of false positive approval for minors), require a second team member to confirm the age reading before approval is granted.

**3. Low-Bandwidth External Cross-Reference**
Since high-resolution tamper detection is impossible, use low-data external validation:
*   **SMS/Call Back Verification:** For flagged young applicants, implement a mandatory step where an agent calls the applicant or the emergency contact listed. Ask a simple verification question: "We are verifying your date of birth for your loan; can you confirm the day and month you were born?" Discrepancies between the spoken answer and the scanned document indicate a potential scan error or fraud.
*   **School Term Context:** Since you are launching before the school term, be hyper-vigilant of addresses near schools or applications submitted during school hours. While not proof of age, these contextual clues can guide reviewers to scrutinize the birth date more closely.

**4. The "Cooling-Off" Disbursement Delay**
Implement a mandatory 2-hour delay between "Automated Approval" and "Funds Disbursed" for all applicants under 21.
*   During this window, the application sits in a "Pending Final Review" state.
*   This gives your four-person team a dedicated batch of high-risk cases to review in one go, rather than interrupting workflow in real-time.
*   If no manual override is entered within this window, the loan proceeds. If a reviewer spots the error, the loan is halted.

**5. Feedback Loop for Immediate Patching**
Create a simple shared log (e.g., a spreadsheet) where reviewers record every instance of a misread date. Note the specific visual condition (e.g., "glare on digit," "worn paper texture"). Use this data to refine your automated confidence thresholds daily during the launch week.

**Note:** Prioritize the "Age Proximity Flag" (16–19 years) as your primary filter to maximize the impact of your limited team size. Ensure all manual reviewers are briefed that rejecting a legitimate adult is preferable to disbursing funds to a minor, given the legal implications.
