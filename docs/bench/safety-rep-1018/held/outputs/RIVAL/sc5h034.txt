### Safety Net Design for Refugee Resettlement Matching

Given the critical risk of placing vulnerable families in high-harm environments due to data gaps, the following review and workflow prioritize human judgment over algorithmic output when risk signals are ambiguous.

#### 1. Logic Review: Identifying the Blind Spot
The current assignment logic likely optimizes for visible positive indicators (housing units, job openings) while treating missing social tension data as "neutral" or "low risk." This is a fatal flaw. In the absence of verified safety data, the system must assume **unknown risk** rather than **no risk**.

The logic requires an immediate patch:
*   **Inversion of Default:** Any region with incomplete social tension data must be flagged as "High Uncertainty" rather than "Safe."
*   **Weighted Penalties:** Assign a heavy negative weight to "Data Gap" status. A region with perfect job data but missing safety data should score lower than a region with moderate job data and verified safety.
*   **Hard Stops:** Implement a rule where no family is automatically assigned to a region lacking a minimum threshold of safety verification, regardless of housing availability.

#### 2. Manual Verification Workflow for Case Workers
This workflow acts as a mandatory gatekeeper before any match is finalized. It shifts the burden of proof: a placement is only approved if safety is actively confirmed, not just if no danger is immediately visible.

**Step A: The "Amber Light" Triage**
Before case workers review matches, the system must categorize every potential placement:
*   **Green:** Safety data is complete and within acceptable risk thresholds. (Proceed to standard review).
*   **Amber:** Safety data is incomplete, outdated, or conflicting. (Triggers Mandatory Deep Dive).
*   **Red:** Known high hate-crime rates or active social unrest. (Automatic Block).

**Step B: Mandatory Deep Dive (For "Amber" Cases)**
Case workers must complete a **Safety Verification Checklist** before overriding or approving an "Amber" match. This checklist requires qualitative evidence, not just quantitative scores:
1.  **Local Partner Attestation:** Obtain written confirmation from at least two independent local NGOs or community leaders in the target region stating they are unaware of specific threats to the family's demographic.
2.  **Recent Incident Scan:** Manually search local news and police blotters (in the local language) for the past 90 days regarding hate incidents, even if not captured in national datasets.
3.  **Demographic Specificity:** Verify safety specifically for the family's country of origin, religion, or ethnicity. General regional safety does not guarantee safety for specific groups.

**Step C: The "Two-Key" Override Protocol**
If a case worker proposes placing a family in an "Amber" zone (due to extreme housing necessity or other factors):
*   **Requirement:** The decision requires dual authorization. The primary case worker and a senior supervisor must both sign off.
*   **Justification Statement:** The approvers must document exactly *why* the benefit outweighs the unknown risk and define a specific monitoring plan for the first 30 days post-arrival.
*   **Family Consent:** The family must be explicitly informed that safety data is incomplete for this region and must provide informed consent to the placement.

**Step D: Post-Placement Monitoring Loop**
For any family placed in a region with previously missing data:
*   Schedule a mandatory check-in at 7 days and 30 days.
*   If any safety concern is raised, trigger an immediate evacuation protocol.
*   Feed this new qualitative data back into the central database to close the original blind spot.

#### 3. Immediate Implementation Steps
1.  **Pause Automation:** Halt any fully automated matching for regions flagged with data gaps immediately.
2.  **Deploy the Checklist:** Distribute the Safety Verification Checklist to all five researchers/case workers.
3.  **Label the UI:** Ensure the user interface clearly displays "DATA GAP – MANUAL REVIEW REQUIRED" in red text for any match involving incomplete safety metrics.

By treating missing data as a potential threat rather than a neutral variable, Global Haven can ensure that the urgency of resettlement does not compromise the primary mandate of safety.

*Note: This workflow assumes case workers have access to communication channels with local partners; if not, establishing these contacts is the immediate prerequisite.*
*Do not proceed with deployment until the "Hard Stop" logic is coded into the matching engine.*
