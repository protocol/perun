# VoteEasy Pre-Launch Safety Review Checklist

## Core Scenarios to Test for Tap Discrimination

**Intentional Single Tap Validation**
- Standard deliberate tap on candidate selection (baseline)
- Deliberate tap followed by immediate hand withdrawal
- Deliberate tap with varying pressure levels (light to firm)
- Deliberate tap at different screen locations (edges, center, corners)

**Involuntary Movement Simulation**
- Rapid successive taps within 100ms, 200ms, 300ms intervals
- Sustained contact with micro-movements (tremor simulation)
- Accidental brush/swipe across candidate boundaries
- Multiple finger contacts occurring simultaneously
- Tap followed by involuntary jerk causing second contact

**Edge Cases for Accessibility**
- User with limited mobility making slow, deliberate selections
- User requiring extended contact time before registration
- Sequential selections across multiple races on same ballot
- Correction attempts (user trying to change selection)
- Fatigue-induced movement degradation over extended voting session

**System Response Validation**
- Visual confirmation timing (does feedback appear before registration?)
- Auditory confirmation synchronization
- Ability to review and confirm before final submission
- Clear error messaging when double-contact is detected
- Recovery path when accidental double-click is prevented

## Low-Resource Validation Strategies

**Internal Team Simulation Protocol**
1. **Controlled tremor simulation**: Team members hold lightweight objects (phone, water bottle) for 2 minutes to induce mild fatigue tremors, then perform test selections
2. **Non-dominant hand testing**: Use non-dominant hand for all interactions to simulate reduced motor control
3. **Distraction protocol**: Perform selections while counting backward or reciting text to simulate cognitive load affecting motor control
4. **Time pressure simulation**: Add mild time constraints to induce stress-related movement variations

**Proxy User Recruitment**
- Partner with local senior centers (often have members with tremors)
- Contact university occupational therapy programs (students need clinical hours)
- Reach out to Parkinson's support groups (many welcome technology feedback)
- Engage with veterans' organizations (high prevalence of movement disorders)
- Test with elderly community members at libraries or community centers

**Technical Validation Without Large Groups**
- **Video analysis**: Record test sessions at 60+ fps to manually verify tap timing and distinguish intentional vs. accidental contacts
- **Touch data logging**: Capture raw touch coordinates, pressure, duration, and inter-tap intervals for pattern analysis
- **Threshold iteration**: Test multiple timing windows (150ms, 200ms, 250ms, 300ms) with small groups to find optimal balance
- **A/B comparison**: Have same users test both current and modified versions to measure improvement

**Success Metrics for Small Samples**
- Zero false negatives (intentional taps must always register)
- False positive rate below 2% for simulated tremor conditions
- User confidence score ≥4/5 after each selection
- Task completion time increase <20% compared to baseline
- Zero ballot invalidations in 50+ test cycles across all scenarios

**Documentation Requirements**
- Log all test conditions, participant demographics (age, known conditions), and environmental factors
- Capture video evidence of edge cases both passing and failing
- Document threshold decisions with supporting data points
- Create clear handoff notes for post-election refinement based on real usage patterns

Focus initial testing on the 200-250ms window as starting point, then adjust based on your small-sample results. Prioritize preventing false negatives over false positives—users can retry an accidental prevention, but cannot recover a misrecorded vote.
