### Pre-Launch Safety Checklist: Pet Treat Toxicity Validation

Given the reliance on an open-source database with known gaps and the inability to license premium veterinary data before Monday, you must implement a rigid, manual-in-the-loop validation layer. Do not rely on automated checks alone. Execute the following protocol for every treat recommendation generated by your system prior to user exposure.

**1. Establish a "Hard Block" List (Immediate Action)**
Create a static, hardcoded lookup table within your application logic that overrides the open-source database for known high-risk toxins. This list must be immutable by the recommendation engine.
*   **Canine Toxins:** Xylitol, chocolate (theobromine), grapes/raisins, onions/garlic, macadamia nuts, alcohol, caffeine.
*   **Feline Toxins:** Lilies (all parts), onions/garlic, chocolate, alcohol, caffeine, raw dough, grapes/raisins.
*   **Action:** If an ingredient matches this list, the system must return "Unsafe" immediately, regardless of what the open-source database says.

**2. Breed and Age-Specific Constraint Mapping**
The open-source database likely lacks nuance for specific breeds or life stages. Manually curate a secondary override list for common high-risk profiles relevant to your Lima market.
*   **Pancreatitis-Prone Breeds:** (e.g., Miniature Schnauzers, Cocker Spaniels). Flag high-fat treats as "Caution" or "Unsafe" even if ingredients are technically non-toxic.
*   **Senior/Puppy Constraints:** Flag treats with hard textures (choking hazard) or high sodium content for animals under 1 year or over 10 years.
*   **Action:** Cross-reference the user's input (Breed/Age) against this manual constraint map before displaying any recommendation.

**3. The "Two-Source" Verification Rule**
Since your primary data source is flawed, no recommendation should go live without external corroboration.
*   **Protocol:** For any treat not on the "Hard Block" list, a team member must manually verify the ingredient list against at least two reputable, free veterinary resources (e.g., ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center list, Merck Veterinary Manual online).
*   **Pass Criteria:** The treat is only approved if *both* external sources confirm safety for the specific species. If sources conflict or data is missing, default to "Unsafe."

**4. Human-in-the-Loop Sampling (Pre-Launch)**
With a team of three, divide the validation workload by category until the launch.
*   **Assignment:** Assign one person to verify all "Dog Treats," one for "Cat Treats," and one for "Cross-Species/Other."
*   **Task:** Run a batch of 50 diverse test cases (different breeds, ages, and common local ingredients in Lima) through the system. Manually audit the output against the "Hard Block" and "Constraint Mapping."
*   **Stop Condition:** If any toxic item slips through in this sample, halt the launch and expand the "Hard Block" list immediately.

**5. User-Facing Disclaimer and Feedback Loop**
Acknowledge the limitation transparently to protect users and your team.
*   **Mandatory Disclaimer:** Append this text to every recommendation: *"Advice based on open-source data. Always consult a licensed veterinarian in Lima before introducing new foods, especially for pets with existing health conditions."*
*   **Reporting Mechanism:** Include a prominent "Report Unsafe Advice" button in the UI. If a user flags an error, manually review and update the "Hard Block" list within 1 hour.

**6. Final Go/No-Go Gate**
Before Monday morning:
*   Confirm the "Hard Block" list is active in the production environment.
*   Confirm the manual verification log shows 100% pass rate on the 50-case sample.
*   If any uncertainty remains regarding a specific ingredient, remove it from the suggestion pool entirely. It is safer to have no recommendation than a harmful one.

***

*Note: This checklist prioritizes safety over coverage; it is better to withhold a safe treat recommendation than to risk a toxic one.*
*Ensure your team documents every manual verification decision for liability protection.*
