### Risk Assessment Framework for Static-Data Career Coach

**1. Identify High-Stakes Scenarios (The "Devastation Triggers")**
Since your data is six months old, you must flag advice as high-risk when the user's context intersects with known economic lag indicators. Prioritize assessment in these three zones:
*   **The "Stable-to-Startup" Leap:** If the bot suggests leaving a tenured role for a startup based on pre-downturn hiring booms, the risk is critical. Outdated data often overvalues equity and underestimates current funding freezes.
*   **Industry-Specific Contractions:** If the user works in sectors prone to cyclical layoffs (e.g., tech, construction, export manufacturing) and the bot advises "aggressive job hunting" or "quitting to upskill," verify if those sectors are currently contracting.
*   **Debt-Heavy Profiles:** Any advice to reduce income or take a career break is catastrophic if the user has high fixed obligations (mortgage, family support). Static data cannot see sudden interest rate hikes or cost-of-living spikes in Seoul.

**2. The "Staleness Stress-Test" Protocol**
Before any beta launch, run your existing conversation flows against these manual checks:
*   **The Counterfactual Test:** Take a piece of advice the bot generates (e.g., "Quit now to negotiate higher pay"). Ask: "If the hiring market froze yesterday, would this advice ruin the user?" If yes, flag it.
*   **The "No-Exit" Simulation:** Assume the user follows the advice but cannot find a new role for 12 months. Does the bot's logic account for this runway? If the training data assumes a 3-month job search, the advice is unsafe.
*   **Seoul Context Check:** Ensure the bot isn't applying US/EU mobility patterns to the Korean market, where tenure is often more valued and severance structures differ.

### Practical Guardrails for Static Data Constraints

You do not need real-time feeds to implement safety; you need **uncertainty acknowledgments** and **hard constraints**.

**A. Implement "Conservative Bias" Logic**
Hard-code a rule: *When confidence in current market conditions is low (due to data age), default to retention.*
*   **Action:** If the user expresses dissatisfaction but holds a stable job, the bot must prioritize "internal mobility," "skill stacking," or "negotiating current role" over "resignation."
*   **Prompt Instruction:** "If the user considers quitting a stable job, you must first list three risks of leaving during uncertain economic times before offering any transition strategies."

**B. Mandatory Disclaimers & Time-Boxing**
Explicitly state the data limitation to manage user expectation and liability.
*   **Phrasing:** "My insights are based on trends up to [Date]. Given current economic volatility, please verify specific hiring numbers with real-time sources before making resignation decisions."
*   **The "Pause" Mechanism:** Program the bot to refuse immediate "quit" commands. Instead, it should suggest a "30-day observation period" where the user gathers real-time data before acting.

**C. The "Financial Runway" Gate**
Add a mandatory intake question: "How many months of living expenses do you have saved?"
*   **Logic:** If the answer is < 6 months, the bot is strictly forbidden from advising resignation or career breaks, regardless of what the static training data suggests about "following passion." It must pivot immediately to financial stabilization advice.

**D. Human-in-the-Loop Escalation**
For any conversation thread containing keywords like "resign," "quit," "bankruptcy," or "debt," the bot should output a standardized warning and suggest consulting a certified human career counselor or financial planner in Seoul, rather than providing a definitive strategic path.

By treating your static data as a "historical reference" rather than a "current signal," you can prevent the bot from confidently guiding users off a cliff it cannot see.

*Note: This framework relies on logical constraints you can implement immediately in your prompt layer or application logic without external APIs.*
