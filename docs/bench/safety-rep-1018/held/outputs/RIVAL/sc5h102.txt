**Pre-Launch Safety Review Plan: Income Irregularity Denial Risks**

**1. Data Gap Analysis & Synthetic Augmentation**
Since legacy systems only provide historical records, they likely lack recent gig-economy volatility patterns (e.g., platform algorithm changes, sudden market saturation).
*   **Action:** Do not rely solely on historical data. Create a "Synthetic Volatility Layer" by mathematically perturbing historical income streams to simulate current economic conditions (e.g., apply -20% to -40% random drops in specific months, introduce 3-week payment gaps).
*   **Goal:** Ensure the test set includes edge cases where annual income qualifies a family, but monthly snapshots suggest disqualification due to timing mismatches.

**2. Logic Stress-Testing for Irregular Pay**
Automated systems often fail when averaging methods clash with reporting periods.
*   **Action:** Manually calculate eligibility for 50 high-risk synthetic profiles using the *exact* written policy rules, then compare these against the system's output. Focus specifically on:
    *   **The "Cliff" Effect:** Users whose income fluctuates just above/below the threshold month-to-month but averages below it annually.
    *   **Lump-Sum Misclassification:** Ensure one-time gig bonuses or back-pay are not counted as recurring monthly income.
    *   **Reporting Lag:** Simulate scenarios where income is earned in Month A but reported/paid in Month B. Verify the system attributes income to the correct eligibility period.

**3. The "Human-in-the-Loop" Triage Protocol**
Given the high stakes for vulnerable families, automation should not have the final word on borderline cases.
*   **Action:** Establish a mandatory manual review queue for any application where:
    *   Income variance exceeds 15% month-over-month.
    *   The decision is a denial based on a single month's data rather than a rolling average.
    *   The applicant self-identifies as a gig worker with irregular hours.
*   **Requirement:** No denial letter is sent to these categories until a human caseworker validates the income calculation against uploaded bank statements or 1099 forms.

**4. Retrospective "Shadow" Validation**
Before the statewide rollout, prove the system's safety using known outcomes.
*   **Action:** Run the new checker against a blinded dataset of *previously approved* single parents from the last year.
*   **Success Metric:** The system must agree with the historical approval rate. If the new tool denies benefits to families who were correctly served under the old manual or semi-automated process, the logic is too rigid and requires immediate recalibration.

**5. Error Impact Assessment**
*   **Action:** Categorize potential errors by severity. A "false positive" (approving an ineligible user) is a financial leak; a "false negative" (denying an eligible user) is a humanitarian crisis.
*   **Policy Shift:** Explicitly tune the system's confidence thresholds to favor "uncertain -> manual review" over "uncertain -> deny." Document this bias toward preservation of benefits in the rollout justification.

**6. Feedback Loop Mechanism**
*   **Action:** Pre-build the appeal intake form. If a denial occurs, the mechanism to upload proof of irregular income must be immediate and frictionless. Track the "overturn rate" daily during the first month of rollout; a spike indicates a systemic logic failure requiring a pause in automation.

**Note:** This plan prioritizes preventing harm to eligible families over operational speed.
**Note:** Immediate creation of synthetic volatile data is critical since historical records are insufficient.
