### Plan to Identify and Route Historic Property Claims

**Objective:** Prevent systematic underpayment by flagging potential historic properties for manual review without incurring new data licensing costs.

**Phase 1: Define Heuristic Indicators (Days 1–2)**
Since updated cost databases are unavailable, rely on existing policy data to create a "Historic Probability Score." Collaborate with senior adjusters to define low-cost proxy variables already present in your system:
*   **Year Built:** Set a dynamic threshold (e.g., pre-1940 or pre-1950) based on local architectural eras.
*   **Keywords in Property Description:** Scan for terms like "Victorian," "Craftsman," "Colonial," "original hardwood," "plaster," "slate," or "masonry."
*   **Location Data:** Cross-reference zip codes or specific neighborhoods known for historic districts (often public knowledge or already mapped in your GIS tools).
*   **Square Footage vs. Room Count:** Older homes often have different room-to-square-footage ratios compared to modern builds; flag outliers.
*   **Previous Claim Notes:** Search historical text fields for adjuster comments mentioning "custom," "antique," or "difficult to source materials."

**Phase 2: Implement a Triage Logic Layer (Days 3–5)**
Develop a lightweight script or database query that runs against the current UAT environment. This logic should not alter the valuation engine but rather act as a pre-processor:
1.  **Ingest** the pending claim queue.
2.  **Score** each claim based on the presence of the indicators defined in Phase 1. Assign higher weights to specific combinations (e.g., Year Built < 1920 + Keyword "Plaster").
3.  **Thresholding:** Establish a conservative cutoff score. Any claim exceeding this score is automatically flagged.
    *   *Strategy:* Prioritize false positives (sending modern homes to humans) over false negatives (missing a historic home) to ensure no underpayment occurs during this interim phase.

**Phase 3: Routing and Workflow Integration (Days 6–8)**
Modify the UAT workflow to handle flagged claims:
*   **Automatic Reassignment:** Configure the system to route flagged claims to a dedicated "Special Review" queue instead of the automated calculation path.
*   **Human-in-the-Loop:** Assign your most experienced adjusters to this queue. Their task is not to re-calculate from scratch immediately, but to verify the "historic" status.
*   **Manual Override Protocol:** If confirmed as historic, the adjuster applies a manual contingency factor or uses a separate, simplified lookup table for common historic materials (created internally from general knowledge) to adjust the final estimate before approval.

**Phase 4: Validation and Feedback Loop (Days 9–10)**
*   **Sample Audit:** Randomly select 10% of the *non-flagged* claims for manual spot-checking to ensure the heuristic thresholds aren't missing unique historic properties that lack standard keywords.
*   **Calibration:** After the first 50 manual reviews, refine the scoring weights. If too many modern homes are being flagged, tighten the year-built threshold. If historic homes are slipping through, broaden the keyword list.

**Long-Term Data Collection**
While operating this manual filter, instruct adjusters to tag confirmed historic claims with a specific code. This builds an internal proprietary dataset of "confirmed historic" vs. "modern" claims, which will be valuable for training a more precise model or justifying the ROI of a future paid database license.

**Note:** This approach relies entirely on data you already possess and requires only logic changes, not new vendor contracts.
Ensure legal/compliance reviews the "manual contingency factor" process to maintain regulatory adherence during the interim period.
