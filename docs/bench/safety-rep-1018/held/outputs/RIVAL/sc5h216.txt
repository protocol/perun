# Pre-Launch Risk Communication & Verification Plan: FireLine Analytics

**To:** Incident Commanders and Ground Firefighting Crews
**From:** FireLine Analytics System Integration Team
**Subject:** Critical Operational Advisory: Image Processing Latency and Verification Protocols

### 1. The Latency Constraint
The FireLine wildfire-perimeter mapper provides near-real-time labeling of active fire lines and contained areas using satellite imagery. **Operators must be aware that the current image processing pipeline has a fixed latency of fifteen (15) minutes.**

This means the fire perimeter displayed on your device reflects the state of the fire as it existed 15 minutes ago, not its current location. In rapidly spreading fire fronts, the actual fire line may have advanced significantly beyond the labeled boundary during this window. **Do not treat the displayed perimeter as a real-time safety boundary.**

### 2. Communication Protocol
To ensure this risk is understood and mitigated in the field, the following communication measures are implemented immediately:

*   **Visual Warning Overlay:** A persistent, high-contrast banner will appear at the top of the FireLine interface stating: *"WARNING: DATA IS 15 MINUTES OLD. FIRE MAY HAVE SPREAD BEYOND SHOWN PERIMETER."* This banner cannot be dismissed during active sessions.
*   **Color-Coded Uncertainty Zones:** Areas within 500 meters of the labeled active fire line will be shaded with a pulsing amber overlay, visually representing the "uncertainty zone" where the fire could currently exist based on maximum observed spread rates.
*   **Briefing Integration:** Prior to rollout, FireLine Analytics representatives will brief Incident Commanders on this limitation. A one-page "Latency Fact Sheet" will be included in all pre-season operational packets.

### 3. Mandatory Manual Verification Steps
Due to the 15-minute latency, automated data **must** be corroborated by human observation before tactical decisions are made. Crews must adhere to the following verification protocol:

1.  **Triangulate with Direct Observation:** Before deploying crews near a labeled "contained" line or advancing toward a labeled "active" line, spotters must visually confirm the fire's current position using binoculars, thermal imaging cameras, or drone feeds if available.
2.  **Establish Buffer Zones:** Treat the labeled active fire line as a minimum baseline. Establish a safety buffer of at least 1 kilometer (or greater, depending on terrain and wind) beyond the displayed perimeter before positioning personnel or equipment.
3.  **Cross-Reference Ground Reports:** Correlate the satellite data with real-time radio reports from ground teams already in the sector. If ground reports indicate fire activity outside the labeled perimeter, prioritize the ground report over the satellite map.
4.  **Time-Stamp Checks:** Always note the timestamp on the image layer. If the image is older than 20 minutes due to transmission delays, consider the data stale and rely exclusively on direct observation and ground intelligence.

### 4. Operational Directive
The FireLine mapper is a **strategic planning aid**, not a **tactical safety sensor**. It is designed to help identify general trends and large-scale containment status, not to guide immediate movement in the hot zone. No firefighter should cross a fire line or enter a burn area based solely on the FireLine display without independent visual confirmation.

Your safety depends on recognizing that the map shows where the fire *was*, not where it *is*.

***

*This plan assumes the 15-minute latency is a hard technical constraint of the current satellite downlink and processing architecture.*
*Verification distances (e.g., 1km buffer) should be adjusted by Incident Command based on specific fuel loads and wind conditions.*
