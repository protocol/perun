### Validation and Override Strategy for Fuel Load Estimation

**1. Targeted Ground-Truthing Protocol**
Given the constraint of low-resolution imagery, ground validation must focus exclusively on "high-risk ambiguity zones" rather than random sampling. Deploy field teams to areas where the model outputs a "moderate" fuel load but historical data indicates high fire severity.

*   **Stratified Site Selection:** Identify 20–30 pilot plots where satellite pixel homogeneity is low (e.g., mixed shrub/tree edges) or where spectral signatures of dry vegetation overlap with non-flammable surfaces (e.g., dry soil vs. cured grass).
*   **Standardized Metrics:** At each site, measure Actual Fuel Load (tons/acre) using standard line-intercept or quadrat methods. Record specific vegetation moisture content and canopy closure, as these are often obscured in low-res data.
*   **Bias Quantification:** Compare field measurements against model estimates to calculate a systematic bias factor. If the model consistently undercounts by 15% in shrub-dominated zones, apply this correction factor globally to similar pixel classifications before finalizing priority lists.

**2. Manual Override Procedures for Borderline Confidence**
Establish a "Human-in-the-Loop" review tier for any zone where the model's confidence score falls between 40% and 60%, or where the estimated fuel load is near the threshold for immediate clearing contracts.

*   **The "Second-Look" Workflow:**
    1.  **Flagging:** Automatically route borderline zones to a senior analyst dashboard.
    2.  **Contextual Enrichment:** Analysts must cross-reference the low-res estimate with auxiliary free data sources not fully ingested by the model, such as recent local weather station dryness indices, topographic aspect (south-facing slopes dry faster), and recent land-use reports from local fire departments.
    3.  **Conservative Escalation:** If auxiliary data suggests heightened risk, the protocol must default to "Over-estimate." Mark the zone for clearing unless there is definitive evidence of low fuel. It is safer to clear a low-risk area than miss a high-risk one.
    4.  **Documentation:** Every manual override requires a brief justification tag (e.g., "Local drought report," "Steep south slope") to build a dataset for future model retraining.

**3. Community Verification Loop**
Leverage the end users (rural homeowners and fire departments) as distributed sensors before the dry season begins.

*   **Reporting Mechanism:** Provide a simple interface for local fire chiefs to flag discrepancies in the draft priority lists. If a chief identifies a dense, un-cleared thicket that the model rated as "low priority," treat this as an immediate high-confidence ground truth.
*   **Rapid Response Update:** Incorporate these verified flags into the final deployment list within 48 hours. This creates a feedback loop that compensates for satellite resolution limits using local human observation.

**4. Deployment Safeguard**
Until the model is proven robust against low-resolution artifacts, attach a mandatory disclaimer to all prioritization lists: *"These estimates are derived from public satellite data and may undercount fuel loads in complex terrain. Local verification is required before finalizing defense plans."* This shifts the operational mindset from "automated certainty" to "decision support," ensuring human judgment remains the final authority on life-safety issues.

***

*Note: This strategy prioritizes safety over efficiency, accepting some false positives (unnecessary clearing) to prevent false negatives (missed hazards).*
*Ensure all field teams are briefed on safety protocols before entering high-risk vegetation zones for validation.*
