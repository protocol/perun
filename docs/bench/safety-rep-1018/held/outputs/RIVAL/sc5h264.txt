**Risk Mitigation Strategy: "Dispute-Ready Drafts" Model**

To meet your regulatory deadline without integrating new verification services or delaying launch, you must immediately pivot the tool's function from **automated execution** to **automated drafting**. The current behavior of automatically deleting entries is unacceptable given the risk of removing accurate data. The following strategy isolates the risk while maintaining your timeline:

**1. Disable Automatic Deletion (Immediate Code Change)**
Modify the tool's final step so it no longer sends deletion commands to credit bureaus or databases. Instead, when the tool identifies a negative entry for removal, it must halt and generate a "Dispute Package." This package includes:
*   The specific entry flagged.
*   A pre-written dispute letter template citing the alleged error.
*   A checklist of evidence the user *should* attach (e.g., payment receipts, bank statements).
*   A prominent, mandatory warning: *"Review this entry carefully. Disputing accurate information can result in the re-insertion of the item and potential flags on your credit file for 'frivolous dispute'."*

**2. Implement a Mandatory Human-in-the-Loop Gate**
Since you have a team of five, utilize this human capital as the verification layer that the budget cannot buy in software.
*   **Workflow:** The tool generates the draft → The user reviews it → The user submits it to your team (via a simple form or email) → A team member performs a quick sanity check → The team member manually files the dispute if it looks legitimate.
*   **Triage Protocol:** If an entry looks suspiciously like accurate data (e.g., recent activity, consistent reporting across bureaus), the team member flags it for "Enhanced Review" and contacts the user for proof before filing. This shifts the liability of verification from the algorithm to your staff, who can exercise judgment the code cannot.

**3. Shift Liability via Explicit User Attestation**
Update your terms of service and the tool's interface to require an explicit user attestation before any dispute package is finalized. The user must check a box stating: *"I confirm that I have reviewed my records and believe this entry is inaccurate. I understand that disputing accurate information may harm my credit standing."* This creates a legal and psychological barrier against users blindly accepting the tool's suggestions.

**4. Post-Launch Monitoring and "Stop-Switch"**
Establish a daily review cadence for the first two weeks. Track the ratio of disputes filed vs. disputes successful. If you notice a pattern where users are disputing items that are later verified as accurate by bureaus, pause the tool's generation capabilities immediately. Since the tool is now only drafting, pausing it stops the flow of bad disputes without breaking the underlying data infrastructure.

**5. Regulatory Compliance Framing**
Frame this approach to regulators not as a "buggy automation tool" but as a "consumer empowerment assistant." You are providing the *means* to dispute errors (which is a legal right) but refusing to automate the *act* of disputing without human oversight. This aligns with consumer protection principles by preventing the mass removal of accurate data while still helping users meet filing deadlines.

By converting the tool into a draft generator, you eliminate the risk of automated harm, utilize your existing team for quality control, and meet the launch deadline without new expenditures.

*Note: Ensure your team is trained to recognize common signs of accurate vs. inaccurate reporting to make the manual review efficient.*
*Note: Document every manual override and decision to create an audit trail for the upcoming regulatory review.*
