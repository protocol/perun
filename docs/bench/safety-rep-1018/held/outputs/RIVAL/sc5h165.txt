# ConnectCity Pre-Launch Privacy Transparency Strategy

To address the critical risk of collecting personal data without adequate transparency, while respecting your hardware constraints and tight deadline, implement the following three-tiered "Layered Notice" strategy immediately. This approach satisfies basic privacy expectations by prioritizing clarity and accessibility over exhaustive legal text on the device itself.

### 1. The "Just-in-Time" Iconographic Notice (On-Device)
Since screen resolution prevents full legal text, replace dense paragraphs with a standardized, high-contrast visual layer.
*   **Universal Icons:** Deploy universally recognized icons for data collection types (e.g., a silhouette for "Identity," a globe for "Location," a gear for "Usage Data"). These transcend language barriers better than text.
*   **The "One-Sentence" Rule:** Display only one core sentence in the user's detected browser language (or English as fallback): *"We collect [Icon] and [Icon] to provide free Wi-Fi. Tap for details."*
*   **Action-Oriented Design:** Make this screen the mandatory gateway. The "Connect" button remains inactive until the user acknowledges this summary. This ensures active consent rather than passive browsing.

### 2. The Digital Deep-Dive (Off-Device)
Move the burden of lengthy legal text off the kiosk screen and onto the user's own high-resolution device.
*   **The Pre-Connection Portal:** Before granting internet access, redirect users to a lightweight, mobile-optimized landing page hosted on the kiosk's local server.
*   **Dynamic Language Detection:** This page must automatically detect the user's browser language settings and serve the privacy policy in that language. Prepare translations for the top 10 languages relevant to your festival location (e.g., Spanish, Mandarin, Arabic, French) plus English.
*   **Human-Readable Summary:** Alongside the full legal text, include a "Plain English" (or plain language) summary using bullet points. Explicitly state: what is collected, why it is needed, how long it is kept, and who it is shared with.
*   **Mandatory Acknowledgement:** Require a checkbox click ("I have read and agree to the Privacy Policy") on this external page before the kiosk releases the authentication token for internet access.

### 3. Physical Contextual Signage (Ambient)
Compensate for the digital interface limitations with physical environmental cues.
*   **QR Code Stands:** Affix weather-proof stickers or small stands next to each kiosk containing a QR code labeled "Privacy Policy / Politique de Confidentialité / [Local Languages]." This allows users to read terms on their own phones before even touching the kiosk.
*   **Data Minimization Statement:** On the kiosk chassis, print a short, large-font statement: *"Your privacy matters. We collect minimal data only to keep this network safe. Full details available via QR code or upon connection."*

### Implementation Roadmap for Final Integration
1.  **Immediate Content Audit:** Draft the "One-Sentence" summary and the "Plain Language" digital summary today. Do not wait for the full legal review to finalize the short-form content; legal teams can review the summary faster than full contracts.
2.  **Rapid Translation:** Use a professional service for the top 5 critical languages immediately. For the remaining languages, use high-quality machine translation followed by a native speaker review (even if it's a team member or contractor) to ensure cultural nuance is correct.
3.  **Firmware Update:** Program the kiosk to serve the local landing page immediately upon connection attempt. Ensure the page loads instantly without needing external internet access (host it locally on the kiosk).
4.  **Festival Stress Test:** Before the festival opens, run a usability test with non-native speakers. Verify they understand the icons and can successfully navigate to the policy on their own phones.

By shifting the detailed disclosure to the user's personal device and using the kiosk screen strictly for high-level, visual transparency, you comply with the principle of informed consent without compromising your hardware limitations or missing your deployment deadline.

*Note: This strategy addresses transparency mechanics; ensure your actual data collection practices match the simplified promises made in the short-form notices.*
*Consult your legal counsel to validate that this layered approach meets specific local jurisdiction requirements for the festival location.*
