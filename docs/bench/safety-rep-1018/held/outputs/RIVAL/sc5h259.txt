**Pre-Launch Safety Plan: Autonomous Crowdfunding Validator**

**1. Define a "Safe Harbor" Exemption Logic**
Before the AI scans for scams, implement a deterministic pre-filter for medical emergencies. If a campaign contains specific high-confidence keywords (e.g., "hospital," "surgery," "medical bills") combined with verified document uploads (even if not fully read by AI yet), route it to a "High-Risk/High-Value" bucket. For these cases, the system must **never** autonomously block. Instead, the default action shifts from "Block if suspicious" to "Allow unless proven fraudulent with >99% certainty." This inverts the risk model for medical cases, prioritizing the prevention of false positives over the capture of every potential scam.

**2. Implement "Explainable Denial" Constraints**
Configure the model to output a structured decision reason for every flag. If the model cannot cite specific, objective contradictions (e.g., "Bank account location mismatches claimed hospital location" or "Story text matches known scam template X exactly"), the denial is invalid. Vague reasons like "tone seems urgent" or "language is unusual" must be programmatically rejected as insufficient grounds for an autonomous block. The system should only execute a block if the evidence meets a strict, predefined schema of fraud indicators, ensuring that linguistic quirks common in emergency situations do not trigger rejections.

**3. Synthetic Adversarial Testing (Red Teaming)**
Before launch, generate 50 synthetic campaign descriptions representing valid medical emergencies with varying degrees of urgency, broken grammar, and emotional distress. Run these against your validator. If *any* legitimate synthetic campaign is flagged, lower the sensitivity threshold or refine the "Safe Harbor" rules until the false positive rate on this specific set is 0%. Do not launch until the system consistently distinguishes between "urgent medical need" and "fraudulent urgency."

**4. The "Human-in-the-Loop" Fallback via User Appeal**
Since you cannot afford pre-screening moderators, shift the human oversight to a post-decision rapid appeal channel. If a campaign is blocked, the automated response must immediately provide a clear, one-click "Request Human Review" button. Commit to a Service Level Agreement (SLA) where these appeals are reviewed by your core team of five within 2 hours. This ensures that if the autonomous system fails, the delay in funding is measured in hours, not days, mitigating the harm to critical medical needs while maintaining your cost constraints.

**5. Confidence Threshold Tuning**
Set the autonomous blocking threshold extremely high (e.g., 95% confidence). Cases falling between 50% and 95% suspicion should not be blocked; they should be allowed to launch with a "Under Review" badge visible to donors, or simply allowed if the "Safe Harbor" logic applies. It is safer to let a potentially suspicious campaign raise funds while under observation than to definitively block a dying patient's family due to algorithmic uncertainty.

**Note:** This plan prioritizes preventing harm to vulnerable users over maximizing scam detection efficiency.
**Note:** Ensure your terms of service clearly state that autonomous decisions are subject to immediate human appeal.
