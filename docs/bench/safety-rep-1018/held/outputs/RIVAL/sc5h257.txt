### Internal Bias Review Strategy for Job-Interview Simulator

**1. Construct a "Bias Stress-Test" Script Library**
Since external diversity is unavailable, your internal team must artificially generate it through scripted personas. Create a matrix of 12–16 distinct interviewee profiles that systematically vary only by gender markers and behavioral traits while keeping qualifications identical.
*   **Variables:** Combine gender (Male/Female/Neutral) with traits (Assertive, Emotional, Passive, Aggressive).
*   **Inputs:** Prepare identical text transcripts and synthetic voice audio files for each profile. Ensure the voice files use neutral content but vary pitch/tone to simulate gender, or use text-to-speech engines with different gender settings if real actors are unavailable.
*   **Execution:** Have every team member run these exact scripts through the simulator. Do not let them improvise; the input must be controlled to isolate the algorithm's reaction to the gender/trait combination.

**2. Define Specific "Bias Indicators" for Auditing**
Before running tests, agree on what constitutes a biased output so the review is objective, not subjective. Create a checklist for the feedback generated by the system:
*   **Language Analysis:** Flag specific adjectives. Does the system call assertive women "aggressive" or "shrill" while calling assertive men "leadership material"? Does it label emotional men "unstable" while calling emotional women "empathetic"?
*   **Recommendation Disparity:** Check if the suggested improvements differ based on gender. For example, does it tell women to "smile more" or "soften tone" while telling men to "speak louder" for the same input performance?
*   **Scoring Variance:** Record the numerical scores (if any). A statistically significant gap between identical performances solely based on gender markers indicates a training data flaw.

**3. The "Blind" Review Protocol**
To prevent the reviewers' own biases from influencing the audit:
*   **Anonymize Runs:** Label test runs as "Candidate A, B, C" rather than "Male Assertive" or "Female Emotional."
*   **Independent Scoring:** Have two team members independently review the simulator's feedback against the "Bias Indicators" checklist without knowing which persona generated the result.
*   **Consensus Meeting:** Only reveal the persona details after the independent scoring is complete. Discuss discrepancies where the system treated identical inputs differently.

**4. Corrective Action Planning**
If biases are detected, prioritize fixes based on severity:
*   **Immediate Mitigation (Pre-Launch):** If specific keywords trigger biased responses, implement a "blocklist" or rule-based override to suppress those specific feedback strings temporarily.
*   **Data Re-weighting:** Document exactly which trait/gender combinations failed. This becomes your specification for re-training or fine-tuning the model. You will need to source or synthesize new training data that explicitly pairs "female + assertive" with positive outcomes to counteract the existing bias.
*   **Human-in-the-Loop:** Given the high risk and limited testing, consider launching with a mandatory human review step for the first month, where the AI's feedback is vetted by a staff member before being shown to the candidate.

**5. Documentation for Stakeholders**
Compile a report detailing the test matrix, the specific instances of biased output found, and the planned timeline for model retraining. This transparency is crucial for ethical compliance and managing user trust when you eventually expand testing.

*Note: This strategy relies entirely on controlled inputs to compensate for the lack of diverse human testers.*
*Ensure all synthetic voice data complies with your internal privacy policies even for staff trials.*
