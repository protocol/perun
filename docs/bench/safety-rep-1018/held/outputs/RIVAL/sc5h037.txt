### Mitigation Plan for Parking Enforcement Camera System

**1. Immediate Recognition Failure Review (Data-Only Approach)**
Since hardware cannot be updated remotely, the review must rely on backend data aggregation rather than edge-device diagnostics.
*   **Data Collection:** Aggregate all fine records from the central server where the violation reason is tagged as "Missing/Invalid Permit" but the vehicle class is standard. Cross-reference these with timestamps and locations known to have high volumes of disability permit holders (e.g., near hospitals, clinics, and accessible parking zones).
*   **Pattern Analysis:** Identify clusters of fines issued during specific lighting conditions or angles where temporary placards (often paper-based and hanging from rearview mirrors) are most likely to be obscured or misclassified as non-permits.
*   **Manual Sampling:** Randomly select 5% of fines issued in the last 30 days from high-risk zones for human review of stored snapshot evidence to establish a baseline false-positive rate specifically for temporary placards.

**2. Expedited Appeals Process Implementation**
Establish a "Fast-Track Disability Appeal" channel to minimize user burden while the hardware limitation persists.
*   **Dedicated Portal/Hotline:** Create a specific submission path (web form and phone line) labeled "Disability Placard Appeal."
*   **Reduced Burden of Proof:** Accept a clear photo of the valid temporary placard (showing the expiration date and issuing authority) and the fine notice as sufficient evidence. Do not require additional notarization or medical documentation for the initial reversal.
*   **Automatic Reversal SLA:** Guarantee a decision within 24 hours. If the uploaded placard appears valid visually, the fine is automatically voided, and the case is closed without further investigation.
*   **Proactive Notification:** If the backend analysis (Step 1) identifies a specific camera unit with a >15% error rate for potential placard misses, proactively suspend fines from that unit for 48 hours while a technician is dispatched, and notify drivers who received fines in that window to expect an automatic reversal.

**3. Manual Override Mechanism (Field Operations)**
Empower field enforcement officers to act as the "human-in-the-loop" since the cameras cannot be patched.
*   **Mobile Validator App:** Equip parking enforcement officers with a tablet/mobile app connected to the central database. When an officer encounters a vehicle with a temporary placard that the camera previously flagged (or if they are patrolling a high-error zone), they can scan the placard QR code (if available) or visually verify it against the database.
*   **Real-Time Whitelisting:** Upon successful visual verification by an officer, the vehicle's license plate is added to a local "do-not-fine" whitelist for that specific zone for the duration of the placard's validity or a set time window (e.g., 4 hours). This prevents the static camera from issuing repeated fines while the car remains parked.
*   **Retroactive Clearance:** If an officer verifies a placard on a car that has already been fined that day, the app should trigger an immediate automatic reversal of the active fine.

**4. Communication Strategy**
*   **Public Advisory:** Issue a clear public statement acknowledging the technical limitation with temporary placards. Explicitly state that valid placard holders will not be penalized and provide the direct link/number for the Fast-Track Appeal.
*   **Signage:** Place temporary notices in high-traffic restricted zones informing drivers of the appeals process specifically for disability permits.

**Note:** This plan prioritizes administrative and procedural controls to bypass the immutable hardware constraint.
**Note:** Field technician dispatch schedules should be adjusted to prioritize physical lens cleaning or angle adjustments on units identified in the Data Collection phase.
