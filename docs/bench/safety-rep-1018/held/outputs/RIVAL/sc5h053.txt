PayFast Solutions Biometric-Payroll Contingency Procedures

Given the constraints of legacy hardware and the specific risk of hand injuries affecting biometric scans, the following fallback mechanisms and procedures should be implemented immediately to ensure wage continuity. These procedures prioritize manual verification over automated failure to prevent financial distress.

1. Supervisor-Assisted Manual Override Protocol
Establish a strict "Two-Person Verification" rule for any failed scan. When a worker cannot clock in due to injury or scan error:
- The employee immediately notifies a designated shift supervisor.
- The supervisor manually logs the entry time in a secure, tamper-evident digital ledger or physical logbook.
- A second supervisor or HR representative must countersign or digitally approve this entry within 24 hours.
- This manual entry bypasses the biometric gate but flags the record for audit. The system must be configured to accept these manual timestamps as valid for payroll calculation, overriding the missing biometric data.

2. Temporary "Injury Exemption" Status
Create an immediate administrative process to flag employees with known temporary injuries:
- Upon reporting an injury, HR places the employee in a "Temporary Exemption" status in the payroll database.
- While in this status, the employee is authorized to use a secondary authentication method compatible with existing hardware, such as a PIN code entry on the keypad (if available) or a dedicated RFID badge swipe if the legacy terminals support it.
- If no secondary hardware method exists, the employee defaults to the Supervisor-Assisted Manual Override without needing to attempt a failed scan first, reducing frustration and queue times.

3. The "Presumption of Presence" Rule
Implement a policy stating that in the event of a system error or ambiguous scan failure where the employee claims they attempted to clock in, the benefit of the doubt goes to the employee.
- If the system logs a "failed attempt" timestamp, payroll processing must treat this as a successful clock-in pending verification, rather than a missed shift.
- Verification occurs post-shift via the supervisor log, ensuring the worker is paid on the regular cycle without waiting for an investigation to conclude.

4. Daily Reconciliation Window
Mandate a 30-minute window at the start and end of every shift dedicated to resolving clock-in discrepancies.
- Supervisors must review the "Failed Scan" report daily.
- Any employee listed must be physically located and their time corrected before the shift begins or ends.
- This prevents errors from accumulating until the monthly payroll run, allowing immediate correction of time records.

5. Audit Trail and Fraud Deterrence
To balance empathy with security, maintain a rigorous audit trail for all manual overrides:
- Every manual entry must include the reason code (e.g., "Bandage," "Scanner Error," "Cut").
- HR will perform random weekly audits of manual entries against security camera footage (if available) or witness statements to ensure the fallback is not being exploited.
- Clear communication must be sent to all staff that while the fallback protects injured workers, fraudulent claims of injury to bypass scanning will result in disciplinary action.

These steps ensure that physical limitations do not translate to financial penalties, maintaining trust during the pilot phase while adhering to hardware limitations.

Note: These procedures require immediate configuration of the payroll software to accept manual overrides as valid payment triggers. Ensure all supervisors are trained on the verification protocol before the pilot launch.
