### Immediate Action Plan for FrostGuard Shelter Locator

Given the three-week deadline and the critical safety risk of directing motorists to closed facilities, you must shift from passive data validation to active, multi-layered verification. Do not rely on a single data source. Implement the following "Defense-in-Depth" strategy immediately:

**1. Implement a "Stale-Data" Hard Stop**
Configure your system to automatically suppress any shelter record that has not been positively verified within the last 24 hours during a declared storm event. If a facility's status is unknown or the data feed is older than the threshold, the app must display "Status Unverified – Do Not Rely On" rather than directing the driver there. It is safer to show fewer options than to show dangerous ones.

**2. Establish a Human-in-the-Loop Verification Network**
Since automated feeds are failing, you must manually verify critical nodes.
*   **Partner Direct Lines:** Immediately contact the top 20 most frequently recommended shelters in your target regions. Establish a direct SMS or voice line with their on-site managers for real-time status updates.
*   **Crowdsourced "Last-Mile" Checks:** Integrate a simple feature for emergency responders (police, tow trucks) or verified volunteers to ping a shelter's status as "Open," "Full," or "Closed" via a lightweight web form. Weight these recent human reports higher than automated database feeds.

**3. Triangulate Data Sources**
Do not trust your primary feed alone. Cross-reference your data against:
*   State Department of Transportation (DOT) road closure and facility maps.
*   Local emergency management agency social media feeds (scrape for keywords like "closed," "evacuated," "power loss").
*   If two sources disagree, default to "Closed/Unverified."

**4. User-Side Safety Warnings**
Update the app interface immediately to include a prominent disclaimer on every shelter result: *"Facility status changes rapidly during blizzards. Call ahead if possible. If you arrive and the facility is closed, proceed to the next nearest option."* Provide one-tap dialing for local emergency services if the user reports a closed facility.

**5. The "Three-Week" Sprint Schedule**
*   **Days 1–3:** Hard-code the 24-hour stale-data filter and update user warnings. Manually call the top 50 high-traffic shelters to establish baseline status.
*   **Days 4–10:** Build the simple "Status Update" portal for emergency partners. Integrate DOT data feeds.
*   **Days 11–17:** Beta test with a small group of local first responders. Simulate failure scenarios (e.g., feed says open, human says closed) to ensure the app defaults to safety.
*   **Days 18–21:** Final load testing and deployment. Prepare a rapid-response protocol to push hotfixes if a specific region's data proves unreliable.

**Core Principle:** In life-safety systems, a false negative (failing to show an open shelter) is an inconvenience; a false positive (showing a closed shelter) is a life-threatening failure. Your system must be biased toward caution.
