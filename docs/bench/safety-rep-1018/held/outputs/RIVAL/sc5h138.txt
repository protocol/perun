# Fairness Review Process for UrbanClean Waste Management

## 1. Define Equity Metrics
Establish quantitative baselines to replace subjective observations. Calculate the **Service Equity Ratio**: the average time a bin remains >90% full before collection in low-income districts versus high-income districts. A ratio >1.0 indicates disparate impact. Track **Overflow Incidents per 1,000 Residents** normalized by population density to distinguish between volume issues and service frequency issues. Set a threshold where service delays cannot exceed 10% variance across any two demographic zones.

## 2. Data Stratification and Baseline Audit
Segment historical route data by census tract, linking each stop to demographic indicators (median income, population density, housing type). Run a retrospective analysis on the last 30 days of pilot data to quantify the current disparity. Visualize heat maps of "time-to-collection" overlaid with demographic boundaries to identify specific corridors where the algorithm systematically deprioritizes service. This establishes the "before" state for the council demonstration.

## 3. Simulation-Based Stress Testing
Since vehicle availability is fixed, use simulation to test routing logic without risking real-world service gaps. Create synthetic scenarios representing peak waste generation periods in high-density areas. Run the current algorithm against these scenarios to observe if it consistently sacrifices poorer neighborhoods to maintain efficiency metrics in wealthier ones. Introduce a "fairness constraint" into the simulation that forces a minimum service frequency for high-density zones, regardless of fill-rate predictions, and measure the resulting impact on total fleet mileage.

## 4. Algorithmic Logic Review
Inspect the objective function weighting. If the system optimizes purely for "fuel efficiency" or "tons collected per mile," it will naturally favor low-density, high-fill areas (wealthy neighborhoods) over high-density, variable-fill areas. Propose a multi-objective optimization model that weights **Equity** equally with **Efficiency**. Test a modified logic where route priority is determined by a composite score: `(Fill Rate × 0.6) + (Population Density × 0.4)`. This ensures high-density areas receive proactive service even if individual bins fill slower.

## 5. Human-in-the-Loop Override Protocol
Implement a manual override mechanism for the pilot phase. Allow dispatchers to flag specific zones for "mandatory coverage" that the algorithm cannot skip. Establish a daily review where route managers compare predicted vs. actual service times in vulnerable districts before finalizing truck dispatches. This creates a safety net while the automated logic is being recalibrated.

## 6. Demonstration Strategy for City Council
For Monday's demonstration, present a transparent "Equity Dashboard" showing real-time service metrics across all districts. Explicitly acknowledge the identified bias and demonstrate the proposed fairness constraint simulation. Show the council the trade-off curve: how much additional mileage is required to achieve 100% equity. This shifts the narrative from "system failure" to "active management of trade-offs," demonstrating responsible AI governance.

## 7. Continuous Monitoring Plan
Deploy automated alerts that trigger when the Service Equity Ratio exceeds the 10% threshold for three consecutive days. Mandate a weekly fairness audit during the pilot phase, requiring sign-off from both engineering and community liaison teams before any algorithm updates are pushed to production.

**Note:** Immediate manual adjustment of route weights is recommended prior to Monday's demo to prevent live overflow incidents. Long-term resolution requires retraining the optimization model with equity constraints hard-coded into the objective function.
