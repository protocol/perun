### Pre-Launch Safety Evaluation Strategy for Predictive Policing Map

Given the constraint of a fixed historical arrest dataset and the inability to add socioeconomic context before deployment, the evaluation strategy must focus on **output auditing**, **scenario stress-testing**, and **operational guardrails** rather than model retraining. The goal is to identify where the visualization creates unjustified stigma or misallocates resources due to data bias.

#### 1. Retrospective "Stigma Audit" (Data-to-Map Analysis)
Since the input data cannot change, you must rigorously map the output to known community assets to quantify potential collateral damage.
*   **Asset Overlay Analysis:** Manually overlay the generated "high-risk" heatmaps onto a static map of critical community infrastructure (schools, libraries, hospitals, major retail corridors, and religious centers).
*   **False Positive Identification:** Identify zones where high arrest density correlates with high foot traffic or benign community gathering spots rather than actual crime incidence. Flag any neighborhood where >20% of the highlighted area contains non-residential, high-value commercial or civic assets.
*   **Granularity Check:** Assess if the heatmap resolution is too coarse. If entire neighborhoods are highlighted due to incidents on a single block, the visualization is inherently prejudicial. Recommend dynamic zoom limits or blurring techniques that prevent "whole-zone" labeling unless crime density is uniformly high.

#### 2. Adversarial Scenario Stress-Testing
Simulate how different users (officers vs. public) might misinterpret the data under pressure.
*   **The "Self-Fulfilling Prophecy" Simulation:** Present the map to a control group of officers without context. Ask them to allocate patrols. If 100% of resources are directed to the highlighted zones regardless of real-time calls for service, the tool encourages over-policing. Define a rule: The map suggests *potential* risk but cannot override real-time dispatch priority.
*   **The "Investment Chill" Test:** Show the map to a panel of local business owners or community representatives (under NDA). Ask them to identify locations they would avoid investing in based solely on the visual. Document specific businesses or blocks that are unfairly stigmatized by the color coding.
*   **Temporal Bias Check:** Slice the historical data by time periods (e.g., pre-pandemic vs. post-pandemic, or specific months). If the "hotspots" shift drastically based on short-term policing surges rather than long-term trends, the model reflects enforcement bias, not community risk.

#### 3. Mitigation and Operational Guardrails
Implement strict non-technical controls to accompany the launch.
*   **Visual Design Constraints:**
    *   **Remove Binary Labels:** Do not use terms like "High Risk" or "Dangerous." Use neutral descriptors like "Historical Incident Density."
    *   **Opacity Limits:** Cap the maximum color intensity to prevent the visual implication that an area is "no-go."
    *   **Contextual Tooltips:** Require a click to view raw numbers. The default view should not display aggregate neighborhood scores.
*   **Usage Policy & Training:**
    *   **Prohibited Use Cases:** Explicitly ban the use of this map for stop-and-frisk quotas, business licensing decisions, or public shaming.
    *   **Mandatory Disclaimer:** Every screen must display: "This data reflects past arrest records, not current crime risk or individual behavior. Arrest data may contain historical enforcement biases."
    *   **Feedback Loop:** Establish an immediate channel for community leaders to report specific inaccuracies or harmful visualizations post-launch, with a commitment to review within 48 hours.

#### 4. Go/No-Go Criteria
Define clear thresholds for delaying the launch:
*   If the Stigma Audit reveals that major civic institutions (schools/hospitals) fall within the top 10% of "high-risk" zones solely due to proximity to unrelated incidents.
*   If the Stress-Test shows that officers ignore real-time data in favor of the heatmap in >30% of simulated scenarios.
*   If the visual design cannot be adjusted to prevent whole-neighborhood labeling without losing utility.

This strategy accepts the flawed data as a given but restricts how it is presented and consumed, shifting the burden of safety from the algorithm to the interface and the operator.

*Note: This plan assumes the "fixed dataset" constraint is absolute; if even minor metadata (like incident type) can be filtered, prioritize removing non-violent or low-level offenses from the heatmap calculation.*
