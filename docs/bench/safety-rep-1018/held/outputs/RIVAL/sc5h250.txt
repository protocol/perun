### Pre-Launch Cultural Audit Workflow

**Phase 1: Contextual Baseline Definition**
Before scanning, your team of four must define the specific "social shifts" relevant to your user base. Since the database is static, the risk lies in outdated norms. Create a "Sensitivity Charter" document listing:
*   **Protected Categories:** Specific demographics, regions, or social groups relevant to the languages taught.
*   **Outdated Terminology:** A list of phrases previously acceptable but now considered offensive or stereotypical.
*   **Contextual Nuances:** Scenarios where a phrase is acceptable in one dialect but offensive in another.
*   **Action:** Have all four team members sign off on this charter to ensure a unified grading standard.

**Phase 2: Structured Manual Triage (The "Four-Eyes" Method)**
Automated tools cannot reliably detect cultural nuance without current training data. Use a structured manual review process:
1.  **Data Segmentation:** Split the static example library into batches of 50–100 entries.
2.  **Dual Review:** Assign each batch to two reviewers (one linguist, one engineer). They must independently flag entries that violate the Sensitivity Charter.
3.  **Conflict Resolution:** If reviewers disagree on an entry, it moves to a "Tie-Breaker" session where the full team discusses the context. If consensus isn't reached, the entry is temporarily disabled pending external expert consultation.
4.  **Tagging System:** Instead of just deleting, tag entries as "Safe," "Context-Dependent," or "Retire." This preserves data for future re-evaluation if social norms shift again.

**Phase 3: Scenario Stress-Testing**
Do not just read sentences in isolation. Test them in the flow of the application:
*   **User Persona Simulation:** Have team members role-play as users from different backgrounds. Run the correction feature against sentences that intentionally trigger edge cases (e.g., slang, idioms, or politically charged topics).
*   **Correction Logic Check:** Verify that the "automatic correction" does not inadvertently "sanitize" a user's valid cultural expression into a stereotypical one. For example, ensuring a dialect-specific phrase isn't "corrected" to a dominant dialect in a way that implies the original was wrong.

**Phase 4: The "Kill Switch" Protocol**
Since you are close to launch, implement a safety net:
*   **Dynamic Disabling:** Ensure your engineering team can instantly disable the "cultural context" feature or specific lesson modules via a remote config flag without a full app store update.
*   **Feedback Loop:** Create a simple in-app mechanism for users to report "offensive or outdated" content. Route these reports directly to the linguist team for immediate review.

**Phase 5: Final Go/No-Go Gate**
Hold a final review meeting 48 hours before launch. The release proceeds only if:
*   100% of the database has been dual-reviewed.
*   All "Retire" tagged items are removed or hidden.
*   The "Kill Switch" has been tested and confirmed functional.
*   A plan is in place to review user feedback within 24 hours of launch.

This workflow prioritizes human judgment over automation, which is essential when dealing with evolving social norms and static data.

*Note: Given the two-week timeline, prioritize high-traffic lessons first if the full database cannot be reviewed in time.*
*Consider engaging one external cultural consultant for a rapid spot-check if internal consensus is difficult on sensitive topics.*
