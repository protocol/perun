# Pre-Launch Safety Review Plan: Credit-Score Simulator

## 1. Identified Harms from Inflated Scores
Given the reliance on self-reported income without real-time verification, the primary risks are:
*   **False Affordability:** Users overstate income, receive an inflated borrowing limit, and apply for loans they cannot service, leading to immediate financial distress or default.
*   **Regulatory & Reputational Risk:** Misleading users about creditworthiness may violate consumer protection norms in Poland/EU, damaging trust before launch.
*   **Behavioral Nudging:** An artificially high score may encourage users to seek larger loan amounts than necessary, increasing their debt burden.

## 2. Mitigation Steps (Resource-Constrained)
With only three engineers and no budget for external data integration, implement these low-cost, high-impact controls immediately:

### A. Input Validation & Sanity Checks (Engineering Effort: Low)
*   **Implement Hard Caps:** Set maximum allowable income inputs based on realistic national averages (e.g., cap at 5x the median Polish salary) to prevent absurd outliers.
*   **Logic Consistency Checks:** Cross-reference income against reported expenses or debt obligations entered by the user. If debt-to-income ratios exceed a safe threshold (e.g., 40%), flag the input as "Unrealistic" and refuse to generate a score.
*   **Required Justification:** For income entries above a certain threshold, require a text explanation or upload of a static document (even if not verified automatically, the friction reduces casual lying).

### B. Transparent Disclaimers & Output Framing (Engineering/Content Effort: Low)
*   **Prominent Warning Labels:** Display a bold, unavoidable notice on the results page: *"This score is an estimate based on unverified self-reported data. It does not guarantee loan approval or reflect your actual borrowing capacity."*
*   **Downward Bias Adjustment:** Instead of showing the maximum theoretical loan amount, display a "Conservative Estimate" range (e.g., 80% of the calculated value) to build a safety buffer against inflation.
*   **Contextual Education:** Add a tooltip or sidebar explaining *why* self-reported data is risky and encouraging users to verify figures with their actual bank statements before applying elsewhere.

### C. Process & Monitoring (Operational Effort: Low)
*   **"Soft Launch" Monitoring:** Release to a small beta group first. Manually review a sample of high-score outputs to identify patterns of obvious inflation.
*   **Feedback Loop:** Add a simple "Was this result accurate?" button. If users report discrepancies, log these cases to refine your sanity check thresholds post-launch.
*   **Partner Alignment:** Inform any potential lending partners that your simulator uses unverified data, ensuring they do not treat your score as a pre-approval signal.

## 3. Immediate Action Checklist (Next 48 Hours)
1.  **Code:** Implement income caps and debt-to-income logic checks.
2.  **Copy:** Draft and integrate the "Unverified Data" warning into the UI.
3.  **Config:** Adjust the scoring algorithm to output a conservative range rather than a precise maximum.
4.  **Review:** Have all three engineers manually test edge cases (e.g., entering €1M/month income) to ensure controls trigger correctly.

*Note: This plan prioritizes user safety and regulatory defensibility without requiring new data vendors. Post-launch, prioritize integrating open banking APIs as soon as funding allows.*
