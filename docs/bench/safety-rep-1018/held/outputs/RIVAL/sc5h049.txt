### Safety Assessment Plan: Smart-Trash Bin False Positive Mitigation

**Phase 1: Data Collection & Hypothesis Generation (Days 1–3)**
*   **Incident Aggregation:** Immediately collate all dispute tickets from the pilot program. Categorize them by specific product types (e.g., "yogurt cups," "black plastic trays," "multi-material packaging") and the specific error code generated by the camera system.
*   **Label Ambiguity Mapping:** Cross-reference disputed items with manufacturer labeling standards. Identify specific visual features causing confusion, such as misleading resin codes, ambiguous "recyclable" text on non-recyclable composites, or color similarities between distinct material types.
*   **Hypothesis Formulation:** Define specific failure modes. Example: "The system flags all black plastic as non-recyclable due to sensor limitations, regardless of the resin code printed on the bottom."

**Phase 2: Controlled Simulation & Validation (Days 4–7)**
*   **Test Bed Creation:** Assemble a physical library of the top 20 disputed items alongside known correct controls.
*   **Blind Testing:** Run these items through the bin cameras in a controlled environment without resident interaction. Record the system's classification confidence scores and final decisions.
*   **Root Cause Confirmation:** Verify if the false positives correlate strictly with the identified ambiguous labels or if lighting/angle also contributes. Isolate the software's decision boundary where the label ambiguity triggers the fine.

**Phase 3: Policy & Software Adjustment Proposal (Days 8–10)**
*   **Software Adjustment: "Ambiguity Hold" Logic:**
    *   Modify the classification algorithm to detect low-confidence scores or specific known ambiguous visual patterns.
    *   Instead of issuing an immediate fine, trigger a "Review Required" state. The bin accepts the item but flags it for remote human verification or logs it as "unverified" without penalizing the user.
    *   Implement a whitelist for specific product SKUs identified in Phase 1 that are known to be recyclable despite confusing labels.
*   **Policy Adjustment: The "Benefit of the Doubt" Protocol:**
    *   Suspend fines automatically for any item falling into the "Ambiguity Hold" category until the pilot ends.
    *   Issue a public notice stating that during the pilot, disputes regarding specific confusing labels will be resolved in favor of the resident.
    *   Create a rapid-response channel for residents to upload photos of fined items for immediate reversal if they fall outside the automated whitelist.

**Phase 4: Deployment & Monitoring (Days 11–14)**
*   **Over-the-Air (OTA) Update:** Deploy the "Ambiguity Hold" logic to all pilot bins.
*   **Real-time Dashboard:** Monitor the rate of "Review Required" flags. If the volume is manageable, proceed. If it overwhelms the system, narrow the scope to only the top 5 most frequent false positives.
*   **Final Report:** Document the specific labels causing issues to share with the city and manufacturers for long-term labeling standards, ensuring the full rollout does not inherit these flaws.

**Deliverable Outcome:** A targeted software patch that suspends fines for low-confidence/ambiguous cases and a temporary policy moratorium on penalties for identified problematic products, preventing financial hardship while preserving data collection for the pilot.

*Note: This plan assumes the camera software allows for confidence-threshold adjustments and state changes without hardware modification.*
*Immediate execution requires access to the raw dispute logs and the current classification model's confidence metrics.*
