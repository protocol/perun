### Critical Safety Review Plan: Asylum Translation App

**Immediate Recommendation: Do Not Deploy**
Given the potential for "immediate deportation" and "life-threatening situations," deploying an AI translation tool with known "significant errors" for legal asylum claims is ethically indefensible and legally hazardous. No testing framework can guarantee safety in high-stakes legal contexts without certified human validation. The following plan is designed to **quantify risk to justify delaying deployment**, not to clear the app for use by next Friday.

#### 1. Define Critical Failure Modes (The "Harm Catalog")
Before testing, explicitly define what constitutes a catastrophic error. Do not test for general fluency; test for legal survival.
*   **Negation Flips:** Translating "I was *not* persecuted" to "I was persecuted" (or vice versa).
*   **Temporal Distortions:** Confusing past events (evidence of past persecution) with future fears (basis for asylum).
*   **Identity/Relationship Errors:** Misidentifying family members, ages, or roles in alleged incidents.
*   **Legal Terminology Substitution:** Replacing specific legal grounds (e.g., "political opinion") with generic terms that disqualify the claim.
*   **Hallucinated Details:** Inventing events, dates, or locations not present in the source audio.

#### 2. Construct a "High-Risk" Synthetic Dataset
Since access to certified translators is limited, you must maximize the impact of every validation hour.
*   **Curate Edge Cases:** Create 50–100 audio clips in relevant dialects containing the specific failure modes above. Use native speakers from your team to record these, ensuring they include hesitations, emotional distress, and background noise typical of border crossings.
*   **Focus on "Untranslatables":** Include culture-specific concepts or idioms common in asylum narratives that often confuse machine translation.
*   **Stress Testing:** Record samples with varying audio quality (wind, crowd noise, low battery) to simulate real border conditions.

#### 3. The "Human-in-the-Loop" Validation Protocol
You cannot automate safety here. You must structure your limited expert resources for maximum triage efficiency.
*   **Blind Adversarial Review:** Have one native speaker record a claim and a different native speaker (ideally with legal training) transcribe/translate it blindly. Compare the AI output against both.
*   **The "Red Flag" Triage:** Instead of validating every word, instruct reviewers to scan *only* for the Critical Failure Modes defined in Step 1. If any single critical error is found in a sample, the entire session is marked "Unsafe."
*   **Confidence Thresholding:** If the app provides confidence scores, correlate them with errors. If high-confidence outputs still contain critical errors, the scoring mechanism is broken and must be disabled.

#### 4. Documentation of Limitations (The "Kill Switch")
If the app must exist in any form, its limitations must be hardcoded into the user experience and legal documentation.
*   **Explicit Disclaimers:** The interface must state in bold, clear language: *"This tool is not a legal interpreter. Errors can lead to deportation. Do not use for official asylum claims."*
*   **Output Watermarking:** Every translated text output should be visually stamped: "MACHINE TRANSLATION - UNVERIFIED - NOT FOR LEGAL USE."
*   **Failure Reporting:** Implement a one-tap mechanism for users or aid workers to flag incorrect translations, logging the audio and output for immediate audit.

#### 5. Go/No-Go Decision Matrix
Establish a hard metric for deployment.
*   **Zero-Tolerance Policy:** If *any* critical failure mode (negation flip, identity error) occurs in the high-risk dataset, the app is **failed**.
*   **Deployment Block:** If the error rate for critical modes exceeds 0%, deployment must be halted regardless of the policy deadline. The risk of deporting a legitimate refugee outweighs the risk of missing a policy window.

**Conclusion:**
Your primary duty is to prevent harm. If the testing reveals even a single instance where a user could be deported due to a translation error, the only safe path is to withhold the tool and advocate for an extension or alternative human-centered solutions.

***

*Note: This plan assumes you have the ethical authority to halt deployment; if external pressure forces release despite known risks, document this objection formally in writing to leadership immediately.*
*Real-world legal translation requires certified human interpreters; AI should currently only be used for informal, non-binding communication.*
