### Pre-Launch Simulation Plan for Alert Timing and Community Protocols

**1. Communication Failure Simulation Plan**
To verify alert timing within safe margins under solar-power constraints, model three specific latency scenarios based on your cloud-cover/storm data:
*   **Scenario A (Moderate Latency):** Simulate a 30–60% reduction in transmission speed. Measure the total time from "ground shift detected" to "alert received."
*   **Scenario B (Severe Latency):** Simulate an 80–90% reduction, mimicking heavy storm conditions. Determine if the system queues alerts correctly or drops them.
*   **Scenario C (Intermittent Blackout):** Simulate complete transmission failure for 15, 30, and 60-minute windows. Test if the system sends a "catch-up" burst once power returns and if this burst is still actionable.

**Verification Metric:** Compare the simulated "Time-to-Community" against your pre-defined "Safe Evacuation Window." If (Detection Time + Max Simulated Latency) < (Safe Evacuation Window), the timing is verified. If not, the threshold for triggering alerts must be lowered (i.e., trigger on smaller ground shifts earlier) to compensate for transmission delays.

**2. Community Notification Protocols for Delays**
Since automated signals may arrive late, the human protocol must bridge the gap between the physical event and the digital alert.
*   **Tiered Trigger System:** Do not rely solely on the automated alert. Establish a visual/auditory backup: if local community leaders observe heavy rain or minor soil slips (precursors), they initiate "Pre-Alert" protocols immediately, without waiting for the sensor network.
*   **"Late Alert" Procedure:** Train residents that an alert arriving during active heavy rain/storms indicates the event is already underway. The instruction must shift from "Prepare to evacuate" to "Execute immediate emergency movement" to the nearest designated high-ground assembly point.
*   **Drill Integration:** Conduct UAT drills specifically injecting "delayed message" cards. Have families react to a signal that arrives 20 minutes after the simulated ground shift. Debrief whether their reaction time was fast enough to overcome the latency.
*   **Redundant Signaling:** Equip village leads with satellite phones or long-range radio as a manual override. If the automated system is silent during visible danger signs, the human chain activates the siren/manual alert immediately.

**3. Actionable Instructions for Residents**
Distribute simple, pictorial guides stating:
*   **If the alert arrives on time:** Follow the standard evacuation route.
*   **If the alert arrives late (during rain/shaking):** Abandon standard routes if blocked; move vertically to higher ground immediately.
*   **If no alert arrives but danger signs exist (heavy rain, cracks, rumbling):** Trust your senses over the silence. Initiate self-evacuation and alert neighbors manually.

*Note: Ensure all simulation data is logged to refine the "Safe Evacuation Window" calculation before full deployment.*
*Note: Protocols must be translated into all local dialects and tested with non-technical community members.*
