### Incident Response Protocol: False Positive Mitigation

**1. Immediate Verification Window (0–5 Minutes)**
Upon receiving a "Leak Detected" signal triggering a main supply shutoff, the system must not remain in a hard-lock state. Since firmware cannot verify locally, the protocol shifts verification to the human-in-the-loop.
*   **Action:** The central dashboard flags the event as "Unverified Critical."
*   **Requirement:** Property management staff must physically inspect the specific unit cited by the sensor within 5 minutes.
*   **Constraint:** No remote "all-clear" reset is permitted without visual confirmation or a secondary manual check by a maintenance lead to prevent remote spoofing.

**2. Temporary Bypass Procedure**
If the initial inspection reveals no water (false positive):
*   **Action:** Staff engages a manual "Temporary Restoration" switch at the main valve control panel.
*   **Duration:** This restores water flow for a fixed window (e.g., 2 hours) to minimize resident impact while maintaining the alarm state on the dashboard.
*   **Monitoring:** During this window, the specific sensor is monitored at 15-minute intervals. If the signal persists without physical evidence, the system defaults to a "Maintenance Required" state rather than a full shutoff, isolating that specific zone if the hardware architecture allows, or logging for immediate technician dispatch.

**3. Escalation Matrix**
*   **Single Event:** Handled by on-site property manager.
*   **Recurring False Positives (Same Unit >2 in 24hrs):** Automatic dispatch of a hardware technician to disable that specific sensor node manually. The main valve remains open under "Maintenance Mode" until the sensor is replaced.
*   **System-Wide Anomaly:** If multiple units trigger simultaneously, assume network noise or power surge; do not shutoff. Initiate manual building sweep before any valve action.

### Communication Strategy for Property Managers

**Objective:** Empower managers to prioritize resident welfare (water access) while maintaining safety, shifting the narrative from "system failure" to "cautious verification."

**Key Message Pillars:**
1.  **"Safety First, Verification Second":** Explicitly state that the system is designed to be overly sensitive during the pilot phase. Emphasize that a false alarm is a feature of the current safety margin, not a defect.
2.  **Empowered Decision Making:** Provide a clear "Green Light" authority. Managers are authorized to restore water immediately after a 5-minute visual check confirms no leak. They do not need corporate approval to prevent resident deprivation.
3.  **Transparency with Residents:** Provide a script for managers: *"Our new safety system detected a potential issue and paused water as a precaution. Our team has verified there is no leak, and water is being restored now. We are currently calibrating the system to prevent future interruptions."*

**Operational Deliverables:**
*   **One-Page "Cheat Sheet":** A laminated card for every manager with the 5-minute checklist and the exact steps to engage the Temporary Restoration switch.
*   **Dedicated Pilot Hotline:** A direct line to the AquaGuard engineering team (available 24/7 during the 30-day pilot) for real-time support during any shutoff event.
*   **Incident Log Template:** A simplified form to record time of alarm, time of inspection, finding (Wet/Dry), and action taken. This data is critical for your post-pilot firmware updates.

By formalizing the "verify-then-restore" loop, you turn a hardware limitation into a managed operational procedure, ensuring resident needs are met while you collect the necessary field data to refine the sensor logic.

*Note: Ensure all manual bypass actions are logged automatically by the controller for audit purposes. Review local fire/safety codes to confirm manual override permissions for water supplies in senior living facilities.*
