### Pre-Launch Safety Audit Plan for MagmaWatch Sciences

Given the critical nature of automating evacuation orders and the identified gap in historical data for rare wind patterns, the following audit structure focuses on stress-testing the model's boundaries and establishing robust human-in-the-loop safeguards.

#### 1. Synthetic Weather Scenarios for Edge-Case Testing
To address the lack of historical data, you must generate synthetic inputs that represent physically plausible but statistically rare atmospheric conditions. These scenarios should specifically target the model's interpolation limits. Run the dispersion model against the following synthetic datasets:

*   **Rapid Wind Shear Reversal:** Simulate a sudden 180-degree wind shift at varying altitudes within a 15-minute window. This tests if the model correctly updates the lethal concentration zone when the plume direction contradicts the previous trend, preventing false security in areas that were previously safe.
*   **Thermal Inversion Trapping:** Create scenarios where a warm air layer traps cold, gas-laden air in valley floors despite moderate surface winds. This tests the model's ability to predict high ground-level concentrations even when horizontal dispersion appears adequate.
*   **Low-Velocity Turbulence:** Simulate near-calms (wind speeds <1 m/s) combined with high thermal turbulence from solar heating. This challenges the model to predict unpredictable, patchy gas distribution rather than a smooth plume, which is critical for determining "safe" vs. "unsafe" binary thresholds.
*   **Channeling and Acceleration:** Model wind forcing through narrow topographical gaps that accelerate flow unexpectedly. This tests if the model underestimates the reach of the gas cloud due to localized acceleration not present in broader weather station data.
*   **Multi-Source Interference:** If multiple vents are active, simulate conflicting plume interactions where gases from different sources merge or cancel out in complex ways, testing the model's aggregation logic.

For each scenario, define the expected physical outcome based on first-principles physics before running the model. Any significant deviation between the synthetic ground truth and the model's prediction indicates a failure mode that must be resolved before launch.

#### 2. Manual Oversight Triggers and Intervention Protocol
To ensure safety during active unrest, establish a "confidence gate" that automatically halts automated evacuation orders and routes decisions to your team of six experts.

*   **Confidence Threshold Definition:** Define a quantitative confidence score (e.g., 0.0 to 1.0) derived from the model's internal variance or ensemble disagreement. Set a hard trigger (e.g., confidence < 0.85) that immediately flags the output for review.
*   **Input Validity Checks:** Implement pre-processing checks that compare real-time sensor data against the range of your training and synthetic data. If live wind speed, direction, or temperature falls outside the bounds of your validated synthetic scenarios, trigger a manual override.
*   **Rate-of-Change Alerts:** Trigger manual review if input variables change faster than a defined threshold (e.g., wind direction shifting >45 degrees in 5 minutes), as rapid changes often correlate with lower model reliability in sparse data regions.
*   **The "Two-Key" Protocol:** During active unrest, require that any automated evacuation order generated by the model be digitally "countersigned" by at least one on-duty expert if the confidence score is between 0.85 and 0.95. Below 0.85, no automated order is issued; experts must manually construct the advisory based on raw data and heuristic knowledge.
*   **Audit Trail Logging:** Ensure every trigger event, the specific data causing it, the model's raw output, and the final human decision are logged immutably. This is vital for post-event analysis and refining the confidence thresholds.

This approach ensures that the automation serves as a decision-support tool rather than a black box, keeping human expertise as the final authority when data certainty is low.

*Note: Ensure all synthetic scenarios are reviewed by a meteorologist to confirm physical plausibility before testing.*
