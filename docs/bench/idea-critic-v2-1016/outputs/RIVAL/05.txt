**Where your leaning is weakest**
- **"Most clinics won't notice in the first month" is probably backwards.** Silent failures in a scheduling tool surface as no-shows, double bookings or an angry patient, so the first notice is the worst kind. Also, 4% of syncs is a rate, not a share of clinics: nearly every clinic is exposed. If the count per clinic is a few a week, they will notice. Don't rely on this assumption.
- **Beta data may understate it.** 35 clinics is a friendly audience; launch-month clinics will be less forgiving and more numerous.
- **Onboarding is a separate risk.** 9 of 35 (26%) is large. Confirm whether the 4-week estimate covers it, or only sync.
- **Check the timeline.** If the 4 weeks starts Monday 20 October, it ends around 17 November with no time for testing, which makes mid-December plausible and a faster ship unlikely.

**Where a delay might be wrong**
- If failures cluster in one calendar integration, you could launch with the others and mark that integration "coming soon." That would cut the delay or remove it.
- If you can make failures visible and recoverable quickly, the harm drops sharply even before the root cause is fixed.
