# Recommendation: conditional yes, not the full custom build

**Tell Marcus:** "We'll commit to Harbor's need, but as a phased, contract-backed build, not a blank-check quarter." Don't decide on Harbor's implied threat alone. Spend the days before Wednesday converting your unknowns into facts.

## Where your leaning is weak

1. **The build probably lands after the renewal.** If work starts in November, a full quarter ends around late January or February, after the 15 January renewal. What Harbor is really buying is a credible commitment, and you can give that without the whole build.
2. **"Half generalizable" is untested.** Treat it as zero in the base case. If it proves true, it's upside.
3. **You don't know whether Harbor will walk.** Your decision depends on this, and it's the cheapest thing to find out. Ask them.
4. **The comparison is lopsided.** $2.9M is concrete, but the onboarding lift is a forecast. Still, onboarding is deferred, not dead, and every quarter of delay costs compounding trial conversion.
5. **Concentration and precedent.** Accepting makes your 11% customer even more able to steer the roadmap, and other large accounts will notice.
6. **Estimates slip.** "A quarter" for 8 people often becomes more.

## Break-even to calculate

Accept only if (probability Harbor churns without it × $2.9M, plus lost expansion) exceeds roughly 12% × annual new ARR from trial conversion, plus the delay cost. Finance can give you the new-ARR figure in a day. If Harbor's churn probability is under about 25%, the math likely favors onboarding.

## Before Wednesday

- **Harbor:** Ask their executive sponsor directly: what happens at renewal without this, is anyone competing for the work, and would they sign a multi-year renewal early against a phased delivery plan?
- **Customers and pipeline:** Ask 5 to 8 other accounts, plus sales on lost deals, whether they need approval routing. That tests the generalizable half.
- **Engineering:** Ask for a Phase 1 scope of 4 to 6 weeks with 3 to 4 people, built as a configurable core that Harbor-specific rules sit on top of.
- **Finance:** Get the new-ARR figure for the break-even above.

## The offer to Harbor

- Phase 1 delivered by a date before or near renewal, with a design-partner role for Harbor.
- A renewal of two years or more, signed before you build past Phase 1.
- A checkpoint at week 6 to decide on Phase 2, with the onboarding project restarting on a defined date.

## Decision rules for Marcus

- **Harbor says it will leave without the feature, and others want it:** commit to Phase 1 plus the contract terms.
- **Harbor is vague or negotiating, and nobody else needs it:** offer a roadmap commitment and workarounds, and keep onboarding.
- **Harbor won't sign a multi-year deal against phases:** that suggests the threat is softer than implied.
