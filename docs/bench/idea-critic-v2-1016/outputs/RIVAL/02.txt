**Recommendation for Ravi: raise prices for existing customers, but not uniformly. Apply 15% to the long tail at January renewals, and handle the top 30 account by account. Don't approve "everyone, 15%" on 29 October.**

**Why your current leaning is weaker than it looks**

1. **Your churn assumption is untested.** A 2-point rise rests on one old test with 40 accounts. At that size, 2 points is under one account, so the test can't distinguish "no effect" from "big effect."
2. **The risk is concentrated, not averaged.** Top 30 is about $5.3M, roughly $177k per account. Losing two of them (about $350k) erases the entire new-customer-only upside. Losing five costs about $890k. A blended churn percentage hides this.
3. **The break-even is generous, but only for the tail.** The gap between the two options is about $1.25M ($1.6M minus $350k). Each point of extra revenue churn costs roughly $140k, so across the whole base you could absorb many points of churn before new-only wins. That argument holds for 1,170 small accounts. It does not hold for 30 accounts you can't forecast.
4. **Raising only for new customers is also a bad option.** It yields about $350k and creates new customers paying more than loyal ones, which is a worse position to defend in 12 months.

**Proposed shape**

- **Long tail (about 1,170 accounts, about 62% of revenue):** 15% at renewal. My rough arithmetic puts the gross uplift near $1.3M. Ask Finance to restate their $1.6M by segment, since I'm inferring this split.
- **Top 30:** individual plans owned by CS and an exec sponsor. Options are a smaller increase (for example 5–10%), or a larger one tied to a multi-year term or added value. Avoid a blanket notice letter.
- **Guardrails:** track churn and downgrades monthly by segment from the first renewals. Pre-agree a trigger (say, extra churn above 3 points in the tail, or any top-30 loss) that pauses the rollout.

**What to get before 29 October**

- Renewal dates for the top 30. Many may fall after January, which gives you time and data.
- Contract price-increase and notice clauses. If notice is 60–90 days, January renewals may already be at risk, so confirm this first.
- Usage, health scores and known competitor activity for the top 30.
- Informal CS conversations with 8–10 of the top 30 on likely reaction.
- A better price signal: even a quick test on a sample of tail renewals beats the 40-account result.

**Questions to put to the exec team**

- What extra churn in the top 30 would make us regret this, and what is our tolerance?
- Does Finance's $1.6M already account for the share of customers who renew in 2027?

**Notes:** All figures except Finance's two estimates and your 38% concentration are my arithmetic from your numbers, not verified data. If the contract-notice check fails, the realistic start date moves, and that matters more than any pricing choice above.
