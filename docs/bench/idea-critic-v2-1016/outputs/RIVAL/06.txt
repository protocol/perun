# Recommendation: Open-sourcing the routing library

**Recommended decision for 5 November: keep the core library internal, and revisit by a named date (suggest Q1 review) against specific tests. Don't approve open-sourcing yet, and don't close the door either.**

## Why internal is the right default now

1. **The decision is irreversible one way only.** Once published, we can't take it back. Waiting costs us a few months of hiring-brand benefit. Publishing too early could cost us a bid advantage. That asymmetry favors waiting until the unknowns are resolved.
2. **We don't know our license exposure.** Legal reports in late October, so we should have the answer before the meeting. If it's still open on 5 November, we can't responsibly approve release.
3. **Our key defense is untested.** The claim that competitors need at least a year to match us is an assumption, and the whole case for staying closed or opening up depends on it.

## Stress test of the current leaning

**Where "keep internal" is weak:**
- The one-year estimate may be wrong in either direction. If the 30% gain comes from a well-known technique, rivals could replicate it faster than a year with or without our code. In that case, secrecy protects little and we'd be forgoing the hiring and contribution benefits for nothing.
- "For now" has no end date or trigger. Without one, it quietly becomes "never," and the engineers who proposed this will read it as a no.
- The hiring benefit hasn't been quantified. If we have open roles that are hard to fill, that changes the weighing.

**Where it's strong:**
- The library is tied to bid wins, which is revenue, not a side project.
- The 0.5 engineer estimate is probably low for a library with real outside users (issue triage, security fixes, releases, review of external PRs). Even so, the cost is modest at our size. Cost is not the main objection. Competitive exposure is.

## Tests to run before 5 November (cheap, mostly this month)

1. **Source of the 30%:** Have one or two engineers write up whether the gain comes from algorithms, data-specific tuning, or implementation. Algorithmic gains are easier for others to rebuild. Tuning to our data is less so.
2. **Rebuild estimate:** Ask engineers who didn't write the library how long they'd need to reproduce the gain if they had the code, and without it. Treat this as a rough check, not proof.
3. **Dependency inventory:** Engineers can run a license scanner now, so legal confirms rather than starts from zero.
4. **Bid dependence:** Check how many recent wins cited planning performance.
5. **Hiring data:** Count open roles and time-to-fill where this would matter.

## Middle options to put on the table

- **Open-source a non-core part** (utilities, data formats, a benchmark harness) and keep the optimization core internal.
- **Publish technical talks or blog posts** without code, which captures some hiring brand at much lower risk.

## Proposed ask of Samir

Approve "internal for now," with owners and the five tests above, and a decision date once legal and the tests are in. If the tests show the edge is easily copied, or hiring pressure is high, move toward a partial release.

*Note: I've treated the 30% figure and the one-year estimate as your stated assumptions, not verified facts. The write-up should say so plainly.*
