# Recommendation

**Don't decide yet. Spend the next 5 working days closing the evidence gaps, then promote Tomas conditionally or offer a split role.** Your leaning (promote plus coaching) is defensible, but it currently rests on an untested assumption that he can change. That is too thin for a role where his dismissiveness reaches six people instead of one reviewer at a time.

## Before 24 October

1. **Talk to Tomas directly, with specifics.** Name the pattern (dismissive code reviews, one transfer) and watch the response. Defensiveness tells you more than any coaching plan. Ask how he would run reviews and handle disagreement as a lead. This is the best coaching-readiness evidence you can get in a week.
2. **Check with HR** how the two complaints were resolved, whether he was told, and whether behavior changed afterward.
3. **Ask the six prospective members** (or at least four) confidentially what they need from a lead. Don't pitch Tomas. Separately, speak to the engineer who transferred out.
4. **Sample his recent code review comments** yourself. Is the tone improving or not?
5. **Pre-brief Aisha** now so sign-off isn't a surprise.

## Decision rule

- **Promote with conditions** if he takes the feedback seriously, the team has no strong objection, and the review sample shows no recent pattern. Set a written 90-day checkpoint with concrete signals: a 30- and 90-day anonymous team pulse, review-tone sampling, and no new HR complaints. State up front that the role is reviewed at 90 days.
- **Otherwise, offer a tech lead role** (architecture, design direction, review standards) with you or another manager owning performance and people decisions. Give him a defined path to full lead in 6 to 9 months with the same signals. Most of what he wants (scope, status, impact) is preserved.

## Stress test of your leaning

- **Coaching is carrying too much weight.** "Can improve" is a hope, not evidence. If coaching fails, you've given a person with a documented pattern authority over reviews, ratings and promotions, and removing him later is far harder than not granting it.
- **The lead role amplifies the exact flaw.** Dismissing people in reviews is a daily lead behavior. With six reports, his reports can't easily avoid him the way peers can.
- **The pattern is plural.** Two complaints plus a transfer is not a one-off. A transfer is the quiet version of leaving, and others may have left without complaining.
- **His ultimatum shouldn't set the decision.** Promoting under threat teaches the team and your other engineers that threats work. It's also a data point about how he handles not getting what he wants. That said, losing him is a real cost, so the split role matters.
- **Top IC performance doesn't predict lead performance.** Shipping the two biggest projects shows delivery skill, not that he can develop others.
- **Unknown team reaction cuts both ways.** They may be fine with him. You don't know, and asking is cheap.
- **Counterweight to your caution:** passing him over may cost you a high performer and customer goodwill on payments, a sensitive area. That's why I'd offer a real path rather than a flat no.
