# Recommendation for Elena

**Do not sign the quote as written, and do not simply decline it. Run the current provider against it before 14 November. Keep the 1 March launch on the current provider, and only migrate after launch if the conditions below are met.**

## Why not just stay put

- **The savings are material.** $480k a year is $1.44M gross over 3 years, large for an 85-person company.
- **Your real constraint is timing, not the migration itself.** Five months starting now ends mid-March, straddling the launch. Starting after launch (March to August) means savings begin around September 2027, so you'd capture roughly 2 of the 3 committed years, about $0.9M gross.
- **One-time costs eat into that.** Illustratively, 20 engineer-months at an assumed $15-20k loaded is $300-400k. Add egress, dual-running costs and lost roadmap work, and net savings could land around $400-600k. That is still positive, but far less exciting than $1.44M.
- **Declining wastes your leverage.** The quote is your best negotiating tool with the incumbent.

## Stress test of "stay put"

1. **You may be anchoring on launch fear.** The launch risk comes from sequencing, which you can control by migrating after 1 March. It is not a reason to reject the deal.
2. **Staying has risks too.** Renewal pricing could rise, and without a credible alternative you'll pay whatever is asked.
3. **Your performance assumption is thin, but it argues for more testing, not rejection.** A two-week pilot won't show peak-season load or regional behavior under stress.
4. **The 3-year commit is the real danger.** If the commit starts at signing, you pay both providers during migration. If your game's usage declines, you owe unused spend. If it grows, the discount looks better.

## What to do before 7 November

1. **Ask your current provider for a counter-discount**, citing the competing quote. This is free to try.
2. **Get an egress estimate:** total data volume to move times per-GB rate, from your data inventory plus the current provider's published fees. Also ask whether they waive or credit egress for leaving.
3. **Ask the new provider for:**
   - a quote extension of 2-4 weeks;
   - a commit start deferred to cutover, or a ramped commit;
   - migration and egress credits;
   - a clause allowing a commit reduction if usage falls.
4. **Extend the pilot into a peak-load test** in your key regions, measuring p95 latency and error rates against your current baseline.

## Decision rule for Elena

Proceed with the new provider only if all four hold:

- Net savings after one-time costs are at least about $500k over the term.
- Egress is under roughly $100k or credited.
- The commit does not start before cutover.
- Peak-load p95 is within an agreed tolerance of current.

If the incumbent counters with at least 12-15%, staying with a shorter commit is a strong outcome. If neither provider moves and the new one won't defer the commit, stay put through launch and revisit.

*Note: the engineer cost and egress figures are my assumptions, so replace them with your own numbers.*
