# Recommendation for Ravi: Price increase decision, 29 October

**Recommendation:** Don't pick "everyone" or "new only." Raise 15% for new customers and for all renewals outside the top 30 starting January. Handle the top 30 account by account, with executive-led conversations before any notice goes out. Pre-commit a pause rule.

**Why not new-only:** It captures about $350k of the roughly $1.6M available and leaves new customers paying more than existing ones. The price gap widens every year you wait. It's the safe choice mainly because nobody has to make a hard call.

**The hard challenge to raising for everyone**

1. **The 2-point churn assumption is thin.** It comes from one old test on 40 accounts, and a 2-point swing on 40 accounts is under one customer. That test can't distinguish 0 points from 6.
2. **The real exposure is concentration, not average churn.** From your figures, the top 30 hold about $5.3M, roughly $175k each. Losing about nine of them (~$1.6M) erases Finance's whole gain. Your average-churn framing hides this.
3. **The gain is smaller than 15% of ARR.** 15% of $14M is $2.1M, but Finance says $1.6M. I assume that's renewal timing, but ask them to confirm, because the gap is $500k.
4. **Timing is tight.** If contracts require 60 to 90 days' notice, January renewals may already be at or past the deadline. I can't check your contract terms, so verify this first.

**What favors raising for existing customers:** Each point of extra churn costs about $140k of ARR. Using Finance's $1.6M, churn would have to rise roughly 10 points or more before the increase loses money. Your 2-point guess could be wrong by a lot and the policy would still pay off on average. Fifteen percent over 30 months is about 6% a year, which is easy to defend. So your leaning isn't reckless. The problem is that the downside is lumpy and concentrated in accounts you haven't assessed.

**Proposed structure (rough arithmetic from your figures)**
- The non-top-30 base is about $8.7M. At 15% that's up to about $1.3M gross, before any churn.
- Top 30: offer a smaller increase (for example 8 to 10%) or the full 15% in exchange for a multi-year commitment. Decide per account after a health and usage review.
- Pause rule, agreed before launch: if renewal churn in the first 60 days of the January cohort runs more than X points above baseline, or any two top-30 accounts cite price as the reason for leaving, stop and reassess. Pick X with Finance; I'd start around 3 points.

**To do before 29 October**
1. Pull the renewal calendar by month and the notice clauses for all contracts, especially the top 30.
2. Rate each top-30 account on health, usage, and whether it has a credible alternative.
3. Have Finance reconcile $2.1M to $1.6M and show net ARR at +0, +2, +5, and +8 points of churn.
4. Run a pre-announcement check with a few friendly top-30 accounts. This gives you real data instead of the 40-account test.

**What would change my view:** If more than about five top-30 accounts are at risk or have a ready alternative, go more conservative on that tier. If the data shows low switching costs across the whole base, shrink the 15%.

*Note: I haven't seen your contracts or any account data, so every figure here comes from the numbers you gave.*
