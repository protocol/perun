# Board recommendation: Pebble Surveys offer (decision needed 3 Nov)

**Recommendation:** Do not accept the offer as structured. Build is the right default, but not for the reasons assumed so far. Ask the board to authorize a counter-offer with walk-away gates, and to start the build spike now.

## What your numbers say
- **Price per retained dollar:** $2.1M is 2.3x Pebble's ARR. At your 60% retention assumption you keep about $540k ARR, so you pay about 3.9x retained ARR. At 40% retention it is about 5.8x.
- **Build cost:** 20 engineer-months. At an assumed $15–25k loaded per month (replace with your real figure), that is $0.3–0.5M. Even at 2x slip it is $0.6–1.0M, well under $2.1M.
- **What the premium buys:** You would pay roughly $1.6M more than the build cost for about $540k of ARR. Payback is multi-year before margin. Buying only wins if the customers or the speed are worth that.

## Stress test of "build"
1. **The estimate is yours and unvalidated.** Offline sync (conflicts, local storage, mobile) tends to slip. Plan for 7–10 months. Get a written estimate from engineering after a 1–2 week spike before 3 Nov.
2. **Opportunity cost is unquantified.** Four engineers is a large share of a 25-person company. Name what does not ship.
3. **Cost of delay is unmeasured.** Pull from the CRM how many deals were lost or stalled for lack of offline. If it is material, 5–10 months of waiting costs more than the build estimate suggests.
4. **Pebble may sell elsewhere.** A rival could get the feature and 700 accounts.

## Stress test of "buy"
1. **60% retention has no evidence behind it.** You need Pebble's cohort churn, top-10 account concentration, contract terms, and change-of-control or assignment clauses.
2. **Code usability is unknown.** If it is unusable, you pay $2.1M and still build, which is the worst outcome.
3. **Legal diligence is unfinished.** Do not sign without it.
4. **The stock half is unpriced.** You need the dilution, the valuation used, and whether $1.05M cash fits your runway.
5. **The 6 Nov deadline is the seller's.** It leaves three days after your board meets, and you can ask to extend it.

## Resolutions to request
1. Decline $2.1M as structured.
2. Authorize a counter, for example $0.6M upfront plus up to $1.0M earn-out on retained ARR at 12 months. These figures are illustrative. Keep stock small or tie it to the same earn-out. Make closing conditional on a code audit and on legal clearance. Ask for an extension to about the end of November.
3. Start the build spike and CRM lost-deal analysis immediately. They are the fallback and the evidence for the decision.

## What would flip the answer to "buy"
All three of these must hold:
- Evidence supports 80% or higher retention.
- The effective price drops to about 1.5x retained ARR or less, or is mostly contingent.
- Lost-deal data shows the build delay is costly.

Code reuse alone would not justify the price, since it saves only about $0.3–0.5M.
