# Recommendation: don't accept as asked. Take Marcus a conditional yes.

**Proposal for Marcus:** Commit to Harbor on a phased, scoped version of the feature, tied to the renewal terms. Do not give an open-ended quarter of an 8-person team. Make the commitment contingent on three things you can learn before the deadline.

**Before Wednesday**
1. **Ask Harbor directly.** Have an executive-level conversation: "What exactly must be true on 15 January for you to renew?" This tests whether they would walk or are negotiating. Ask whether a dated roadmap commitment, a first milestone, or a pilot would satisfy them.
2. **Ask engineering for a thin slice.** Request a 4-6 week scope that delivers Harbor's core routing case on configurable primitives, plus a cost estimate for the rest. Ask which parts are reusable.
3. **Test generalization.** Ask sales and customer success to put the concept in front of 3-5 other customers or live deals. If at least 2-3 would pay for or adopt it, your "half generalizes" assumption holds. If none do, treat this as pure retention spend.
4. **Quantify the trade-off.** Convert the onboarding lift into ARR. Take the annual self-serve new ARR, multiply by 10-15%, and compare it with Harbor's $2.9M multiplied by your honest probability that they leave. I can't compute this without that baseline.

**Stress test of your leaning**
- **Timing.** A quarter of work that starts after 22 October finishes around late January, after the 15 January renewal. So Harbor is renewing on a promise, not a delivered feature. That gives you room to negotiate milestones and terms, so use it.
- **Unvalidated assumptions.** Both "Harbor would walk" and "half generalizes" are untested, and your decision rests on both.
- **Framing.** The choice isn't "custom feature vs. onboarding." The onboarding project is delayed, not cancelled, and the delay has a cost you can price.
- **Precedent.** An 11% customer who gets a quarter of engineering on an implied threat will ask again. You can limit that with terms: a multi-year renewal, a price uplift, co-funding, or a roadmap-influence agreement.
- **Concentration risk.** Harbor will remain a risk after this renewal, so the answer should also move you toward less dependence on any one customer.

**When to accept in full:** Harbor confirms in writing that the feature decides the renewal, offers a longer term or uplift, and at least two other customers show real interest.

**When to decline or shrink the scope:** Harbor won't say what the renewal depends on, nobody else wants it, or engineering can't cut a slice smaller than the full quarter. In that case, offer professional services or a configuration workaround, and keep the onboarding project on schedule.

**Answer for Marcus (draft):** "We recommend a conditional yes: a phased commitment to Harbor with defined milestones, tied to renewal terms. We'll confirm Harbor's real requirement, the scope slice, and demand from other customers by [date], and decide the full commitment then."
