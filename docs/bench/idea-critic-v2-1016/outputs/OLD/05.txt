# Recommendation

**Don't launch general availability on 17 November with silent sync failures. Don't delay everything either.** Split the launch:

- **17 Nov:** keep the press embargo and booth, but launch as limited early access (invite or waitlist, a small cohort, assisted onboarding). Marketing's commitments stay intact.
- **Mid-December:** general availability, once the sync fix and onboarding changes ship.
- **Fast-track now:** ask engineering whether failure detection (an alert or a visible "sync failed" flag plus retry) can ship well before the full fix. This turns a silent failure into a visible one, which is the real risk.

Your lean (delay a month) is directionally right on GA but costlier than necessary, because it gives up the 17 Nov marketing moment.

## Stress test of your leaning

1. **"Most clinics won't notice in the first month" is probably wrong.** At 4%, a clinic booking 50 appointments a week loses about 2 a week (illustrative, since I don't have your volumes). A failed sync means a double-booking or a missed patient, and clinics notice that within days, usually as a patient complaint. Silent also means they can't trace the cause, so they blame the product. Treat this as a trust problem for a healthcare-adjacent tool, not a cosmetic bug.
2. **The clustering question could change the whole decision.**
   - If the failures concentrate in one calendar integration, you could launch on schedule with that integration excluded or labeled beta, and delay only that piece.
   - If they are spread out, the full delay case is much stronger.

   This is checkable by Monday from existing sync logs.
3. **The 4-week estimate doesn't add up yet.** Four weeks from 20 Oct is about 17 Nov, so why mid-December? Ask what the buffer covers (QA, staged rollout, onboarding work) and whether the sync fix and the onboarding fix are separable.
4. **Onboarding is a weaker blocker than sync.** 9 of 35 (26%) is significant, but with a small early-access cohort you can onboard by hand. It shouldn't drive the date.
5. **Mid-December has its own risk.** Check whether clinics are slower to adopt or busy near year-end, and whether press momentum is lost after the embargo and booth.

## Before Monday

- Break down failed syncs by integration, clinic, and appointment type. Include how many of the 35 clinics were affected and whether any reported it.
- Find out whether failed syncs are recoverable (retry, reconcile) or lose data.
- Ask engineering: what is in the 4 weeks, and what is the earliest date for alerting and retry?
- Ask marketing whether the embargo message can say "early access" and still work.

## Decision rule for Monday

- Failures cluster in one integration and detection can ship before 17 Nov: launch GA on time with that integration flagged.
- Failures are spread out, or detection can't ship early: use the split launch above.
- Failures are lossy and can't be surfaced at all: delay everything, including early access.

Reverse the split-launch plan if early-access clinics report sync problems the alerting doesn't catch.

*Note: I haven't seen your logs or volumes, so the clustering and the "clinics will notice" point are hypotheses to confirm with data, not findings.*
