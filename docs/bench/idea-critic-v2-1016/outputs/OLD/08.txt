# Recommendation for Elena (due 7 Nov)

**Don't migrate before the 1 March launch, but don't default to "stay put" either. Use the quote as leverage, and commit only if the terms protect the launch.**

Your lean is right about timing and weaker on the economics.

## Why staying put isn't automatically safe
- **Savings are material.** 20% is about $480k a year, or $1.44M gross over 3 years.
- **Costs to net against it.** Labor is 20 engineer-months. At a loaded $15-20k per month (my assumption, so replace it with your figure), that's roughly $300-400k. Add egress (unknown), dual-running during cutover, and what those 4 engineers would otherwise build.
- **Rough result.** If egress and dual-running stay modest, the net saving is plausibly $0.5-1M over 3 years. That is worth pursuing, but not worth risking the launch.
- **Timing.** Five months from today ends around mid-March, after launch. Starting now means migrating during the launch window. So the real plan is to start after launch stabilises, which means year-1 savings are smaller than $480k.

## Stress test of "stay put"
1. **It may leave money on the table.** You haven't asked the current provider for a counter-offer. A competing written quote is your strongest leverage.
2. **The case against moving rests on unknowns.** Egress and the counter-offer are both findable within days, so decide after you have them.
3. **The pilot is weak evidence for the risk.** Two weeks at pilot load says little about launch-season peaks. That cuts against migrating, but it can also be tested.

## Stress test of the move
- **Performance parity is an assumption.** Test it under launch-level load in your key regions.
- **A 3-year commit locks you in.** If usage drops or your architecture changes, you pay for unused spend.
- **Savings may be front-loaded or credit-based.** Check that the 20% holds across all three years.
- **Migration estimates slip.** Five months can easily become seven.

## Actions before 7 Nov
1. Ask your current provider for a counter-offer this week, citing the competing quote.
2. Get a written egress estimate. Take your stored data volume and multiply by each provider's per-GB rate, then ask the new provider about egress credits or migration funding.
3. Ask the new provider to extend the quote past 14 Nov, or to lock pricing with a start date after launch.
4. Run a load test in your key regions that mimics launch peak.
5. Replace my labor assumption with your actual loaded cost, then compute the net.

## Decision rule
- **Stay put** if the counter-offer reaches roughly 12-15% or more, or egress plus labor eats more than about half of the 3-year savings, or load-test latency is meaningfully worse.
- **Commit** if the new provider extends the quote or locks pricing, offers a performance exit clause or ramped commitment, egress is manageable, and the load test passes. Start migration after launch.
- **If the quote can't be extended and the terms are rigid,** staying put with the counter-offer is the safe default. A 20% quote is not worth the launch.

Note: the labor cost is my assumption, and the 12-15% threshold is a starting point to adjust once you have real egress and labor numbers.
