# Recommendation: Open-sourcing the routing library

**Recommendation for 5 Nov:** Keep the library internal and do not approve a release. Instead, approve a time-boxed re-decision with named inputs, and ask the engineers for a narrower proposal (below).

## Why keeping it internal holds up
- **Asymmetry:** A release can't be undone, while staying internal can be revisited at any meeting. When the upside is unquantified, the reversible option is the right default.
- **The edge is concrete:** The roughly 30% compute saving is tied to winning bids. The hiring and contribution benefits are, so far, only hoped for.
- **Maintenance cost isn't the issue:** At 0.5 engineer, cost is not the deciding factor. The deciding factors are competitive exposure and legal risk.

## Where the current leaning is weak
1. **The one-year competitor lead time is untested.** If the core technique is published or easy to rediscover, the library protects less than we think, and secrecy buys little. If it's truly hard to replicate, releasing the whole thing is a bigger mistake. Either way, we can't argue it until it's tested.
2. **"For now" has no end.** Without a trigger, the default becomes permanent, and the engineers who proposed this may read it as a silent no.
3. **We haven't separated what creates the advantage.** The 30% may come from one core component, with the rest being generic plumbing. If so, "all or nothing" is a false choice.
4. **The benefits of opening are unmeasured.** We have no data on how much hiring brand or outside contribution is actually worth to us.

## Unknowns that block a decision
- **Licensing:** Unknown third-party entanglements (for example, copyleft dependencies or customer contract IP clauses). Legal finishes in late October, which is before the meeting. Ask legal to deliver a dependency and contract audit by 30 Oct, not just a general opinion.
- **Competitor lead time:** Untested (see above).
- **Source of the 30%:** Not yet attributed to specific components.

## Before 5 Nov (small, bounded work)
- **Replication test:** Have two senior engineers spend a day or two estimating what a competent competitor would need with our code in hand, and with only public knowledge. Record their reasoning, not only a number.
- **Attribution:** Identify which modules produce the compute saving.
- **Bid evidence:** Check how many recent wins plausibly depended on that saving.
- **Value of opening:** Ask recruiting for concrete hiring-pipeline evidence, even a rough estimate.

## Alternative to propose
Release a non-core, generic component (or publish talks or a technical write-up) to capture some hiring brand while the core stays closed. That is only possible if the attribution work shows a clean boundary and legal clears the pieces.

## Triggers to revisit
- Legal finds no blocking licenses **and** the replication test shows the lead is under about 12 months or rests on a separable component.
- A competitor ships comparable performance, which removes the advantage anyway.
- Measurable attrition or hiring loss attributed to the closed policy.

**Decision requested on 5 Nov:** Approve "internal for now," with a re-review date (suggest one quarter) and the inputs above due beforehand.

*Note: My assessment rests only on the facts you gave me. The lead time, the licensing position and the source of the 30% saving are all unverified.*
