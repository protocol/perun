## Recommendation

Don't name Tomas permanent lead on today's evidence. Use the 8 days before 24 October to run three checks. If they pass, appoint him with a time-boxed review. If they don't, offer a role that keeps his strengths without making him the person who sets the team's culture.

**Checks to run by 22 October (so Aisha can sign off):**
1. **Recent behavior.** Look at the last 6 months of code reviews and peer feedback, and at what changed after the HR complaints. Did anyone speak to him, and what did he do differently? Passing means a visible, specific change. "He said he'd work on it" doesn't count.
2. **The six.** Talk to each one individually and ask neutral questions: "What do you need from a lead?" and "How do you feel about Tomas leading?" You are checking for fear, not running a vote. If two or more raise concerns about how they'd be treated, that is a fail.
3. **Tomas himself.** Ask him how he'd run reviews and handle disagreement, and what he thinks the two complaints were about. Passing means he names the behavior and accepts it as a problem. If he frames it as others being too sensitive, treat that as strong evidence the coaching assumption is wrong.

**If it passes:** appoint him with written expectations (review tone, upward and peer feedback at 30/60/90 days), an external coach, and a stated review at 90 days with an agreed alternative if it fails. Tell Aisha up front that you will use that review.

**If it fails or is unclear:** offer a technical lead or principal role with real scope, a pay and title bump, and someone else handling people management. Give him an honest account of why, and a path to lead later.

## Stress test of your leaning

- **"He can improve with coaching."** This is your weakest assumption, and the evidence is limited. Coaching works when the person sees the problem. You don't yet know whether he does.
- **The complaints are about the core job.** Code review is how a lead shapes a 6-person team. Two HR complaints and one transfer in 12 months is a pattern in exactly the behavior he'd be responsible for modeling.
- **Top performer is not the same as good lead.** His shipped projects show delivery skill. They tell you nothing about whether he can develop or retain others.
- **The leave threat is leverage, not a reason.** Promoting under an ultimatum teaches the team and others how promotions work. Also test whether the threat is real: ask what he'd actually do and what the market looks like for him. Losing him would hurt, but a lead who drives out two or three of six would cost more.
- **Unasked six.** You're committing a team you haven't consulted. That's a gap you can close this week.
- **Cost asymmetry.** Demoting a lead is much harder than not promoting him. A 90-day review only works if you and Aisha will really act on it.

**What would change my view:** strong recent evidence of change plus a team that is neutral or positive would make the conditional promotion a sound call. Evidence that he dismisses the complaints, or fear among the six, would make the alternative role the right one.
