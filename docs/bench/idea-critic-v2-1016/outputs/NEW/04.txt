**Recommendation: build the offline feature, and do not accept the deal as structured. Ask Pebble for an extension and a different structure.** The board should be asked to approve this as a go-if, not a straight "build."

**Why buy looks weak on your numbers**
- $2.1M for $900k ARR is 2.3x headline. At your 60% retention assumption you keep $540k, so you pay about 3.9x retained ARR. At 40% retention it is 5.8x, and at 80% it is 2.9x.
- Building costs about 20 engineer-months. At an assumed $15k/month loaded, that is roughly $300k, or about $600k if the estimate doubles. That is a fraction of the price, though building buys no customers.
- Half the price ($1.05M) is cash, which is a large draw for a company your size. The stock half dilutes you at a valuation you haven't stated.

**Stress test of "build"**
- Your 5-month estimate is yours alone. Have the engineers who would build it sign off, with a range. Offline sync and conflict handling tends to overrun.
- Four engineers is a big share of a 25-person company. Name what they stop doing for 5+ months.
- You haven't shown that the missing feature costs you deals. Count lost deals and churn that cite offline. If the number is small, neither option is urgent.
- Building does not get you the 700 accounts. The real question is whether those customers are worth more than $1.05M cash plus the stock. At about $1,290 per account, they are small accounts, which tend to churn when a vendor changes hands.

**Weak points in the buy case, which your own leaning doesn't test**
- The 60% retention is an assumption with no support. Ask Pebble for cohort churn and the age of its accounts.
- The code is unassessed. If it is largely reusable, the build time falls and buying looks better. Get an engineer to review it.
- Diligence on contracts is unfinished. Check change-of-control and assignment terms, and whether respondent data can legally be transferred.

**Counter-proposal to take to Pebble**
1. Ask to move 6 November to at least 4 weeks after your diligence completes. The board meets 3 November, so there are three days between decision and deadline.
2. Offer an asset or customer deal in which most of the price is an earn-out tied to ARR retained at 6 and 12 months. A smaller cash payment up front, with no stock or limited stock.
3. Meanwhile, scope the build and start only the part that is needed regardless.

**Kill criteria and review**
- Walk away if Pebble refuses retention-linked pricing, if diligence turns up blocking contract terms, or if the code review shows less than about 30% is reusable and the price stays above about 3x retained ARR. The 30% and 3x are my placeholders, so set your own.
- Switch toward buying if the code review cuts your build estimate to about 2 months or less, or if lost-deal data shows offline is costing you substantial ARR.
- Leading metric: share of lost deals and churn citing offline, and the engineers' re-estimate. Review at the 3 November board meeting.

**Missing from your numbers:** your cash and runway, gross margin, Pebble's churn history, the size of the lost-deal problem, and the valuation behind the stock.
