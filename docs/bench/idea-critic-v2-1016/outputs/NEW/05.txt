## Recommendation

**Don't ship ungated on 17 November, and don't commit to a full one-month slip on Monday. Commit to a gated launch on 17 Nov, with a hard checkpoint that converts it to a December slip if the fix isn't on track.**

**What to decide with Priya**
1. **Gated launch.** Keep 17 Nov for press and the booth, but onboard a limited cohort (the beta clinics plus a small waitlist) rather than open signup. Marketing keeps its dates and you cap the exposure.
2. **Make failures loud first.** The silent part is the real danger. Ask engineering whether alerting, automatic retry, and a visible sync-status indicator for clinics can ship in about 2 weeks, separately from the root-cause fix.
3. **Split the 4 weeks.** Ask engineering to break the estimate into sync root cause, detection, and onboarding. Onboarding confusion (9 of 35, about 26%) may be addressable with guided setup, content, and support-led onboarding rather than engineering.
4. **Diagnose before Monday.** Pull sync logs from the beta and break failures down by calendar integration and by clinic. This is a day or two of work and decides a lot.

**Kill criteria (agree these now)**
- By 31 Oct, the root cause isn't isolated, or the fix or detection is not in staging: move to the December date and tell press and marketing the same day.
- Any beta clinic reports a missed or double-booked appointment from a silent failure: no launch until detection is live.
- Failure rate after the fix is above an agreed threshold (suggest 0.5%): hold the cohort and don't expand.

**Leading metric:** silent-failure rate per clinic per week, plus the percentage of failures that are surfaced to the clinic. Review weekly from 27 Oct.

## Stress test of your leaning

- **"Most clinics won't notice" is probably backwards.** A silent failure is noticed when a patient arrives without an appointment, or two patients arrive for one slot. At 4%, a clinic with 100 appointments a week sees about 4 problems weekly. The harm is trust and patient experience, not just a bug count. Check whether any beta clinic has already reported one.
- **The clustering question changes everything.** If failures sit in one calendar integration, you can launch without that integration and the slip may be unnecessary. If they're spread out, it's a core reliability problem and delay is more justified.
- **The timeline arithmetic doesn't fit.** Four weeks from 20 Oct is about 17 Nov, which is launch day. A move to mid-December implies about 4 more weeks of QA, re-beta, or a later start. Ask what's in that buffer, because it may be compressible.
- **Delay costs are partly recoverable.** The booth can run as early access with a signup list, and embargoes can often be renegotiated. A launch story that includes data loss is harder to undo.
- **Where your leaning may still be right:** if the diagnosis shows the failures are broad, or detection can't ship in time, a one-month delay is the correct call.
