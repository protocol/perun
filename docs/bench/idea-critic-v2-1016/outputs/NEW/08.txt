## Recommendation for Elena

**Don't sign the quote as written, and don't simply stay put. Go only if three conditions are met by 7 November. Otherwise stay and use the quote as leverage.**

**Why not just stay put:** Staying costs about $40k a month relative to the quote, or $1.44M gross over 3 years. Your three unknowns (counter-offer, egress, performance) can each be resolved or priced in the next three weeks, so none of them justifies a "no" today.

**Why not sign now:** Your timeline doesn't work. A 5-month migration starting today finishes in mid-March, straight through the 1 March launch. A launch-critical migration with a 2-week pilot and unknown egress isn't ready for a 3-year commitment.

### The three conditions
1. **Counter-offer in hand.** Ask your current provider for a written counter now, using the competing quote. Even half the discount with no migration risk likely beats the full discount with it.
2. **Egress and dual-running cost priced in writing.** Get your data volumes, ask the new provider for egress and migration credits, and add a dual-running estimate. If the total one-time cost exceeds roughly 12 months of savings (about $480k), the case weakens a lot.
3. **Performance evidence at launch scale.** Extend the pilot to a load test in your key regions, covering peak concurrency, latency and failure behavior. Two quiet weeks don't validate the assumption.

### Negotiating asks of the new provider
- Extend the 14 November expiry, or hold the price for a start after launch.
- Start the commitment clock at cutover, or ramp the committed spend.
- Credits covering egress and migration.

### Stress test of your leaning to stay
- **Strong for staying:** launch risk is real, and 20 engineer-months is a real cost. At an assumed ~$200k loaded cost per engineer (replace with yours), that is about $330k before egress. Payback is likely 12–18 months after cutover, and a 3-year lock-in leaves little margin if performance disappoints.
- **Weak for staying:** launch risk is a scheduling problem, not a verdict. Migrating after launch, say April to September 2027, still captures roughly 2 of the 3 years. Also, "the pilot was short" is a reason to test more, not to stop.
- **Easy to miss:** the quote may only matter as leverage. If your current provider matches most of it, you win either way.

### Kill criteria (any one means stay)
- The counter-offer is at least about 70% of the discount.
- One-time costs exceed about $480k.
- The load test shows meaningfully worse latency or reliability in a key region.
- The new provider won't move the commitment start past launch.
