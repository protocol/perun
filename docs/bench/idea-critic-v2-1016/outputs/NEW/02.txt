# Recommendation for Ravi: raise prices for everyone, but not as one flat 15%

**Go with a split rollout:**
1. **New customers:** 15% from 1 November. This is nearly free and is the cleanest signal on willingness to pay.
2. **Long tail (about 1,170 accounts, 62% of revenue):** 15% at January renewals, with 60-90 days' notice, subject to a contract check on notice periods and price caps.
3. **Top 30 (38% of revenue, about $5.3M):** no blanket notice. Each account gets a named owner, a conversation before notice, and a pre-agreed range of 8-15%. Offer a multi-year lock or added value where an account pushes back.

**Why not new customers only:** it captures about $350k against roughly $1.6M, so it gives up about $1.25M. It also leaves a 30-month-stale price on the whole installed base, and new customers would pay more than loyal ones.

**Challenge to "raise for everyone":**
- **The $1.6M doesn't reconcile.** 15% of $14M is $2.1M. Ask Finance what explains the $0.5M gap (renewal timing, discounts, contract caps). If it is staggered renewals, the in-year figure differs from the run-rate, and the exec team needs to know which one it is seeing.
- **Your churn guess is the weakest input.** It rests on 40 accounts from an old test, and a sample that small can't tell 1 point from 6. It also says nothing about the top 30, which is where the risk sits.
- **The average case is safe, but the tail is not.** Revenue lost to churn cancels a 15% uplift only once it reaches about 13% (0.15/1.15). A uniform 2-point rise costs about $280k, which is fine. The danger is concentration. The top 30 average about $177k each and the uplift on them is about $800k, so losing 4-5 of them wipes it out. A flat rule applied blindly to those accounts risks that trade for little gain.
- **You can't yet answer how the top 30 will react.** A flat notice letter removes your chance to find out before committing.

**Before 29 October (13 days):**
- Have Finance reconcile the $1.6M and show the base, downside and upside cases.
- Have CSMs score the top 30 on health, usage, contract end date and competitive exposure, and sound out 8-10 of them informally.
- Legal checks notice and price-cap clauses for January renewals.
- Pull any available data on past discounting and churn by segment, so the 2-point guess has more behind it than the 40-account test.

**Tripwires to put in the exec decision:**
- If January-February long-tail renewal churn runs more than 3 points above baseline, pause the rollout for the remaining cohorts.
- If any 2 of the top 30 signal non-renewal because of price, freeze top-tier increases and re-review.
- Leading metrics: renewal rate, discount requests per renewal and downgrade rate. Review monthly, with a first checkpoint at the end of February.

**Decision to ask Ravi for:** approve the split rollout, with a reversible long-tail increase and individually managed top accounts. It captures most of the upside and caps the loss from your biggest unknown.

*Assumed:* contracts permit mid-term price changes only at renewal, and 15% is within market norms for your segment. Neither has been checked.
