# Recommendation: go-if, not a flat yes

**Promote Tomas only if three checks pass by 24 October. If they do, make it a 90-day appointment with a defined review, not an open-ended promotion.**

## Checks to run in the next 8 days
1. **Ask the 6 prospective members.** Talk to each one privately and ask what they need from their lead. Don't mention Tomas by name unless you must. Right now you have no data on the group who will live with this decision.
2. **Get the facts on the complaints from HR.** Find out what was alleged, whether it was substantiated, whether Tomas was ever given formal feedback, and why the engineer transferred. If he was never told, your "he can improve" assumption is untested, not weak.
3. **Hold a direct feedback conversation with Tomas.** Give him the specific pattern (dismissive code reviews, the complaints, the transfer) and see how he responds. Defensiveness or dismissal is the strongest evidence you can get before the deadline.

## Stress test of your leaning
- **The coaching assumption is your weakest point.** You have limited evidence he can change, and the lead role amplifies the behavior. A lead controls reviews, assignments, hiring and performance feedback, so the people he dismisses can't easily push back.
- **Top performer is not the same as good lead.** His record shows he delivers, not that he can lead six people. Payments integrations also depend on people raising mistakes and near-misses quickly, which a dismissive lead suppresses.
- **The ultimatum shouldn't drive the choice.** Promoting under a "promote me or I leave" threat sets a precedent, and a lead who got the role by threat starts with less credibility. Still, losing him is a real cost. Price it against the cost of a damaged team, not against nothing.
- **The pattern is already costly.** Two HR complaints and one transfer in 12 months, in a 70-person company, is a pattern, not noise.
- **You have cheaper alternatives.** (a) Make him technical lead with someone else managing the people side. (b) Appoint him acting lead for 90 days with explicit criteria. (c) Name someone else and give Tomas a senior technical track with a promotion path tied to behavior. Alternatives (a) and (b) keep the decision reversible. A full promotion is hard to walk back, so it's close to a one-way door.
- **Do nothing is not free.** Delay past 24 October risks Tomas leaving or the team starting without a lead on 1 December.

## Kill criteria
- **No-go on direct reports** if two or more of the six express serious reluctance, or if Tomas dismisses the feedback conversation. Fall back to alternative (a) or (c).
- **After appointment, reverse or restructure** if any of these appears: a new substantiated complaint, any team member leaving and citing him, or a pulse survey showing the team doesn't feel safe disagreeing with him.

## Leading metric and review
Run an anonymous pulse survey and a sample of his code-review comments at 30 days (about 4 January) and 90 days (1 March 2027). Check specifically whether people feel they can disagree with him and whether review tone has changed. Put these terms in writing for Tomas and show Aisha the dissent, not just the recommendation.

*Note: I haven't seen the HR files or your team's views, so the checks above could change this answer in either direction.*
