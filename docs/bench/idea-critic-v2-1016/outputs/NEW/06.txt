# Recommendation: Corvid routing library, open-source or keep internal

**Decision for 5 November: no-go on a full open-source release now. Keep it internal, and pre-commit to a review in February 2027.** Your leaning holds, but for a different reason than the one you gave.

**Why it holds**
- Release is a one-way door: you can't unpublish. Staying internal is a two-way door. Waiting a quarter costs some hiring brand and outside contributions, which is small next to exposing the thing that may help win bids.
- Two inputs are unknown, and either could change the answer: the license entanglements (legal is due late October) and your one-year replication assumption, which is untested.

**Stress test of your leaning**
1. **The one-year assumption is the weakest link, and it cuts both ways.** If competitors really need a year even with your code, the moat is not the code. It is probably tuning, data or domain knowledge, and the code is safer to release than you think. If they don't, the 30% edge is exposed. Either way you need to test it, not assume it.
2. **"Internal for now" is a decision with costs.** The engineers who proposed this may leave or disengage, and hiring brand erodes quietly. Name that cost rather than treating inaction as free.
3. **The 30% figure and the bid link are unverified in this write-up.** "Roughly 30 percent versus off-the-shelf" and "one reason we win some bids" need a source, such as a benchmark or a bid post-mortem, before the directors rely on them.
4. **The license question may decide this regardless.** If a dependency is copyleft or restricted, release may be blocked or require a rewrite.

**Alternatives considered**
- **Do nothing:** cheapest, but it ignores the hiring goal.
- **Cheaper route to the hiring goal:** technical talks or blog posts on the approach, without the code.
- **Partial release:** open-source a non-differentiating layer, such as a benchmark harness or tooling, and keep the core internal.
- **Full release:** worth it only if the checks below pass.

**Do before 5 November**
- Ask legal to prioritize the third-party dependency and license scan, separate from the full review. *Resolved when:* you have a list of dependencies and their licenses.
- Have one senior engineer identify which parts of the 30% gain come from the code versus tuning or data, and estimate what a competent team would still need to build with the code in hand. *Resolved when:* you have a written estimate that supports or breaks the one-year figure.
- Pull the bid post-mortems that cite compute speed. *Resolved when:* you know whether speed was decisive in any actual win.

**Go-if:** revisit full or partial release if legal clears the licenses, the differentiator proves separable from the released code (or replication takes a year or more even with it), and the 0.5 engineer of maintenance is funded.

**Kill criteria (stay internal):** a copyleft or restricted dependency that can't be removed cheaply, or an estimate that a competitor could replicate the gain in under six months.

**Leading metric:** engineering offer-acceptance and referral rates, plus how often the library is cited in bid wins, reviewed in February 2027.

Note: the check on the one-year assumption is a leader's estimate, not proof. Treat its result as a range.
