Thanks for the breakdown. After reviewing the numbers and the timeline constraints, I cannot approve the 12,000-copy run at this time.

While covering the full 9,300-unit forecast plus a buffer is ideal in theory, the risks currently outweigh the benefits. Committing to 12,000 units exposes us to approximately 2,700 unsold copies if returns hit the projected 15%. More critically, the larger run requires a new paper order that adds four days to production. Since we are already racing against the Thursday commitment deadline to guarantee the May 15 ship date, introducing a four-day variable is too dangerous. If the paper delivery slips even slightly, we miss the ship date entirely, which defeats the purpose of avoiding a mid-semester reprint.

The 8,000-copy option is the safer immediate move. It utilizes existing paper stock, securing the May 15 shipment without delay. Although this leaves a gap of 1,300 units against the sales forecast, it avoids the capital outlay of the extra $26,300 ($87,500 vs. $61,200) and eliminates the risk of significant overstock. We can monitor early adoption rates and trigger a smaller, targeted reprint later in the term if demand outpaces the initial 8,000, rather than gambling on a massive upfront run with a compromised timeline.

Additionally, the corrupted cover art file creates a bottleneck. Since the designer is not resending the file until Friday, we cannot finalize the press-ready materials before the Thursday commitment deadline regardless of the run size. Proceeding with the 8,000 run allows us to lock in the text block printing immediately while we wait for the corrected cover file, keeping the project moving.

Please proceed with booking the press for the 8,000-copy run using the existing paper stock. Let's plan to review sales velocity in six weeks to determine if a second run is necessary.
