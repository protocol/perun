**PR: Depot Charger Scheduling – Approach Selection for Birmingham Pilot**

**Context**
Prepares the Birmingham depot pilot (starts 1 March) for the 42-bus e-fleet. Code must merge by 20 Feb to allow testing before the night shift handover at 6 am. We need sign-off on the scheduling algorithm by Friday 14th.

**Proposed Approaches**
We evaluated two strategies for managing charger contention:

*   **Approach A: Fixed Time-Slot Batching**
    *   **Logic:** Assigns charging in 15-minute granular slots.
    *   **Alignment:** Mirrors the ops team's existing shift patterns.
    *   **Effort:** +340 lines of code; 12% cheaper to implement than B.
    *   **Trade-off:** Higher peak charger contention compared to B.

*   **Approach B: Dynamic Priority Queue**
    *   **Logic:** Prioritizes charging based on State of Charge (SOC) and departure time.
    *   **Performance:** Reduces peak charger contention by 18% compared to A.
    *   **Effort:** +520 lines of code; higher implementation cost.
    *   **Trade-off:** More complex logic that diverges from current shift-based workflows.

**Recommendation**
I recommend **Approach A (Fixed Time-Slot Batching)**.

While Approach B offers an 18% reduction in peak contention, Approach A aligns directly with the ops team's shift structure, which simplifies troubleshooting and adoption during the pilot phase. Given the tight timeline (merge by 20 Feb) and the 12% cost saving, the simplicity of the 15-minute batching outweighs the efficiency gains of the dynamic queue for this initial rollout. We can revisit dynamic prioritization in a follow-up iteration once the pilot data validates our baseline contention levels.

**Action Required**
Please review and approve **Approach A** so we can finalize the implementation before the Friday 14th deadline.
