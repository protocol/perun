**To:** Dana, Deputy City Manager
**From:** Lena
**Date:** March 12
**Subject:** Decision Needed: Water Leak Audit & Meter Replacement Strategy

The leak-detection audit results are in. Our non-revenue water stands at 19.7% of total flow, significantly exceeding the 12% target. Two primary factors are driving this loss: aging infrastructure and data gaps.

First, approximately 4,300 meters installed before 1995, concentrated in the Elmwood district, are under-registering flow by an average of 8%. Second, our calibration contractor, AquaMetrix, missed the February 28 deadline, leaving us without verified data for certain sectors. Additionally, the audit identified 37 minor leaks. While none require immediate emergency repair, they collectively account for roughly 40,000 gallons of lost water per day. Please note that the new billing software rollout has been delayed to April, so current consumption data will remain partially unreliable until then.

We have two options to address the metering issue:

*   **Option A (Finance Preference):** Replace all 4,300 pre-1995 meters over 18 months.
    *   *Estimated Cost:* $2.1 million.
    *   *Benefit:* Captures bulk pricing discounts and resolves the issue comprehensively in one cycle.
*   **Option B (Audit Recommendation):** Execute a phased replacement, starting with the 1,200 worst-performing meters immediately, followed by a re-audit in Q3.
    *   *Benefit:* Lower initial outlay and allows us to validate performance improvements before committing to the full replacement.

**Decision Required**
Please select an approach by **Thursday, March 15**, so we can finalize the council packet.

*   If you choose **Option A**, we will proceed with budget allocation for the full replacement.
*   If you choose **Option B**, I need your approval to issue a Request for Proposals (RFP) to three vendors for the initial phase of 1,200 meters.

Given the data gaps from the missed calibration deadline and the upcoming billing software update, Option B offers a cautious path to verify results before full expenditure. However, Option A secures immediate economies of scale. Let me know which direction you prefer.
