Our nightly charging demand is 3.1 MWh for 40 buses. This exceeds our current grid connection limit of 2.4 MW, making the current infrastructure insufficient without modification.

**The Options**

*   **Approach A:** Utilize the existing grid connection (max 2.4 MW) and install a 1.2 MWh battery buffer.
    *   *Pros:* Avoids immediate transformer costs; aligns with the depot manager's note that drivers prefer slow-charge profiles, which a battery facilitates.
    *   *Cons:* Adds battery maintenance and replacement cycles to the long-term opex.

*   **Approach B:** Upgrade the transformer to handle the full load and skip the battery.
    *   *Pros:* Simpler architecture (no battery degradation risks); higher throughput capacity.
    *   *Cons:* Requires $180k upfront CAPEX for the transformer upgrade.

**Recommendation: Approach A (Existing Grid + Battery)**

I recommend we proceed with **Approach A**.

While Approach B offers architectural simplicity, the $180k upfront cost for the transformer upgrade is significant. Approach A allows us to meet the 3.1 MWh demand within the existing 2.4 MW limit by smoothing the load. Additionally, the battery setup naturally supports the slower charge rates the drivers prefer, potentially extending bus battery life.

Crucially, the CFO confirmed we can cover the battery costs using the EV incentive fund, provided we apply by May 5. This funding window gives us a clear path to offset the initial hardware expense, whereas the transformer upgrade in Approach B likely falls outside these specific incentives.
