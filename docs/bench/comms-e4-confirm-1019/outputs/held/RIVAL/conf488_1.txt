**Context**
The depot must support a nightly charging demand of 3.1 MWh for 40 buses. Our current grid connection caps at 2.4 MW, creating a capacity gap for the required overnight load.

**Option A: Add Battery Storage**
*   **Approach:** Utilize the existing 2.4 MW grid connection and install a 1.2 MWh battery system.
*   **Pros:** Avoids immediate transformer upgrade costs; aligns with driver preference for slow-charging profiles; leverages the battery for load shifting.
*   **Cons:** Higher upfront hardware cost for the battery; added system complexity.

**Option B: Upgrade Grid Infrastructure**
*   **Approach:** Upgrade the transformer to increase grid capacity and omit the battery.
*   **Cost:** $180,000 for the transformer upgrade.
*   **Pros:** Simpler architecture (no battery integration); directly resolves the 2.4 MW bottleneck.
*   **Cons:** Fixed infrastructure cost; misses opportunity for energy arbitrage or backup power provided by storage.

**Financial Note**
The CFO confirmed we can access the EV incentive fund to offset costs, provided we submit the application by May 5. This deadline applies regardless of which technical approach we select, but the specific equipment costs will influence the funding amount requested.

**Recommendation**
I recommend **Option A (Add Battery Storage)**. While Option B offers a simpler setup, the 1.2 MWh battery bridges the gap between our 2.4 MW limit and the 3.1 MWh demand without requiring the $180k transformer upgrade immediately. Additionally, this option accommodates the depot manager's note regarding driver preference for slower charging rates and positions us to maximize the EV incentive fund by including storage components in the application.
