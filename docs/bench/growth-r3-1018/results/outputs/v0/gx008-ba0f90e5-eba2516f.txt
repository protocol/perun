### Measurement Plan: ShopViet Mobile Migration Pivot (First 30 Days)

**1. The North Star Metric**
**Weekly Active App Users Completing a Core Purchase.**
Do not track "Total Downloads" or "App Installs" as your primary success metric; these are vanity metrics that rise regardless of value delivery. Your North Star must reflect actual customer value. A user who downloads but never buys contributes zero LTV. This metric isolates users who have not only migrated but are deriving value on the new platform.

**2. The Funnel: Read Bottom-Up (Retention First)**
Your assumption of a 30% LTV lift is `UNVERIFIED` until retention is proven. Do not pour marketing spend into acquisition if the bucket leaks. Diagnose in this order:
*   **Retention:** Of the users who installed, what percentage returned in Week 2 to complete a purchase? If this is low, the app experience or value proposition is broken, and migration goals are irrelevant.
*   **Activation:** Did they complete the "Aha!" moment (e.g., first successful checkout or enabling push notifications) within 24 hours of install?
*   **Acquisition/Migration:** Only after retention and activation are stable, measure the volume of web users successfully transitioning.

**3. Event Taxonomy: Answering Named Questions**
Instrument only events that answer specific strategic questions. Do not instrument "everything."
*   *Question:* "Which web behaviors predict successful app retention?"
    *   *Event:* `web_session_depth` (pages viewed), `web_cart_abandonment_rate`, and `device_type` prior to install. Correlate these properties against the `app_retention_cohort`.
*   *Question:* "Is the LTV assumption holding?"
    *   *Event:* `app_purchase_value` linked to a **stable, pseudonymous user ID** that matches the web identity.
    *   *Seam Note:* Use a stable pseudonymous ID to link web and app cohorts. Do not use raw PII here; route identity resolution and consent checks to your privacy compliance protocol.

**4. Stage Awareness: MVP Focus**
You are in the `mvp` stage for the app. Ignore mature-stage metrics like referral rates or complex segmentation for now. Focus entirely on:
*   Activation rate of the core action (purchase).
*   Week 1 and Week 2 retention cohorts.
*   The single strongest predictor variable from web behavior.

**5. Experiment Rigor**
If you A/B test onboarding flows to improve migration:
*   Pre-commit to a sample size and duration. Do not peek at results on Day 3 and declare a winner; this inflates false positives.
*   Define a primary metric (activation) and a guardrail (e.g., support ticket volume).
*   Any result read before the pre-committed end date is `UNVERIFIED`.

**6. This-Week Steps**
1.  **Define the Core Action:** Explicitly document what constitutes a "Core Purchase" in the app to ensure engineering instruments this specific event, not just "screen views."
2.  **Build the Retention Cohort Query:** Create a dashboard view that shows Week 1 and Week 2 retention for users installed in the first 7 days, segmented by their top 3 pre-install web behaviors.
3.  **Establish the Baseline:** Pull the current web-only retention and purchase frequency for the target demographic. You cannot measure a "30% lift" without your own baseline data; do not use industry benchmarks.

**Note:** The 30% LTV lift is a hypothesis, not a fact. Your first 30 days must validate whether app retainers actually spend more than web retainers before scaling migration targets.
Present the retention curve to the Board, not just the install count; rising installs with flat retention signals a failed pivot.
