### Investigation Plan: NordicStream Discount Cohort Analysis

**1. Isolate the Cohort and Define the Baseline**
Stop comparing the September discount cohort to the "historical average" of all users. That comparison is flawed because it mixes organic users with promotion seekers.
*   **Action:** Create a strict cohort of users who converted in September *only* via the 50% discount code.
*   **Comparison:** Compare this group against a control group of users who converted in September *without* the discount (if volume exists) or against the same "promotion-seeker" profile from previous years. If no clean control exists, compare their behavior *during* the trial month to their behavior *after* the price hike.

**2. Diagnose the "Value Gap" vs. "Price Shock"**
You must distinguish between users who didn't see value and users who saw value but refused to pay full price.
*   **Metric A (Engagement Depth):** Measure the "Core Action Frequency" (e.g., hours streamed, titles completed) for the churned group in Week 2 and Week 3 of their subscription.
    *   *Hypothesis:* If engagement was low (<2 sessions/week) before cancellation, the issue is **product fit/content dissatisfaction**. The discount merely accelerated a churn that would have happened anyway.
    *   *Hypothesis:* If engagement was high (>4 sessions/week) but they still churned at the price jump, the issue is **price sensitivity**. They loved the product but not at full price.
*   **Metric B (The Cliff Event):** Track the exact day of cancellation relative to the billing date.
    *   *Signal:* A spike in cancellations 2 days *before* the renewal charge indicates price shock. Cancellations spread randomly throughout the month indicate content dissatisfaction.

**3. Triangulate the Contradictory Survey Data**
Self-reported survey data is often unreliable ("I didn't like the content" vs. "It was too expensive").
*   **Action:** Re-segment the 200 survey responses by the **Engagement Depth** metric defined above.
*   **Analysis:** Do high-engagement churners cite price? Do low-engagement churners cite content? Ignore the aggregate average; the truth lies in the correlation between *behavior* and *stated reason*.

**4. Calculate the Revised Unit Economics (LTV)**
Present two scenarios to the CEO/Finance:
*   **Scenario A (Bargain Hunters):** If low engagement drove churn, the LTV of this cohort is negative once marketing costs are included. The 8% conversion was vanity; these users were never viable.
*   **Scenario B (Price Sensitive):** If high engagement drove churn, calculate the break-even retention rate at full price. If 40% retention at $X price yields less LTV than 65% retention at $0.5X price, the discount strategy destroys value.

**This Week's Steps:**
1.  **Query:** Pull engagement logs for the September discount cohort, segmented by those who renewed vs. those who churned.
2.  **Define:** Establish the "Minimum Viable Engagement" threshold (e.g., 3 streams/month) that correlates with retention in your historical data.
3.  **Decision Rule:** If >60% of churned users were below the "Minimum Viable Engagement" threshold before cancelling, **stop the discount strategy immediately**; it attracts non-viable users. If >60% were above the threshold, **test a graduated price increase** (e.g., 25% off month 2) rather than a hard cliff.

The 8% conversion rate is a vanity metric if the resulting LTV is negative. Focus entirely on the behavior of the 60% who left.

*Note: Do not present the 40% renewal rate in isolation; it is meaningless without the engagement context. Ensure all user IDs used in this analysis are pseudonymized before sharing with Finance.*
