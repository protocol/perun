**Recommendation: Adopt "Certified Skill Application" as the Q2 North Star Metric**

Do not select "Course Completions" or "Weekly Active Minutes" as your primary goal. Completions are a vanity metric in this context because they penalize users who efficiently extract specific value (skimming) without finishing every module, while potentially rewarding those who finish without learning. Active minutes measure effort, not outcome. Instead, define your North Star as **the weekly count of users who both earn a certification AND verify a career outcome** (e.g., updating a LinkedIn profile with the credential, downloading a portfolio asset, or self-reporting a specific skill application within 14 days).

This metric satisfies the requirement for a single customer-value indicator because it rises only when a user achieves the platform's core promise: career advancement. It bridges the gap between immediate engagement (earning the cert) and long-term retention (career utility). If users skim content but still achieve the career outcome, this metric captures their success; if they complete every module but never apply the skill, it correctly identifies a lack of real value.

**Implementation Plan for April 12th Steering Committee:**

1.  **Redefine the Funnel:** Shift the primary measurement focus from "Activation = Start Course" to "Activation = First Career Verification Event." Retention must be measured as the recurrence of these verification events or progression to advanced tracks, not just return visits.
2.  **Instrument the "Outcome" Event Immediately:** You currently track completions and minutes. You do not yet track the correlation between certificate sharing and retention. Before April 5th, instrument a specific event: `career_outcome_verified`. Properties must include `outcome_type` (social_share, profile_update, self_report) and `days_to_outcome`. Without this data, any claim about social sharing correlating to retention is `UNVERIFIED`.
3.  **Establish the Baseline:** Do not seek external benchmarks for "good" application rates. Calculate the current baseline using historical data for users who manually updated profiles or shared certs, even if imperfect. Compare Q2 performance strictly against this internal baseline and the week-over-week trend.
4.  **Reject Vanity Growth:** If marketing drives a spike in signups (Acquisition) but the rate of `career_outcome_verified` per active user drops, the product is leaking value. Do not pour more budget into acquisition until the ratio of completions to verified outcomes stabilizes or improves.

**Immediate Next Steps (This Week):**
*   **Define the Schema:** Finalize the exact definition and technical properties of the `career_outcome_verified` event with engineering. Ensure the user identifier used for this event is stable across sessions but pseudonymized, routing any PII collection to your privacy compliance review.
*   **Build the Cohort Query:** Construct the dashboard query that segments users by "Certified" vs. "Certified + Outcome Verified" to visualize the drop-off.
*   **Pre-commit Experiment Design:** If you plan to test incentives for social sharing to boost this metric, pre-declare the sample size and duration. Do not peek at results after two days; a mid-test spike in sharing is not a win until the pre-committed endpoint confirms a lift in long-term retention without regressing guardrail metrics like course quality ratings.
