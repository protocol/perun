**Proposal: Incremental Touchpoint Valuation Framework for Q3 Budget Allocation**

**Objective**
Resolve the attribution conflict between Marketing (early-stage influence) and Sales (closing interaction) by shifting from "credit assignment" to "incremental value measurement." This approach prevents double-counting revenue while quantifying the specific lift provided by webinars, whitepapers, and demos without requiring a true holdout test on enterprise clients.

**Methodology: The "Necessary Condition" & Time-Decay Hybrid**

1.  **Define the Baseline Conversion Rate (The Counterfactual)**
    Instead of removing clients from the pipeline (which is risky for enterprise relationships), we will establish a statistical baseline using historical data from deals that closed *without* specific early-stage touches.
    *   Calculate the close rate and average sales cycle length for opportunities that entered the pipeline via direct sales outreach or referrals only (no marketing content).
    *   This group represents the "organic" close probability. Any improvement in close rate or reduction in cycle time for deals *with* marketing touches represents the incremental lift.

2.  **Assign Value via "Stage-Gated Necessity"**
    Rather than splitting the $4.5M revenue pie arbitrarily, we assign value based on whether a touchpoint moved a deal from one stage to the next faster than the baseline.
    *   **Awareness (Whitepapers):** Measure the *acceleration* effect. If deals with whitepaper downloads move from "Lead" to "Qualified" 15% faster than the baseline, attribute 15% of the *value of that stage transition* to the whitepaper. If the deal would have qualified anyway at the same speed, the incremental value is zero.
    *   **Consideration (Webinars):** Measure the *expansion* effect. Compare the average deal size of opportunities attending webinars vs. the baseline. If webinar attendees close at 20% higher ACV, attribute that specific premium to the webinar.
    *   **Decision (Demos):** Measure the *conversion* effect. Since Sales owns the last interaction, attribute the final close probability lift here. However, cap the total attributed revenue at 100% per deal by treating earlier touches as "enablers" that increased the probability of reaching the demo, not as separate revenue generators.

3.  **The "First-Match, Last-Match, Shared-Uplift" Rule**
    To satisfy both teams without double-counting:
    *   **Sales** retains credit for the final conversion event (the demo/close).
    *   **Marketing** receives credit only for the *delta* they created.
    *   *Calculation:* If a deal closes for $100k:
        *   Baseline close probability (no marketing): 10%. Expected value: $10k.
        *   Actual close probability (with marketing touches): 25%. Expected value: $25k.
        *   **Incremental Lift:** $15k. This $15k is attributed to Marketing's touchpoints.
        *   **Base Value:** The remaining $85k (or the base probability portion) is attributed to Sales/Product fit.
    *   This ensures the sum of parts never exceeds the whole ($100k), satisfying the no double-counting rule.

4.  **Implementation for the $12M Pipeline**
    *   Tag all 300 active opportunities with their specific touchpoint history.
    *   Segment them into "Marketing-Touched" vs. "Sales-Only" cohorts.
    *   Run a weekly regression analysis comparing the velocity and close rates of these two cohorts.
    *   Allocate Q3 budget proportional to the calculated *incremental lift* per channel, not the volume of touches. If whitepapers show no acceleration over the baseline, budget shifts to channels that do.

**Outcome**
This method provides the CRO with a defensible, data-driven budget allocation. It validates Marketing's role in accelerating deals and expanding size while respecting Sales' role in closing. It relies entirely on your internal historical baselines, requiring no external benchmarks or risky client holdouts.

*Note: Ensure CRM data hygiene is verified before running the cohort analysis, as missing touchpoint logs will skew the baseline comparison.*
