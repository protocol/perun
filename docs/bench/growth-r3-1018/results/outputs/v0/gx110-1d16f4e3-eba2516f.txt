### Revised Activation Definition & Validation Plan

**Proposed Activation Criterion:**
Shift from "Publishes Site" to **"Publishes Site AND completes one 'Stickiness' Action within 7 days."**
Based on your survey data (60% of churned publishers never edited), the new definition must require evidence of *ongoing intent*, not just a one-time setup. Recommended candidate actions to test as the second pillar:
1.  **Content Update:** Editing the published site or adding a new page/post after the initial publish.
2.  **Value Connection:** Connecting a custom domain or adding a lead-capture element (contact form/signup block).
3.  **Return Visit:** Logging back in within 48 hours of publishing to make a change.

**Validation Method (Historical Cohort Analysis):**
Do not guess the threshold; calculate it using your existing data.
1.  **Isolate the Cohort:** Pull all users who signed up 6–12 months ago (ensuring enough time to measure long-term retention).
2.  **Simulate Candidates:** For each candidate action (e.g., "Connected Domain"), tag users who performed it within 7 days of signup.
3.  **Compare Retention Curves:** Plot the Month-1 and Month-3 retention rates for:
    *   Group A: Published only (Current definition).
    *   Group B: Published + Candidate Action.
4.  **Select the Winner:** The robust activation metric is the candidate action where Group B's retention rate is statistically significantly higher than Group A's, while still capturing a viable volume of users (aim for >10–12% of total signups to ensure the metric isn't too narrow).

**Threshold Setting:**
Avoid arbitrary benchmarks like "industry standard." Your threshold is defined by the **lift in retention** relative to your baseline.
*   **Baseline:** Your current 18% activation yields a specific Month-1 retention rate (e.g., X%).
*   **Target:** The new definition should identify a smaller group of users who exhibit a Month-1 retention rate of at least **1.5x to 2x** the baseline rate.
*   **Verification:** If the new group is too small (<5% of signups) or the retention lift is negligible (<10% improvement), the action is either too hard to achieve or not predictive enough. Iterate to a different candidate action.

**This Week's Steps:**
1.  **Query:** Run a SQL/Analytics query comparing Month-1 retention for users who "Published Only" vs. "Published + Edited Content" vs. "Published + Connected Domain" for the last 9 months of data.
2.  **Decide:** Select the single action combination that maximizes the retention lift while keeping the activation rate above 10%.
3.  **Instrument:** Ensure the event taxonomy explicitly tracks the timestamp of the *second* action to verify it occurs within the 7-day window; if this event is missing, mark the metric as `UNVERIFIED` until instrumentation is deployed.

**Note:** Do not adopt a new metric until the historical backfill confirms it predicts retention better than the current one; publishing volume alone is a vanity metric if it doesn't correlate with stickiness.
