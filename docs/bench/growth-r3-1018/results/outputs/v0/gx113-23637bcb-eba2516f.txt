**Recommendation: Establish a "New Baseline" with a Bridging Footnote**

Do not recalibrate historical data to match the new definition. Retroactively altering twenty years of records to absorb a 15% discrepancy introduces unverifiable assumptions and corrupts the long-term trend line. Instead, present the migration as a formal **baseline reset**.

**1. The Narrative: Quality Over Continuity**
Frame the 15% drop not as lost traffic, but as **increased measurement fidelity**. Your legacy system counted 2,000 daily bot visits as valid; your new system correctly identifies them as noise. The "loss" is actually a gain in data integrity. Present the new baseline as the first accurate reflection of *human* readership, which is the only metric that matters for digital subscriptions.

**2. The Presentation Strategy**
*   **The Cut-off:** Declare the start of the new event stream as "Day 1" for all digital growth projections.
*   **The Bridge:** Include a single appendix slide showing the three-month overlap. Explicitly label the 2,000 daily visits as "Filtered Bot Traffic" and show the net human traffic side-by-side. This proves the new system is working, not failing.
*   **The Projection:** Build fiscal year targets strictly on the new, cleaner baseline. Do not attempt to force a year-over-year percentage growth calculation against the old, inflated numbers. Instead, report YoY growth as "N/A due to methodology upgrade" for this quarter, with a note that *verified human engagement* is the new standard.

**3. Addressing the Leadership Concern**
When the Editor-in-Chief asks about the missing 15%, your answer is: "We stopped counting robots." Explain that subscription revenue comes from humans, and the old dashboard was diluting our view of the actual addressable market by 15%. The new framework protects the business from making decisions based on inflated vanity metrics.

**Immediate Next Steps for Your Meeting:**
*   **Define the North Star:** Shift the primary metric from "Total Page Views" to "Verified Unique User Sessions" (excluding the flagged suspicious visits).
*   **Instrument the Gap:** Ensure the new system tags the 2,000 filtered visits with a specific "bot/suspicious" property so you can audit the filter logic later if needed, but keep them out of the core growth dashboard.
*   **Set the Guardrail:** Commit to reviewing the bot-filter rules quarterly to ensure legitimate users aren't being accidentally flagged, but hold the line that the current baseline is the truth.

**Note:** This approach sacrifices a continuous historical chart to gain a reliable forward-looking compass, which is essential for a subscription model.
