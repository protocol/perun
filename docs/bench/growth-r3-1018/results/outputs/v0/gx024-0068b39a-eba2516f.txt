**Primary Outcome Metric: Delivered-to-Open Conversion Rate**
Do not track "Authentication Pass Rate" or "Delivery Rate" as your success metric; these are vanity metrics that rise regardless of user value. Instead, track the percentage of *successfully delivered* emails that result in an *open*. This isolates the variable you are testing: does fixing the technical delivery actually unlock engagement, or is content relevance the bottleneck? If authentication rises to 98% but the Delivered-to-Open rate remains flat, the feature is technically successful but commercially neutral, proving content is the dominant factor.

**Guardrail Metrics (Unintended Consequences)**
1.  **Spam Complaint Rate per 1,000 Delivered:** A rise here indicates your new authentication logic is inadvertently flagging legitimate mail or altering headers in a way that triggers provider filters. Set a hard stop if this rate increases by more than 10% over the pre-launch baseline.
2.  **Unsubscribe Rate per 1,000 Delivered:** If delivery improves but users immediately opt-out, it suggests the content reaching the inbox is still irrelevant or perceived as spammy due to the delivery change. This protects against the scenario where "delivery improves but engagement plummets."

**Measurement Protocol**
Compare the beta cohort (5,000 users) against a holdout group with identical sending patterns but without the new feature. Do not compare pre-launch vs. post-launch aggregates, as seasonality or list fatigue could skew results. The "win" condition is a statistically significant lift in the Delivered-to-Open rate for the beta group while both guardrails remain within ±5% of the control group. If the primary metric does not move, the initiative is UNVERIFIED as a growth driver, regardless of the 98% authentication achievement.

**Note:** Ensure user identifiers used for cohorting are pseudonymous; route any PII handling to your privacy compliance review. Do not peek at results before the pre-committed sample size is reached to avoid false positives.
