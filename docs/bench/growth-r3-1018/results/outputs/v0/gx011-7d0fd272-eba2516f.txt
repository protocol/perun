**Decision: Do not roll out permanently yet; revert to the old version for the New Year flash sale.**

The 12% lift is `UNVERIFIED` as a measure of design success because the concurrent 40% page load spike on December 24 introduces a severe negative confounder. You cannot isolate the design variable from the performance variable with the current aggregate data. A simplified form (design win) likely offset some friction caused by slow loading (performance loss), masking the true potential of the new flow. Rolling out now risks cementing a design that may only be "net positive" because it survived a performance hit, rather than proving it drives the targeted 15% lift on its own. Conversely, if the design itself is weak, the performance crash hid the failure, and a clean rollout will underperform.

**Analysis of Variables**
1.  **Design Variable:** The hypothesis was a ≥15% lift. The observed net result was 12%.
2.  **Performance Variable:** A 40% load time spike typically correlates with significant conversion drop-off (often exceeding 10-20% depending on baseline).
3.  **Interaction:** The observed 12% lift is likely the result of `True Design Lift` minus `Performance Drag`. If the performance drag was substantial, the `True Design Lift` could still be >15%. However, without segmenting users who experienced the slowdown versus those who did not, this remains an unproven assumption.

**Immediate Actions (This Week)**
*   **Revert for New Year Sale:** Restore the stable, known checkout flow to ensure maximum reliability during the next high-traffic event. Do not test two variables (design + infrastructure stability) simultaneously during critical revenue windows.
*   **Segmented Retrospective Query:** Instruct analytics to split the Dec 20–26 data into two cohorts: "Normal Load" (Dec 20–23, 25–26) and "High Load" (Dec 24). Compare the conversion lift of the new flow *only* during normal load periods. If the lift during normal periods exceeds 15%, the design is valid, and the issue was purely infrastructure. If the lift remains <15% even during normal loads, the design hypothesis failed.
*   **Controlled Re-test:** Schedule a new A/B test for mid-January (low traffic) with a pre-committed sample size and a strict guardrail metric for page load time. Only declare a win if the design lifts conversion ≥15% *and* performance remains within baseline SLOs.

**Tripwire for Future Tests**
Define a hard stop rule: If page load time increases by >10% in any experimental variant, the test pauses automatically regardless of conversion trends. This prevents performance degradation from corrupting design learnings.

The current data proves the new flow is resilient, not that it is optimal. Validate the design in a stable environment before committing engineering resources to a permanent rollout.
