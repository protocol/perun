**Decision: Do not adopt the emotional headline style based on current data.**

The observed 25% lift is **UNVERIFIED** as a causal effect of the headline style. The massive external traffic surge (200%) during a major election cycle introduces a critical confounder: **composition bias**. Your assumption that randomization balances external spikes holds only if the *type* of user arriving remains consistent. In breaking news scenarios, the influx often consists of high-intent, transient users who click indiscriminately regardless of headline tone. If the treatment group inadvertently captured a higher proportion of these "surge" users due to timing or referral source skew, the lift reflects user intent, not headline efficacy. Adopting this variant risks optimizing for noise, potentially alienating core readers once the news cycle calms.

**Salvage Strategy & Next Steps**

You cannot statistically "adjust" for this post-hoc without granular data on user intent or referral sources that isolates the surge effect. Instead, proceed with the following three actions this week:

1.  **Segment by User Cohort (Immediate Analysis):**
    Query your analytics to separate **returning users** (your stable baseline) from **new users** (the volatile surge). Recalculate the click-through rate (CTR) lift *only* for returning users. If the lift disappears or shrinks significantly in this cohort, the original 25% was driven entirely by the external news surge. This provides the evidence needed to tell leadership the result is not generalizable.

2.  **Define a Guardrail for Future Tests:**
    Establish a protocol that pauses or flags A/B tests when daily traffic deviates >20% from the 30-day rolling average. High-variance environments invalidate standard sample size calculations because the underlying population distribution shifts mid-test.

3.  **Re-test Under Calmer Conditions:**
    Propose a re-run of the test for 14 days during a non-election period with a pre-committed sample size based on normal traffic volumes. Pre-declare **CTR of returning users** as the primary metric to insulate against future traffic composition shifts.
