The 85/15 traffic split fundamentally compromises the validity of your conclusion for three specific reasons that volume cannot fix:

An uneven split amplifies the risk that external factors (e.g., a weekend spike, a specific mobile OS update, or marketing campaign timing) disproportionately affected the larger variant group. With such a severe imbalance, standard statistical controls often fail to fully isolate the treatment effect from these temporal or segment-based confounders. You cannot verify post-hoc if the 2.5% lift was caused by the code change or by the variant group accidentally capturing a higher proportion of high-intent users during peak hours.
2.  **Statistical Power & Precision:** While the variant group is large, the effective sample size of an A/B test is driven heavily by the smaller group (the control). With only 15% in control, your ability to detect a true difference with precision is severely reduced, resulting in the wide confidence intervals you observed. A "directional signal" is insufficient for a permanent roadmap decision; it is merely a hypothesis generator. Acting on it risks deploying a change that provides no value or, worse, degrades experience for the majority of users.
3.  **Experiment Rigor:** A valid experiment requires a pre-committed design where the analysis plan matches the data collection. Analyzing a test with a known configuration error violates the principle of rigorous measurement. The result is technically `UNVERIFIED` because the randomization assumption—the bedrock of causal inference—is suspect.
