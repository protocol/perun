**Recommendation: Do not mandate biometrics yet; keep the flow optional while investigating the "verification failed" spike on older devices.**

The 7% reduction in drop-off confirms that removing SMS friction improves activation for many users. However, the surge in support tickets for older phone models indicates a critical segmentation failure. Mandating biometrics now would trade a general conversion gain for a hard block on a specific user cohort, likely increasing total churn among those users and inflating support costs beyond the value of the saved drop-offs.

**Trade-off Analysis for the COO:**

*   **User Experience:** The net experience is positive for modern device users (faster, seamless) but catastrophic for older device users (blocked entirely). A mandatory rollout converts a "friction" issue (SMS wait) into a "failure" issue (cannot verify) for a subset of the population.
*   **Support Costs:** The current optional model contains the cost spike to users with older devices who encounter errors. A mandatory rollout would force *all* older-device users into this error state, potentially multiplying support volume. Until we distinguish whether this is a fixable bug or a hardware limitation, the cost liability is undefined.
*   **Security:** Biometrics generally offer superior security to SMS (preventing SIM swapping). However, security is irrelevant if the user cannot complete the onboarding. The "verification failed" error suggests the authentication attempt is not completing, leaving the account in an unverified limbo rather than securely verified or securely rejected.

**Immediate Next Steps (This Week):**
1.  **Segment the Data:** Isolate the drop-off and error rates specifically for the "older phone model" cohort versus the general population to quantify the exact trade-off.
2.  **Define the Error:** Categorize the "verification failed" tickets to determine if they stem from a technical bug (e.g., SDK crash) or a usability gap (e.g., users not understanding the prompt).
3.  **Decision Trigger:** If the error is a bug, fix it and re-test. If it is a hardware limitation, maintain the optional SMS fallback permanently for those devices.

*Note: The 7% figure is an aggregate; the decision depends entirely on the segmented performance of the older device cohort.*
