**Recommendation: Reframe the North Star from "Time in App" to "Value Delivered per Shift"**

The conflicting signals you observe are not a failure; they are a validation of your hypothesis. Your team assumed faster planning would increase app time by enabling more deliveries. The data suggests the opposite mechanism is occurring: the feature is successfully reducing friction, allowing drivers to complete tasks *outside* the app faster. In logistics, time spent in the app is often a cost (overhead), not a value signal. A drop in session duration coupled with high adoption (42% vs 15% target) strongly indicates the "Quick-Route" feature is working as an efficiency tool, not a engagement trap. Marcus's concern about disengagement is a classic confusion of "vanity metrics" (time spent) with "customer value" (deliveries completed).

To resolve this for the executive team, you must immediately shift your measurement framework:

1.  **Redefine the North Star:** Stop measuring "Total Time in App." Adopt **"Deliveries Completed per Driver per Shift"** as your primary North Star. This metric rises only when the driver gets real value (finishing work) and the business benefits (higher throughput). If session time drops but deliveries per shift rise, the product is a success.
2.  **Analyze the Funnel Bottom-Up (Retention First):** Do not look at acquisition or adoption rates yet. Verify **Retention of the Core Action**: Are drivers who use "Quick-Route" returning to complete full shifts at the same or higher rate than non-users? If retention is stable or up, the shorter sessions are efficient, not indicative of churn.
3.  **Evaluate Revenue/Time, but Carefully:** Your instinct to measure "Revenue per Minute" is directionally correct but operationally risky if used as a real-time dashboard metric. Instead, calculate **"Revenue per Shift"** or **"Cost per Delivery."** If the average session dropped from 28 to 19 minutes (a ~32% reduction in overhead) and deliveries per shift increased by even 10%, your unit economics have improved.
4.  **Immediate Action Plan (This Week):**
    *   **Query:** Segment your fleet into "Quick-Route Users" and "Non-Users." Compare the **average deliveries completed per shift** and **total shift duration** (clock-in to clock-out, not just app time) between these two cohorts for the period Oct 12–Present.
    *   **Event Taxonomy:** Ensure you are tracking the event `delivery_completed` with a property `route_method` (quick-route vs standard). If you cannot link completed deliveries to the routing method used, this data gap is `UNVERIFIED` and must be instrumented immediately.
    *   **Decision Rule:** If `deliveries_per_shift` for users is ≥ non-users, the drop in session time is a win. Present this to Marcus as "reduced overhead," not "disengagement."

**Note:** Do not fabricate a benchmark for "good" session duration; compare only against your own pre-Oct 12 baseline. If you lack the `delivery_completed` event linked to route types, you cannot make a causal claim yet—prioritize that instrumentation over further dashboard analysis.
