**Risk of False Positive (Type I Error)**
Shipping based on this data carries a high risk that the "18% reduction" is an illusion caused by variance in rare events. With only 14 clients and infrequent downtime, a single facility having a "good winter" due to weather rather than your software could skew the entire result. If you ship a feature that does not actually work:
1.  **Revenue Risk:** You risk churn if the feature fails to deliver promised uptime to enterprise clients paying €45,000/year.
2.  **Credibility Risk:** In B2B industrial software, false alarms or missed predictions erode trust faster than in consumer apps.
3.  **Opportunity Cost:** Engineering resources will shift to maintaining a flawed feature rather than fixing the actual algorithm.

**Why Bayesian Methods Are Not a Magic Bullet**
While Bayesian methods allow you to incorporate prior beliefs (e.g., "we expect maintenance alerts to help"), they cannot manufacture signal from silence. With only 14 clients and events occurring "a few times a year," your data likelihood is too weak to overcome any reasonable prior. A Bayesian analysis would likely yield a posterior distribution so wide that the probability of the feature having *negative* or *zero* impact remains substantial. It would not give you the certainty needed for a Q1 enterprise mandate.
