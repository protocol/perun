### Weekly Monitoring Plan: New Pricing Model Viability

To assess the financial viability of the extended discount before long-term data exists, track these four leading indicators weekly. These metrics predict whether the higher conversion rate justifies the risk of post-discount churn.

**1. The "Box 4 Cliff" Retention Rate**
*   **What it is:** The percentage of users who remain subscribed *immediately after* receiving their fourth discounted box.
*   **Why it matters:** This is your earliest signal of "discount hunter" behavior. If users cancel the moment the full price hits (after box 4), your lifetime value (LTV) will collapse regardless of how many signed up.
*   **Target:** Compare this week's rate against the baseline group (users who only got one discounted box). If the new group's retention at Box 4 is significantly lower than the old group's retention at Box 1, the strategy is attracting low-quality customers.

**2. Cost to Acquire a *Paying* Subscriber (CPA-Paid)**
*   **What it is:** Total marketing spend divided only by users who convert to a *full-price* plan, not just those who start the trial.
*   **Why it matters:** Your conversion target is rising from 25% to 35%, but if the discount is too deep, you may pay more to acquire a customer who leaves immediately.
*   **Target:** This number must stay flat or decrease. If CPA-Paid rises while conversion rises, the extra volume is too expensive to sustain.

**3. Early Engagement Drop-off (Weeks 3–4)**
*   **What it is:** The rate of skipped deliveries, paused subscriptions, or customer support tickets regarding "how to cancel" during the third and fourth weeks of the subscription.
*   **Why it matters:** Users intending to churn after the discount often stop engaging before they formally cancel. A spike in skips or cancellation inquiries in week 3 is a leading indicator that the "Box 4 Cliff" will be steep.
*   **Target:** Watch for a divergence from the control group. If the new group shows higher hesitation in weeks 3–4 than the old group did in week 1, expect higher churn next month.

**4. Contribution Margin per Active Subscriber (Week 4)**
*   **What it is:** Revenue from the subscriber in their fourth week minus the direct cost of goods and shipping for that box.
*   **Why it matters:** This tells the CFO if the unit economics hold up deep into the discount period. Even if they stay, are they profitable by the time the discount ends?
*   **Target:** Ensure this figure does not drop below your break-even point. If you are losing money on every box up to box 4, the customer must stay significantly longer to repay that debt, which is risky if retention is weak.

**Recommended Action for This Week:**
Pull a cohort report comparing the "New Pricing" group vs. the "Old Pricing" group specifically for **cancellation intent signals** (skips/pauses) in weeks 3 and 4. If the new group shows >15% higher hesitation than the baseline, recommend pausing the test expansion before the holiday rush to prevent locking in unprofitable subscribers.

*Note: Do not rely on total sign-up counts; rising sign-ups with falling Box 4 retention is a vanity trap. All benchmarks must be calculated against your own historical control group, not industry averages.*
