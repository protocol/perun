### Success Definition: PayBridge Instant Conversion Launch (Vietnam & Thailand)

**1. North Star Metric (Customer Value)**
*   **Weekly Active Transacting Merchants (WATM):** Count of unique small businesses completing at least one instant conversion transaction per week.
    *   *Why:* Raw transaction volume ($500k target) is a vanity metric that can be skewed by one-off large transfers. WATM proves recurring value adoption. If this rises, the business grows; if volume rises but WATM stagnates, you have one-time users, not a sustainable product.

**2. Input Metrics (The Funnel, Read Bottom-Up)**
*   **Retention (Primary Diagnostic):** **Week-2 Repeat Rate.** Percentage of merchants who perform a second instant conversion within 14 days of their first.
    *   *Hypothesis Test:* If speed is the driver, retention should be high immediately. If fees matter more, retention will lag until merchants perceive total cost savings.
*   **Activation:** **Time-to-First-Conversion.** Duration from feature access to first successful instant swap.
    *   *Target:* <24 hours. Long delays suggest friction in onboarding or trust barriers, not just product availability.
*   **Acquisition:** **Feature Adoption Rate.** Percentage of eligible existing merchants in VN/TH who attempt the feature within the first 30 days.

**3. Viability & Guardrail Metrics**
*   **Average Transaction Size (ATS):** Monitor for a "small ticket" skew. Small businesses may use instant conversion only for emergency micro-transactions if fees are too high, limiting total volume potential.
*   **Failure/Revert Rate:** Percentage of transactions where the user abandons the instant flow and reverts to the standard 48-hour option mid-process. This directly validates the "speed vs. fee" trade-off in real-time.
*   **Guardrail:** **Cost-per-Transaction Margin.** Ensure the push for volume does not erode margins below sustainability due to liquidity costs of instant settlement.

**4. Cultural & Market Context for Data Interpretation**
*   **Trust over Speed (Vietnam):** In emerging markets, "instant" can sometimes signal risk or lack of audit trails. If activation is low but retention is high among those who do try it, the barrier is initial trust, not product utility. Do not interpret low adoption as "speed doesn't matter"; it may mean "speed feels unsafe."
*   **Relationship-Driven Finance (Thailand):** Merchants often rely on personal banking relationships. A spike in support tickets or manual inquiries alongside digital usage indicates a need for hybrid human-digital reassurance. High drop-off rates during the first transaction may reflect a need for local language confirmation steps rather than interface issues.
*   **Fee Sensitivity vs. Cash Flow:** Small businesses in these regions often operate on thin cash flows. If the "Revert Rate" is high, it confirms that while speed is desired, the fee premium is prohibitive for daily operations. The data will likely show a bifurcation: high-speed usage for urgent supplier payments, and standard usage for routine settlements.

**5. Immediate Next Steps (Before Nov 12)**
*   **Instrument the "Revert" Event:** Tag every instance where a user starts the instant flow but switches to standard. This is the single most important signal for the speed-vs-fee debate.
*   **Define Cohorts by Transaction Size:** Pre-segment your analysis into "Micro" (<$500), "Small" ($500-$5k), and "Medium" (>$5k). Cultural adoption patterns often differ drastically by ticket size in Southeast Asia.
*   **Establish Baseline:** Record the current Week-2 Repeat Rate for standard 48-hour transactions in these regions to compare against the instant feature's performance.

*Note: Do not fabricate benchmarks for "good" retention rates; compare the new feature's performance strictly against your own existing 48-hour transaction baseline in these specific countries.*
