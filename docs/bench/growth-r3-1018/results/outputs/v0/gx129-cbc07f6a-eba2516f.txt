**Primary Metric: 4-Week Repeat Booking Rate by Cohort**

For a two-sided marketplace like PawsSchedule, **Gross Merchandise Value (GMV)** is a vanity metric in the seed stage; it can be inflated by marketing spend on users who never return, masking a leaky bucket. Your assumption is correct: **Repeat Booking Rate** is the true North Star. It directly measures the core value proposition—trust and reliability—which is the only defensible moat in local services. Investors know that acquisition is easy; retention is hard. A high GMV with low repeat rates signals you are buying transactions, not building a business.

**The Data Story for Your Pitch:**
Do not present a single aggregate percentage. Instead, present **cohort retention curves**. Group users by their first booking month (e.g., "January Cohort," "February Cohort") and track what percentage booked a second, third, and fourth time within 4 weeks.
*   **Why this wins:** It proves product-market fit by showing that value increases over time. If your "January Cohort" has a 40% repeat rate by week 4, and your "March Cohort" has 45%, you demonstrate improving product quality and network effects.
*   **The Input Metrics:** Pair this North Star with two inputs: (1) **Walker Response Time** (supply side health) and (2) **Activation Rate** (percentage of signups who complete a first booking). These explain *why* the repeat rate is moving.

**Addressing the Seasonal Unknown:**
Do not normalize the data yet; you lack the historical baseline to do so accurately. Instead, use the six-week runway to **establish the pre-summer baseline**.
1.  **Isolate the variable:** Track the repeat rate of users who booked *before* the summer holiday surge versus those acquired *during* it.
2.  **The Narrative:** Tell investors, "We are currently establishing our baseline repeat rate at X%. Our hypothesis is that summer demand will strain supply, potentially dipping retention to Y%. We are stress-testing our walker supply now to ensure the curve stays flat." This turns an unknown risk into a demonstrated operational discipline.

**Immediate Actions (This Week):**
1.  **Query your database** to generate the 4-week repeat booking rate for the last three monthly cohorts. If this data is not instantly available, your instrumentation is insufficient for a seed raise; prioritize this query over any new marketing campaigns.
2.  **Define the "Repeat" event strictly:** A repeat is a *paid* booking by the *same* owner with *any* walker within 30 days of their previous booking. Exclude cancellations.
3.  **Freeze the GMV narrative:** Use GMV only to show total market size captured, but explicitly label it as an output of retention, not a primary health indicator.

**Verdict:** Lead with the cohort retention curve. If the curve flattens or rises, you have product-market fit. If it drops to zero after the first booking, no amount of GMV will secure your seed round.

*Note: Ensure your user identifiers are pseudonymous when sharing cohort data with the advisory board to maintain privacy compliance.*
*Do not fabricate a "good" retention benchmark; compare your current curve only against your own previous months.*
