### Framework for Evaluating the Date Picker Experiment

**1. Primary Metric Status: Unverified Win**
Report the 3.2% lift in bookings as **statistically inconclusive**. Because the confidence interval (-1% to 7.4%) crosses zero, the result is indistinguishable from noise at standard significance levels. Do not present this as a "win" or a "loss." Explicitly state that the test failed to validate the hypothesis of a ≥5% lift. The wide variance indicates the sample size (12,000 DAU over 15 days) was insufficient to detect the targeted effect size with high confidence, or that external variance (noise) overwhelmed the signal.

**2. Required Contextual Data Points (The "Why" Behind the Variance)**
To explain the uncertainty to the CEO and Regional Director, gather and plot the following before the meeting:
*   **Daily Time-Series Plot:** Overlay daily booking rates for both control and variant groups. Look specifically for a divergence or convergence during the "second week" you suspected was affected by a local holiday. If both lines dip simultaneously, it confirms an external shock (seasonality) rather than a product failure.
*   **Guardrail Metrics:** Check **page load latency** and **error rates** for the new date picker. A "win" on conversion that degrades performance is a technical debt risk. Conversely, if the variant had higher errors, that explains the suppressed lift.
*   **Segmentation by Device/OS:** Break down the 3.2% lift by iOS vs. Android. If the lift is driven entirely by one OS, the "mobile" aggregate is masking a specific compatibility issue or win.

**3. Decision Framework for Saudi Arabia Rollout**
Do not roll out to desktop users in Saudi Arabia based on these results. Use this logic tree for your recommendation:
*   **Condition A (Stop):** If the daily time-series shows the variant underperforming specifically during the holiday period while the control held steady, the feature may be fragile under high-load or specific cultural contexts. **Action:** Kill the project or redesign.
*   **Condition B (Extend):** If the daily trends are parallel (both moved up or down together due to the holiday) and the point estimate remains positive (3.2%), the issue is purely sample size. **Action:** Extend the test duration in the UAE mobile segment until the confidence interval narrows below 0% on the lower bound. Do not expand geography or platform yet.
*   **Condition C (Pivot):** If the data shows the 5% assumption was flawed (e.g., the maximum possible lift appears to be ~3% even with infinite data), recalculate the ROI. If a 3% lift does not justify the engineering maintenance, **Action:** Kill the project.

**4. Immediate Next Steps**
*   **Recompute Power Analysis:** Calculate the required sample size to detect a 3.2% lift with 80% power. This will quantify exactly how many more days/weeks the UAE test needs to run.
*   **Isolate the Holiday:** Run a cohort analysis excluding the specific holiday dates. Does the lift become more stable without those days? This isolates the external factor.
