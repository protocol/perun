**Experimental Approach: Fixed-Horizon Test with Manual Sampling**

Do not attempt continuous monitoring or "peeking" at results. With one developer and limited traffic, the highest risk is a false positive caused by checking the data daily and stopping early. Instead, commit to a **fixed-horizon design**: decide the sample size and end date *before* launching, and read the data exactly once at the end.

**1. The Design (Minimizing Engineering Effort)**
*   **Duration:** 14 days. This captures two full weekly cycles (weekday/weekend donation patterns) without requiring complex seasonality adjustments.
*   **Sample Size Calculation:** To detect a 10% relative lift (from 3.0% to 3.3%) with standard statistical power (80%) and significance (5%), you need approximately **26,000 visitors per variant**.
*   **Feasibility Check:** At 800 visitors/day, you generate ~5,600 visitors/week. Over 14 days, you will reach ~11,200 total visitors. Split evenly, that is only ~5,600 per variant.
*   **The Hard Truth:** You **cannot** statistically detect a 10% lift in one month with your current traffic. The test would be underpowered, meaning a "win" could easily be noise.
*   **Pivot Strategy:** To make this viable for the board, change the success metric. Instead of a subtle 10% lift, design the test to detect a **larger effect size (e.g., 25% relative lift, moving conversion from 3% to 3.75%)**. This requires only ~3,000 visitors per variant, which is achievable in 8–9 days. If the new page cannot drive a massive 25% improvement, it is likely not worth the engineering maintenance anyway.

**2. Implementation Plan for David (4 Hours/Week)**
*   **Week 1 (Setup - 4 hrs):** Hard-code the B variant into the landing page. Use a simple cookie or URL parameter to split traffic 50/50. No complex platform integration.
*   **Weeks 2–3 (Data Collection - 0.5 hrs/week):** David does not need to build dashboards. He only needs to ensure the server logs or database correctly flag which variant a donor saw.
*   **Week 4 (Analysis - 2 hrs):** Export two numbers manually: *Total Visitors per Variant* and *Total Donations per Variant*. Calculate the conversion rate and confidence interval using a standard online calculator.

**3. Manual Data Points to Collect**
If automation is impossible, David must manually extract these four figures at the end of the test:
1.  **Variant A Visitors:** Count of unique users shown the control page.
2.  **Variant A Donations:** Count of completed transactions from Variant A.
3.  **Variant B Visitors:** Count of unique users shown the new page.
4.  **Variant B Donations:** Count of completed transactions from Variant B.
*Do not track intermediate clicks or time-on-page; they add engineering overhead and do not answer the primary question of revenue impact.*

**4. Framing Limitations for the Board**
Present the findings with this specific narrative to justify budget:
*   **The Constraint:** "We tested for a 'home run' (25% lift) because our current traffic volume cannot reliably detect small improvements without expensive tooling or more engineering time."
*   **The Result:** If the test wins, it proves the new design is a major breakthrough worth scaling. If the test is inconclusive or loses, it proves that **our current traffic volume is too low to iterate quickly.**
*   **The Ask:** "To run sophisticated, continuous experiments that detect smaller 5–10% gains (which compound over time), we need to invest in automated experimentation infrastructure or increase traffic acquisition. Our current manual process limits us to detecting only massive changes."

This approach protects David from maintenance hell, avoids statistical errors from peeking, and turns a potentially "failed" small-lift test into a concrete business case for more resources.

*Note: Ensure the traffic split is truly random (e.g., based on session ID hash) to avoid selection bias. Do not switch variants mid-test.*
