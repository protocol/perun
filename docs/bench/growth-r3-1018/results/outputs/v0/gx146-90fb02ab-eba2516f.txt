### Growth Measurement Strategy for CreatorHub Hyper-Growth

**1. The North Star: Weekly Active Creators Completing a "Value Loop"**
Reject "Daily Active Users" (DAU) as your primary metric; it is a vanity metric that rises with marketing spend even if product value decays. As you scale from 10k to 1M users, DAU will obscure the dilution of user quality.
*   **New North Star:** *Weekly Active Creators who complete a full Value Loop.*
*   **Definition:** A user who uploads/creates content AND receives at least one meaningful community interaction (comment, share, or collaboration request) within 7 days.
*   **Why:** This measures *realized value*, not just presence. It aligns with your hypothesis that community interaction drives retention. If this number rises, the business is healthy; if it falls while DAU rises, you are acquiring low-quality traffic.

**2. Input Metrics (The Levers)**
Track these three inputs to move the North Star:
*   **Activation Rate:** % of new signups who complete their first "Value Loop" within 48 hours.
*   **Community Density:** Average interactions per active creator (measures if the network effect is holding as volume grows).
*   **Retention Cohort Slope:** Week-2 and Week-4 retention rates specifically for users who *did* vs. *did not* experience a community interaction in Week 1.

**3. Diagnosing Quality vs. Quantity (The Funnel Approach)**
Do not analyze acquisition until you have secured retention. Read your funnel bottom-up:
*   **Step A (Retention First):** Segment retention cohorts by "Interaction Status." If users who interact retain at 60% but non-interactors retain at 5%, your problem is not acquisition quality; it is failing to onboard mass-market users into the community loop.
*   **Step B (Activation):** Measure the time-to-first-interaction. If this latency increases as you scale, the community is becoming noisy or unwelcoming to new users.
*   **Step C (Acquisition):** Only optimize acquisition channels once the above two are stable. Compare channels by the *quality* of users they deliver (their subsequent retention slope), not just signup volume.

**4. Event Taxonomy & Instrumentation Rules**
Instrument only events that answer specific questions. Do not track "page views."
*   **Event:** `content_created` (Properties: user_id, content_type, timestamp). *Question: Are they creating?*
*   **Event:** `community_interaction_received` (Properties: user_id, interaction_type, source_user_id). *Question: Did they get value?*
*   **Event:** `value_loop_completed` (Derived: creation + interaction within 7 days). *Question: Did the North Star move?*
*   **Identifier Seam:** Use a stable, pseudonymous `user_id` to track cohorts across sessions. Do not use raw PII here; route personal data handling to your privacy compliance protocol.

**5. Detecting Shifts in User Quality**
To prove whether early adopter behaviors predict mass market habits:
*   **Cohort Comparison:** Plot retention curves for "Early Adopters" (first 10k) vs. "Mass Market V1" (next 50k).
*   **The Signal:** If the *shape* of the curve changes (e.g., early adopters flatten at 40%, mass market drops to 10%), your product-market fit is narrowing.
*   **The Action:** If the mass market curve drops, do not spend more on ads. Investigate the "Activation" step. The mass market likely requires a different onboarding path to trigger that first community interaction.

**6. Experimentation Rigor for VC Reporting**
When testing features to improve these metrics:
*   **Pre-commit:** Define the sample size and duration before launching an A/B test.
*   **No Peeking:** Do not stop a test early because "p < 0.05" on day 2. This inflates false positives. Read the result only at the pre-committed end date or use a valid sequential testing method.
*   **Guardrails:** Every experiment optimizing for the North Star must have a guardrail metric (e.g., "server latency" or "report spam rate") to ensure growth doesn't break the platform.
