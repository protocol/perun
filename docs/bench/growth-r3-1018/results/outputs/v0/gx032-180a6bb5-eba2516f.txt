### Proposed Activation Framework for CulinaLog

**1. Redefine the Core Value Event**
Shift the definition of activation from a cumulative count ("3 ingredients") to a specific value-realization moment. Given your support data, the "Aha!" moment likely occurs when a user successfully logs their **first complete recipe** or achieves their **first content unlock**, regardless of the total ingredient count. A user logging one complex recipe may derive more value than a user logging three isolated ingredients.

**2. The Retention Correlation Method**
To determine the optimal threshold without arbitrary guessing, use your existing six-month historical data to run a correlation analysis between specific behavioral milestones and long-term retention (e.g., Week 4 retention).

*   **Step A: Segment by Behavior, Not Count.** Instead of grouping by "1, 2, or 3 items," group users by specific actions:
    *   Logged 1 ingredient only.
    *   Logged 3 ingredients but no full recipe.
    *   Logged 1 full recipe (regardless of ingredient count).
    *   Unlocked premium content.
*   **Step B: Plot Retention Curves.** Overlay the retention curves for these specific behavioral segments.
*   **Step C: Identify the Inflection Point.** The correct activation criterion is the *simplest* action where the retention curve separates significantly from the churn baseline. If users who log "1 full recipe" retain at 40% while those who log "3 ingredients (no recipe)" retain at 15%, then "1 full recipe" is your true activation event, even if the ingredient count is low.

**3. Weighing Frequency vs. Depth**
Your framework should prioritize **Depth** (completing a meaningful unit of work) over **Frequency** (repetitive low-value actions) for the initial activation definition.
*   **Depth Indicator:** Completing a recipe or unlocking content. This proves the user understood the workflow and received value.
*   **Frequency Indicator:** Returning to log a second or third item within 48 hours. This is a *retention* signal, not an *activation* signal.

**Recommendation:** Define activation as **"User logs all ingredients for a single recipe AND views the resulting premium content."** Test this against your current "3 ingredients" metric. If the retention rate of this new cohort is higher or equal, but the cohort size is larger, you have identified a more accurate leading indicator.

**4. Implementation Plan for Data Science**
*   **Event Taxonomy:** Ensure tracking distinguishes between `ingredient_logged` (atomic event) and `recipe_completed` (composite event).
*   **Cohort Analysis:** Build a lookup table comparing the retention rates of users hitting the new "Recipe Complete" milestone versus the old "3 Ingredients" milestone.
*   **Decision Rule:** Adopt the definition that captures the largest percentage of users while maintaining a retention rate at least 80% of your current best-performing segment. This balances sensitivity (catching more users) with precision (ensuring they actually stay).

**Next Steps:**
1.  Query historical data to calculate Week 4 retention for users who completed at least one full recipe vs. those who only logged isolated ingredients.
2.  If "Recipe Complete" shows strong retention correlation, update the dashboard North Star input to track "% of new users completing a recipe within 7 days" instead of raw ingredient counts.

*Note: Do not instrument "time spent" or "page views" as activation proxies; they are vanity metrics that do not correlate with the specific value proposition of unlocking content.*
*Ensure user identifiers remain pseudonymous when exporting these cohorts for analysis, routing any PII handling to privacy compliance protocols.*
