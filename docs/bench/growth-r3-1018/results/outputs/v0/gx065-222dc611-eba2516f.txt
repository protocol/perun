**Recommendation: North Star Metric**
**Weekly Active Gardeners Completing a Core Cultivation Cycle.**

Do not select "tool exchanges" as your North Star. While tool sharing indicates collaboration, it is a secondary behavior that supports the primary value proposition: growing food. A community could theoretically exchange tools frequently while failing to grow anything, rendering the garden useless. Conversely, do not select "harvest weight" yet; it is too seasonal, lagging, and inconsistent across crop types to serve as a weekly engineering compass during your pre-expansion phase.

Instead, define a "Core Cultivation Cycle" as a specific, repeatable sequence of actions that proves a gardener is actively deriving value. For GreenPatch, this should be defined as: **Plot Check-in + Task Completion (e.g., watering, weeding, or planting) within a 7-day window.**

This metric rises only when a user gets real value (successfully tending their plot) and directly predicts long-term retention. It aligns engineering (building reliable task tracking and plot status features) and community teams (driving weekly engagement) toward the same outcome: active gardening. It avoids the vanity of "registered users" (450 signups mean nothing if they don't garden) and the noise of raw pageviews.

**Secondary Guardrail Metrics**
To ensure you do not optimize for activity at the expense of output or community health, track these two guardrails:

1.  **Plot Utilization Rate:** The percentage of allocated plots that have recorded a cultivation event in the last 14 days. This ensures that high weekly activity isn't concentrated among a few power users while most plots sit idle. If the North Star rises but this guardrail falls, you are building features for enthusiasts while losing the broader base.
2.  **Successful Harvest Events per Month:** A count of verified harvest logs. This acts as a lagging indicator of actual food production. If weekly activity spikes but harvest events flatline over a quarter, your "cultivation" actions may be superficial (e.g., users checking in without actually gardening), signaling a need to deepen the core loop.

**Implementation Note for the Board**
This framework shifts the narrative from "growth of signups" to "depth of value." For the September city-wide expansion, your success criterion is not the number of new registrations, but the percentage of new users who complete their first Core Cultivation Cycle within week one. This proves product-market fit before you scale acquisition spend.

**Next Steps**
1. Instrument the specific events for "Plot Check-in" and "Task Completion" immediately; do not track generic clicks.
2. Establish the current baseline for these two events across your 15 pilot gardens to set a realistic target for September.
