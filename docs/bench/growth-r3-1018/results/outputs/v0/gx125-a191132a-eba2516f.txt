### Evaluation Framework: Distinguishing Network Growth from Incentive Arbitrage

To determine if the 15% sign-up uplift represents genuine network value or low-quality arbitrage, you must shift your analysis from **Acquisition** (top of funnel) to **Retention and Activation** (bottom of funnel). Do not present the sign-up lift as a success until these downstream metrics are validated.

**1. Define the "Real Value" North Star for this Cohort**
Reject "New Sign-ups" as your primary success metric for this specific test. Instead, define a North Star metric that only increases when a user derives actual value. For ConnectHub, this is likely **"Weekly Active Users completing a core networking action"** (e.g., sending a connection request, messaging, or updating a profile beyond the minimum).
*   **Action:** Segment the August treatment sign-ups by source (Referral Credit vs. Organic). Compare the Week 1, Week 2, and Week 4 retention rates of the core action between these two groups. If the referral cohort drops off significantly faster after claiming the credit, you are buying empty profiles.

**2. Analyze the Referrer-Referee Dyad (The Network Effect)**
A genuine network effect requires an ongoing relationship, not a one-time transaction.
*   **Action:** Measure the **interaction rate** between the specific referrer and the new referee. Did they connect? Did they message? Did the referee connect with *other* users on the platform within 14 days?
*   **Signal:** If the referee connects only with the referrer and never returns, the "network" has not expanded; you have simply subsidized a private introduction. If the referee branches out to other users, the network effect is strengthening.

**3. Implement Guardrail Metrics for Quality**
You must explicitly test your assumption that quality has not degraded.
*   **Action:** Track **fraud signals** and **completion depth**.
    *   *Profile Depth:* Compare the average number of profile fields completed beyond the mandatory minimum.
    *   *Device/IP Fingerprinting:* Check for clusters of sign-ups from identical IP ranges or device IDs, which indicates farm-like behavior.
    *   *Time-to-Claim:* Calculate the median time between sign-up and credit claim. An unusually short duration suggests the user's sole intent was the payout.

**4. Long-Term Value (LTV) Projection**
Since the test only ran for one month, you cannot observe long-term retention yet. You must model it.
*   **Action:** Compare the **Day-7 and Day-14 activation rates** of this referral cohort against your historical baseline for organic referrals (without credit). Use these early retention curves to project the 90-day LTV. If the projected LTV of the credit-driven user is lower than the cost of the $10 credit plus acquisition overhead, the strategy destroys value despite the volume lift.

**5. Required Data Queries for Your Readout**
Prepare these three specific views for the CSO:
*   **Cohort Retention Curve:** A line chart comparing Week 1–4 retention of the "Credit Referral" group vs. "Organic Referral" group.
*   **Core Action Funnel:** The percentage of new sign-ups in the treatment group who performed a second networking action (beyond profile completion) within 7 days.
*   **Referrer Quality Split:** Segment the referrers by their own historical activity. Are your power users driving high-quality referrals, or is the incentive attracting dormant/low-quality accounts to game the system?

**Conclusion Strategy**
Do not declare a "win" based on the 15% lift. Present the sign-up growth as a **hypothesis generator** that is currently **unverified** regarding value. Your recommendation should be conditional: "Scale the program only if the Week-2 core action retention of the referral cohort matches within 10% of our organic baseline."
