Do not measure file upload volume as your North Star; it is a vanity metric that rises regardless of whether real scientific value is created. Your pilots already proved this: researchers discuss data in chat without uploading, meaning high upload counts could coexist with zero actual collaboration progress.

Instead, define your North Star as **"Weekly Active Research Groups completing a Core Knowledge Loop."** A "loop" is only complete when a specific hypothesis or dataset is referenced, discussed, and explicitly linked to a next step (e.g., a simulation parameter update or a scheduled review). This measures *value delivery*, not just activity noise.

Apply the AARRR funnel bottom-up, starting with **Retention** of this core loop. If groups do not return to close the loop week-over-week, increasing file uploads or chat messages is wasted infrastructure cost. Diagnose retention first: Are groups returning to finish their discussions? Only then look at activation (did they start a loop?) and acquisition (did they join the tool?).

For your lean event taxonomy, instrument **only** events that answer these three specific questions. Do not log "everything"; every event costs your limited storage:

1.  **"Do teams close the knowledge loop?"**
    *   **Event:** `knowledge_loop_closed`
    *   **Definition:** A chat message containing a link to a file/version OR a meeting link, followed by a status update or parameter change within 48 hours.
    *   **Why:** This proves asynchronous discussion led to synchronous action or data evolution. It replaces raw "comment thread" counts.

2.  **"Is the shared data actually being used?"**
    *   **Event:** `version_contextualized`
    *   **Definition:** A user checks out a file version *and* subsequently posts a comment referencing that specific version ID.
    *   **Why:** A version control check alone is noise (someone might just be looking). Contextualizing it in chat proves coordination. This is superior to tracking raw "version control checks."

3.  **"Does coordination lead to execution?"**
    *   **Event:** `sync_action_triggered`
    *   **Definition:** A meeting scheduling link is clicked *and* the meeting occurs (or a summary is posted) with an attached decision log.
    *   **Why:** Tracking "meeting scheduling links" alone is vanity; many are scheduled and never happen. This measures successful coordination.

**Infrastructure & Privacy Seam:**
To track these loops, you need a stable, pseudonymous group identifier to follow a team's progress across sessions. Do not use raw user IDs or PII. Route all personal identifiers to your privacy compliance protocol; the analytics engine should only see the pseudonymous group token. This inverts the standard ops rule of avoiding high-cardinality labels because here, the *user group* is the unit of analysis, not the server health.
