### 1. Diagnostic Method: Isolating Price vs. Competition
To separate price sensitivity from the competitor's January 15 promotion, we must move from aggregate regional averages to **cohort-based difference-in-differences** analysis. Aggregate data (7% churn) conflates the two variables.

**Required Data Segmentation:**
*   **Treatment Group A (Pre-Promo):** Subscribers who received the price increase (Jan 10) but cancelled *before* Jan 15.
    *   *Logic:* Any churn here is purely price-driven, as the competitor had not yet launched.
*   **Treatment Group B (Post-Promo):** Subscribers who received the price increase and cancelled *after* Jan 15.
    *   *Logic:* Churn here is a mix of price sensitivity and competitive pressure.
*   **Control Group (If available):** A comparable region (e.g., Spain or France) with no price change and no major competitor promo, or Italian users on a legacy plan exempt from the hike.

**Analysis Steps:**
1.  **Calculate Baseline Price Elasticity:** Determine the churn rate of Group A. If this exceeds 3%, the price increase alone invalidates the initial assumption, regardless of the competitor.
2.  **Measure Competitive Lift:** Compare the daily churn rate of Group B against Group A. A sharp inflection point on/after Jan 15 indicates the competitor's influence.
3.  **Sign-up Attribution:** Correlate the 20% sign-up drop with the specific dates. If the drop started Jan 10, it is price-driven. If it accelerated Jan 15, it is competitive.

### 2. Preliminary Findings & Confounders
*   **Price Sensitivity Confirmed (Likely):** The spike to 7% within three weeks suggests the "added value" of analytics features did not offset the 12% cost hike for the Italian SMB segment. The initial <3% assumption appears incorrect based on early signals.
*   **Competitor Confounder:** The Jan 15 promo likely exacerbated the issue, but cannot be the sole cause if cancellations began immediately on Jan 10.
*   **New Sign-up Collapse:** A 20% drop in acquisitions suggests the new price point has broken the market entry barrier, not just affected retention.

### 3. Decision Framework & Tripwires
Do not guess; use the data to trigger a specific action.

**Option A: Rollback Pricing (Recommended if Price Elasticity is High)**
*   **Trigger:** If Group A (Pre-Jan 15) churn > 4%.
*   **Rationale:** The core value proposition is misaligned with the local willingness-to-pay. A competitor promo is secondary; the base price is the root cause.
*   **Action:** Revert to previous pricing for Italy immediately. Communicate as a "local market adjustment."

**Option B: Targeted Discount Campaign (Recommended if Competition is Primary)**
*   **Trigger:** If Group A churn < 3% BUT Group B churn spikes significantly post-Jan 15.
*   **Rationale:** The base price is acceptable, but the competitor's offer created a temporary arbitrage.
*   **Action:** Issue a limited-time "loyalty credit" or feature bundle to match the competitor's perceived value without permanently lowering the list price.

**Option C: Hold and Monitor (High Risk)**
*   **Trigger:** Only if data shows the 7% churn is an anomaly (e.g., billing system error) or confined to a specific non-representative sub-segment.
*   **Tripwire:** If total active subscribers drop by >5% by Jan 31, execute Option A immediately.
