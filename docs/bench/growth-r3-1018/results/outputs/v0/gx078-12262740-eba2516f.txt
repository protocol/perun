**North Star Metric: Compliant Rides per Available Bike per Day**

Do not use raw "ride counts" (vanity) or purely operational "rides per bike" as your sole North Star. Raw counts ignore supply costs; operational efficiency ignores the regulatory risk that will shut you down. Instead, adopt a composite North Star that rises only when a user successfully completes a trip *and* the bike remains in good standing with the city.

**Definition:**
`(Total Rides Ending in Designated Zones) / (Average Daily Available Fleet)`

This metric directly balances your two conflicting goals:
1.  **Business Health:** It demands high utilization (numerator up, denominator optimized). If bikes sit idle or are broken, the metric drops.
2.  **Regulatory Satisfaction:** It penalizes non-compliance. A ride ending in a non-designated zone (your current 20% leak) counts as **zero** toward this metric. This forces the product and operations teams to solve parking compliance *before* scaling acquisition, aligning perfectly with the mayors' concerns about sidewalk clutter.

**Supporting Input Metrics (The Levers):**
To move this North Star, track these specific inputs:
*   **Compliance Rate:** Percentage of rides ending in designated zones (Current baseline: ~80%). Target: >95%.
*   **Redistribution Cost per Compliant Ride:** Measures the operational expense required to achieve a valid trip.
*   **Zone Saturation Index:** Ratio of bikes to designated slots in high-demand areas to predict clutter before it happens.

**Why this works for the October 15th Review:**
Presenting this metric to city mayors demonstrates that VeloChina prioritizes *orderly* mobility over chaotic growth. It proves you are measuring success by "clean trips," not just volume. If you present raw ride growth alongside a 20% violation rate, you invite restrictions. If you present growth in *compliant* rides, you position yourself as a partner in urban management.

**Immediate Actions (This Week):**
1.  **Instrument the "Valid End" Event:** Update your event taxonomy to tag every ride end as `compliant` or `non_compliant` based on geofence data. Do not count a ride as "successful" in your dashboard unless it is `compliant`.
2.  **Run a Redistribution A/B Test:** In one district, test aggressive pre-emptive rebalancing (moving bikes before slots fill) vs. reactive moving. Measure the impact on the *Compliance Rate* and *Redistribution Cost*. Pre-commit to a 14-day readout; do not peek at day 2.
3.  **Calculate the Break-Even:** Determine how much the Compliance Rate must improve to offset the cost of removing 20% of the fleet if regulators demand it. Use this as your guardrail.

**Note:** Your current 20% non-compliance rate is a critical leak; pouring more bikes (acquisition) into this funnel will increase regulatory friction, not value. Fix the retention of compliant behavior first.
