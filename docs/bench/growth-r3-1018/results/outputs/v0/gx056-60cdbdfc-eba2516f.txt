**Decision: Do not ship the new dashboard as a permanent change based on current data.**

The 25% day-1 spike is a classic **novelty effect**, confirmed by the convergence to the 18% baseline by day-7. Since the test ran its full pre-committed 14-day duration, the day-7 result is the valid read for habit formation. The data indicates the design successfully drives curiosity but fails to alter long-term behavior. Shipping now would incur engineering maintenance costs for zero net retention gain.

**Method to Detect Latent Value (Without Waiting Months)**

To determine if value exists beyond the novelty window without extending the wait indefinitely, execute these three steps immediately:

1.  **Segment the "Explorers" vs. "Ignorers"**
    Isolate the treatment users who actually interacted with the new gamified elements (e.g., clicked badges, viewed streaks) versus those who saw the UI but did not engage.
    *   *Query:* Compare day-14 retention between "High Engagement with Gamification" and "Low Engagement" within the treatment group.
    *   *Signal:* If "High Engagement" users retain at >20% while "Low Engagement" drops to 18%, the mechanic works but requires better discovery/onboarding. If both segments converge at 18%, the mechanic itself holds no latent value.

2.  **Analyze Secondary Behavioral Metrics (Input Metrics)**
    Retention is a lagging indicator. Check if the treatment group increased *frequency* or *session depth* even if they didn't stay longer overall.
    *   *Query:* Compare average weekly sessions and core actions completed per user (e.g., workouts logged) for days 8–14.
    *   *Signal:* If frequency is higher in the treatment group despite equal retention, the design increases intensity for existing users. This may justify a ship for revenue reasons (if monetization ties to usage), even if it doesn't fix churn.

3.  **Run a "Re-Exposure" Micro-Test**
    Novelty fades because the stimulus becomes static. Test if *dynamic* gamification sustains interest.
    *   *Action:* Create a small subset (5% of users) who see the current dashboard but receive a new, time-limited challenge mid-week.
    *   *Signal:* If this micro-intervention spikes day-7 retention again, the issue is content freshness, not the dashboard layout. If not, the fundamental loop is unchanged.

**Recommendation for Kenji (Go/No-Go)**

*   **No-Go on Full Rollout:** Revert the default experience to the control version to avoid cluttering the UI with ineffective features.
*   **Conditional Pivot:** Only proceed if Step 1 reveals a high-retention segment of "power users." In that case, the action is not a full ship, but a targeted onboarding flow designed to convert new users into "gamification engagers."
*   **Tripwire:** If the "High Engagement" segment in Step 1 does not show at least a 2 percentage point lift over baseline by next Monday, terminate the gamification initiative entirely.

**Note:** The initial day-1 win was real but transient; treating it as a success would be a false positive driven by peeking at early vanity metrics.
