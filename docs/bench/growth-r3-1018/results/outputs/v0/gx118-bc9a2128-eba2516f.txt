**Recommendation: Do not ship based on this test; treat the 12% lift as an unverified hypothesis and kill the current A/B experiment to preserve roadmap velocity.**

The observed 12% improvement with a p-value of 0.14 is statistically `UNVERIFIED`. **Framing the Trade-offs for the Product Council**

1.  **Risk of Shipping (False Positive):** If the 12% lift is noise, you introduce complexity and maintenance costs for a feature that does not move the North Star (task completion). For a niche tool, clutter is a retention killer. The cost here is **user confusion and technical debt**.
2.  **Opportunity Cost of Waiting (False Negative):** If the lift is real, delaying six more months for significance means leaving value on the table. However, given your acquisition plateau, waiting for frequentist significance may be impossible without changing the traffic source. The cost here is **stagnant growth**, but this is preferable to building on a broken foundation.

**Decision Framework: Beyond P-Values**

Since frequentist methods require sample sizes you cannot currently generate without delaying the roadmap, use the following approaches to support a decision:

*   **Bayesian Estimation (Probability of Benefit):** Instead of asking "Is it significant?", ask "What is the probability the lift is >0%?" or "What is the expected loss if we ship?" Calculate the posterior distribution of the lift. If there is an 85% probability the feature is positive, and the cost of reversal is low (e.g., a feature flag), the risk profile changes. Present the **expected value** rather than a binary pass/fail.
*   **Qualitative Signal Amplification:** With only 400 users, quantitative noise drowns out signals. Immediately interview the 20–30 users in the variant group who completed tasks. Did the collaboration feature *cause* the completion, or was it a specific use case? If qualitative feedback reveals a clear "aha" moment for a specific freelancer persona, the 12% is likely a real signal for that segment, even if the aggregate p-value is weak.
*   **Segmented Rollout (The "Soft Ship"):** Do not ship to 100% of users. Enable the feature for **new sign-ups only** or a specific high-intent cohort. This isolates the variable for future measurement without committing the entire existing base. This turns the "failure" into a new, narrower experiment with a clearer baseline.
