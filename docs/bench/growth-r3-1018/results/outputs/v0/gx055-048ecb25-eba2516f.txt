### Pilot Success Framework: FreshBox Ontario

**North Star Metric: 4-Week Rolling Retention of Core Action**
Do not present weekly retention in isolation; the 60% vs. 85% swing is noise, not signal. Your North Star must be the percentage of subscribers who complete a **core value action** (receiving and keeping a box) over a **4-week rolling window**. This smooths out single-week anomalies like the local festival. If a user skips a week due to an event but returns the next, they are still retained in a 4-week view. Define "Core Action" strictly as a box delivered and not returned/cancelled within 48 hours.

**The Measurement Strategy: Cohorts over Aggregates**
Stop looking at total active users. Break your 450 subscribers into **weekly acquisition cohorts** (e.g., "Jan 15–21 Starters," "Jan 22–28 Starters").
*   **Why:** A local festival affects all users equally in a specific week, distorting the aggregate. By comparing how the "Jan 15" cohort behaves in Week 3 vs. how the "Jan 22" cohort behaves in Week 3, you isolate the product's stickiness from the calendar's chaos.
*   **The Metric to Present:** "Cohort Retention Curve Stability." Show the board that while absolute numbers fluctuate, the *shape* of the retention curve for different cohorts is converging. If newer cohorts retain as well as older ones by Week 4, the model is viable regardless of weekly spikes.

**Input Metrics (The Drivers)**
Pair the North Star with two input metrics that explain the volatility rather than hiding it:
1.  **Event-Adjusted Order Rate:** Tag orders with known local events (Festival, Holiday, Payday). Present retention *with* and *without* these weeks annotated. This proves to the board you understand the market dynamics and aren't being blindsided by them.
2.  **Qualitative Churn Reason:** In a town of 12,000, you can know *why* people leave. Categorize churn into "Product Fit" (didn't like veggies), "Logistics" (delivery failed), and "External" (away for festival, budget timing). Success is defined when "Product Fit" churn is <10%, even if "External" churn spikes.

**Experiment Rigor for the Pilot**
Do not run A/B tests on pricing or features yet; your sample size (450) is too small for statistical significance on secondary metrics, and peeking at daily data will yield false positives.
*   **Rule:** Any claim of improvement must be based on a **pre-committed 4-week observation window**.
*   **Guardrail:** Monitor "Support Tickets per 100 Orders." If retention rises but support tickets spike, the growth is fragile (e.g., people are staying but angry).

**Definition of Viability for the Board**
Success is not a specific percentage (e.g., "80% retention") because no external benchmark applies to your specific town. Success is defined by **internal consistency**:
1.  **Stability:** The 4-week rolling retention rate varies by less than ±5% month-over-month.
2.  **Cohort Convergence:** New cohorts match the Week-4 retention of the earliest cohort within a 10% margin.
3.  **Churn Quality:** >70% of churn is attributed to "External/Temporary" factors, not "Product Fit."
