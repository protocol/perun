**Diagnosis: The "Decoy Effect" Reversal and Missing Value Signal**

Your data indicates a classic failure in tier architecture: the new $49 "Professional" tier is acting as a **negative anchor** rather than a bridge. By introducing a mid-priced option, you inadvertently validated the $19 Basic plan as a "bargain" while failing to justify the jump to $49 with perceived value. The 10% lift in Basic sign-ups suggests users who might have hesitated at $19 now see it as the obvious rational choice compared to $49. The flat Enterprise numbers confirm the middle tier is not cannibalizing high-value users; it is merely confusing the entry-level segment.

The assumption that 20% would select this tier was an **unverified benchmark** not grounded in your historical baseline. Without prior data on willingness-to-pay at this specific feature gap, that target is vanity. The real metric of failure is not the 4% uptake, but the **conversion rate drop-off** at the pricing page compared to the pre-test baseline.

**What to Measure: The "Value Gap" Taxonomy**

Stop guessing whether it is price, features, or presentation. Instrument these three specific events to answer named questions before the April 25th meeting:

1.  **Event: `pricing_page_hover_time` (by tier)**
    *   **Question:** Are users ignoring the Professional card entirely, or are they reading it and rejecting it?
    *   **Hypothesis:** If hover time is low, the presentation is invisible or confusing. If hover time is high but click-through is low, the value proposition (features vs. price) is unconvincing.

2.  **Event: `feature_comparison_toggle` (specifically tracking clicks on features differentiating Basic vs. Pro)**
    *   **Question:** Which specific feature gap is failing to justify the $30 price increase?
    *   **Hypothesis:** Users likely see the Basic plan as "good enough" because the Pro features do not solve an immediate, painful problem. You need to identify exactly which feature fails to trigger the "aha" moment required for upgrade.

3.  **Event: `checkout_abandonment_step` (segmented by selected tier)**
    *   **Question:** Is the friction at the payment entry or the plan selection?
    *   **Hypothesis:** If users select Pro but abandon at payment, the price point is the barrier. If they never select it, the feature set is the barrier.

**Immediate Next Steps (This Week)**

1.  **Pull the Cohort Retention Curve:** Compare Day-7 retention of the 4% who bought Pro vs. the new Basic users. If Pro users retain significantly better, the issue is **acquisition messaging** (you aren't attracting the right users to the tier). If retention is flat, the tier itself is **value-deficient**.
2.  **Run a "Smoke Test" on Features:** Before reworking the whole tier, A/B test a landing page that sells *only* the key differentiating feature of the Pro plan at $49 to see if the feature itself converts.
3.  **Recompute the Break-Even:** Calculate the revenue impact. If Basic volume increased by 10% but ARPU (Average Revenue Per User) dropped overall, the tier is destroying value. If ARPU is stable but total conversion volume dropped, the tier is creating friction.

**Verdict for Leadership:** Do not kill the tier yet; the data suggests a **positioning failure**, not necessarily a product failure. The tier is currently serving as a discount validator for the Basic plan. Pivot the hypothesis: The problem is likely that the "Professional" features are not perceived as essential enough to double the price from $19. Focus your investigation on the **perceived value gap**, not the price tag.
