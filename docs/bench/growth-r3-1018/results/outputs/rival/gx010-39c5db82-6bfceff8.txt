### ProcureMiddleEast Credit Feature: KPI Framework for Q1 Review

To satisfy the CFO's ROI requirements and the Head of Strategic Partnerships' growth narrative, you must track metrics that distinguish between **cannibalization** (shifting existing spend) and **incrementality** (generating new volume). Given your hypothesis that approval speed drives adoption over interest rates, the measurement plan prioritizes velocity and conversion friction.

#### 1. Direct Credit Product Performance (The "Engine")
These metrics validate the operational viability of the financing feature itself.

*   **Application Conversion Rate:** % of eligible users who start an application vs. those who complete it.
    *   *Why:* Identifies friction in the signup flow.
*   **Approval Velocity (Median Time-to-Decision):** The time elapsed between application submission and final approval/rejection.
    *   *Why:* Directly tests your hypothesis that speed is the primary driver. Benchmark this against industry standards for traditional trade credit.
*   **Take-Up Rate:** % of approved users who actually utilize the credit line within 30 days of approval.
    *   *Why:* Measures product-market fit; high approval but low usage indicates terms or limits are unattractive.
*   **Cost of Capital vs. Yield:** The spread between your funding cost and the interest/fees charged to contractors.
    *   *Why:* Essential for the CFO to determine unit economics profitability per transaction.

#### 2. Marketplace Halo Effect (The "Incrementality")
These metrics isolate whether the credit feature is expanding the total pie or merely slicing it differently.

*   **Incremental GMV Ratio:** `(GMV from Credit Users - Baseline GMV for same users) / Total Credit GMV`.
    *   *Calculation:* Compare the average order value (AOV) and frequency of credit users *post-approval* against their 6-month historical baseline.
    *   *Why:* If a contractor usually spends $10k/month and now spends $10k/month using credit, GMV is flat (cannibalization). If they spend $15k, the $5k is incremental.
*   **Basket Expansion Lift:** The percentage increase in average order value for transactions utilizing credit compared to cash transactions for similar material categories.
    *   *Why:* Credit often allows contractors to buy higher-quality materials or larger bulk quantities upfront.
*   **Supplier Retention Delta:** Churn rate of suppliers whose buyers use credit vs. those whose buyers do not.
    *   *Why:* For the Head of Partnerships, this proves the feature locks in supply-side liquidity. If suppliers get paid faster (via your platform) while contractors pay later, supplier stickiness should increase.
*   **New Contractor Acquisition Cost (CAC) Payback:** Time to recover CAC for new contractors who sign up specifically citing "financing availability" as a reason.
    *   *Why:* Measures if the feature is a unique selling proposition (USP) driving net-new user acquisition.

#### 3. Strategic Risk & Quality (The "Guardrails")
Investors will scrutinize risk exposure in a B2B lending model.

*   **Early Stage Delinquency Rate (30+ DPD):** Percentage of loans past due by 30 days within the first 90 days of origination.
    *   *Why:* Leading indicator of credit quality; critical for Q1 investor confidence.
*   **Concentration Risk:** % of total credit exposure held by the top 5 contractors.
    *   *Why:* Ensures the portfolio isn't overly reliant on a single large player in the UAE or Saudi market.

**Implementation Note:** To accurately calculate "Incremental GMV," you must establish a control group of similar contractors who were eligible for credit but did not apply (or were approved but haven't used it yet) to normalize for seasonal construction trends in the region.

**Reporting Cadence:** Present "Approval Velocity" and "Application Conversion" weekly to optimize the funnel; present "Incremental GMV" and "Delinquency" monthly for the CFO's risk/return modeling.
