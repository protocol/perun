**Recommendation: Hold Prices Steady for Q2 in Germany**

Do not proceed with the 20% or 30% price increases in Germany for Q2. The data indicates a critical threshold breach at the 30% hike, where a 25% conversion drop suggests demand is far more elastic than hypothesized. While a moderate increase (10%) might theoretically yield higher revenue per user, the sharp spike in cancellations four days post-notification signals immediate churn risk that outweighs potential ARPU gains.

**Strategic Rationale**

The core conflict lies between your hypothesis (German inelasticity) and the observed reality (sharp drop-off). In pricing elasticity, a 25% volume loss requires a price increase of roughly 33% just to break even on total revenue, assuming no other costs change. Since the test only applied a 30% increase, the net result is almost certainly a decline in total revenue, not a gain. Furthermore, the cancellation spike indicates that existing customers are highly sensitive to price changes, suggesting that raising prices now could trigger a churn wave that damages long-term Lifetime Value (LTV).

The timing variable (holiday period) introduces noise but does not invalidate the churn signal. Holiday spending constraints may have amplified the reaction, but the direction of the data (high sensitivity) is a safer risk parameter than assuming the holiday suppressed true demand. If the holiday made users *more* sensitive, proceeding now risks repeating the error in a non-holiday period where budget fatigue might be lower but price resistance remains.

3.  **Market Positioning:** The assumption that Germany is less price-sensitive than Southern Europe appears incorrect for this specific tier. Spain's resilience (only 10% drop) suggests the value proposition resonates differently there; do not conflate the two markets.
