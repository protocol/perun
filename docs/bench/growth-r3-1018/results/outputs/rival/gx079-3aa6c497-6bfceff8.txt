### Executive Summary: Test Status and Strategic Recommendation

**Verdict:** The A/B test conducted from July 1–21 in Vienna and Salzburg is **statistically invalid** for measuring the isolated impact of the in-app offer. The external radio campaign created significant **control group contamination**, violating the fundamental assumption of isolation required for causal inference.

**Interpretation of Results:**
The "negligible difference" observed between the treatment and control groups does not indicate that the discounted annual plan is ineffective. Instead, it suggests that the **net lift of the in-app offer was likely masked** by the 25% exposure of the control group to the radio campaign.
*   **Treatment Group:** Received In-App Offer + (potentially) Radio Ads.
*   **Control Group:** Received Standard Pricing + (25% exposed to) Radio Ads.

Because the radio ads successfully drove annual sign-ups in the control group, the baseline conversion rate for the control was artificially inflated. This compressed the delta between the two groups, creating a false negative (Type II error). Your internal modeling predicting a 15% increase may still be accurate regarding the *in-app mechanic*, but this specific experiment cannot prove it.

**Can We Adjust for Contamination?**
**No, not with statistical rigor.** While you know ~25% of the control group was exposed, you lack the critical variable required to adjust the data: **the specific conversion lift of the radio ads alone.**
*   If the radio ads had the same conversion power as the in-app prompt, the results might actually be hiding a massive success (or failure) depending on the overlap.
*   Without a "pure" control group (0% exposure) or precise attribution data linking individual radio exposures to conversions, any mathematical adjustment would be speculative and unreliable for executive decision-making.

**Strategic Recommendation for Q3 Pricing:**
Given the August 5th deadline for the CMO, do not attempt to salvage this data for a definitive go/no-go decision on the pricing strategy. Instead:

1.  **Pivot to Observational Analysis:** Immediately segment the July 1–21 data by **geographic granularity** (e.g., specific postal codes). If the radio campaign had varying intensity across different neighborhoods within Vienna/Salzburg, you may find a correlation between radio spend density and conversion rates. This is weaker than an RCT but may provide directional support.
2.  **Launch a "Clean" Micro-Test:** If technically feasible, run a rapid 7-day follow-up test in a **new, isolated market** (or digitally geo-fenced area) with a strict media blackout on the control group. Use the same in-app offer. This is the only way to validate the 15% lift hypothesis before Q3 planning finalizes.
3.  **Decision Framework:**
    *   **If the radio campaign was highly successful** (high absolute sign-ups in control): Proceed with the annual plan rollout, assuming the combination of media + offer is strong, but acknowledge you cannot isolate the discount's specific contribution.
    *   **If absolute sign-ups were low** despite the radio push: The offer itself may be weak, regardless of the contamination.

**Immediate Next Step:** Present the contamination fact to the CMO as a learned constraint. Recommend proceeding with the annual plan *only* if the business can tolerate the risk of unverified margin impact, or authorize the rapid micro-test to de-risk the Q3 strategy.

*Note: Do not report the original "negligible difference" as a finding of "no effect"; explicitly label it as "inconclusive due to external interference."*
*Ensure all future experimental protocols include a "media blackout" clause for control groups in matched geographic tests.*
