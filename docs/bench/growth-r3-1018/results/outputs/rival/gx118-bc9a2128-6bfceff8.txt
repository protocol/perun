# Recommendation: Ship as a "Limited Beta" with Guardrails

**Decision:** Do not declare the test a winner, but do not kill the feature. Recommend shipping the collaboration feature to **20% of new users** as a controlled rollout (Limited Beta) while simultaneously launching a targeted qualitative research sprint.

**Rationale:** With a p-value of 0.14 and a 12% lift, the data suggests a likely positive signal that your sample size was too small to confirm, rather than evidence of no effect. Killing the feature now incurs a high opportunity cost given the promising direction. However, shipping to 100% of users risks implementing a change that might be neutral or negative. A partial rollout allows you to gather the remaining required data organically without halting the roadmap, treating the "noise" as an ongoing experiment rather than a finalized result.

### Framing the Trade-offs for the Product Council

Present the decision using this risk/reward framework:

| Option | Risk | Opportunity Cost | Verdict |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | : |
| **Kill Feature** | **High.** You likely discard a genuine 12% growth lever because of underpowered data. | Wasted 6 weeks of dev time; stagnation in task completion metrics. | **Reject** |
| **Full Ship** | **Medium.** If the 12% lift is noise, you clutter the UI and incur maintenance costs for zero gain. | Low immediate cost, but potential long-term technical debt if the feature fails. | **Too Risky** |
| **Limited Beta (Recommended)** | **Low.** Exposure is capped at 20%. If the effect is negative, impact is contained. | **None.** You continue collecting data while delivering value to a segment. | **Accept** |

### Supporting the Decision Without Statistical Significance

Since frequentist methods (p-values) cannot validate the result yet, use these two approaches to build confidence:

**1. Bayesian Estimation (The "Probability of Gain" Argument)**
Instead of asking "Is this significant?", ask "What is the probability this feature is beneficial?"
*   Run a simple Bayesian A/B test analysis using your current data (400 users, 12% lift).
*   Even with wide credible intervals, you will likely find a high probability (e.g., >85%) that the true effect is greater than 0%.
*   **Presentation Tip:** Tell the council: *"While we can't prove this works with 95% certainty, Bayesian analysis suggests an 88% probability that this feature improves task completion. The most likely outcome is positive; the risk is merely that the benefit is smaller than 12%."*

**2. Qualitative "Why" Data**
Quantitative data tells you *what* happened; qualitative data tells you *if* it matters.
*   Immediately interview 10–15 users from the variant group who completed tasks.
*   Ask: "Did the collaboration feature change how you worked?" and "Would you miss this if it disappeared?"
*   **Presentation Tip:** If users express strong reliance on the feature ("I couldn't have finished this project without it"), this validates the *direction* of the metric, even if the sample size is small. Strong qualitative love + positive quantitative trend = Green light for beta.

### Implementation Plan
1.  **Rollout:** Enable feature for 20% of incoming traffic only.
2.  **Monitor:** Set a guardrail metric (e.g., support tickets regarding collaboration). If tickets spike, kill immediately.
3.  **Re-evaluate:** In 4 weeks, re-run the significance test. The additional data from the 20% cohort should push the sample size over the threshold for a definitive conclusion.

This approach satisfies the founder's need for speed while protecting the product from unverified changes.
