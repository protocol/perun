### Recommended Approach: Revenue Per Active User (RPAU) as North Star

Do not choose between order frequency and basket size as your primary metric. Selecting one creates a blind spot that validates your hypothesis while potentially hiding revenue erosion. Instead, adopt **Revenue Per Active User (RPAU)** over a fixed 30-day observation window as your North Star metric.

**The Logic**
Your goal is to boost revenue. Revenue is mathematically defined as:
$$Revenue = Users \times Frequency \times Basket Size$$

By isolating basket size, you risk optimizing for a "pantry loading" effect where users buy more once but return later than usual, flattening your monthly revenue curve. By isolating frequency, you might encourage smaller, inefficient orders that increase logistical costs without lifting top-line growth. RPAU captures the net effect of both variables simultaneously.

**Measurement Plan for the VP**

1.  **Primary Metric:** **30-Day Revenue Per Active User.**
    *   *Definition:* Total revenue generated by the cohort exposed to the new checkout flow divided by the number of unique users in that cohort over a closed 30-day window.
    *   *Why:* It automatically penalizes the test if increased basket size causes a disproportionate drop in frequency.

2.  **Guardrail Metrics (Must not degrade):**
    *   **Order Frequency:** Monitor to ensure it does not drop by more than 5%. If basket size rises 10% but frequency drops 15%, RPAU will fall, correctly signaling a failed test.
    *   **Cart Abandonment Rate:** Simplifying selection should not confuse users at the final payment step.
    *   **Refund/Return Rate:** Ensure impulse buys are not leading to higher rejection rates.

3.  **Secondary Diagnostic Metrics:**
    *   **Items Per Order:** To validate the "impulse buy" hypothesis directly.
    *   **Time to Next Order:** To detect if users are delaying their next shop.

**Interpreting Conflicting Results**

You will likely see scenarios where metrics move in opposite directions. Use this decision matrix for the VP:

| Scenario | Basket Size | Order Frequency | RPAU Trend | Decision |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **The Win** | ⬆️ Increases | ➡️ Stable or ⬆️ | ⬆️ **Positive** | **Launch.** The simplification drives value without penalty. |
| **The Trap** | ⬆️ Increases | ⬇️ Decreases | ⬇️ **Negative** | **Kill.** Users are "pantry loading" and churning slower. The gain per order does not offset the lost volume. |
| **The Efficiency Loss** | ➡️ Stable | ⬆️ Increases | ➡️ **Neutral/Neg** | **Analyze.** If RPAU is flat but frequency is up, basket size likely dropped (users buying less per trip). This may increase delivery costs, hurting margin even if revenue is flat. |
| **The False Negative** | ⬆️ Increases | ⬇️ Slight Drop | ➡️ **Neutral** | **Extend or Segment.** If RPAU is flat, check if the new users acquired are higher quality. Consider running the test longer to see if frequency stabilizes. |

**Implementation Timeline**
*   **Nov 8:** Finalize tracking plan for RPAU and guardrails.
*   **Nov 15:** Launch A/B test (50/50 split).
*   **Dec 15:** Close 30-day observation window.
*   **Dec 16:** Compute final RPAU and present recommendation.

**Critical Constraint:** Do not peek at results before the 30-day window closes. Checking frequency at day 7 will yield false positives because the "delayed next purchase" effect has not yet manifested.

*Note: Ensure your analytics tool groups users by their initial exposure date (cohort analysis) rather than calendar date to accurately measure the 30-day window.*
*This approach shifts the debate from "which metric looks better" to "which behavior drives total revenue."*
