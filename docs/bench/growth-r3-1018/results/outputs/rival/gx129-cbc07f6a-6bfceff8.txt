**Primary Metric Recommendation: 4-Week Cohort Repeat Booking Rate**

For a two-sided marketplace like PawsSchedule seeking seed funding, **Repeat Booking Rate** is the superior primary metric over Gross Merchandise Value (GMV). While GMV demonstrates scale, it is a vanity metric in early stages that can be artificially inflated by heavy marketing spend or one-off transactions without proving underlying value. In the high-stakes pet care category, trust is the product; repeat behavior is the only objective proof that trust exists.

Investors at the seed stage prioritize evidence of product-market fit (PMF) over raw volume. A high repeat rate signals that your supply (walkers) is reliable and your demand (owners) is satisfied, creating the retention flywheel essential for marketplace viability. GMV without retention indicates a "leaky bucket" where customer acquisition costs will eventually outpace lifetime value.

**The Data Story for Your Advisory Board**

To construct a compelling narrative for your six-week deadline, frame your metrics as follows:

1.  **The Trust Proxy (Primary):** Report the percentage of users who book a second time within 30 days of their first booking. Break this down by cohort (e.g., users who joined in Jan vs. Feb) to show consistency. With 300 weekly bookings and 1,200 users, if even 40-50% of customers return monthly, you demonstrate strong unit economics.
2.  **The Scale Context (Secondary):** Present GMV ($13,500/week) not as the headline, but as a function of your retention. Show that GMV is growing *because* repeat rates are high, not just because you are buying new users. This links your top-line growth to product quality.
3.  **Marketplace Health:** Include a brief note on supply-side retention (walker return rate). A two-sided marketplace fails if walkers churn, regardless of owner demand.

**Addressing Seasonality and Normalization**

Your concern regarding summer holiday fluctuations is valid but should not lead to complex data normalization for a seed pitch. Normalizing data can appear defensive or opaque to investors. Instead:

*   **Use Raw Data with Context:** Present the actual numbers. If summer typically sees a 20% dip in demand due to travel, acknowledge this verbally as a known market dynamic, not a product failure.
*   **Highlight Counter-Cyclical Strength:** If your repeat rate holds steady even when absolute volume dips, this is a powerful signal of resilience. It proves that while total market activity fluctuates, your *share of wallet* among active users remains sticky.
*   **Forward-Looking Guidance:** Rather than adjusting historical data, use the six-week runway to gather early signals on summer booking intent (e.g., advance bookings). Include this qualitative insight to show you are managing the seasonality proactively.

**Strategic Conclusion**

Lead with Repeat Booking Rate to prove you have built a trusted brand in a sensitive category. Use GMV to show the monetization potential of that trust. This combination tells a story of a healthy, sustainable business rather than a transactional platform dependent on constant ad spend. Investors fund retention curves that flatten high, not top-line spikes that crash.
