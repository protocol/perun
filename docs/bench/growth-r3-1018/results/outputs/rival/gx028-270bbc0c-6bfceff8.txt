# Referral Program Diagnostic Framework: Saturation vs. Technical Failure

To distinguish between natural user saturation and a technical breakage for James's May 20th decision, execute the following four data slices immediately. Do not rely on the aggregate viral coefficient alone, as it masks the specific point of failure.

### 1. The "Day 3 Cohort" Link Health Check
Isolate all users who signed up or attempted to share *during* the server maintenance window (Day 3).
*   **Metric:** Click-through rate (CTR) of referral links generated specifically on Day 3 vs. Day 1 and Day 2.
*   **Diagnosis:**
    *   **Technical Failure:** If Day 3 links have a near-zero CTR while Day 1/2 links remain stable, the maintenance window broke link generation or routing.
    *   **Saturation:** If Day 3 links have similar CTRs to previous days but fewer *total* shares per user, the issue is behavioral, not technical.

### 2. Invites Per User Distribution (The Power Law Test)
Analyze the distribution of invites sent per user, segmented by signup date (Day 1–2 vs. Day 3–4).
*   **Metric:** Average invites sent per active referrer and the 90th percentile of invites sent.
*   **Diagnosis:**
    *   **Saturation:** If the *average* drops because your top 10% of users (power users) stopped sending invites entirely, but new users still send 1–2 invites successfully, this confirms your hypothesis: power users exhausted their immediate circles. The curve is flattening naturally.
    *   **Technical/Reward Fatigue:** If *all* segments (including new, non-power users) drop to zero or near-zero invites simultaneously, this indicates a systemic block (broken UI, missing reward display, or global link failure).

### 3. Conversion Funnel Drop-off Point
Break down the referral funnel into three distinct steps for Day 4 traffic only:
1.  **View Referral Page**
2.  **Click "Share" / Generate Link**
3.  **Friend Clicks Link**
4.  **Friend Converts**
*   **Diagnosis:**
    *   **Reward Fatigue:** High drop-off at Step 2. Users see the page but choose not to share because the incentive no longer motivates them.
    *   **Technical Break:** High drop-off at Step 3 or 4. Users are sharing, but friends aren't receiving valid links or cannot complete signup.
    *   **Saturation:** High completion rates on Steps 2–4, but a sheer volume decrease in Step 1 (fewer people entering the flow).

### 4. Time-to-First-Invite Latency
Measure the time delta between a user's signup and their first referral action.
*   **Diagnosis:**
    *   **Saturation:** Latency remains low (users share immediately), but the *volume* of users entering the funnel decreases.
    *   **Fatigue:** Latency increases significantly. Users wait, hesitate, or abandon the referral page after reading the offer, suggesting the reward value proposition has degraded.

### Decision Matrix for James

| Data Pattern | Root Cause | Recommendation by May 20 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Day 3 links dead; others alive** | Technical Break | **Pause & Fix.** Do not spend more budget. Engineering must patch link generation before scaling. |
| **Power users stop; new users active** | Natural Saturation | **Iterate Rewards.** The loop works for new users, but early adopters need a "tier 2" reward to dig deeper into networks. |
| **All users drop at "Share" button** | Reward Fatigue | **Pivot Offer.** The mechanism works, but the incentive is weak. Test higher value or different reward types before scaling. |
| **All users drop at "Friend Convert"** | Broken Landing Page | **Fix UX.** The invite is sent, but the destination is broken or unconvincing. |

**Immediate Action:** Run Slice #1 first. If Day 3 links are technically broken, the other data is contaminated and irrelevant until fixed.

*Note: Ensure your analytics window excludes pending conversions that haven't matured yet to avoid false negatives on Day 4.*
*Recommendation hinges on isolating the Day 3 cohort; do not average their data with healthy days.*
