### Evaluation Framework: Referral Credit Experiment Readout

To determine if the 15% sign-up uplift represents genuine network growth or low-quality acquisition, structure your analysis around three pillars: **Identity Verification**, **Early Engagement**, and **Network Integration**. Do not rely on sign-up volume alone; instead, compare the treatment vs. control cohorts across the following specific dimensions.

#### 1. Identity & Quality Filters (The "Empty Profile" Check)
Isolate accounts created solely for the credit by measuring friction completion and identity authenticity.
*   **Profile Completion Rate:** Compare the percentage of new users who complete *beyond* the minimum required fields (e.g., adding a bio, uploading a photo, listing experience) within 48 hours of signup. A significant drop in the treatment group suggests "minimum viable" accounts created just to trigger the reward.
*   **Email Domain & Provider Analysis:** Segment new sign-ups by email provider. A spike in disposable email domains or a surge in generic providers (vs. corporate/verified domains) in the treatment group indicates low-intent users.
*   **Verification Step Drop-off:** If you have optional identity verification (LinkedIn import, phone number), compare the opt-in rates. Low-quality users typically abandon flows requiring extra effort.

#### 2. Early Engagement Signals (The "Real User" Check)
Determine if the new users are interacting with the platform or disappearing after claiming the credit.
*   **Day 1 & Day 7 Retention:** Compare retention curves. Genuine users usually return to explore; credit-seekers often churn immediately after the referral is confirmed.
*   **Core Action Frequency:** Measure the average number of "value actions" per user in the first week (e.g., sending connection requests, viewing profiles, posting content). If the treatment group signs up but performs zero core actions, they are likely inert.
*   **Time-to-First-Value:** Calculate the time elapsed between signup and the first meaningful interaction. A delayed or non-existent time-to-value in the treatment cohort signals low intent.

#### 3. Network Integration (The "Network Effect" Check)
Validate your hypothesis that these users strengthen the network by examining their connectivity patterns.
*   **Connection Reciprocity:** Analyze the ratio of sent vs. accepted connection requests. Low-quality accounts often send spammy requests that go unaccepted. If the treatment group has a low acceptance rate, they are not integrating well.
*   **Referrer Quality Correlation:** Segment the results by the *referrer's* historical quality. Did high-value users bring in high-value referrals, or did the incentive encourage your best users to game the system with low-quality friends?
*   **Secondary Referrals:** Track if these new users (the referees) subsequently refer anyone else within 30 days. Genuine network participants often become referrers; credit-seekers rarely do.

#### 4. Economic & Long-Term Impact
*   **Cost Per Active User (CPAU):** Recalculate your efficiency metric. Divide the total credit cost by the number of *active* users (not just sign-ups) in the treatment group. Compare this to your standard CAC.
*   **30-Day Monetization Proxy:** If you have premium tiers, track the upgrade rate of this cohort. Even if they don't buy immediately, do they exhibit behaviors that historically precede conversion?

#### Recommended Visualization for the CSO
Present a **Cohort Quality Matrix** plotting "Volume Lift" (X-axis) against "Quality Score" (Y-axis, composite of the metrics above). This visually demonstrates whether you moved into the "High Growth/High Quality" quadrant or the "High Growth/Low Quality" trap.

***

*Ensure all metrics compare the Treatment Cohort (Aug 1–30) against a holdout Control Cohort from the same period to rule out seasonality.*
*Flag any segment where sign-ups increased but core engagement decreased by >10% as a critical risk area.*
