**Re-Analysis Strategy: Isolating Causal Impact from Network Effects**

To determine whether the 25% lift was driven by the incentive or by geographic clustering, you must move from a user-level analysis to a cluster-level analysis that accounts for spillover. The current result is likely inflated because control users in Manila benefited from treated neighbors (violating the Stable Unit Treatment Value Assumption, or SUTVA).

**1. Redefine the Unit of Analysis (Cluster Randomization)**
Stop analyzing individual users. Instead, aggregate data by geographic community (e.g., barangay or zip code).
*   **Action:** Classify each community as "Treatment-Dense" (>50% treated users), "Control-Dense" (>50% control users), or "Mixed."
*   **Exclusion:** Immediately exclude "Mixed" communities from the primary causal estimate. These are the source of your contamination. In these zones, control users transacted more because their friends had double credits, artificially depressing the difference between your test and control groups.

**2. Calculate the "Pure" Effect vs. Spillover**
Run two separate comparisons using only the pure clusters:
*   **Direct Effect:** Compare "Treatment-Dense" communities against "Control-Dense" communities located in *low-density/dispersed* areas. This isolates the incentive's power without network interference. If this lift drops significantly (e.g., from 25% to 8%), the original result was mostly network-driven.
*   **Spillover Quantification:** Compare "Control-Dense" communities in Manila (near treatment) against "Control-Dense" communities in dispersed regions (far from treatment). The difference here is your quantified spillover effect. This tells you exactly how much free lift the network provides.

**3. Segment by Market Density**
Since your decision hinges on global expansion vs. urban restriction, segment the re-analyzed data by population density quartiles.
*   **Hypothesis Check:** If the incentive only works in the top density quartile, the program is a "Market Specific Feature," not a global growth lever.
*   **Metric Adjustment:** Report "Lift per 1,000 Users" rather than total percentage. Dense markets naturally have higher transaction velocities; normalizing by population prevents high-density bias from masquerading as program success.
