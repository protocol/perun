### Executive Recommendation: Reframe Success from "Time Spent" to "Throughput Efficiency"

The conflicting signals you are seeing are not a failure of the product; they are a validation of your hypothesis. Your team assumed faster planning would allow drivers to handle *more* deliveries. If drivers are completing their routes faster, they naturally spend less time in the app. Therefore, **a drop in session duration is the expected outcome of a successful efficiency tool**, not a sign of disengagement. Marcus's concern stems from measuring "engagement" (time) rather than "productivity" (output).

To resolve this debate and present a clear recommendation by Friday, you must shift your primary metric from **Session Duration** to **Revenue (or Deliveries) Per Minute**.

#### Immediate Analysis Plan

**1. Calculate "Throughput Efficiency" (The North Star)**
Stop looking at total time. Instead, calculate the rate of value creation.
*   **Metric:** `Total Deliveries Completed / Total App Session Time`
*   **Hypothesis:** If Quick-Route is working, this ratio should increase significantly, even if the denominator (time) shrinks.
*   **Action:** Compare this rate for the cohort using Quick-Route vs. those who are not. If Quick-Route users are completing 30% more deliveries per minute of app time, the feature is a massive win, regardless of the 9-minute drop in session length.

**2. Analyze "Shift Utilization" vs. "App Time"**
Drivers are paid for their shift, not their app usage. The goal is to maximize billable driving time, not administrative time.
*   **Metric:** `Deliveries per Shift` and `Idle Time Between Stops`.
*   **Pattern to Find:** Are drivers finishing their routes earlier? Are they accepting more deliveries within the same 8-hour shift?
*   **Interpretation:** If session time drops but deliveries per shift rise, you have successfully converted "app admin time" into "revenue-generating drive time." This is a direct labor cost saving and revenue increase.

**3. Segment by Driver Tenure and Volume**
Ensure the drop in time isn't isolated to a specific group that might be struggling.
*   **Action:** Break down the data by high-volume vs. low-volume drivers.
*   **Pattern to Find:** If high-performers are seeing the biggest time savings and the biggest delivery increases, the tool is amplifying your best assets. If only low-volume drivers are using it and spending less time without increasing output, you may have an adoption quality issue.

**4. Check for "Premature Exit" (The Only Real Risk)**
The only scenario where Marcus is right is if drivers are quitting the app *before* finishing their work because the tool is confusing or broken.
*   **Metric:** `Route Completion Rate` and `Support Tickets per 100 Routes`.
*   **Pattern to Find:** If completion rates remain stable (or improve) while time drops, the process is smoother. If completion rates drop, drivers might be abandoning complex routes. Given the 42% daily adoption, widespread abandonment is unlikely, but verify this to silence concerns about "disengagement."

#### Final Recommendation Strategy for Friday

Present the findings to Marcus and the executive team with this narrative:

*"The 32% reduction in session duration is not a loss of engagement; it is a gain in efficiency. We are successfully reducing the administrative burden on our drivers. By shifting our success metric from 'Time in App' to 'Deliveries per Minute,' the data shows that Quick-Route is allowing our fleet to generate more revenue in less time. We recommend officially adopting 'Throughput Efficiency' as our primary KPI for logistics tools and expanding Quick-Route access to 100% of the fleet to maximize shift utilization."*

**Note:** Do not defend the feature based on adoption rates alone; adoption proves usability, but efficiency proves business value.
**Note:** If "Revenue per Minute" data is unavailable, use "Deliveries per Shift" as the immediate proxy for your Friday presentation.
