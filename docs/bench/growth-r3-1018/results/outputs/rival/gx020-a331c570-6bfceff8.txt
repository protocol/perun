**Recommendation: Prioritize "Qualified Viewing Rate" (QVR) as the Primary Metric**

Do not optimize for total lead volume, nor for raw viewing count alone. Instead, make **Qualified Viewing Rate** your North Star metric for the Q3 roadmap.

**Definition:**
QVR = (Number of Viewings that Result in a "Sales-Qualified Opportunity" or Second Showing) / (Total Unique Visitors to Landing Page)

**The Rationale**
Your current dilemma exists because you are optimizing for intermediate steps that have decoupled from revenue.
*   **Lead Volume** is a vanity metric in this context; your test proved that adding friction reduces volume, but the sales team confirmed these fewer leads are not necessarily better.
*   **Viewing Rate** is a flawed proxy; a user can attend a viewing without being a serious buyer (e.g., curious neighbors, unqualified renters, or tire-kickers). The 15% viewing rate in your test did not translate to better sales conversations, indicating you are successfully filtering for "people willing to fill out forms," not "people ready to buy."

To align with actual revenue, the experiment must measure the *outcome* of the viewing, not just the attendance. By defining a "Qualified Viewing" (e.g., a viewing where the agent rates the buyer as "High Intent" or schedules a second visit/offer discussion), you force the optimization to find users who are both serious enough to convert and qualified enough to buy.

**Implementation for Q3 Experiment**
1.  **Redefine Success:** Work with Sales to define a binary "Qualified" flag for every viewing within 48 hours of the appointment.
2.  **The Hypothesis:** "Adding specific qualifying questions (e.g., 'Timeline to buy' or 'Pre-approval status') will decrease total leads by ~25% but increase the *Qualified Viewing Rate* by >40%, resulting in a net increase in sales-ready opportunities."
3.  **The Guardrail:** Monitor "Cost Per Qualified Viewing." If the friction increases ad costs so much that the cost per *qualified* viewing exceeds your lifetime value threshold, the test fails regardless of the rate.

**Decision Framework for the CEO**
*   **If QVR increases:** The friction is working. You are paying for fewer clicks but acquiring more revenue-ready buyers. Proceed with the high-friction form for the July campaign.
*   **If QVR stays flat or drops:** The friction is merely annoying serious buyers or filtering incorrectly. Revert to the low-friction form and investigate other quality levers (e.g., ad targeting, landing page copy) rather than form length.

This approach shifts the debate from "volume vs. quality" to "revenue efficiency," ensuring the July ad budget is spent on acquiring customers, not just data points.

*Note: Ensure your CRM captures the "Qualified" status automatically to prevent sales team bias in reporting. This metric requires a closed-loop feedback system between marketing and sales to function.*
