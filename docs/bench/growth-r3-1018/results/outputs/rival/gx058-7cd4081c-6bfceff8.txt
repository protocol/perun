**To:** Elena, Chief Product Officer
**From:** UX Lead
**Subject:** Navigation Test Analysis & Recommendation (April 5–19)

**Executive Summary**
The redesigned navigation successfully increased destination discovery (+15% CTR) but introduced friction downstream, causing an 8% drop in booking completions. Given our $1,200 average booking value, this represents a significant revenue risk. The data suggests the new menu attracts lower-intent traffic or disrupts the post-click flow. We recommend **pausing the full rollout** and initiating a targeted funnel dissection before deciding to iterate or abandon.

**Dissecting the Funnel: Where the Breakdown Occurs**
To distinguish between "curiosity clicks" and "friction," we must analyze the drop-off points immediately following the destination page view. Do not look at aggregate conversion rates alone; segment the data as follows:

1.  **Intent vs. Exploration Analysis**
    Compare the *bounce rate* and *time-on-page* for users arriving via the new menu versus the control.
    *   **Hypothesis A (Curiosity):** If bounce rates are significantly higher and time-on-page is lower for the variant, users are clicking out of curiosity but lack booking intent. The menu is too prominent for passive browsers.
    *   **Hypothesis B (Friction):** If engagement on the destination page is similar but drop-off spikes at the *search results* or *date selection* stage, the menu is successfully driving intent, but the subsequent flow is broken or misaligned with user expectations set by the new nav.

2.  **Path Length & Looping**
    Analyze the average number of clicks from "Destination Page" to "Booking Start."
    *   If the variant users take more clicks or exhibit "looping" behavior (returning to the home page or menu repeatedly), the new navigation may have removed critical contextual cues or filters that helped users narrow choices efficiently.

3.  **Device-Specific Performance**
    Check if the 8% drop is isolated to mobile. Expanded menus often consume critical screen real estate on mobile devices, pushing primary booking CTAs below the fold or making date pickers harder to access.

**Strategic Recommendation**
Do not abandon the design entirely yet, as the 15% increase in destination engagement indicates a successful improvement in discoverability. However, the 8% revenue loss is unacceptable without mitigation.

**Immediate Next Steps:**
1.  **Rollback to 50% Traffic:** Reduce exposure to limit revenue bleed while we investigate.
2.  **Session Replay Audit:** Review 50–100 session recordings of users who clicked a destination but did not start a booking. Look specifically for "rage clicks," hesitation on date pickers, or immediate exits.
3.  **Iterative Hypothesis:** If the issue is *curiosity*, add a "Plan Trip" qualifier or secondary CTA to filter intent before the click. If the issue is *friction*, streamline the search results page to match the simplified aesthetic of the new nav.

**Decision Gate:**
If the funnel dissection reveals that >60% of the drop-off is due to low-intent bounces (Hypothesis A), we should iterate the menu to better signal commitment (e.g., changing copy from "Explore" to "Book"). If the drop-off occurs deep in the funnel (Hypothesis B), the navigation is working, and the engineering team must fix the downstream booking flow immediately.

We will present a finalized root-cause analysis and a revised A/B test plan by next Tuesday's roadmap review.
