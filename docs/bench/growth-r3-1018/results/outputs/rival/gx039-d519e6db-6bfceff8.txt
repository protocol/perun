### Holistic Analysis: Contextual Tooltip Experiment

**Executive Summary**
The experiment presents a classic growth tension: **adoption efficiency vs. user sentiment**. While the 25% lift in click-through rate (CTR) proves the tooltips successfully drive discovery, the 14% drop in satisfaction (4.2 → 3.6) signals a friction cost that threatens your North Star: retention. Do not ship this globally yet. The immediate risk is that "successful" clicks are actually frustration clicks or that the intrusion drives passive churn among power users who value a clean interface.

#### 1. Additional Behavioral Data to Analyze
To determine if the clicks translate to genuine value (and thus retention), you must move beyond CTR to **post-click engagement** and **segmented retention**.

*   **Feature Retention Cohorts:** Isolate users who clicked the tooltip vs. those who discovered the feature organically. Do the "tooltip-clicked" users return to that feature in Week 2 and Week 4? If they click once and never return, the tooltip drove curiosity, not utility.
*   **Power User Segmentation:** Your hypothesis identifies power users as the dissatisfied group. Segment the satisfaction drop by usage intensity (e.g., sessions per week, tenure). If the drop is isolated to the top 20% of users, the tooltip is likely redundant for them. If the drop is universal, the execution is fundamentally flawed.
*   **Session Depth & Duration:** Did the tooltip interrupt a flow? Check if session duration decreased or if bounce rates increased immediately after the tooltip interaction. A "successful" click that ends a session is a failure.
*   **Dismissal Rates:** If the tooltip has a "close" or "don't show again" option, analyze the dismissal rate. High dismissal rates combined with low satisfaction confirm the "intrusive" hypothesis.

#### 2. Weighing Quantitative Lift vs. Qualitative Feedback
You cannot trade retention for adoption; adoption is only valuable if it sustains retention. Use this framework to weigh the data:

*   **The "Empty Victory" Check:** If the 25% CTR lift does not correlate with a lift in **Day-7 or Day-30 retention** for the treatment group, the quantitative win is vanity. Adoption without retention is noise.
*   **Sentiment as a Leading Indicator:** A 0.6 point drop in satisfaction is statistically massive in a sample of 20,000. In SaaS, satisfaction scores often precede churn by 30–60 days. Treat the sentiment drop as a lagging indicator of future retention loss.
*   **Net Value Calculation:** Estimate the Lifetime Value (LTV) of a user who adopts the feature via tooltip vs. the churn risk of a dissatisfied power user. If the churn risk of annoying 1,000 power users outweighs the LTV gain of 5,000 new feature adopters, the net impact is negative.

#### Recommendation for the Product Design Lead
**Do not remove the feature entirely, but do not ship the current version.** The data suggests the *mechanism* works (users click), but the *experience* fails (users feel patronized).

1.  **Iterate on Triggers:** Restrict the tooltip to users who have *not* yet used the feature after X sessions. Power users who already know the feature should never see it.
2.  **Refine Copy & Tone:** Shift from instructional ("Click here to...") to value-driven ("Unlock X capability"). Test a less intrusive UI pattern, such as a subtle badge rather than a pop-up.
3.  **Run a Follow-up Test:** Launch a variant with smarter triggering logic. Measure **retention** as the primary metric, with CTR and Satisfaction as guardrails. Only ship if retention lifts *without* a significant satisfaction penalty.

The goal is not just to make users click, but to make them stay. Prioritize the long-term retention signal over the short-term adoption spike.

*Note: Ensure your satisfaction survey sample is representative of the entire treatment group, not just those who interacted with the tooltip, to avoid self-selection bias.*
