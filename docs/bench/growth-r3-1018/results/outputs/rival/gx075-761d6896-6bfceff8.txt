### Experiment Evaluation: Tooltip Frequency Test

**Recommendation:** Do not make this change permanent for all users. The risk of churning your highest-value segment (power users) outweighs the support cost savings. Instead, implement a segmented rollout where tooltip frequency is dynamic based on user expertise.

#### 1. Quantitative Trade-off Analysis
To present a clear case to the VP of Product, you must quantify the "hidden cost" of the support reduction.

*   **Calculate the Churn Risk Value:**
    *   **Current State:** 800 power users (10% of 8,000).
    *   **Threat:** 150 complaints represent ~19% of your power user base voicing active dissatisfaction. In B2B engineering software, vocal frustration often precedes churn.
    *   **Action:** Estimate the Lifetime Value (LTV) of a power user. If losing just 5% of this segment (40 users) costs more annually than the savings from the reduced support tickets, the test is a net financial loss.
*   **Support Ticket Attribution:**
    *   Verify if the ticket drop is statistically significant or within normal variance.
    *   Analyze *which* tickets dropped. If they are trivial queries that power users wouldn't ask anyway, the feature is solving problems for new users while punishing experts.

#### 2. Critical Engagement Metrics to Identify the "Tipping Point"
You need to isolate where "helpful" becomes "hindrance." Analyze the following metrics specifically for the power user segment during the test window (Feb 10–24):

*   **Task Completion Time (Time-on-Task):** Compare the average time to complete core workflows (e.g., generating a specific report or model) between the test group and a control group (if available) or pre-test baselines. A statistically significant increase indicates workflow friction.
*   **Tooltip Dismissal Rate:** Track how quickly users close the tooltips.
    *   *Metric:* Time from hover-appearance to close.
    *   *Signal:* If power users are dismissing tooltips in <0.5 seconds consistently, they are treating them as obstacles, not aids.
*   **"Rage" Interactions:** Monitor for rapid, repeated clicking or erratic mouse movements immediately following a tooltip appearance. This is a strong behavioral proxy for frustration.
*   **Feature Adoption/Usage Frequency:** Did power users reduce their usage frequency of the software during the two-week test compared to their historical average? A drop in daily active usage is a leading indicator of churn.

#### 3. Strategic Path Forward: Segmentation
Your assumption that guidance is "universally welcome" was the flaw. Engineering tools require different interfaces for novices and experts.

*   **Immediate Action:** Revert to the original tooltip frequency for all users until a segmented solution is deployed. This stops the bleeding with power users before the March 1st deadline.
*   **Proposed Solution:** Implement an "Expert Mode" or dynamic frequency logic.
    *   **Logic:** If a user has completed a specific action >X times or has an account age >Y months, reduce tooltip frequency by 80% or disable them entirely.
    *   **New Hypothesis:** "Targeted tooltips for novice users will reduce support tickets by 20% *without* impacting power user workflow efficiency or satisfaction."

**Conclusion for VP:** The test proved that a blanket approach damages the experience of your most critical users. The data suggests a segmented approach will capture the support benefits while protecting revenue from power users.

*Note: Ensure your analytics tool can distinguish user segments by tenure or usage volume before re-running the test.*
*Do not rely solely on support ticket volume; prioritize retention metrics for the top 10% of users.*
