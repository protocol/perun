**Recommendation: Declare this a technical failure and discard the experiment results.**

Do not attempt to statistically adjust for the missing data or salvage signal from the first three days. The imbalance in group sizes (4,500 control vs. 2,100 variant) combined with the specific nature of the crash (affecting the new code path harder) indicates **selection bias** that cannot be corrected post-hoc.

**Why the data is unusable:**
1.  **Broken Randomization:** A/B testing relies on the assumption that users are randomly assigned and remain in their assigned group regardless of technical performance. Because the variant code caused or exacerbated the crash, the users remaining in the variant group are no longer a random sample. They are specifically the users who *didn't* encounter the fatal errors that drove others away. Comparing this "survivor" group to the stable control group will produce misleadingly positive or negative results that reflect server stability, not user preference.
2.  **Time-Bound Bias:** Isolating the first three days (Oct 12–14) introduces time-based bias. User behavior on Tuesday–Thursday often differs significantly from weekends or the following week. Furthermore, if the marketing push drove the traffic spike that caused the crash, the early traffic composition may differ from the later intended audience.
3.  **No Statistical Fix:** There is no statistical method to reliably reconstruct what the missing 50% of variant users would have done. Any adjustment would require assuming the behavior of the crashed sessions, which is exactly the unknown variable you are trying to measure.
