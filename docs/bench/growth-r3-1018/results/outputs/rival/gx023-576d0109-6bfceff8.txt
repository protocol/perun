# Weekly Churn Risk Indicators Report for WorkFlow DK

**To:** Lars, Customer Success Director
**From:** Analytics Team
**Date:** October 24, 2023
**Subject:** Top Three Churn Predictors for Enterprise Intervention Protocol

Based on current data patterns regarding administrative breadth and support volume, the following three indicators should be monitored weekly to identify at-risk enterprise accounts before cancellation talks begin.

### 1. Administrative Breadth Ratio (Active Admins vs. Total Seats)
**Why it matters:** Your data indicates a sharp divergence in retention based on admin count. Clients with <10 active admins churn within six months, while those with ≥15 rarely leave. This suggests that "administrative breadth" is a proxy for organizational integration; more admins imply the software is embedded across multiple departments, increasing switching costs.
**Weekly Action:** Flag any enterprise account where the number of *active* administrators (logged in within 14 days) drops below 12 or remains stagnant below 10 for three consecutive weeks.
**Sector Nuance:** Given that 80% of your base is Tech, this threshold may need adjustment for Manufacturing. Manufacturing firms often have more rigid hierarchies; they may require fewer admins to achieve deep integration compared to agile Tech firms. Do not apply the "15 admin" safety benchmark to Manufacturing clients without segmenting the data first. A Manufacturing client with 12 admins might be fully integrated, whereas a Tech client with 12 might be under-utilized.

### 2. The Support-to-Login Efficiency Delta
**Why it matters:** You observed a 15% rise in support tickets prior to churn, but causality is unclear. High support volume alone is ambiguous; it can signal "growing pains" (high engagement while learning) or "disengagement" (friction preventing value realization). The differentiator is login frequency.
**How to distinguish signals:**
*   **Growing Pains (Safe):** Support tickets increase **AND** login frequency/feature adoption remains stable or increases. This indicates users are struggling but persisting to solve problems.
*   **Genuine Disengagement (Risk):** Support tickets increase **WHILE** login frequency or feature adoption declines. This indicates users are encountering barriers, losing confidence, and disengaging rather than pushing through.
**Weekly Action:** Calculate the ratio of *Support Tickets per Active User* week-over-week. Trigger an alert if this ratio rises for two consecutive weeks *concurrently* with a drop in Weekly Active Users (WAU) or core feature adoption rates.

### 3. Core Feature Adoption Concentration
**Why it matters:** Broad usage protects against churn. If an account relies on only one or two features, the perceived value is fragile. Deep integration implies usage across the platform's value proposition.
**Weekly Action:** Monitor the "Feature Spread" per account—the percentage of available core modules used by at least one admin in the last 30 days. A contraction in this spread (e.g., dropping from 5 modules to 2) is a leading indicator of shrinking internal advocacy, often preceding the admin count drop.

### Implementation Note for December 1st Protocol
To validate the Manufacturing vs. Tech hypothesis before full rollout, run a retrospective cohort analysis on your existing Manufacturing clients. Compare their average admin count at the 6-month mark against their retention status. If the "15 admin" safety threshold does not hold for this sector, adjust the "Administrative Breadth" alert threshold specifically for Manufacturing accounts to avoid false positives.
