**Recommendation: Do Not Implement. Re-run the Test.**

You cannot trust these results to make a permanent change. While the sheer volume of data (170,000 users in the variant) provides statistical power, the 85/15 split introduces critical risks that invalidate the finding for a roadmap decision.

**1. The Bias Risk is Real and Uncorrectable**
Your assumption about device and time-of-day bias is correct. In e-commerce, conversion rates fluctuate significantly by hour (e.g., lunch breaks vs. late night) and device (mobile vs. desktop).
*   **Time-of-Day:** If the 85% variant group disproportionately captured high-intent traffic windows (e.g., weekday evenings) while the 15% control captured low-intent windows (e.g., early morning), the 2.5% lift is an artifact of timing, not the design change.
*   **Device Mix:** If the routing algorithm inadvertently sent more high-converting desktop users to the variant, the result is skewed.
*   **Post-Hoc Correction Failure:** You cannot fully correct this post-hoc. While you can segment by device, you cannot reconstruct the counterfactual for every specific time slot or micro-segment with only 30,000 control users. The control group is too small to serve as a reliable baseline for complex segmentation.

**2. The Cost of a False Positive is High**
A 2.5% lift sounds attractive, but with wide confidence intervals, the true effect could easily be zero or negative.
*   **Scenario A (False Positive):** You implement a change that actually hurts conversion by 0.5%. On 200,000 visitors, this compounds to significant revenue loss over a year.
*   **Scenario B (Missed Opportunity):** The wide intervals might also hide a larger win, but acting on noise prevents you from finding the real signal later.

**3. The Path Forward: Re-run with Proper Allocation**
Given the deadline is "end of month," you have time for a corrected, shorter test.
*   **Re-allocate:** Run a standard 50/50 split.
*   **Sample Size:** To detect a 2.5% lift with 80% power and 95% confidence (assuming a ~3% baseline), you need approximately 40,000–50,000 users *per variant*.
*   **Timeline:** At your current traffic volume (20k/day), a valid test requires only **5 days** of runtime.
*   **Action:** Launch the corrected test immediately. You will have statistically valid, bias-free results in one week, leaving three weeks for implementation if the win holds.

The current data is directionally interesting but operationally dangerous. The imbalance creates unquantifiable exposure to temporal and device bias.
