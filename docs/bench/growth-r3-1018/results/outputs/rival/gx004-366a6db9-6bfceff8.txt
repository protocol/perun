# Pricing Strategy Impact Analysis: Short-Term Friction vs. Long-Term Risk

**Executive Summary**
The 15% price increase has successfully lifted Average Contract Value (ACV) by 12.5%, partially offsetting a 25% decline in deal volume. While short-term revenue is down 15.6%, the strategy's long-term viability hinges entirely on whether the higher entry price induces churn at the first renewal. Current data confirms a "price friction" effect on acquisition; however, the risk of "value mismatch" churn remains unquantified until the first renewal cycle completes.

### 1. Short-Term Financial Impact (Q1 Performance)

The immediate effect of the pricing change is a net reduction in booked revenue, driven by a volume drop that outpaces the price hike.

| Metric | Previous Period (Baseline) | Current Period (New Pricing) | Variance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Deal Count** | 60 | 45 | **-25%** |
| **Avg Contract Value** | €12,000 | €13,500 | **+12.5%** |
| **Total Booked Revenue** | **€720,000** | **€607,500** | **-15.6%** |

**Analysis:**
The 15% price increase resulted in only a 12.5% increase in ACV, suggesting a slight mix shift toward smaller seat counts or discounting to close deals. More critically, the 25% drop in volume indicates high price elasticity in the short term. The sales team is experiencing friction, confirming your hypothesis that the pitch requires adjustment. The net result is a **€112,500 revenue gap** for this period compared to the baseline.

### 2. Long-Term Revenue Scenarios

Long-term success depends on the retention rate of these 45 new clients. We model two scenarios based on potential churn spikes at the first renewal (typically 12 months).

**Assumptions:**
*   Baseline renewal rate for historical cohorts: 90%.
*   Annual expansion (upsell) on surviving accounts: 5%.

#### Scenario A: Temporary Friction (Your Hypothesis)
*Assumption: Churn remains at baseline (90% retention). The lower volume is purely an acquisition hurdle.*
*   **Year 1 Revenue:** €607,500 (Initial Bookings)
*   **Year 2 Renewal Revenue:** 45 deals × 90% retention × €13,500 × 1.05 expansion = **€574,593**
*   **Cumulative 2-Year Value:** €1,182,093
*   **Verdict:** If retention holds, the higher ACV eventually outweighs the volume loss by Year 3 as the pipeline adjusts. The short-term dip is an acceptable investment for higher margin per seat.

#### Scenario B: Value Mismatch Churn (Risk Case)
*Assumption: Higher entry cost leads to dissatisfaction, dropping retention to 75%.*
*   **Year 1 Revenue:** €607,500 (Initial Bookings)
*   **Year 2 Renewal Revenue:** 45 deals × 75% retention × €13,500 × 1.05 expansion = **€478,828**
*   **Cumulative 2-Year Value:** €1,086,328
*   **Comparison:** This is only **8.4% higher** than the baseline 2-year value (€999,000), despite the significant sales effort and risk.
*   **Verdict:** If churn exceeds 20% (retention <80%), the strategy fails to generate meaningful long-term uplift relative to the risk and the lost volume opportunity.

### 3. Strategic Recommendations

1.  **Immediate Sales Enablement:** The 25% volume drop suggests the value proposition is not landing at the new price point. Revise sales scripts to emphasize ROI over features immediately. Consider temporary approval for larger discretionary discounts to stabilize volume while messaging is refined.
2.  **Implement "Early Warning" Cohort Tracking:** Do not wait for renewal to measure churn risk. Track **Day-30 and Day-90 activation rates** for these 45 deals against historical baselines. Low early usage is a leading indicator of renewal failure in SaaS.
3.  **Define a Kill-Switch Metric:** Establish a threshold for intervention. If activation rates fall >15% below baseline or if early cancellation requests exceed 5% before month 6, initiate a pricing review or targeted success intervention program.

**Conclusion:**
The pricing increase has improved unit economics but damaged top-line velocity. The strategy is viable only if the new cohort proves stickier or more valuable than historical averages. Without immediate intervention in sales messaging and proactive customer success monitoring, the risk of a "churn spike" negates the ACV gains.

*Note: This analysis assumes static market conditions; competitor pricing reactions could further impact Q2 volume. Data for activation rates should be pulled from your product analytics platform to validate early health.*
