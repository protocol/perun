# Experiment Report: Recommendation Engine Accuracy (October)

## Executive Summary
The new recommendation engine successfully increased purchase volume but negatively impacted order value and product quality. While users added **5% more items** to their carts, the **3% decrease in Average Order Value (AOV)** and **2% increase in return rates** suggest the algorithm is prioritizing low-cost, impulse titles over relevant, premium books. A final go/no-go decision requires calculating the net profit impact, as current data shows conflicting signals between volume growth and margin erosion.

## Key Findings

| Metric | Change vs. Control | Interpretation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Items per Cart** | +5% | **Positive:** Users are engaging more and adding more titles. |
| **Average Order Value** | -3% | **Negative:** Added items are lower-priced; premium discovery failed. |
| **Return Rate** | +2% | **Critical Risk:** Relevance is low; users are returning recommended items. |

## The Core Conflict
The hypothesis assumed better relevance would drive both volume and value. The data indicates the opposite: the engine is driving **volume through cheap, low-relevance items**. This creates a "hollow growth" scenario where top-line unit counts rise, but profitability likely declines due to lower margins on cheap books and increased operational costs from returns.

## Required Analysis: Net Profit Impact
Before the merchandising director can decide on algorithmic tweaks (Price vs. Relevance), we must quantify the financial outcome. Please provide or calculate the following four data points to determine the **Net Profit Delta**:

1.  **Gross Margin Profile of Recommended Items:**
    *   What is the average margin % of the *incremental* books added via recommendations compared to the control group? (Cheap books often have lower absolute margin dollars even if volume is higher).
2.  **Cost of Returns:**
    *   What is the fully loaded cost of a return (shipping + restocking + lost inventory value) for the recommended titles?
    *   *Calculation:* `(Total Recommended Sales × 2% Return Rate) × Cost Per Return`.
3.  **Revenue Quality Adjustment:**
    *   Recalculate the "lift" in revenue by subtracting the value of returned goods. Does the 5% volume increase still result in net positive revenue after returns?
4.  **Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) Risk:**
    *   Is there a correlation between these specific returns and a drop in subsequent 30-day purchase frequency? (High return rates may suppress future visits).

## Preliminary Recommendation
**Do not scale the current algorithm.** The increase in return rates and decrease in AOV suggests the model is optimizing for the wrong variable (click/add probability vs. value/relevance).

**Decision Framework for Thursday:**
*   **If Net Profit is Negative:** Immediately revert to the previous engine or apply a "price floor" constraint to the algorithm.
*   **If Net Profit is Neutral/Positive:** Investigate if the profit is sustainable or if the increased return rate will degrade brand trust long-term.
*   **Next Step:** Run a follow-up test weighting the algorithm toward **margin** and **historical retention** rather than pure add-to-cart probability.
