### Recommendation: Pause National Rollout; Treat as Critical Blocker

Do not proceed with a national rollout based on the current data. The 12% increase in completed transactions is likely a **data artifact**, not a genuine improvement in user experience or revenue. The 25% spike in bounce rate on the payment confirmation screen for devices with <2GB RAM indicates a critical rendering failure that breaks the post-purchase trust loop.

**Interpretation of Conflicting Signals**

Your hypothesis that users believe the transaction failed due to rendering errors is the most logical explanation for the data divergence:
1.  **The "Win" is Illusory:** The increase in "completed transactions" likely captures users who successfully charged their cards but abandoned the session immediately because the confirmation screen appeared broken or blank. The system recorded a success, but the user did not receive visual validation.
2.  **The "Loss" is Real:** The bounce rate spike confirms that the interface is failing for a specific, high-value segment (older Android users in Indonesia). In emerging markets like Indonesia, older devices often represent a significant portion of the user base.
3.  **Risk Assessment:** Rolling out nationally risks scaling a bug that causes mass customer support tickets ("Did my payment go through?"), chargebacks, and brand erosion. Users who think a payment failed may attempt to pay again (double charging) or abandon the platform entirely.

**Decision Framework for the CTO**

*   **Exclude Low-End Devices from Analysis?** No. Excluding them now would hide the severity of the bug. While you might calculate a "cleaned" metric for theoretical potential, the business reality is that these devices *are* your market. Ignoring them validates a broken experience for a large user segment.
*   **Treat as Critical Blocker?** Yes. A failure in the payment confirmation flow is a P0 (critical) incident. The primary success metric (completed transactions) is currently untrustworthy because it cannot distinguish between a happy path and a "success followed by confusion."

**Immediate Next Steps**

1.  **Halt Rollout:** Keep the feature limited to the test environment or disable it for devices with <2GB RAM immediately via feature flag if possible.
2.  **Qualitative Validation:** Rapidly test the checkout flow on physical devices matching the failure profile (older Android, <2GB RAM) in Jakarta/Surabaya network conditions. Confirm if the confirmation screen renders correctly.
3.  **Refine Metrics:** Re-analyze the test data looking for "double payment" attempts or support tickets correlated with the treatment group. If double-payment attempts rose, the "success" metric is definitely contaminated.
4.  **Fix and Retest:** Deploy a fix for the rendering issue and re-run the test specifically on the low-end device segment before considering a broader release.

**Conclusion**
The experiment did not prove the new interface is better; it proved it is fragile. The conflicting signals are a classic symptom of a broken feedback loop, not a complex trade-off. Fix the rendering bug first; the 12% lift is irrelevant if the user thinks the product failed.

*Note: Ensure your analytics events on the confirmation page are firing client-side only after the DOM has fully rendered to avoid false positives in future tests.*
