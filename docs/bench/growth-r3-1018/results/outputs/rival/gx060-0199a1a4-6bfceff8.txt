# Referral Program Cohort Analysis & Q3 Strategy Recommendation

**To:** David, VP of Marketing
**From:** Growth Lead
**Date:** May 6
**Subject:** Q3 Referral Program Strategy: Leveraging Social Variance Within CPA Constraints

### Executive Summary
Our 30-day test yielded an overall viral coefficient (K-factor) of 0.8, missing the 1.2 target. However, aggregate data masks a critical divergence: **Social Media cohorts achieved K=1.5** (exceeding targets), while **Email cohorts stalled at K=0.4**. This indicates the incentive structure is not universally perceived; it resonates strongly in social contexts but fails to motivate email-driven users. To meet Q3 goals without exceeding the $15 CPA limit, we must pivot from a uniform strategy to a segmented approach that scales social acquisition while diagnosing or pausing email referrals.

### Factors to Investigate: Why the Variance?

Before finalizing the budget, investigate these three friction points to explain the 0.4 vs. 1.5 disconnect:

1.  **Contextual Relevance & Social Capital**
    *   **Hypothesis:** Social sharing leverages "social capital" (users look helpful/cool sharing with friends), whereas email feels transactional or spammy.
    *   **Investigation:** Review qualitative feedback and share mechanics. Did email users feel awkward sending a promotional blast to their contacts? Did social users feel they were sharing a valuable tool? Check if the default email copy feels too "salesy" compared to the social copy.

2.  **Friction in the Sharing Flow**
    *   **Hypothesis:** The number of clicks or friction points differs between channels.
    *   **Investigation:** Audit the user journey. Does the social share open a pre-populated, one-click modal? Does the email flow require manual entry of recipient addresses or subject line editing? High friction in email could explain the low conversion from invite to signup.

3.  **Audience Intent & Trust**
    *   **Hypothesis:** Users acquired via email may have lower product engagement themselves, leading to lower-quality referrals.
    *   **Investigation:** Compare the activation rates of the *referrers* in both cohorts. If email-acquired users are less engaged, they are less likely to successfully advocate for the product. Verify if the email cohort consists of colder leads compared to the social cohort.

### Strategic Recommendation: Segmented Allocation

**Recommendation:** **Double down on Social Channels immediately; Pause and Pivot Email.**

Attempting to "fix" the email flow to match social performance carries high risk and uncertainty. With a K-factor of 0.4, email referrals are likely costing significantly more than the $15 CPA limit when factoring in the incentive payout per successful conversion. Conversely, the social channel (K=1.5) is already efficient and scalable.

**Q3 Action Plan:**

1.  **Reallocate Budget to Social:** Shift 80% of the referral marketing budget to amplify social sharing incentives. Since K=1.5 indicates organic virality, even a modest increase in share volume will compound quickly.
2.  **Implement Channel-Specific Incentives:**
    *   **Social:** Maintain current structure or test small "bonus" tiers for users who hit 5+ referrals (leveraging the high coefficient).
    *   **Email:** Pause the standard incentive. Test a fundamentally different angle, such as a "team collaboration" frame rather than a "refer a friend" frame, or suppress email invites entirely for users who show low engagement scores.
3.  **CPA Guardrail:** Monitor the blended CPA weekly. With social K=1.5, your effective CPA should drop well below $15, providing buffer room to experiment with higher incentive caps for top social performers without breaching the limit.

### Conclusion
The data proves the offer is not broken; the *distribution channel* is the variable. Uniform incentives fail when audience contexts differ. By isolating the high-performing social channel and halting the bleed from low-performing email referrals, we can project a Q3 blended viral coefficient of 1.3–1.4 while maintaining a CPA under $12.
