**Recommendation: Define Activation as "First Outline Generated AND Edited"**

For WriteMate's goal of converting free users to paid plans within 14 days, you should maintain your current hypothesis: **Activation = User generates an outline AND makes at least one edit.**

Do not lower the bar to "Sign-in" or "Profile Complete," and do not raise it to "Export Document."

**Rationale & Trade-off Analysis**

1.  **Value Realization vs. Friction:**
    *   **Low Barrier (Sign-in/Profile):** While 30% drop off at profile setup, counting these users as "activated" inflates your metric with casual curiosity. These users have not experienced the core AI value proposition. Optimizing for this metric will lead to high "activation" rates but low conversion to paid, as these users haven't realized enough value to justify a purchase.
    *   **High Barrier (Export):** Requiring an export adds significant friction. A student may generate and refine an outline (realizing value) but decide to write the essay directly in their LMS or Word processor without using your export feature. Counting this as "unactivated" ignores genuine value realization and underreports your product's success.
    *   **The Sweet Spot (Generate + Edit):** The act of editing proves the user didn't just click a button; they engaged with the AI output, tailored it to their specific assignment, and invested cognitive effort. Your data showing a 60% return rate for this cohort confirms this is the moment value is realized. The "friction" of editing is actually a necessary filter for quality leads.

2.  **Strategic Alignment (14-Day Conversion):**
    To convert within 14 days, users must experience the "aha!" moment early. Editing an outline is that moment. If you define activation too loosely, your welcome emails (starting April 5th) will target users who aren't ready to buy, diluting your conversion rates. If you define it too strictly, you miss users who are ready to convert but haven't hit the specific "export" trigger.

**Implementation Plan for Priya**

*   **Primary Metric:** Track "Generate + Edit" as the official Activation event.
*   **Addressing the 30% Drop-off:** Do not change the activation definition to fix the profile drop-off. Instead, optimize the onboarding flow itself. Consider making profile fields optional or progressive (ask for major only after the first generation) to reduce friction *before* the activation event, rather than lowering the definition of success.
*   **Welcome Email Sequence:** Trigger the "Day 1" value email only upon "Generate + Edit." For users who sign up but don't reach this step, trigger a separate "nudge" sequence focused on removing barriers to that first edit (e.g., "Stuck? Try generating an outline for your next history paper in 30 seconds").

**Note:** Monitor the "Generate only" cohort separately; if a significant portion converts without editing, revisit this logic. Ensure your analytics tool distinguishes between auto-generated events and user-initiated edits to maintain data integrity.
