### Executive Summary: Recommendation Engine A/B Test Verdict

**Recommendation: Do not roll out globally yet.** Proceed with a targeted "diversity-constrained" iteration or revert if catalog health metrics degrade further in the next 7 days.

While the new engine delivers a **15% lift in total clicks**, this short-term gain is accompanied by a critical warning sign: **impression concentration has spiked from 40% to 60% within the top 10 titles**. This indicates the algorithm is optimizing for immediate engagement by surfacing already popular content rather than discovering new relevance for users. This is a classic "filter bubble" formation, not a stabilization phase.

#### The Trade-Off: Short-Term Clicks vs. Long-Term Health

| Dimension | Current Outcome | Long-Term Risk |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Engagement (Clicks)** | **+15% Lift** | **Diminishing Returns:** Users may click more now but burn through content faster, leading to earlier churn once the "popular" loop becomes repetitive. |
| **Catalog Health** | **Severe Concentration** | **Inventory Starvation:** 90% of your catalog is receiving significantly less exposure. New or niche content cannot gain traction, reducing the platform's unique value proposition over time. |
| **Personalization** | **Failing Hypothesis** | **Generic Experience:** The system is reverting to a "global top 10" list rather than true personalization. Users lose the feeling that the platform understands their specific tastes. |

#### Critical Metrics to Prioritize for the VP

To determine if the catalog is at risk, shift the discussion from "total clicks" to these three health indicators:

1.  **Gini Coefficient of Impressions:** Quantifies inequality in content distribution. A sharp rise (as seen here) confirms the algorithm is starving the long tail.
2.  **Repeat Consumption Rate:** Measure how often users click the *same* title or franchise within a 7-day window. An increase suggests users are looping on familiar hits rather than exploring.
3.  **Long-Tail Click-Through Rate (CTR):** Isolate CTR for content outside the top 50 titles. If this drops while overall clicks rise, the engine is actively suppressing discovery.

#### Strategic Options for Tuesday's Meeting

*   **Option A (Revert):** Safest for catalog health. Preserves the diverse ecosystem but forfeits the 15% click lift. Use if retention data shows early signs of fatigue.
*   **Option B (Constrained Rollout):** Implement a "diversity cap" (e.g., max 2 items from the top 10 per row) to force exploration while retaining the new ranking logic. This attempts to capture the engagement lift without the extreme concentration.
*   **Option C (Monitor & Sunset):** Roll out globally but set a hard automatic revert trigger if the "Top 10 Concentration" exceeds 65% or if Day-7 retention drops by >2%.

**Final Note:** A 15% click lift driven solely by popularity bias is often a "sugar high" that masks underlying erosion in user satisfaction and content viability. Prioritize the health of the catalog to ensure sustainable growth.
