**Recommendation: Do Not Roll Out the "No Survey" Variant**

Based on the April test results (10,000 sign-ups), removing the mandatory onboarding survey is a net negative for TaskFlow's long-term health. While the variant achieved a **15% increase in setup completion**, it resulted in a **20% decrease in 30-day retention**.

In a productivity tool reliant on network effects and team configuration, retention is the primary driver of Lifetime Value (LTV). A 20% drop in retention typically outweighs a 15% gain in activation because the cost of acquiring a user who churns immediately is higher than the opportunity cost of a slightly frictioned signup. The data suggests the survey acts as a critical "commitment device" or filtering mechanism, ensuring that only serious users with clear intent complete the setup. Removing it likely allowed low-intent users to enter the product, inflating activation metrics while diluting the quality of the active user base.

**Strategic Next Steps:**
1.  **Maintain Current Flow:** Keep the mandatory survey for the immediate next sprint to protect retention metrics.
2.  **Investigate the Mechanism:** The drop in retention is likely due to a lack of personalization (users aren't configuring the app correctly for their role/team size) rather than just self-selection. Users who skip the survey may be entering a "generic" experience that fails to demonstrate value quickly.
3.  **Run a Follow-up Test:** Instead of removing the survey, test **optimizing** it.
    *   *Hypothesis:* "By reducing the number of survey questions from [X] to [Y] or changing the format to a progressive profile build-out, we can maintain the personalization benefits (retention) while reducing the friction (activation)."
    *   *Metric:* Measure the ratio of Activation to Retention, not just activation in isolation.

**Decision Framework for the Head of Product:**
Do not optimize for "signups" or "setup completion" in isolation. For TaskFlow, a completed setup that does not lead to Day-30 retention is a false positive. The current survey, despite its friction, correlates with higher quality users. The goal for the next sprint should be refining the value exchange of the survey (making it feel less like a hurdle and more like a setup wizard) rather than eliminating it.

*Note: Ensure your analytics track "time-to-value" for both cohorts to confirm if the non-survey users are failing to configure key features.*
*This analysis assumes the 20% retention delta is statistically significant given the 10k sample size; verify confidence intervals before finalizing the roadmap.*
