### Executive Framework: Mobile Date Picker Experiment (UAE)

**1. Primary Metric Presentation: Effect Size Over Significance**
Report the **3.2% lift** as the observed effect size, but explicitly frame the **95% Confidence Interval (-1% to 7.4%)** as the measure of uncertainty.
*   **Interpretation for Leadership:** The data does not support a definitive "win" because the interval crosses zero (includes negative values). However, it also does not support killing the project, as the upper bound (7.4%) exceeds your 5% success threshold.
*   **Key Narrative:** "The result is inconclusive, not negative. We cannot rule out a meaningful gain, but we lack the statistical power to confirm it."

**2. Diagnostic Metrics to Explain Variance**
To address the wide interval and potential external factors, prioritize these three specific data points in your report:
*   **Daily Conversion Rates (Time-Series):** Plot daily conversion for Control vs. Variant from Oct 1–15. Look specifically for a divergence or convergence during the second week. If the lift disappears entirely during the local holiday period, the aggregate 3.2% is diluted by external noise, not experimental failure.
*   **Sample Size vs. Required Power:** Calculate the required sample size for detecting a 5% lift at 80% power. Compare this against the actual 12,000 daily users × 15 days. If the actual sample is below the requirement, explicitly state that the wide interval is a mathematical certainty of under-sampling, not data quality issues.
*   **Segment Stability Check:** Isolate the "Holiday Week" (Oct 8–15) data. If the variance within the treatment group spikes only during these dates compared to the control, this confirms the external factor hypothesis.

**3. Decision Framework: Rollout vs. Kill vs. Iterate**
Do not recommend a binary decision yet. Instead, present the following three paths based on the missing context you must gather:

*   **Path A: Extend the Test (Recommended if Holiday Impact is Confirmed)**
    *   *Condition:* If time-series data shows the holiday suppressed results.
    *   *Action:* Run the test for 7–10 more days post-holiday to normalize the data.
    *   *Goal:* Narrow the confidence interval to exclude zero.

*   **Path B: Kill the Project**
    *   *Condition:* If the lower bound of the confidence interval drops further (e.g., below -2%) upon re-analysis, or if the "holiday" data shows no anomaly and the wide interval persists due to low baseline conversion.
    *   *Rationale:* The risk of a negative impact on revenue outweighs the potential upside.

*   **Path C: Cautious Rollout to Saudi Desktop (Not Recommended Yet)**
    *   *Condition:* Only if qualitative data (session recordings) shows universal usability improvements despite statistical noise.
    *   *Risk:* Rolling out to a new device (desktop) and region (KSA) based on inconclusive mobile/UAE data introduces compounding variables. This is high-risk without a clear winner.

2.  **Session Replay Samples:** Pull 10 recordings from the Variant group during the holiday week. Did users struggle with the picker, or did they simply not travel? This distinguishes between UX failure and market behavior.
