**Executive Summary: Search Ranking Rollout Analysis**

**Current Status:** The 15% increase in click-through rate (CTR) confirms the new algorithm successfully surfaces more engaging niche products. However, the 5% decrease in Average Order Value (AOV) presents a revenue risk that requires immediate segmentation analysis before altering the rollout schedule.

**Distinguishing Signal from Noise**
Do not treat the 5% AOV drop as statistical noise yet. In search relevance tests, a divergence between engagement (CTR) and monetization (AOV) is often a structural signal, not a sample size anomaly. Niche products frequently have lower price points than mainstream "head" items. If the algorithm successfully shifts user clicks from high-priced mainstream items to lower-priced niche items, AOV will mathematically decrease even if user satisfaction increases.

To verify if this is structural:
1.  **Segment by Price Tier:** Compare AOV drops only within users who clicked niche items versus those who clicked mainstream items. If the drop persists within specific product categories, it is a structural pricing issue.
2.  **Check Conversion Rate (CVR):** If CVR has increased significantly alongside CTR, the lower AOV may be offset by higher volume, resulting in neutral or positive total revenue. If CVR is flat or down, the lower AOV represents a genuine revenue loss.

**Revenue Projection Framework**
To project the final impact on Q4 revenue, stop looking at AOV in isolation. Calculate **Revenue Per Search (RPS)** for the 5% cohort:
$$RPS = \text{Searches} \times \text{CTR} \times \text{CVR} \times \text{AOV}$$

Compare the RPS of the 5% test group against the 95% control group.
*   **Scenario A (Accelerate):** If RPS is neutral or positive despite lower AOV, the volume lift (CTR/CVR) compensates for the price drop. Accelerating to 50% is safe and aligns with engagement goals.
*   **Scenario B (Pause/Adjust):** If RPS is negative, the algorithm is cannibalizing revenue. The trade-off is structural. You must pause the rollout to re-weight the ranking formula to balance relevance with price signals before proceeding.

**Recommendation for CEO**
Do not accelerate to 50% by November 1st based solely on CTR gains. The risk to Q4 revenue targets is tangible if the AOV drop is structural.

1.  **Immediate Action:** Pause the scheduled ramp-up. Maintain the 5% exposure but extend the observation window by 7 days to stabilize CVR data.
2.  **Analysis Required:** Compute RPS and segment AOV by product category. Determine if the revenue loss is concentrated in specific high-margin categories.
3.  **Decision Gate:**
    *   If **RPS ≥ Control**: Resume rollout to 50%. The "niche" strategy drives volume that offsets lower prices.
    *   If **RPS < Control**: Halt rollout. The algorithm requires tuning to prevent revenue erosion. Present a revised timeline to the CEO contingent on fixing the revenue leakage, prioritizing Q4 targets over engagement metrics.

**Note:** Early rollout data often exaggerates variance; ensure confidence intervals are calculated before finalizing the RPS comparison.
**Note:** Communicate to the CEO that protecting Q4 revenue requires validating the *net* revenue impact, not just engagement wins.
