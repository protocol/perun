### Analysis Plan: Separating Novelty from Habit Formation

**Executive Summary for Kenji**
The data indicates a **pure novelty effect**. The 25% Day-1 spike represents users reacting to visual changes, not forming new habits. Since Day-7 retention converged exactly to the 18% baseline, the treatment group did not retain any of the initial gain. There is currently **no statistical evidence of latent value** in the new design to justify a rollout based on retention alone.

**Recommended Decision:** Do not roll out the design as a habit-forming solution. However, do not immediately revert if the Day-1 engagement spike drives other valuable metrics (e.g., immediate monetization or feature discovery).

**Method to Validate Without Months of Waiting**

To confirm this conclusion and check for hidden value without waiting for a new long-term cohort, execute the following three-step analysis on the existing 14-day dataset:

**1. Cohort Bridge Analysis (Days 2–6)**
Do not just compare Day 1 and Day 7. Plot the daily retention drop-off rate for both groups from Day 2 through Day 6.
*   **Hypothesis:** If the treatment group has a *steeper* daily drop-off than control between Days 2–6, the novelty actively cannibalized future retention (users got bored faster).
*   **Action:** Calculate the daily churn rate ($Churn_t = \frac{Retention_{t-1} - Retention_t}{Retention_{t-1}}$). If the treatment churn is significantly higher in this window, the design is harmful long-term, not just neutral.

**2. Behavioral Segmentation of the "Spiked" Users**
Isolate the specific users in the treatment group who returned on Day 1 *only* because of the new design (the incremental 25%).
*   **Analysis:** Compare the *depth* of their Day 1 session against the control group. Did they complete more workouts? Did they explore more features?
*   **Value Check:** If these users generated 20% more revenue or completed 30% more core actions on Day 1, the design has **monetization value** even if it lacks **retention value**. This changes the decision from "revert" to "optimize for short-term revenue."

**3. Leading Indicator Correlation**
Check if Day-1 behavior in the *control* group historically predicts Day-7 retention.
*   **Method:** Run a correlation on historical data: Does a user who logs in on Day 1 usually make it to Day 7?
*   **Interpretation:** If Day-1 login is a strong predictor of Day-7 retention historically, but the treatment group broke this link (high Day-1, normal Day-7), the design successfully decoupled initial interest from habit. This confirms the design is a "sugar rush" with no nutritional value.

**Final Recommendation Framework**
*   **Revert Immediately:** If Day 2–6 churn is higher in treatment AND Day-1 session depth is unchanged.
*   **Iterate, Don't Revert:** If Day-1 session depth/revenue is significantly higher. Keep the visuals but add a "habit hook" (e.g., a streak counter or push notification) specifically targeting the drop-off between Days 2–4 to sustain the initial interest.
*   **Rollback:** If the goal was strictly long-term retention habit formation. The experiment failed this specific objective.
