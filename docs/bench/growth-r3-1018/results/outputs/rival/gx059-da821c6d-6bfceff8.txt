# Contract Review Module: Metric Framework for LexFlow

## Primary Metric: Risk-Adjusted Efficiency Score
**Definition:** A composite metric weighing time saved against accuracy validation, specifically designed to address the legal profession's dual mandate of efficiency and risk mitigation.

**Formula:** `(Baseline Time - LexFlow Time) × Accuracy Validation Rate`

**Why this works for UK law firms:**
- **Addresses the core tension:** Lawyers won't sacrifice accuracy for speed. This metric proves you deliver both.
- **Quantifies the value proposition:** If LexFlow reduces contract review from 4 hours to 1 hour with 99% accuracy validation, the score captures the full value, not just the time delta.
- **Resonates with risk-averse buyers:** The accuracy validation component forces explicit measurement of error rates, satisfying the "do no harm" principle inherent in legal work.

**Measurement approach:**
- Track time per contract (automated via module timestamps)
- Implement a confidence scoring system where lawyers flag sections requiring human review
- Calculate accuracy validation as: `(Total Sections - Flagged Errors) / Total Sections`
- Benchmark against the 4-hour manual baseline from your pilot

## Secondary Metrics

### 1. Error Prevention Rate
**Definition:** Number of potential errors, inconsistencies, or compliance issues identified by LexFlow that would have been missed in manual review.

**Why it matters:** This directly addresses the "risk mitigation" priority. UK firms face significant liability for missed clauses or compliance failures. Quantifying prevented errors translates directly to risk reduction value.

**Measurement:** Track instances where LexFlow flags issues later confirmed by senior partners, plus any post-review corrections caught before client delivery.

### 2. Adoption Velocity by Seniority Level
**Definition:** Rate of module usage segmented by lawyer seniority (Partner, Associate, Junior).

**Why it matters:** Legal firms have hierarchical adoption patterns. If juniors use it but partners don't trust the output, the tool fails. This metric reveals whether the tool is penetrating decision-making levels or remaining siloed in low-stakes work.

**Measurement:** Weekly active users per seniority tier, plus percentage of contracts fully processed without manual override by each tier.

### 3. Client Delivery Time Reduction
**Definition:** Time from contract receipt to client-ready delivery, comparing LexFlow-assisted vs. manual contracts.

**Why it matters:** This is the ultimate business outcome that partners care about. It connects tool usage to revenue-generating activity (faster turnaround = more billable work or competitive advantage).

**Measurement:** Track timestamp from contract upload to final approval/client send, segmented by contract complexity.

## ROI Calculator Inputs for James

For the sales director's ROI model, structure the value proposition as:

**Annual Value = (Hours Saved × Billable Rate × Risk Adjustment Factor) + (Error Prevention Value)**

Where:
- **Hours Saved:** Baseline 4 hours minus LexFlow average, multiplied by contracts processed annually
- **Billable Rate:** UK law firm average (£250-£600/hour depending on firm tier)
- **Risk Adjustment Factor:** 0.8-1.0 multiplier based on accuracy validation rate (acknowledges that unvalidated time savings are worthless)
- **Error Prevention Value:** Estimated cost of potential claims avoided (use professional indemnity insurance claim averages as proxy)

## Implementation Notes

Start measuring from day one of each pilot deployment. The baseline 4-hour figure is your anchor—every metric should ladder up to proving improvement against this while maintaining or improving accuracy. Avoid leading with "time saved" in sales conversations; lead with "risk-adjusted efficiency" and let time savings be the supporting evidence.

**Critical success factor:** Ensure your accuracy validation mechanism is transparent and auditable. UK law firms will not trust a black box. The ability to show exactly what LexFlow flagged, what it missed, and how humans intervened is as important as the metrics themselves.
