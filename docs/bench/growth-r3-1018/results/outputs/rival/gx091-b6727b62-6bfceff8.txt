**Recommendation: Define "Conversion" as Fund Disbursement**

For PrestamoRápido's executive dashboard and primary success metric, you must define conversion as **Fund Disbursement** (the completion of identity verification and release of funds), not Loan Acceptance.

In a peer-to-peer lending model, value for lenders is not created when a borrower clicks "accept"; value is created only when the loan is funded and the repayment clock starts. Counting acceptance as conversion inflates your success metrics with 40% "fake" conversions that generate zero revenue and expose lenders to no risk (since no money moved). For your steering committee, reporting high conversion rates based on acceptance will lead to incorrect capital allocation and unrealistic growth projections.

**Addressing the Low Volume Concern**
Your fear of "low volume data" (150 users/day) is a measurement anxiety, not a business reality. 150 daily conversions equals **4,500 conversions per quarter**. This is statistically sufficient for trend analysis, cohort tracking, and weekly reporting. Do not dilute your primary metric with vanity data just to see bigger numbers. A smaller, accurate number is infinitely more valuable than a large, misleading one.

**Proposed Metric Structure for the Dashboard**

To solve your drop-off visibility problem without corrupting your primary metric, structure your analytics as follows:

**1. Primary Metric (North Star)**
*   **Event:** `loan_disbursed`
*   **Definition:** User completes identity verification AND funds are transferred.
*   **Usage:** Executive reporting, lender ROI calculations, growth targets.

**2. Secondary Metric (Operational Health)**
*   **Event:** `loan_accepted`
*   **Usage:** Marketing efficiency (Cost Per Acceptance) and top-of-funnel volume tracking.
*   **Note:** Treat this as a "lead" rather than a "customer."

**3. Critical Drop-off Metric (The 40% Problem)**
*   **Metric:** **Verification Completion Rate**
*   **Formula:** `(loan_disbursed / loan_accepted)`
*   **Target:** Monitor this daily. Your current baseline is ~60%.
*   **Actionability:** This specific metric isolates the friction in your identity verification step. If this rate drops, your product team knows immediately that the verification flow is broken, regardless of how many people accept loans.

**Pros and Cons in Mexico City Context**

| Definition | Pros | Cons |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Fund Disbursement** (Recommended) | **True Revenue Signal:** Aligns perfectly with lender ROI.<br>**Fraud Detection:** Filters out users who fail KYC (Know Your Customer) checks, critical in Mexican fintech compliance.<br>**Clear Accountability:** Forces the product team to fix the 40% drop-off rather than hiding it. | **Lower Volume:** Requires patience; trends take slightly longer to reach statistical significance than top-funnel metrics.<br>**Lag Time:** Includes the time taken for verification, delaying the feedback loop by hours or days. |
| **Loan Acceptance** | **High Volume:** Immediate data for marketing optimization.<br>**Fast Feedback:** Useful for A/B testing headline copy or offer amounts. | **Misleading Success:** Hides the 40% failure rate; you could double acceptances while disbursements stay flat.<br>**Lender Mistrust:** If lenders see "conversions" that never fund, they will lose confidence in the platform's vetting process. |
