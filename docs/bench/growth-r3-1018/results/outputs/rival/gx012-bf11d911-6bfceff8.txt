# Smart FAQ Widget: Success Metrics & Decision Framework

**Primary Success Metric: Net Efficiency Score (NES)**
To balance volume reduction against agent workload, we will track the **Net Efficiency Score** weekly. This metric calculates the total time saved by deflection minus the extra time spent on complex tickets, normalized per 1,000 visitors.

*Formula:* `(Tickets Deflected × Baseline Handle Time) - (Remaining Tickets × Increase in Handle Time)`

A positive NES indicates the widget is generating net operational value, even if average handle time rises. A negative NES suggests the widget is creating more work than it saves.

### Key Indicator Dashboard

Present these four indicators to the Head of Customer Success to diagnose the root cause of the increased resolution time:

**1. Deflection Rate vs. Complexity Shift**
*   **Metric:** Percentage of total sessions using the widget vs. percentage of remaining tickets tagged "Complex" or "Technical."
*   **Diagnostic:** If deflection is high (near your 20% target) AND the share of complex tickets rises proportionally, the widget is successfully filtering easy queries. If deflection is low but complex tickets rise, the widget is confusing users.

**2. Post-Widget Ticket Sentiment & Length**
*   **Metric:** Average word count and sentiment score of tickets submitted *after* a widget session.
*   **Diagnostic:** Compare these against tickets submitted without widget interaction.
    *   *Success Signal:* Higher word count but neutral/positive sentiment (users providing necessary detail for hard problems).
    *   *Failure Signal:* High word count with negative sentiment/frustration keywords (users angry about failed self-service).

**3. First Contact Resolution (FCR) on Remaining Tickets**
*   **Metric:** Percentage of remaining tickets resolved in a single interaction.
*   **Diagnostic:** If FCR remains stable despite longer handle times, agents are solving harder problems effectively. If FCR drops, the widget may be providing incorrect information that agents must first undo before solving the issue.

**4. Cost-Per-Resolved-Issue**
*   **Metric:** `(Total Support Labor Cost) / (Total Issues Resolved including deflected ones)`
*   **Diagnostic:** This is the ultimate financial gate for your hiring freeze. Even if handle time increases, if the cost per resolved issue drops due to volume reduction, the hiring pause is justified.

### Decision Matrix for Enterprise Expansion

Use the following thresholds to determine next week's action:

| Scenario | Net Efficiency Score | Post-Widget Sentiment | Recommendation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | : |
| **A. Ideal Filter** | Positive (>0) | Neutral/Stable | **Expand Immediately.** The widget is doing its job; complexity rise is expected. |
| **B. Confusing UX** | Negative (<0) | Negative | **Pause & Redesign.** The widget is creating friction and extra work. Do not expand. |
| **C. Partial Success** | Near Zero (±5%) | Neutral | **Optimize Logic.** Refine answers for top 5 failure topics before enterprise rollout. |
| **D. Hidden Value** | Negative (<0) | Positive | **Investigate Data.** Users like it, but it's not saving time. Check if "deflected" users are returning later. |

### Immediate Next Steps
1.  **Segment the 18% drop:** Isolate tickets by category. Did "Password Reset" drop while "API Integration" stayed flat? This confirms the "filtering" hypothesis.
2.  **Sample 50 tickets:** Manually review tickets from users who interacted with the widget. Look for phrases like "I already tried..." or "The article didn't help."
3.  **Calculate NES:** Plug last week's numbers into the Net Efficiency Score formula to see if the 18% volume drop outweighs the 25-minute time penalty.

*Note: Ensure your tracking distinguishes between users who found an answer and clicked "close" versus those who clicked "contact support" after viewing an article.*
*Do not expand to Enterprise until Scenario A or C is confirmed; enterprise clients have lower tolerance for failed self-service.*
