# NordicStream Retention Diagnosis Plan

## Executive Summary
The 8% conversion spike coupled with a 40% renewal rate indicates a "leaky bucket" scenario driven by the September 50% discount. The primary hypothesis is **price sensitivity mismatch**: the promotion attracted users whose willingness-to-pay is below the full price point, rather than users who undervalued the product. The investigation must isolate whether churn is driven by the price jump (affordability/intent) or product dissatisfaction (value realization).

## Phase 1: Cohort Segmentation & Data Isolation
Do not analyze all churned users together. Segment the September cohort into three distinct groups to identify patterns:
1.  **The "Discount-Only" Churners:** Users who canceled immediately upon the first full-price billing attempt.
2.  **The "Early Exit" Churners:** Users who canceled *during* the discounted month (indicates product/content failure).
3.  **The "Late Decay" Churners:** Users who paid the first full month but canceled before the second renewal.

**Action:** Pull raw event logs to categorize every churned user from the September cohort into these buckets. The ratio between Group 1 and Groups 2/3 will immediately tell you if this is a pricing problem or a product problem.

## Phase 2: Metric Framework for Root Cause
Calculate these four specific metrics to present to the CEO:

**1. Price Shock Elasticity**
*   *Formula:* (Churn rate at first full-price billing) vs. (Historical churn rate at first full-price billing).
*   *Insight:* If this delta is >20%, the cohort was purely bargain hunters. The discount cannibalized future revenue without building habit.

**2. Feature Adoption Delta**
*   *Formula:* Average number of premium features used per week by [September Cohort] vs. [August Cohort] vs. [Historical Average].
*   *Insight:* If September users used fewer features before churning, they never experienced the "aha" moment, regardless of price. If usage is high but they still churn, the issue is strictly price sensitivity.

**3. Survey Sentiment Clustering**
*   *Method:* Re-code the 200 contradictory survey responses into binary tags: "Price Objection" vs. "Content/Feature Objection." Ignore open-text nuance for the initial slide; count the tags.
*   *Insight:* Cross-reference tags with the segments above. Do "Early Exit" users cite content issues while "Discount-Only" users cite price?

**4. Cohort LTV Projection**
*   *Formula:* (Acquisition Cost + Discount Cost) vs. (Actual Revenue Collected + Projected Renewal Revenue based on 40% rate).
*   *Insight:* Compare this against the LTV of a standard organic user. If the discounted cohort LTV is negative or <50% of organic LTV, the strategy is financially unsustainable.

## Phase 3: Strategic Decision Matrix
Present the findings using this logic flow for the Monday meeting:

*   **Scenario A (High Price Shock, High Usage):** Users loved the product but couldn't/wouldn't pay full price.
    *   *Recommendation:* Pivot to a "阶梯式" (step-up) pricing model or a longer, smaller discount (e.g., 20% off for 3 months) to ease the transition. Stop the 50% one-month blast.
*   **Scenario B (Low Usage, Any Churn Reason):** Users didn't engage with premium features.
    *   *Recommendation:* The discount attracted the wrong audience or onboarding failed. Halt the discount immediately. Invest in onboarding flows that force feature adoption in week 1.
*   **Scenario C (Negative LTV):** The cost of the discount + acquisition exceeds the lifetime value of the retained 40%.
    *   *Recommendation:* Terminate the promotion immediately. It is destroying shareholder value.
