### Executive Summary: Dynamic Pricing Experiment (Weeks 1–3)

**Current Status:** The experiment is generating negative net revenue ($18k loss vs. control) and signaling significant brand risk among your most valuable cohort. The hypothesis that enterprise clients would absorb a 12% increase due to contract lock-ins has been falsified; while they may not be churning immediately, their renewal intent has collapsed by 22%.

#### 1. Root Cause Diagnosis: Visibility vs. Fairness
To distinguish between "invoice shock" (visibility) and "strategic objection" (fairness), prioritize these behavioral signals over top-line revenue:

*   **Support Ticket Sentiment & Volume:** Isolate tickets containing keywords like "unexpected," "increase," or "invoice" from accounts >5 years old. A spike here immediately post-invoice generation indicates a **visibility** issue. If tickets focus on "policy," "competitors," or "value," the issue is **fairness**.
*   **Payment Friction Metrics:** Monitor the time-to-pay and the rate of payment disputes/queries for the premium tier. If clients are paying but complaining, it is a perception issue. If payments are delayed or disputed at higher rates than the control, the price point is breaking operational trust.
*   **Feature Utilization Correlation:** Cross-reference the price increase with usage data. If high-intent drop-offs correlate with low feature adoption, the price exceeds perceived value (Fairness). If heavy users are reacting negatively, the surprise of the cost is the driver (Visibility).

#### 2. Critical Metrics to Prioritize
Stop focusing on the 4% new sign-up lift; it is insufficient to offset the erosion of lifetime value (LTV) in your core base. Shift the dashboard to:

*   **Renewal Intent by Tenure Cohort:** Specifically track the 22% drop metric weekly. If this number stabilizes or worsens, the long-term revenue risk exceeds the short-term pricing gain.
*   **Net Revenue Retention (NRR):** Calculate NRR specifically for the >5-year cohort. A decline here confirms that the pricing strategy is destroying more value than it creates, even if contracts prevent immediate churn.
*   **Sales Cycle Friction:** Measure the length of time to close *new* enterprise deals. If sales teams report increased hesitation or require more concessions to close, the market perception of "fairness" is damaging future pipeline, not just current renewals.

#### 3. Segmentation Recommendations
The current "all enterprise" segment is too broad and masking specific failure points. Immediately slice the data by:

*   **Contract Renewal Horizon:** Separate accounts renewing in <90 days from those with >1 year remaining. The 22% intent drop is likely concentrated in the near-term group; the long-term group may show latency in reaction.
*   **Invoice Recipient Role:** If possible, segment by whether the price change was viewed by a technical user (who sees value) vs. a finance approver (who sees cost). This helps determine if the communication strategy needs to shift from product-led to finance-led.
*   **Usage Intensity:** Compare "Power Users" vs. "Moderate Users." If Power Users are reacting negatively, the price is fundamentally misaligned with value. If only Moderate Users are reacting, the issue may be communication of value rather than the price itself.

#### Strategic Implication for Tomorrow's Meeting
The data suggests a "false negative" on churn but a "true positive" on relationship damage. The 12% increase is currently extracting short-term cash at the expense of long-term retention stability. The 4% new sign-up growth does not mathematically justify the $18k loss plus the latent risk of the 22% intent drop.

**Recommendation:** Do not kill the feature entirely yet, but **pause the universal rollout**. Pivot immediately to a segmented test where the price increase is applied only to accounts with >12 months remaining on contracts, or tie the increase explicitly to a new, visible value-add feature to address the fairness perception.

The current trajectory threatens core account stability more than it aids revenue growth.
