### Executive Recommendation: Revert Immediately, Then Retune

**Decision:** Revert the algorithm to the previous version by end of day.
**Rationale:** The current trade-off is economically negative and operationally unsustainable. The cost of friction ($60,000) currently exceeds the fraud prevented ($50,000), yielding a net loss of $10,000 over just three days, excluding long-term churn risk from 300 blocked customers.

#### 1. Cost Framework: Friction vs. Fraud
To evaluate future models, categorize costs using this formula:
*   **Cost of Fraud (CoF):** Direct loss amount + investigation labor. (Current Benefit: $50k).
*   **Cost of Friction (CoFr):** (False Positives × Avg. Transaction Value) + (Support Tickets × Cost Per Ticket) + (Estimated Churn Value).
    *   *Current Calculation:* 150k transactions/day × 0.7% increase in false positives × 3 days ≈ 3,150 extra blocked transactions. Even at a low $19 average transaction value, direct blockage costs ~$60k. Adding 300 support tickets (est. $15/ticket) and reputational damage confirms the net negative.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** A model update is only acceptable if `CoF Saved > 3 × CoFr Incurred`. High friction drives immediate churn; fraud is often recoverable or insured.

#### 2. Actionable Segmentation for Tuning
Once reverted, analyze the last three days of false positives using these four segments to retune the model:
*   **Geographic Velocity:** Isolate transactions where the location differs from the user's history by >500 miles within <4 hours. This likely confirms your "unusual location" hypothesis.
*   **Merchant Category Code (MCC):** Group false positives by industry (e.g., Travel, Electronics, Grocery). If 80% of errors are in "Travel," apply a temporary whitelist or higher threshold for that sector.
*   **Transaction Value Brackets:** Segment into <$50, $50–$200, and >$200. High-sensitivity models often over-flag low-value, high-frequency legitimate spending.
*   **Customer Tenure:** Compare false positive rates for customers <6 months vs. >2 years. Long-term customers with sudden flags are almost certainly false positives and should have lower sensitivity thresholds.
