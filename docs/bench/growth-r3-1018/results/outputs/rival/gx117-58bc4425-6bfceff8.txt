**Subject:** Urgent: Validity Concerns Regarding Homepage Headline A/B Test Results

**Executive Summary**
We cannot recommend adopting the emotional headline style based on the current A/B test data. While the variant showed a 25% increase in click-through rates (CTR), the results are statistically invalid due to severe external contamination during the election cycle. The observed lift is likely driven by a shift in user composition rather than the headline style itself.

**Why the Results Are Contaminated**
Our experiment relied on the assumption that randomization would evenly distribute user types across both the control (standard headlines) and treatment (emotional headlines) groups. This assumption failed due to the 200% traffic surge caused by breaking news.

1.  **Selection Bias via Traffic Composition:** The external news cycle did not bring a random sample of users; it brought a specific cohort of high-intent, urgency-driven readers. Evidence suggests this surge disproportionately populated the treatment group, skewing the user composition. These users are inherently more prone to clicking headlines regardless of style.
2.  **Confounding Variables:** We cannot isolate the variable of "headline style" from the variable of "user urgency." The 25% lift is a conflated metric reflecting both the design change and the altered behavior of a crisis-driven audience.
3.  **Failure of Randomization:** In stable conditions, randomization balances known and unknown factors. In volatile conditions with non-stationary traffic sources, randomization breaks down if the traffic influx is not perfectly simultaneous and evenly routed, which appears to be the case here.

**Can We Salvage the Data?**
We will attempt a post-hoc segmentation analysis to see if any signal remains, though confidence will be low:
*   **Cohort Isolation:** We will segment users by entry source (e.g., direct vs. social vs. search) and time-of-day to identify periods where traffic composition was most stable.
*   **Behavioral Controls:** We will compare CTR on non-test pages for both groups. If the treatment group clicked more everywhere, the lift is purely a user composition artifact.
*   **Downside:** Given the magnitude of the traffic surge (200%), it is highly probable that no segment is large enough or pure enough to yield a statistically significant adjustment.

**Recommendation**
**Do not implement the emotional headline style permanently based on this test.** Doing so risks optimizing for a temporary crisis mindset that may alienate our core audience during standard news cycles.

**Proposed Path Forward**
1.  **Immediate Action:** Maintain the current standard headline style.
2.  **Re-test Protocol:** Schedule a follow-up A/B test during a period of normalized traffic (post-election calm) to establish a true baseline.
3.  **Future Safeguards:** Implement "guardrail metrics" for future tests that monitor traffic composition and source distribution in real-time. If external events cause traffic volatility >20%, the test should automatically pause to prevent data contamination.

We will provide a final technical addendum by Thursday detailing the results of our segmentation attempts, but leadership should prepare for a conclusive "inconclusive" verdict requiring a re-test.
