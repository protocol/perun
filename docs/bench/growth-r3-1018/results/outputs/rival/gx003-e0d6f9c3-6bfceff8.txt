**Recommendation: Kill the City-Wide Rollout; Pivot to a "Rain-Only" Surge Strategy**

Do not proceed with the current dynamic pricing algorithm city-wide. While the test successfully achieved its supply-side goal (driver availability +22%), the 8% drop in order volume represents a critical failure in demand retention that threatens unit economics in the price-sensitive Mexico City market.

**Critical Assessment of Results**

1.  **Confounded Data:** The 8% volume drop cannot be definitively attributed to price sensitivity due to the uncontrolled variable of the local festival in Polanco. Festivals in CDMX often alter traffic patterns and consumer behavior unpredictably. Rolling out based on this data risks scaling a flaw that may not exist, or worse, ignoring a real price elasticity issue.
2.  **Market Context:** Mexico City consumers are highly sensitive to delivery fees and surge pricing. An 8% volume decline, even if partially caused by the festival, suggests the algorithm's price spikes were too aggressive for the local tolerance threshold. In a competitive market like CDMX, losing volume to competitors during surge events is a long-term brand risk.
3.  **Supply/Demand Imbalance:** A 22% increase in driver availability coupled with an 8% drop in orders creates an inefficient marketplace. You now have excess supply chasing reduced demand, which increases cost-per-delivery and lowers driver earnings per hour despite higher availability. This undermines the retention of the very drivers you sought to attract.

**Strategic Path Forward**

Instead of a binary "rollout or kill" decision, recommend a **targeted iteration** to the VP of Operations:

*   **Pause City-Wide Expansion:** Halt any budget allocation for broad implementation until the festival variable is isolated.
*   **Run a "Rain-Only" Micro-Test:** Re-run the experiment in Polanco and Condesa specifically during rain events, excluding festival dates. This isolates the core hypothesis: does rain surge pricing balance the market without suppressing demand?
*   **Implement Price Caps:** If re-testing confirms volume sensitivity, modify the algorithm to include hard price caps. The goal should be "sufficient" driver availability (e.g., +10-15%) rather than maximizing it at the cost of order volume.
*   **Monitor Unit Economics:** Shift the success metric from "driver availability" to "profit per order." If the marginal gain in speed/reliability does not offset the margin lost from fewer orders, the algorithm is not viable.

**Conclusion for Q3 Budget**

Allocate Q3 budget toward **refining the algorithm's sensitivity** and **running cleaner, controlled tests**, not toward city-wide deployment. The current data indicates the model is too blunt for the nuanced CDMX market. A rushed rollout risks alienating customers and destabilizing the driver ecosystem across the entire city. Prove the model works in clean weather conditions first; only then consider expansion.
