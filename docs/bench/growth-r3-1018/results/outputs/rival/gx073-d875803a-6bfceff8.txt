**Recommendation: Do not roll out the feature nationwide based on these results.**

The observed 40% improvement is statistically invalid for decision-making. The regional bank outages on June 8–9 constitute a severe external confounding variable that violated the test's assumption of equal exposure to external factors. Because the outage affected 30% of your user base and likely impacted the control and treatment groups unevenly (e.g., if the treatment group had fewer users with accounts at the affected banks), the resulting lift reflects infrastructure failure, not feature efficacy. Rolling out now risks implementing a change with unproven value while masking potential usability issues hidden by the skewed data.

**How to Interpret the Anomalies**
The stability in the treatment group during the outage suggests one of two scenarios, neither of which proves the feature works:
1.  **Selection Bias:** The treatment group inadvertently contained fewer users reliant on the affected southern banks. The "lift" is simply the treatment group avoiding the outage that crushed the control group.
2.  **Masked Failure:** The one-click flow might actually perform *worse* than standard checkout, but the outage suppressed the control group's performance so severely that it artificially inflated the treatment's relative standing.

You cannot quantify the contamination without granular bank-level data, making any aggregate conversion rate meaningless for this period.

If the treatment group's absolute conversion also dipped significantly compared to pre-outage baselines, the feature did not solve the outage problem; the result is purely relative skew.

3.  **Check Pre-Test Balance:**
    Verify the randomization integrity. Compare the percentage of users holding accounts at the affected banks in both the control and treatment groups *before* the test started. If these proportions differ by more than 1-2%, your randomization failed, and the test is irrecoverable.

    Given the contamination, the most rigorous path is to extend the test for another 7–10 days (post-June 14) to gather clean data that dilutes the outage impact, or restart the test entirely if the sample size allows. Do not make a binary go/no-go decision on the contaminated dataset.
