### Proposed Activation Framework for SiteCraft

**1. Hypothesized "Deep Activation" Signals**
Based on your survey data indicating that publishing without editing leads to churn, the new definition must measure *ongoing engagement* rather than a one-time setup. Prioritize tracking these behaviors:
*   **Content Iteration:** User edits the published site (adds/removes sections) at least once after the initial publish.
*   **Functional Integration:** User connects a custom domain OR adds a functional element (contact form, analytics, SEO metadata).
*   **Return Visit:** User logs back into the editor within 3 days of publishing.

**2. Recommended Composite Definition**
Replace the single "Publish" event with a **"Value Realized"** event defined as:
> *User publishes a site AND completes at least one of the following within 7 days: (a) Edits content post-publish, (b) Connects custom domain, or (c) Adds a functional block.*

**3. Validation Methodology (Historical Analysis)**
To validate this against your historical data without new A/B tests:
*   **Cohort Segmentation:** Query your database for all users who signed up in the last 6–9 months.
*   **Flagging:** Create a binary flag for each user indicating if they met the *new* composite criteria vs. the *old* "Publish only" criteria.
*   **Retention Curve Comparison:** Plot two retention curves (Day 30 and Day 90) for:
    1.  Users who met the old definition only (Published but no deep action).
    2.  Users who met the new composite definition.
*   **Success Metric:** The new definition is valid if the retention rate of the "Newly Activated" group is significantly higher (e.g., 20–30% lift) than the old group, even if the total percentage of "activated" users drops from 18% to perhaps 10–12%.

**4. Setting the Threshold**
Do not aim to keep the activation rate at 18%. A lower rate with higher retention is the goal.
*   **Target Threshold:** Aim for a definition where the activated cohort shows >40% Day-30 retention.
*   **Calibration:** If the rate drops below 8%, the bar may be too high (excluding valid users). If it stays above 15% but retention doesn't improve, the selected signals are still too weak.
*   **Iterative Tuning:** Start with the strictest combination (Publish + Edit + Domain). If the sample size is too small for statistical confidence, relax to (Publish + Edit OR Domain).

**5. Immediate Next Steps for Noam**
*   **Data Pull:** Extract the last 6 months of user event logs (publish, edit, domain_connect, form_add).
*   **Correlation Check:** Calculate the correlation coefficient between each specific action and Day-30 retention. Select the top 2 predictors.
*   **Dashboard Update:** Build a temporary view showing "Legacy Activation Rate" vs. "Proposed Activation Rate" side-by-side to visualize the quality shift.
*   **Email Trigger Update:** Configure onboarding emails to trigger on the *new* composite event, not just publishing, to encourage the second action immediately.

This approach shifts your metric from "setup complete" to "value demonstrated," aligning your activation data with actual long-term user success.

*Ensure your data team excludes users who published but never logged in again when calculating the new baseline.*
