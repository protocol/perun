### Recommended Segmentation Cuts

To isolate the true effect of the Trastevere banner and address your specific concerns about contamination and user behavior, analyze the data using these four critical segments:

1.  **Resident vs. Commuter Status**
    *   **Definition:** Split users based on their primary delivery address (Trastevere zip code) versus their actual location during order placement.
    *   **Purpose:** This directly tests your spillover hypothesis. If "Control" users with Trastevere addresses who placed orders while physically in the city center show lifted metrics, your control group is contaminated. Compare order frequency of users who *live* in Trastevere but ordered elsewhere against those who stayed within the zone.

2.  **New vs. Existing Customer Cohorts**
    *   **Definition:** Separate users who created an account before June 1st (Existing) from those who signed up between June 1–14 (New).
    *   **Purpose:** This answers your question about the lift driver. If the 12% increase is concentrated entirely in the "New" cohort, the banner acts as an acquisition tool. If it is driven by "Existing" users increasing frequency, it is a retention/play-frequency play. The rollout strategy differs significantly for each.

3.  **Social Referral vs. Direct/App Open**
    *   **Definition:** Segment traffic by entry point, specifically isolating users arriving via social media referrers or campaign UTMs versus direct app opens.
    *   **Purpose:** To quantify the "social media spillover." If control group users in surrounding neighborhoods are arriving via social links at higher rates than baseline, the geo-fence failed to contain the ad exposure. This validates whether the control group saw the creative organically.

4.  **Pre-Test Baseline Behavior (June 1–14 vs. May 1–14)**
    *   **Definition:** Compare the test period against the immediate prior period for both Test and Control groups individually.
    *   **Purpose:** To rule out seasonality. Trastevere may naturally see higher order frequency in early June due to weather or local events unrelated to your banner. Ensure the *delta* between Test and Control is greater than the natural growth trend of the Control group itself.

### Essential Caveats for Giulia's Presentation

Include these three specific warnings to prevent overestimating impact:

*   **Contamination Risk Lowers Confidence Interval:** Explicitly state that if commuter analysis shows >5% of the control group was exposed to the banner (via physical presence or social spillover), the reported 12% lift is likely an *underestimate* of the true effect (because the control baseline is artificially inflated), but the statistical significance is compromised. Recommend a "cleaner" follow-up test if contamination is high.
*   **Short-Term Frequency vs. Long-Term Value:** Clarify that a 12% increase in *frequency* over two weeks does not guarantee a 12% increase in *revenue* or *lifetime value*. Existing users may be "pulling forward" orders they would have made later in the month rather than generating net-new volume.
*   **Tourist Season Volatility:** Note that Trastevere is a high-tourist zone. If the test period coincided with a specific local event or surge in tourism not present in the rest of Rome, the results may not be replicable when rolling out to residential-heavy zones in Rome.
