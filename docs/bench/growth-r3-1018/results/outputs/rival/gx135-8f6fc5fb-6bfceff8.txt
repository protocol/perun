### Action Plan: Northwind Dashboard Redesign Rollout Decision

**1. Immediate Halt to Full Rollout**
Do not proceed with a 100% rollout. The 3% conversion drop on tablets constitutes a failed guardrail metric for that segment. Rolling out now would intentionally degrade performance for a specific user cohort, violating the core principle of "do no harm" even if aggregate numbers look positive. The aggregate 6.5% lift is masking a material failure on a specific platform.

**2. Diagnostic Investigation (Days 1–3)**
Before running new tests, determine if the tablet drop is a functional bug or a UX mismatch.
*   **Technical Audit:** Engage QA to replicate the checkout flow on all major tablet browsers (iPad Safari, Android Chrome, Surface). Look specifically for layout shifts, unclickable buttons, or form validation errors that do not exist on desktop.
*   **Session Replay Analysis:** Pull 50–100 session recordings of tablet users from the test period who abandoned the checkout. Look for "rage clicks," excessive scrolling, or hesitation at the specific step where desktop users converted.
*   **Funnel Step Isolation:** Break down the conversion rate by individual funnel steps for tablets only. Identify exactly where the drop-off occurs compared to the control group. If the drop happens at the payment entry, it may be a keyboard interaction issue; if it happens at the review stage, it may be a layout readability issue.

**3. Segmented Rollout Strategy (Days 4–14)**
Assuming no critical code bugs are found, the design likely requires device-specific optimization. Proceed with a segmented release:
*   **Desktop:** Prepare for 100% rollout immediately. The 9% lift is statistically significant and robust.
*   **Mobile:** Since results were flat, the risk is neutral. You may roll out to mobile but must monitor closely for delayed negative effects (e.g., support tickets).
*   **Tablet:** Keep the control version active for tablets. Do not force the new design on this segment until a specific hypothesis for the 3% drop is validated and re-tested.

**4. Required Follow-Up Measurements**
To satisfy the Head of Growth's risk concerns, report the following before any further expansion:
*   **Revenue Per Visitor (RPV) by Device:** Conversion rate is a proxy; ensure the tablet drop didn't disproportionately affect high-value orders.
*   **Support Ticket Volume:** Tag and track tickets related to "checkout difficulty" segmented by device type for the test duration. A spike in tablet-related tickets confirms a usability failure.
*   **Time-on-Task:** Compare the average time to complete checkout on tablets between variants. A significant increase suggests friction even if some users eventually convert.

**Recommendation to Head of Growth:**
Frame this not as a failed test, but as a successful segmentation discovery. The data proves the "one-size-fits-all" hypothesis was incorrect. The recommended path is an immediate **Desktop-First Rollout**, maintaining the status quo for Tablets while a dedicated "Tablet Optimization" sprint is initiated. This captures 60–70% of the projected revenue gain immediately while eliminating the risk of alienating tablet users.
