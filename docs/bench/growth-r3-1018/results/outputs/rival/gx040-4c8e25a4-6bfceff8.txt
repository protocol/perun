# ReWear Tokyo Holiday Season Metric Framework

## Primary Optimization Metrics (North Star)
**Metric:** **30-Day Adjusted Sell-Through Rate**
*Definition:* (Items Sold within 30 Days) / (Total Listings) where the denominator excludes items delisted by sellers due to price dissatisfaction.
*Why:* Prevents gaming the system by forcing sellers to delist rather than drop prices.

## Balanced Scorecard

### 1. Buyer Conversion & Velocity (The Accelerator)
*   **Median Time-to-First-Sale:** Track the distribution, not just the average, to identify outliers.
*   **Price Drop Velocity:** Average hours from listing to first price reduction.
*   **Conversion Rate by Price Drop Tier:** Compare conversion rates for items dropped within 24h vs. 48h vs. 72h.

### 2. Seller Health & Economics (The Guardrails)
*   **Realized Yield Ratio:** (Final Sale Price) / (Original Listing Price). Monitor if this ratio decreases as Price Drop Velocity increases.
*   **Seller Retention Rate (90-Day):** Percentage of sellers who list again within 90 days of a sale. Segment by "Fast Sale" (<48h) vs. "Slow Sale" (>14 days).
*   **Support Ticket Volume per 1,000 Listings:** Specifically tagging "pricing strategy" or "earnings concern."

### 3. Marketplace Liquidity & Trust (The Ecosystem)
*   **Listing Churn Rate:** Percentage of items delisted by sellers before selling. A spike here indicates sellers feel forced to lower prices too aggressively.
*   **Buyer Return Rate:** Monitor if fast-discounted items have higher return rates (indicating impulse buys or quality mismatches).
*   **Net Promoter Score (Seller):** Specific question: "How satisfied were you with the final sale price?"

## Warning Signals: Optimizing for Velocity at Expense of Health

Stop or pivot the campaign immediately if you observe these correlations:
1.  **The "Race to the Bottom" Signal:** Price Drop Velocity decreases, but Realized Yield Ratio drops *faster* than Conversion Rate increases. (You are burning seller margin for negligible volume gain).
2.  **The Liquidity Crunch:** Listing Churn Rate increases by >5% week-over-week. Sellers are removing inventory rather than accepting the algorithmic price pressure.
3.  **The Trust Erosion:** Seller Retention Rate for "Fast Sale" cohorts drops below "Slow Sale" cohorts. If sellers who sell quickly don't come back, you have optimized for one-time transactions, not marketplace growth.

## Tracking Seller Lifetime Value (LTV) by Sale Speed

To answer your specific LTV question, do not track revenue alone. Construct a **Cohort-Based LTV Model**:

1.  **Segment Sellers:** Group sellers by their *first* holiday sale speed (e.g., Cohort A: Sold <24h; Cohort B: Sold 2-7 days; Cohort C: Sold 8-30 days).
2.  **Track Forward:** For each cohort, calculate:
    *   **Repeat Listing Rate:** % who list a second item within 60 days.
    *   **Average Gross Merchandise Value (GMV) per Seller:** Total value of all items sold by this seller over 12 months.
    *   **Support Cost per Seller:** Cost of support interactions related to pricing disputes.
3.  **The Formula:**
    `Seller LTV = (Avg Repeat Listings × Avg Sale Price × Take Rate) - (Acquisition Cost + Support Cost)`

**Hypothesis to Validate:** If "Fast Sale" sellers have a lower LTV than "Slow Sale" sellers due to lower repeat listing rates or lower average prices in future listings, your assumption that "faster is better" is incorrect for long-term health.

## Implementation Note for Operations Director
Align seller support incentives to **Realized Yield Ratio** and **Seller Retention**, not just volume of items sold. Support agents should be rewarded for coaching sellers on optimal initial pricing that minimizes the *need* for rapid drops, rather than encouraging immediate discounts.
