# Diagnostic Plan: StyleCart Checkout Abandonment

To isolate whether the 40% abandonment spike is caused by shipping costs, layout prominence, or coupon hunting, execute this four-step diagnostic immediately. Do not subsidize shipping until these results confirm the root cause.

### 1. Quantitative Funnel & Cohort Analysis
Compare the specific drop-off rate at the "Shipping Display" step between two cohorts:
*   **Cohort A (Pre-Change):** Users from August 1–September 4.
*   **Cohort B (Post-Change):** Users from September 6–Present.

**Action:** Calculate the exact percentage point increase in abandonment at this specific step. If the drop-off increased significantly only in Cohort B, the change is causal.
**Segmentation:** Break Cohort B down by **Cart Value**. If abandonment is highest in low-cart-value orders (where the $3 increase represents a larger percentage of the total) and lower in high-cart-value orders, this confirms **price sensitivity** rather than a layout issue. A layout bug would affect all cart values equally.

### 2. Session Replay & Heatmap Audit
Since you suspect layout changes may have made the total price "more prominent," visual validation is required to rule out UI confusion.
**Action:** Watch 50–100 session recordings of users who abandoned at the shipping step post-September 5th.
**Look for:**
*   **Rage clicks** or erratic mouse movement around the shipping total (indicates confusion or frustration with the display).
*   **Scrolling behavior:** Do users scroll up and down comparing item prices to shipping, or do they leave immediately upon the number appearing?
*   **Layout shifts:** Did the new total placement obscure the "Continue" button or break the visual flow?
If users leave instantly without interacting with the new layout, it is a price decision. If they struggle with the interface, it is a UX issue.

### 3. Exit Intent & Coupon Behavior Tracking
To determine if users are leaving to find codes versus abandoning entirely:
**Action:** Check click data on any "Have a promo code?" link or input field expansion at the checkout step.
*   **High interaction with promo fields:** Users are price-shopping for discounts to offset the new fee.
*   **Zero interaction + direct close:** Users are rejecting the total cost outright.
**Supplement:** If possible, deploy a lightweight exit-intent survey on the checkout page for 48 hours asking: "What stopped you from completing your purchase?" with options for "Shipping costs too high," "Looking for a coupon," or "Just browsing."

### 4. A/B Test Validation (The Golden Standard)
Before negotiating rates, run a targeted 5-day A/B test to prove elasticity.
*   **Variant A (Control):** Shows the new increased shipping cost.
*   **Variant B (Test):** Shows the original shipping cost (subsidized by you for the test) OR displays a "Free Shipping Threshold" progress bar if the cart is close to the limit.
**Metric:** Compare conversion rates. If Variant B recovers >30% of the lost conversions, the $3 increase is the sole culprit. If conversion remains flat, the issue lies elsewhere (e.g., trust, layout, or broader market factors).

### Decision Framework for Merchandising Team
*   **Subsidize Shipping If:** Cohort analysis shows drop-off is isolated to low cart values, session replays show immediate exit without UI struggle, and the A/B test proves a direct correlation between waived fees and recovered revenue that exceeds the $3 cost.
*   **Do Not Subsidize If:** Abandonment is consistent across all cart values (suggesting a layout bug), users are interacting heavily with coupon fields (suggesting a promotional strategy gap), or the A/B test shows negligible lift in conversion.

**Note:** Ensure your analytics window matches the observation dates exactly; do not blend pre- and post-September 5th data in the same calculation. Prioritize the A/B test results over assumptions for the November 1st promotion strategy.
