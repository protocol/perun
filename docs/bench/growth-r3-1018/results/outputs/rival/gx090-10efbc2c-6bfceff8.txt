To determine whether BoxBrazil's churn spike stems from budget constraints or product dissatisfaction, you must segment the data by **cancellation reason** first, then cross-reference with **customer tenure**. Do not rely on a single dimension.

**Step 1: Segment by Cancellation Reason**
Isolate users who selected "Price/Cost" or "Financial Reasons" versus those who selected "Product Quality," "Didn't Like Items," or "Portion Size."
*   **If >70% of the spike is "Price/Financial":** Your assumption holds. This is seasonal budget tightening. The product is fine; the timing is the issue.
*   **If >30% of the spike is "Quality/Product":** Your assumption is incorrect. The January box curation failed relative to the December gift edition. This requires an operational fix, not just a marketing adjustment.

**Step 2: Cross-Reference with Customer Tenure**
Split the "Quality" complainers into two groups: **Holiday Acquisitions** (joined Nov–Dec) vs. **Legacy Subscribers** (joined pre-November).
*   **Scenario A (Holiday Acquisitions only):** If only new customers cite quality issues, they likely experienced "gift shock." They received a premium December box, then a standard January box, creating a false baseline for quality. This is a *marketing expectation* problem.
*   **Scenario B (Legacy Subscribers included):** If long-term subscribers also cite quality drops, you genuinely reduced value. The January curation was objectively weaker than usual, not just weaker than the holiday special. This is a *product curation* problem.

**Step 3: Analyze the "Price" Segment by Tenure**
*   **Holiday Acquisitions citing Price:** These are likely the "lower-quality leads" you suspected. They signed up for the holidays, got what they wanted, and are now churning due to cost. This validates your fear about lead quality.
*   **Legacy Subscribers citing Price:** This is dangerous. If loyal customers are leaving due to price, your core value proposition may no longer match their current budget reality, regardless of product quality.

**Recommendation for Carlos (Q2 Inventory):**
1.  **If the data points to Product Quality (Scenario B):** Immediately audit the January box contents against November. Do not reduce Q2 inventory volume; instead, reallocate budget to higher-quality items to restore trust. Reducing inventory now will worsen the perception of value.
2.  **If the data points to Expectations (Scenario A):** Maintain current quality levels but adjust Q2 marketing. Position the January/February boxes as "Reset & Refresh" rather than "Gift Edition" to manage expectations. You can safely optimize inventory for cost-efficiency if legacy subscribers are happy.
3.  **If the data points to Lead Quality:** Aggressively target retention offers (e.g., "Pause Subscription" instead of "Cancel") for the holiday cohort, but do not let their churn dictate Q2 purchasing for the core product. Focus inventory planning on the behavior of legacy subscribers.

**Immediate Action:** Run this segmentation today. If legacy subscribers are complaining about quality, you have a product crisis, not a seasonal blip. If only new users are leaving due to price, you have a predictable post-holiday churn cycle.
