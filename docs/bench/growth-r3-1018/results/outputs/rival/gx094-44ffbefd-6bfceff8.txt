**Experiment Strategy: Trust Badge Checkout Test**

**Recommendation:** Do not run a standard fixed-horizon A/B test targeting a 10% lift. With 500 daily visitors and a 2.5% baseline, detecting a 10% lift requires ~39,000 visitors per variant (78,000 total). At 250 visitors/day per variant, this takes **312 days (10 months)**. This misses your January deadline entirely.

Instead, adopt a **High-Threshold Sequential Test** strategy targeting a **25% Minimum Detectable Effect (MDE)**.

**1. Revised Evaluation Criteria**
*   **Primary Metric:** Checkout Completion Rate (Visitors who reach checkout → Purchase). Narrowing the funnel increases the baseline rate slightly and reduces noise from top-of-funnel browsing.
*   **Target Lift:** 25% (moving conversion from 2.5% to 3.125%).
*   **Sample Size Required:** ~5,200 visitors per variant (10,400 total).
*   **Duration:** ~42 days (6 weeks).
*   **Decision Rule:** Use a sequential monitoring approach with strict alpha spending. Check results only once weekly (Fridays) to reduce "peeking" bias. Stop early *only* if the p-value crosses a conservative threshold (e.g., p < 0.01) indicating a massive win, otherwise run the full 6 weeks.

**2. Implementation Plan**
*   **Weeks 1-2 (Ramp-up):** Run a 90/10 split (90% Control, 10% Trust Badges). This limits exposure if the badges cause friction (e.g., visual clutter) while gathering initial qualitative data.
*   **Weeks 3-8 (Full Test):** Shift to 50/50 split. Collect data strictly for 6 weeks.
*   **Analysis:** If the result is statistically significant at 6 weeks with >20% lift, implement immediately. If inconclusive, do not extend the test; the opportunity cost of delaying other January optimizations is too high.

**3. Risk Mitigation for Low Traffic**
*   **Guardrail Metric:** Monitor "Time on Checkout Page." If time increases significantly, the badges may be causing distraction rather than trust, even if conversion holds.
*   **Qualitative Supplement:** Since statistical power is low, pair this with 5 user recordings (e.g., Hotjar) specifically looking for hesitation at the payment button. If users pause less with badges, it supports the quantitative trend even if significance isn't reached.

**Why this works for AlpineGear:**
Aiming for a 10% lift is a statistical trap that guarantees no decision before January. By raising the bar to a 25% lift, you accept that you will only detect "home runs." If trust badges only provide a tiny 5% nudge, they are likely not worth the implementation maintenance for a niche site. This strategy forces a binary decision: either the badges are a massive winner (implement by Jan), or they are negligible (shelve the idea and focus on high-impact copy or offer tests).

**Note:** Ensure your trust badges are specific to mountaineering (e.g., "Gear Tested at Altitude" or specific security certs) rather than generic icons, as niche communities react poorly to stock assets. If the 6-week test is inconclusive, consider a "pre-post" analysis over the holiday spike, acknowledging lower rigor but higher speed.
