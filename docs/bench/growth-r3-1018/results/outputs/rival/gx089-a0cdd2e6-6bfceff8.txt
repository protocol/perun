**LearnLagos: High-Impact Learning Metrics Plan (Zero-Budget)**

Stop tracking "quiz completion" as your primary success metric. As you suspected, it is a vanity metric that rewards guessing, not learning. With 5,000 users and no data engineer, you must shift from measuring *activity* to measuring *mastery* using data you already have in your hosting exports.

Focus on these three specific metrics to guide your next feature build:

### 1. The "First-Try Mastery Rate" (Per Lesson)
**Formula:** `(Questions answered correctly on 1st attempt) / (Total questions attempted)`
*   **Why:** This directly exposes guessing. If a student completes a lesson but this rate is below 60%, they are likely clicking randomly to unlock the next level.
*   **Action:** If a specific lesson has a high completion rate but a low First-Try Mastery Rate, **do not build new content**. Instead, build a "remedial loop" that forces a review of concepts before allowing a retry.

### 2. The "Stuck Point" Ratio (Per Question)
**Formula:** `(Total attempts on Question X) / (Unique students who reached Question X)`
*   **Why:** This identifies broken content. A ratio near 1.0 means students get it immediately. A ratio above 3.0 suggests the question is confusing, the tutorial explanation was unclear, or the difficulty spike is too high.
*   **Action:** Target questions with a ratio > 2.5 for immediate revision. This is your highest ROI development task: fixing existing friction rather than adding new features.

### 3. The "Voluntary Return" Rate (Weekly Cohort)
**Formula:** `(Students who log in and attempt >1 question in Week N without external prompt) / (Total active students in Week N-1)`
*   **Why:** Time spent per lesson is misleading; a student can leave a tab open while doing homework elsewhere. Voluntary return proves the product provides enough value (or gamification) to pull them back organically.
*   **Action:** If this rate drops below 20% for any cohort, your content is not engaging enough to compete with other distractions. Prioritize building "streak" features or progress summaries over new curriculum modules.

**Implementation Strategy for Tunde:**
Export your raw logs to CSV. You do not need SQL or Python. Use standard spreadsheet functions (`COUNTIF`, `VLOOKUP`) to calculate these three numbers for your top 10 most-popular lessons.

**Decision Framework:**
*   **Low First-Try Mastery?** → Build better hints or locked retries.
*   **High Stuck Point Ratio?** → Rewrite the specific question or preceding tutorial text.
*   **Low Voluntary Return?** → Build engagement loops (badges, streaks, weekly recaps).

These metrics validate learning outcomes by measuring *struggle* and *retention*, not just completion. They require zero new infrastructure and will stop you from wasting dev time on features that don't improve actual math skills.

*Note: Calculate these weekly, not daily, to smooth out noise from exam cycles or school holidays.*
*Focus only on the top 20% of lessons driving 80% of your traffic first.*
