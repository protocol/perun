# GeoFind A/B Test: Investigative Framework for CPO

**Executive Summary:** The 40% adoption lift is likely a "false positive" driven by dark patterns (frictionless opt-in) that violate the core brand pillar of trust. The 15-point sentiment drop and 200 complaints indicate immediate reputational damage. Do not roll out globally. The priority is determining if this adoption converts to retention or accelerates churn.

### Phase 1: Immediate Diagnostic Questions
Answer these before deciding on a rollback or tweak:

1.  **Opt-Out Velocity:** What percentage of users who adopted the feature in the variant group disabled it within 24 hours vs. the control group? High immediate opt-out confirms "accidental" or "anxious" adoption.
2.  **Support Ticket Sentiment:** Of the 200 complaints, how many explicitly mention "privacy," "stalking," or "didn't know"? Categorize these by severity to gauge legal/regulatory risk versus UX confusion.
3.  **The "Silent Churn" Signal:** Did the variant group show a statistically significant increase in overall app uninstall rates or session frequency drops compared to the control group during the test window?
4.  **Feature Stickiness:** Among users who *kept* the feature enabled after 7 days, did their core retention (Day-30) improve, stay flat, or degrade compared to control users who organically enabled it?
5.  **Referral Impact:** Did the variant group generate fewer organic invites/referrals than the control? (Users anxious about privacy often stop advocating for the product).

### Phase 2: Critical Data Slices to Review
Request these specific cohorts from analytics immediately:

*   **Cohort A (The Regretters):** Variant users who enabled GeoFind but disabled it within 48 hours.
    *   *Metric:* Compare their Day-7 and Day-30 retention against Control users who never enabled the feature.
    *   *Hypothesis:* If retention is lower, the frictionless opt-in caused permanent brand damage.
*   **Cohort B (The Acceptors):** Variant users who kept GeoFind enabled for >7 days.
    *   *Metric:* Compare their referral rate and NPS scores against Control users who organically opted in.
    *   *Hypothesis:* If their advocacy is lower, the "frictionless" method attracted low-quality users who don't trust the platform long-term.
*   **Cohort C (The Complainers):** Users who contacted support during the test.
    *   *Metric:* Track their subsequent usage frequency. Did they reduce usage by >20% post-complaint?
*   **Segment Split:** Break all above metrics by user tenure (New vs. Power Users).
    *   *Reasoning:* Power users may react more negatively to privacy shifts than new users, threatening your most valuable segment.

### Preliminary Recommendation Logic
*   **Rollback Immediately If:** Cohort A shows higher churn than the control baseline, or if support tickets indicate regulatory risk (e.g., users feeling "tracked" without consent). Trust is a lagging indicator; once broken, it is expensive to rebuild.
*   **Tweak Messaging If:** Adoption remains high in Cohort B, but confusion drives the complaints. This suggests the value prop is real, but the "frictionless" mechanism lacked necessary context.
*   **Segment Audience If:** Negative sentiment is isolated to a specific demographic (e.g., older users or specific regions with strict privacy laws), while others show net positive retention.

**Next Step:** Pause the experiment. Do not declare a winner based on adoption alone. Run the retention analysis on Cohort A immediately. If "The Regretters" churn faster than the control group, the 40% lift is a liability, not an asset.

*Note: Given "Trust" is the core brand pillar, a 15-point sentiment drop is a critical failure signal that outweighs short-term adoption gains. Prioritize the churn analysis over feature usage metrics.*
