**Recommendation for Executive Steering Committee: PrestoRápido Success Criteria**

**Primary North Star Metric: Completed Fund Disbursement**
For a peer-to-peer lending platform, real customer value is not the *intent* to borrow (acceptance), but the *delivery* of capital (disbursement). A loan acceptance without fund transfer generates zero revenue for lenders and solves zero problems for borrowers. Therefore, your North Star must be **Daily Completed Disbursements**.

While the volume (~150/day) is lower than acceptance figures, measuring a high-volume vanity metric that leaks 40% of its value will mislead the business into thinking it is growing while the actual engine stalls. Low volume is a data reality, not a reason to choose a misleading metric.

**Secondary Input Metrics & Funnel Structure**
To monitor the health of the North Star without being paralyzed by low volume, track these input metrics that drive disbursement:

1.  **Offer Acceptance Rate:** (Acceptances / Offers Viewed). Measures market fit and offer attractiveness.
2.  **Verification Completion Rate:** (Verifications Completed / Offers Accepted). This is your critical leak. With a 40% drop-off here, this is the single most important diagnostic metric for your launch phase.
3.  **Time-to-Fund:** Median time from acceptance to disbursement. Friction here directly impacts retention and lender confidence.

**Analysis of Definitions**

*Option A: Counting "Offer Acceptance" as Conversion*
*   **Pros:** High data volume immediately; feels good for early morale; easy to track.
*   **Cons:** **Fatal Flaw.** It decouples the metric from revenue. You could double "conversions" while revenue stays flat if the verification leak worsens. It incentivizes the product team to optimize for clicks rather than successful loans, hiding the 40% failure rate until lenders complain about empty portfolios.

*Option B: Counting "Fund Disbursement" as Conversion (Recommended)*
*   **Pros:** Perfectly aligned with business viability and lender value. Every point increase represents actual revenue. It forces the organization to fix the verification bottleneck immediately.
*   **Cons:** Lower daily counts require looking at trends over weeks rather than hours. Statistical significance for A/B tests will take longer to reach.
*   **Mitigation:** Do not ignore the upstream steps. Use the **Verification Completion Rate** as your primary "guardrail" and optimization target. If disbursement is the destination, verification completion is the highway you must widen.

**This Week's Action Plan**
1.  **Instrument the "Verification Start" and "Verification Fail" events** immediately with specific error codes (e.g., document rejection, timeout, API failure) to diagnose *why* the 40% drop occurs.
2.  **Build the dashboard** with "Completed Disbursements" as the headline number, but place "Verification Completion Rate" prominently as the primary lever for the product team to move this week.
3.  **Present to the committee** that success is defined by money moved, not promises made, and that the Q1 goal is to reduce the verification drop-off from 40% to <20% to unlock volume.

*Note: Ensure user identifiers used for tracking verification cohorts are pseudonymous; route any PII handling for identity docs to your privacy compliance protocol.*
