**Subject:** Recommendation on LinguaStep Onboarding Rollout (Malaysia)

**Decision: Do not make the new tutorial the default yet. Extend the experiment for 14 days with a segmented read-out.**

The current data presents a classic "novelty effect" vs. "usability flaw" conflict, but the evidence is currently `UNVERIFIED` as a causal win because the test design likely violates rigor standards regarding interim analysis. Observing a dip at Day 7 and a rise at Day 21 constitutes "peeking" if the test was not pre-committed to a sequential stopping rule. The 8% lift at Week 3 cannot be declared a victory without confirming it holds against the pre-committed sample size or a valid always-valid bound.

**Analysis of the Trajectory**
Your hypothesis (learning curve) is plausible, but so is the "leaky bucket" theory: the tutorial may permanently disengage a specific segment (e.g., non-tech-savvy users) while boosting power users, creating a net positive that hides a critical churn risk. A net 8% gain is meaningless if it masks a 20% drop in a high-value cohort.

We must separate temporary friction from permanent disengagement using **adoption-speed segmentation**:
1.  **Fast Adopters:** Users completing the first core action within 24 hours.
2.  **Slow Adopters:** Users taking >48 hours for the first core action.

If the "dip" is purely a learning effect, both segments should converge to a higher baseline by Week 4. If it is a usability flaw, the "Slow Adopter" segment will show flat or declining retention compared to control, even if the "Fast" segment surges.

**Financial & Risk Context**
With a baseline month-one retention of 40%, an 8% relative lift implies moving to ~43.2%. However, if the initial 15% drop represents users who never return (permanent churn) rather than delayed activation, the long-term LTV impact is negative. We cannot model the "acceptable pain" without knowing *who* dropped off. If the drop is concentrated in a demographic critical to the Malaysia market (e.g., specific device types or language preferences), the aggregate 8% gain is a false positive for a broader rollout.

**Required Actions (This Week)**

1.  **Recompute the Primary Metric with Segmentation:**
    *   Query retention cohorts for Treatment vs. Control, split by **Time-to-First-Lesson** (<24h vs >48h).
    *   *Decision Rule:* If Slow Adopters in Treatment retain <38% (below baseline) while Fast Adopters retain >45%, the tutorial creates a barrier to entry. **Do not roll out.** If both segments trend above control by Day 28, the learning effect hypothesis holds.

2.  **Extend Test Duration & Define Stop Tripwire:**
    *   Extend the test to **April 4th** (14 additional days) to capture a full second cycle of user behavior and stabilize the variance.
    *   *Tripwire:* If the aggregate treatment retention drops below **39%** (a 2.5% relative drop from baseline) at any point in the next 7 days, **stop the test immediately** and revert to control. This guards against the risk that the Week 3 recovery is a statistical anomaly or seasonal artifact.

3.  **Validate Instrumentation:**
    *   Confirm the "lesson completion" event fires identically in both flows. If the new tutorial changes the definition of "completion" (e.g., requires an extra click), the 15% drop is an instrumentation error, not a user behavior change.

**Conclusion**
The aggregate 8% lift is promising but insufficient for a default rollout without verifying the cost of the initial 15% drop. The risk of permanently alienating "Slow Adopters" outweighs the potential gain until segmentation proves the dip is temporary for *all* users.
