Based on your current data (admin count, login frequency, feature adoption, support tickets), here are the top three indicators to monitor weekly to flag at-risk accounts, prioritized by predictive signal strength relative to your baseline.

### 1. The "Admin Breadth" Decay Rate (Primary Indicator)
Your hypothesis that administrative breadth drives retention is strong, but cumulative admin count is a lagging metric. The leading indicator is the **rate of change in active admins**.
*   **The Signal:** Monitor the week-over-week delta in *active* administrators (not just total seats). A drop from 15 to 12 active admins is a stronger churn predictor than a static count of 10.
*   **The Threshold:** Flag any enterprise account where active admins drop by >20% over a rolling 4-week window.
*   **Sector Nuance:** Do not assume the "10 vs. 15" threshold applies equally to Manufacturing vs. Tech yet. Your data is 80% Tech-biased. Treat the Manufacturing segment as `UNVERIFIED` until you isolate their specific baseline. If a Manufacturing client drops from 8 to 5 admins, is that normal consolidation or churn risk? You cannot know without sector-specific baselines.
*   **Action:** Segment your weekly dashboard by sector. If Manufacturing shows a different "cliff" than Tech, adjust the alert threshold per sector.

### 2. The "Core Action" Retention Cohort (Not Just Login Frequency)
Login frequency is a vanity metric; users can log in to complain or check status without deriving value. You must measure retention of the **core action** (the specific HR workflow that delivers value, e.g., "payroll run completed" or "performance review initiated").
*   **The Signal:** Compare the percentage of admins performing the core action vs. those merely logging in.
*   **The Distinction (Growing Pains vs. Disengagement):**
    *   *Growing Pains:* High login frequency + High support tickets + **Stable or Rising** core action completion. This indicates friction during implementation or scaling.
    *   *Genuine Disengagement:* Declining login frequency + High support tickets (or sudden silence) + **Declining** core action completion. This indicates the tool is no longer solving the problem.
*   **Action:** Create a weekly cohort view showing "Active Core Users" vs. "Passive Logins." Flag accounts where the ratio of Core Users to Total Logins drops below their own 3-month average.

### 3. The Support Ticket "Sentiment-Volume" Ratio
A 15% increase in tickets is ambiguous without context. Volume alone does not predict churn; the *type* of volume does.
*   **The Signal:** Correlate ticket volume with feature adoption.
    *   *Scenario A:* Tickets up, Feature Adoption up = Implementation friction (Low churn risk).
    *   *Scenario B:* Tickets up, Feature Adoption flat/down = Usability failure or loss of champion (High churn risk).
*   **The Causality Check:** You cannot assert causality between tickets and churn without isolating the ticket category. Are they "How-to" questions (engagement) or "Bug/Outage" reports (frustration)?
*   **Action:** Tag tickets by intent (Implementation vs. Error vs. Billing). Monitor the ratio of "Error/Frustration" tickets to total logins. A spike in this specific ratio is your pre-cancellation warning.

### Immediate Next Steps (This Week)
1.  **Isolate the Manufacturing Baseline:** Run a query comparing admin churn rates for Manufacturing vs. Tech clients over the last 12 months. Do not apply the Tech "15 admin" rule to Manufacturing until this data confirms or refutes it.
2.  **Define the Core Action:** Explicitly name the one event in your logs that equals "value delivered." If you cannot name it, you cannot measure retention.
3.  **Pre-commit the Intervention Trigger:** Define the exact combination of metrics (e.g., "Admin count drops 20% AND Core Action retention dips below baseline") that triggers Lars's new protocol. Do not rely on gut feeling when the dashboard flashes red.
