**Executive Summary: Tiered Pricing Test Diagnosis (March 10 – April 10)**

The test results indicate a classic failure of value perception rather than simple price sensitivity. With the middle tier capturing only 4% of sign-ups against a 20% target, while Basic rose 10% and Enterprise remained flat, users are not "trading up" to the new option; they are reinforcing the status quo. The data suggests the $49 tier is currently invisible or irrelevant to the user's decision matrix.

**Immediate Measurement Plan to Diagnose Failure**

To determine whether the issue is price, features, or presentation, we must instrument and analyze the following specific behaviors before the April 25th decision:

1.  **Feature Gap Analysis (Activation Events)**
    *   **Question:** Do users on the Basic plan actually hit the limits that the Professional tier solves?
    *   **Measurement:** Track the percentage of Basic users who attempt a "Professional-only" action and fail (e.g., exceed storage, hit API rate limits, attempt advanced export).
    *   **Decision Rule:** If <5% of Basic users hit these limits, the feature set is the problem (users don't need it). If >20% hit limits but don't upgrade, the price or presentation is the barrier.

2.  **Pricing Page Interaction Funnel**
    *   **Question:** Are users ignoring the middle tier entirely, or evaluating and rejecting it?
    *   **Measurement:** Instrument clicks on the "Professional" card, time spent hovering over the $49 price point, and scroll depth on the pricing page.
    *   **Decision Rule:** Low click-through implies a presentation/visibility issue (the "decoy effect" failed). High clicks but low conversion implies a value/price mismatch.

3.  **Cohort Retention by Intent**
    *   **Question:** Are the users who *did* choose Professional retaining better than Basic, validating the value prop for the few who bought it?
    *   **Measurement:** Compare Day-7 and Day-30 retention of the 4% Professional cohort vs. the Basic cohort.
    *   **Decision Rule:** If Professional retention is significantly higher, the product works but the marketing failed. If retention is flat, the tier offers no incremental value.

4.  **Upgrade Latency**
    *   **Question:** Is the middle tier a "later" purchase rather than a sign-up choice?
    *   **Measurement:** Time-to-upgrade from Basic to Professional vs. Basic to Enterprise for users who convert after day 1.
    *   **Decision Rule:** If upgrades happen later, the sign-up friction is too high; consider a trial or delayed offer.

**Recommended Next Steps for April 25th Decision**

*   **If Feature Gap is Low:** Kill the tier. The market does not need these features at any price; simplify to Basic/Enterprise.
*   **If Feature Gap is High but Clicks are Low:** Rework presentation. The middle option is likely being perceived as "too expensive for what it is" or is visually lost. Test a "Most Popular" badge or reorder the layout.
*   **If Clicks are High but Conversion is Low:** Adjust price or bundle. The $49 point may be a psychological barrier; test $39 or add a specific high-value feature to justify the jump from $19.
