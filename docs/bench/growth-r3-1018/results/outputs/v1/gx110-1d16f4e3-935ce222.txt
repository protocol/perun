### Revised Activation Definition & Validation Plan

**Proposed Activation Criterion:**
Shift from "Publishes Site" to **"Publishes Site AND completes one 'Stickiness' Action within 7 days."**
Based on your survey data indicating that 60% of churned publishers never edited content, the new definition must require evidence of *ongoing intent*, not just a one-time setup. Recommended candidate actions to test as the second pillar:
1.  **Content Update:** Editing the published site (adding a section, changing text) within 48 hours of publishing.
2.  **Value Connection:** Connecting a custom domain OR adding a functional element (contact form, analytics, e-commerce widget).
3.  **Return Visit:** A second distinct session where the user logs in specifically to edit (not just view) the site.

**Validation Method (Historical Cohort Analysis):**
Do not guess the threshold; calculate it using your existing data.
1.  **Isolate the Cohort:** Pull all users who signed up 6–12 months ago.
2.  **Flag Candidates:** For each user, tag whether they performed the proposed combined actions (e.g., Published + Custom Domain) within the first 7 days.
3.  **Measure Retention:** Compare the Month-2 retention rate of users who hit this new bar versus those who only published.
4.  **Select the Winner:** The action (or combination) that shows the highest lift in retention *and* covers a viable volume of users (aim for 10–15% of total signups initially) becomes your new North Star input. If "Connecting a Domain" predicts retention perfectly but only 2% of users do it, it is too high a bar for activation; if "Editing once" predicts nothing, it is noise.

**Threshold Setting:**
There is no universal "good" threshold. Your target is the specific percentage of users who hit the new definition *today*. If only 8% of users currently publish and connect a domain, your new activation rate is 8%. The goal is not to artificially inflate this number, but to ensure that the 8% represents users who actually stay. Track this new rate weekly; if onboarding changes work, this percentage will rise organically alongside retention.

**This Week's Steps:**
1.  **Run the Query:** Ask data engineering to output a table showing Month-2 retention for users who (A) only published, vs. (B) published + connected domain, vs. (C) published + edited content.
2.  **Define the Cut:** Select the combination where the retention gap is widest without dropping the activated population below 10% of total signups.
3.  **Update Dashboard:** Replace the "Published Site" metric on Noam's dashboard with the new composite metric, marked as "Beta Definition" until the sprint ends.

**Note:** Ensure the identifier used to track "editing" across sessions is a stable, pseudonymous user ID, not raw PII, to align with privacy compliance. Do not set a target % based on industry benchmarks; use your current baseline as the only valid comparison point.
