**Recommendation: Establish a "New Baseline" with a Bridging Narrative**

Do not recalibrate historical data to match the new definition. Retroactively altering twenty years of records to fit a new bot-filtering logic introduces unverifiable assumptions and corrupts the long-term trend line. Instead, present the migration as a deliberate upgrade in measurement fidelity. Frame the 15% discrepancy not as data loss, but as the removal of noise that previously inflated performance.

**Presentation Strategy for Leadership**

1.  **Define the North Star Shift:** Explicitly state that the organization is moving from a vanity metric (raw page views) to a value metric (verified unique user journeys). Explain that the legacy system counted "traffic," while the new system counts "potential subscribers." The 2,000 daily visits flagged as suspicious are not missing readers; they are non-human noise that would never have converted to paid subscriptions.
2.  **The "Clean Break" Baseline:** Propose starting the official digital subscription baseline today using the new, stricter definition. Label the pre-migration period as "Legacy (Unadjusted)" and the post-migration period as "Digital (Verified)." Do not attempt to force a year-over-year comparison on the raw traffic numbers.
3.  **The Bridge Analysis:** To address the growth projection gap, run a parallel analysis only on the three months of migrated data. Calculate the conversion rate of "Legacy Valid" visits vs. "New Verified" visits. If the conversion rate on the "Verified" set is higher (or even stable), use this to argue that while total volume appears down, *quality* and *revenue potential* are up. This shifts the conversation from "we lost 15% of our audience" to "we identified our real audience."
4.  **Actionable Forecasting:** For the upcoming fiscal year, base targets solely on the "Verified" baseline. Project growth based on the retention and conversion of these unique users, not raw traffic volume. Acknowledge that historical YoY growth rates are no longer applicable benchmarks and must be replaced by cohort-based retention targets.

**Immediate Next Steps**
*   **Instrument the Question:** Configure the new event stream to specifically tag the "2,000 suspicious visits" with a property `traffic_quality: suspicious` rather than deleting them. This allows you to query their behavior later to prove they did not engage with paywalls or newsletters, validating the filter.
*   **Define the Cohort:** Isolate the first cohort of users tracked under the new "Verified" definition. Measure their Week-1 and Week-2 retention of the core action (e.g., "article read beyond paywall" or "newsletter signup"). This retention curve is your new true north, replacing the old page-view trends.

**Note:** The 15% discrepancy is an `UNVERIFIED` impact on revenue until you correlate the filtered bot traffic with actual subscription conversion data; do not assume a 15% revenue loss. Ensure the stable user identifier used for these new cohorts is pseudonymized and routed to your privacy compliance protocol, distinct from raw server logs.
