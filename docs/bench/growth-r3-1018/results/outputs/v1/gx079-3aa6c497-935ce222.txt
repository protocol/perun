**Executive Summary: Test Status and Strategic Recommendation**

**Verdict:** The A/B test conducted from July 1–21 is **invalid** for causal inference and cannot be statistically adjusted to isolate the true effect of the in-app offer. Do not use these results to finalize the Q3 pricing strategy.

**Reasoning:**
The core requirement for a valid A/B test is that the control group remains unexposed to the treatment variable. Your data indicates that ~25% of the control group was exposed to the external radio campaign promoting the annual plan. This creates two fatal flaws:
1.  **Contamination (Dilution):** If the radio ads drove any annual sign-ups, the control group's conversion rate is artificially inflated. This shrinks the observed difference between the test and control groups, masking the true lift of the in-app prompt. The "negligible difference" you observed is likely a false negative caused by this leakage, not evidence that the offer is ineffective.
2.  **Unidentifiable Confounding:** You stated uncertainty regarding whether the radio ads have the "same conversion power" as the in-app prompt. Without knowing the relative conversion rates of the external ads versus the internal prompt, it is mathematically impossible to subtract the "radio effect" from the control group to reveal the pure "in-app effect." Any adjustment would rely on invented assumptions rather than your data.

**Decision:**
**Stop** analysis on this specific dataset. Declaring a "win," "loss," or attempting to model an adjustment factor would be `UNVERIFIED` and risky. The pre-committed design was violated mid-test by an external event that directly targeted the metric of interest.

**Recommended Next Steps for Q3 Strategy:**
Since the August 5th deadline is tight, do not attempt to re-run a full 21-day test immediately if time is insufficient for a new pre-committed sample size. Instead:
1.  **Qualitative Triangulation:** Immediately analyze support tickets, user surveys, or session recordings from the test period specifically for users who mentioned "radio" or "ads." This may provide directional evidence of external influence, though not a precise lift percentage.
2.  **Rapid Re-test (if feasible):** If the Q3 rollout can be delayed slightly, launch a new, shorter test with a strict **guardrail**: pause all external marketing channels targeting the test regions during the experiment window. Pre-commit to a smaller Minimum Detectable Effect (MDE) if sample size is a constraint, but ensure the control group is hermetically sealed.
3.  **Strategic Pivot:** If a re-test is impossible before August 5th, frame the decision to the CMO as a **risk-managed rollout**. Acknowledge that while the specific lift % is unknown, the *direction* of the hypothesis (discounts drive annual plans) remains theoretically sound. Consider a phased rollout to a small, non-contested region to gather fresh, clean data before a full Q3 launch.

**This Week's Action:**
Draft a one-page memo to the CMO stating the test is **inconclusive due to control group contamination**, explicitly rejecting the "negligible difference" as a valid metric. Propose either a 7-day "clean" re-test in a single city (e.g., Salzburg only, with media blackout) or a limited phased rollout with heavy monitoring as the alternative path to the August 5th deadline.
