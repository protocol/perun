**Recommendation for Tunde: The "Real Learning" Metric Set**

Do not track time spent per lesson; it is a vanity metric that rises whether a student is learning or staring at the screen. Do not rely solely on quiz completion rates, as you correctly suspected this encourages guessing. Instead, instrument and manually calculate these three specific metrics using your existing spreadsheet exports:

**1. The North Star: Weekly Active Learners (WAL)**
*   **Definition:** The count of unique students who complete **at least two** distinct lessons in a 7-day window.
*   **Why:** A single completion could be luck or a one-off. Two completions prove the student returned and engaged again. This replaces "total signups" or "total quizzes" as your primary health indicator.
*   **Action:** If WAL stagnates while total signups grow, your retention is leaking. Stop building new content and fix the core loop.

**2. The Integrity Check: First-Attempt Success Rate (FASR) by Question**
*   **Definition:** For every specific question, calculate: `(Users who got it right on the 1st try) / (Total users who saw the question)`.
*   **Why:** This directly validates your fear of guessing. If a question has a 90% completion rate but only a 15% FASR, students are brute-forcing answers to unlock the next level. High completion + low FASR = broken assessment.
*   **Action:** Flag any question where FASR is <40% despite high completion. Rewrite these questions to be less guessable or add a "hint penalty" that prevents level progression until the concept is demonstrated.

**3. The Retention Signal: Day-7 Return Cohort**
*   **Definition:** Group students by their signup week. Calculate what percentage of each group returns to do *any* activity exactly 7 days later.
*   **Why:** This tells you if the product provides lasting value. Acquisition (new signups) is useless if the Day-7 return is near zero.
*   **Action:** Track this weekly. If the Week 1 cohort returns at 20% but Week 4 returns at 5%, your recent changes are hurting retention.

**Implementation Plan (Zero Budget)**
Export your raw logs to CSV. Use a simple script or spreadsheet pivot tables to calculate FASR per question ID and count unique user IDs with >1 session per week. You do not need a data engineer for this; you need a consistent export routine.

**This Week's Steps**
1.  **Run the FASR audit:** Calculate the First-Attempt Success Rate for your top 10 most-completed questions. If any are below 40%, pause new feature work and rewrite those questions immediately.
2.  **Establish the WAL baseline:** Count how many unique users completed 2+ lessons last week. Set this as your baseline; next week's goal is simply to beat this number, not to increase total pageviews.

*Note: Ensure user IDs in your spreadsheets are pseudonymous (e.g., User_123) to protect student privacy; do not store names or school details in these analysis files.*
*These metrics rely entirely on your existing data; if your current exports lack "attempt number" or "timestamp," you must add those two fields to your database logging before calculation.*
