**To:** VP of Product, Northwind Electronics
**From:** Growth Analytics
**Subject:** Recommendation on New Checkout Flow Rollout

**Decision: Do not roll out permanently yet. Extend the A/B test for 7 days post-holiday to isolate design efficacy from performance noise.**

The current 12% lift is `UNVERIFIED` as a measure of design success because the concurrent 40% spike in page load times on December 24 introduces a severe confounder. We cannot determine if the design failed to hit the 15% target or if performance degradation suppressed a potentially higher lift. Rolling out now risks cementing a flow that may underperform once traffic normalizes, or reverting unnecessarily if the design is actually superior.

**Analysis of Variables**
1.  **Design Variable:** The simplified form. Hypothesis: +15% conversion.
2.  **Performance Variable:** Page load latency. Known impact: High correlation with checkout abandonment.
3.  **The Conflict:** The holiday traffic spike degraded performance specifically during the test window. The observed 12% lift is the net result of (Design Gain) minus (Performance Loss). Without separating these, the data is inconclusive regarding the design's true potential.

**Isolation Strategy**
To isolate the performance variable from the design variable using our existing dataset and immediate next steps:
*   **Segment by Load Time:** Query the analytics platform to split the test cohort into deciles based on individual session page load times. Compare conversion lift between the "fast load" deciles (e.g., <2s) and "slow load" deciles (>4s). If the "fast load" group shows >15% lift while the "slow load" group shows negligible or negative lift, the design is valid and performance was the suppressor.
*   **Time-Based Cohort Exclusion:** Isolate data from December 24 (the spike day) and re-calculate the lift excluding this period. If the lift rises significantly closer to or above 15% on stable days, the design is likely successful.
*   **Post-Holiday Extension:** Since holiday traffic patterns are anomalous, the most rigorous method is to continue the A/B test for 7 days into January when traffic and server loads stabilize. This provides a clean baseline where the performance variable is controlled.

**Financial & Risk Context**
Reverting to the old version before the New Year flash sale carries the opportunity cost of the confirmed 12% gain we have already observed. However, rolling out permanently based on contaminated data risks a regression if the 12% was entirely due to external factors or if the design actually performs worse under normal load conditions. The cost of a 7-day extension is minimal engineering overhead compared to the revenue risk of an incorrect permanent deployment.

**This-Week Steps**
1.  **Run Segmented Query:** By EOD today, execute the conversion lift query segmented by page load time deciles to see if the "fast" users drove the 12% gain.
2.  **Extend Test Duration:** Configure the experimentation platform to continue the test through January 2nd without changing traffic allocation, ensuring the sample size calculation is updated for the extended duration.
3.  **Set Guardrail:** Establish a page-load time guardrail metric; if the new flow consistently increases latency by >10% in the post-holiday period, trigger an automatic review regardless of conversion lift.

The 12% lift is a positive signal but not a causal proof of design superiority under normal conditions. Isolate the noise before locking the roadmap.
