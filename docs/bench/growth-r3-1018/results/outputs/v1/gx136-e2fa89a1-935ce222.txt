**Executive Summary: Search Ranking Rollout Decision**

**Current Status:** The 15% increase in click-through rate (CTR) confirms the algorithm successfully surfaces engaging niche items. However, the 5% decrease in Average Order Value (AOV) presents a revenue risk that cannot yet be classified as noise or signal based solely on the provided figures.

**Immediate Recommendation:** **Pause acceleration** at the current 5% traffic level. Do not expand to the next cohort until the revenue impact is verified. Accelerating now risks compounding a structural revenue leak before Q4 targets are secured.

**Analysis of Noise vs. Signal**
The drop in AOV is likely **signal**, not sample-size noise, for two reasons:
1.  **Behavioral Consistency:** Niche items often have lower price points than mainstream bestsellers. If the algorithm successfully shifts mix toward these items, a lower blended AOV is a structural outcome, not a statistical fluctuation.
2.  **Directional Clarity:** While small samples create variance, a consistent 5% drop across the 5% cohort suggests a real shift in user purchasing behavior. Treating this as "temporary anomaly" without verification is a gamble on Q4 revenue.

**Required Verification Steps (This Week)**
To distinguish between a temporary dip and a permanent revenue ceiling, execute the following before November 1st:

1.  **Recompute Total Revenue Per Visitor (RPV):** Stop analyzing CTR and AOV in isolation. Calculate RPV for the test group vs. control.
    *   *Formula:* `(CTR × Conversion Rate × AOV)`.
    *   *Decision Rule:* If RPV is flat or positive despite lower AOV, the higher engagement offsets the price drop. If RPV is negative, the strategy fails financially.
2.  **Segment by Price Tier:** Break down the 5% cohort data by item price band.
    *   *Hypothesis:* If the AOV drop is driven entirely by users buying *more* low-cost items without reducing high-cost purchases, the long-term impact may be neutral or positive (volume growth).
    *   *Hypothesis:* If users are substituting high-cost items for low-cost niche items, this is a structural revenue loss.
3.  **Extend Observation Window:** Niche items may have longer consideration cycles. Check if the 5% cohort shows delayed conversion compared to the control group over a 7-day window rather than immediate purchase.

**Projection Methodology**
Do not extrapolate the current 5% AOV drop linearly to 50% traffic without confirming the *mix shift* mechanism.
*   **Scenario A (Volume Offset):** If niche items drive higher conversion rates that mathematically cancel the lower AOV, project total revenue based on the net RPV lift.
*   **Scenario B (Cannibalization):** If niche items are cannibalizing high-margin sales, project a 5% revenue decline at full rollout. This directly threatens Q4 targets.

**Final Decision Framework**
*   **Accelerate:** Only if verified RPV is ≥ control group RPV.
*   **Rollback:** If verified RPV is < control group AND the AOV drop is due to cannibalization of high-value items.
*   **Hybrid Approach:** If niche items drive engagement but lower revenue, consider a "diversified" ranking that balances niche discovery with high-value anchors, rather than a full shift.

**Action Item:** Run the RPV and price-tier segmentation analysis on the existing 5% data within 48 hours. Present the net revenue impact, not just engagement metrics, to approve any further traffic allocation.
