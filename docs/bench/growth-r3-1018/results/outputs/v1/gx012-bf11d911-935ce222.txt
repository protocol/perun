### Key Indicators for Smart FAQ Widget Evaluation
**Audience:** Head of Customer Success
**Goal:** Determine whether to expand to Enterprise or pause for redesign based on net value, not just volume.

#### 1. The North Star: Net Support Cost per Resolved Issue
Do not track "Tickets Deflected" or "Total Volume" in isolation; these are vanity metrics that rise even if customer satisfaction collapses. Instead, measure **Net Support Cost per Resolved Issue**. This combines the cost of human agent time (which has risen due to longer resolution times) against the savings from reduced ticket volume.
*   **Why:** If the widget deflects 20% of tickets but makes the remaining 80% take 25 minutes longer, you may be spending *more* money overall while annoying customers. This metric forces a trade-off analysis between volume reduction and agent efficiency.

#### 2. The Diagnostic Funnel (Read Bottom-Up)
To distinguish between "successful deflection" (easy problems solved) and "confusion creation" (users failing then writing long tickets), instrument these three specific events. Do not instrument anything else until these questions are answered.

*   **Metric A: The "Deflection Success" Rate**
    *   *Question:* Of the users who interact with the widget, what percentage leave without submitting a ticket *and* do not return to support within 48 hours?
    *   *Action:* If this is low, the widget is not solving problems; it is merely delaying them.
*   **Metric B: The "Complexity Shift" Ratio**
    *   *Question:* Compare the average resolution time of tickets *with* prior widget interaction vs. those *without*.
    *   *Action:* If tickets with prior interaction are significantly longer (as your 25-minute increase suggests), the widget is likely confusing users, causing them to write verbose, frustrated descriptions when they finally reach a human.
*   **Metric C: Post-Interaction CSAT (Micro-survey)**
    *   *Question:* Immediately after a widget session (whether they ticket or not), ask: "Did this answer your question?"
    *   *Action:* A drop here confirms the "confusion" hypothesis.

#### 3. The Decision Framework
Use these thresholds to decide the Enterprise rollout. These are derived from your own baseline, not external benchmarks.

| Scenario | Indicator Pattern | Decision |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Efficient Deflection** | Volume ↓, Resolution Time ↔️ or ↓, CSAT ↔️ or ↑ | **Expand** to Enterprise. The widget is filtering noise effectively. |
| **Confusion Engine** | Volume ↓, Resolution Time ↑↑, CSAT ↓ | **Pause & Redesign.** You are saving ticket count but burning agent hours and customer goodwill. |
| **Partial Success** | Volume ↓, Resolution Time ↑ (slight), CSAT ↔️ | **Optimize Logic.** Restrict widget to specific topics where it works; do not roll out globally yet. |

#### 4. This Week's Steps
1.  **Query your analytics** to segment tickets by "Had Widget Interaction: Yes/No" and calculate the exact difference in average resolution time between these two cohorts.
2.  **Instrument one new event:** `widget_feedback_binary` (Helpful/Not Helpful) triggered immediately upon widget close, linked to a pseudonymous user ID to track if "Not Helpful" users subsequently file a ticket.
3.  **Calculate the break-even point:** Determine how many minutes of agent time can be added per remaining ticket before the cost of the extra time exceeds the salary savings of the two unhired agents. If the current 25-minute increase exceeds this number, the project is currently losing money.

**Note:** Ensure the user ID used for cohort tracking is pseudonymous; route any PII handling to your privacy compliance protocol. Do not compare your 18% drop to industry averages; judge it solely against your internal cost model.
