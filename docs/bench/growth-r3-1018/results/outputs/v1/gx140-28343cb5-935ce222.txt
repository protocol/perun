**Weekly Growth Monitor: FreshBox Pricing Test (Nov 1–30)**
**Audience:** CFO
**Goal:** Predict long-term profitability and churn risk before the test concludes.

**1. The North Star: Paid Conversion Rate (Post-Trial)**
*   **Definition:** Percentage of trial users who pay for Box 5 (the first non-discounted box).
*   **Why it matters:** This is the only metric that confirms if the extended discount builds a habit or just delays the payment decision.
*   **Target:** Monitor the gap between the current rate and the 35% goal. If this does not trend upward by Week 3, the discount depth is likely insufficient to change behavior.

**2. Leading Indicator of Churn: "Box 4 Completion Rate"**
*   **Definition:** Percentage of test-group users who actually receive and log in to manage their 4th discounted box.
*   **Why it matters:** Users who skip or cancel before Box 4 are "discount hunters" who will never convert. A drop-off here predicts immediate post-trial churn.
*   **Action Rule:** If Box 4 completion is <80% of Box 1 starts, the offer is attracting low-intent users. Prepare to tighten eligibility criteria.

**3. Leading Indicator of Profitability: Early Margin per Acquired Subscriber**
*   **Definition:** (Revenue from first 4 boxes) minus (Food Cost + Shipping + Discount Value) for the test group, averaged per user.
*   **Why it matters:** Extending the discount increases the "cost to acquire" a paying customer. This metric tells us how much "profit debt" we are taking on to get a subscriber.
*   **Action Rule:** Calculate the break-even point: How many months must a customer stay to repay the extra discount cost? If the required tenure exceeds 6 months based on current margins, the strategy threatens cash flow during the holiday rush.

**4. Guardrail Metric: Immediate Post-Trial Cancellation Rate**
*   **Definition:** Percentage of users who convert to paid (Box 5) but cancel before Box 6.
*   **Why it matters:** This validates your fear of "lower-quality customers." If users pay once just to lock in a future rate or out of confusion, then churn immediately, the lift in conversion is vanity.
*   **Action Rule:** If this rate exceeds 15% (or your historical baseline), the "win" in conversion is false. The net revenue impact is negative.

**5. Cohort Comparison: Test vs. Control "Day-30 Retention"**
*   **Definition:** Compare the percentage of users still active 30 days after signup between the new pricing group and the old pricing group.
*   **Why it matters:** Since we cannot see 6-month data yet, Day-30 is the earliest reliable proxy for long-term habit formation.
*   **Action Rule:** If the Test Cohort Day-30 retention is lower than the Control Cohort, stop the test early. Do not wait for November 30th.

**This Week's Steps:**
1.  **Query:** Pull the "Box 4 Completion Rate" for the Nov 1–7 cohort immediately. If it is below 85%, flag the risk of attracting non-serious users to the CFO.
2.  **Calculate:** Compute the "Early Margin per Acquired Subscriber" for the test group. Determine exactly how many months of full-price subscription are needed to recover the extra discount cost.
3.  **Monitor:** Set an alert for the "Immediate Post-Trial Cancellation Rate." If the first wave of users hits Box 5 and cancels, pause the test to prevent further margin erosion.

*Note: All benchmarks above are derived from your stated goals and logical constraints; specific threshold values (e.g., 15% cancellation) should be validated against your historical control data if available.*
*No external industry benchmarks were used; analysis relies strictly on internal cohort comparison.*
