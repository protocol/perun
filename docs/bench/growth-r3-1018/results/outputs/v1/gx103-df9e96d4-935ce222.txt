### Report Draft: Trastevere Geo-Experiment Analysis

**Executive Summary**
The observed 12% increase in order frequency is currently **UNVERIFIED** as a causal win due to high risk of control group contamination (spillover) and lack of segmentation between new and existing users. Do not roll out to all of Rome based on the current aggregate figure.

**Required Segmentation Cuts**
To isolate the true effect, recompute the lift using these three specific cuts. Each cut dictates a distinct decision:

1.  **User Status: New vs. Existing**
    *   *Analysis:* Split the 5,000 treated users by those who ordered prior to June 1st versus first-time buyers.
    *   *Decision Rule:* If the lift is driven entirely by **existing users** accelerating orders (pull-forward effect) with no gain in **new users**, the banner merely shifts revenue temporally without growing the base. **Action:** Do not roll out; the cost of the promo outweighs the net-new value. If **new user** acquisition is positive, proceed to cut #2.

2.  **Location Integrity: Strict Geo-Fence vs. Commuter Overlap**
    *   *Analysis:* Filter the control group to exclude any user with a delivery address or GPS ping in Trastevere during the test window. Compare this "clean" control against the treated group.
    *   *Decision Rule:* If the lift disappears or drops below the break-even margin (e.g., <3%) in the clean comparison, the original 12% was inflated by spillover/contamination. **Action:** Kill the test; the creative does not work in isolation. If the lift holds, proceed to cut #3.

3.  **Post-Test Retention (Week 3+)**
    *   *Analysis:* Measure order frequency for the treated group in the two weeks following June 14th compared to the control.
    *   *Decision Rule:* If treated users drop below control levels post-test, the banner caused a "sugar rush" (cannibalization). **Action:** Do not roll out; long-term LTV is likely negative. If retention remains flat or higher, the lift is sustainable.

**Mandatory Caveats for Giulia's Presentation**
Include these three points explicitly to prevent overestimation:

*   **Contamination Warning:** The reported 12% is an upper bound. Due to Trastevere's high connectivity with central Rome, the control group was likely exposed to the promotion via social media or word-of-mouth, artificially suppressing the measured lift. The *true* effect could be higher, but without a clean control, we cannot quantify it.
*   **Pull-Forward Risk:** Without separating new vs. existing customers, we cannot rule out that the banner simply caused loyalists to order twice in one week rather than once a week over two weeks. This creates a short-term spike but zero net growth.
*   **Seasonality Confounder:** June 1–14 coincides with the start of the tourist season in Trastevere. Any lift may be driven by organic tourist influx rather than the banner. A year-over-year comparison for this specific zip code is required to validate the result.
