**To:** CEO, Head of Sales
**From:** Growth Analytics
**Subject:** Q3 Primary Metric Recommendation: Prioritize "Verified Viewings" Over Lead Volume

**Recommendation**
For the Q3 roadmap and the July 1 advertising commitment, the primary metric must shift from **Lead Volume** or **Viewing Rate** to **Verified Viewings Per Week** (leads that result in a scheduled, attended property viewing).

Current data indicates a disconnect: while the recent test increased the *rate* of viewings (10% → 15%), the sales team reports no improvement in conversation quality. This suggests the additional form friction is filtering out serious buyers alongside low-intent users, or creating false positives where users jump through hoops but lack genuine purchase intent. Optimizing for raw lead volume inflates vanity metrics; optimizing strictly for viewing rate ignores the "annoyance factor" that degrades lead warmth.

**The Metric Definition**
*   **Primary Metric:** Count of unique users who complete the contact form AND have a viewing status of "Scheduled" or "Attended" within 7 days.
*   **Why:** This metric only rises if a user provides contact details *and* demonstrates enough intent to commit time to a viewing. It aligns directly with revenue opportunities (agent time spent on viable prospects) rather than intermediate form fills.
*   **Input Metrics to Monitor:**
    1.  Form Completion Rate (to detect excessive friction).
    2.  Sales Qualified Lead (SQL) Rate (to validate conversation quality).

**Experimental Design for Q3**
Do not rely on the small test's "15% viewing rate" as a final win. That result is **UNVERIFIED** for causal impact on revenue because the sample was small and the sales feedback contradicts the quantitative lift.

Run a fixed-horizon A/B test (2–3 weeks) with the following parameters:
*   **Control:** Current simple form.
*   **Variant:** Proposed qualifying questions.
*   **Primary Readout:** Difference in *Verified Viewings* (not total leads).
*   **Guardrail Metric:** Cost Per Verified Viewing (CPV). If the variant reduces volume so drastically that CPV spikes above our margin threshold, the test fails regardless of quality claims.
*   **Decision Rule:** Pre-commit to a sample size sufficient to detect a 10% change in Verified Viewings. Do not peek at results daily; read once at the end. If the variant increases Verified Viewings while keeping CPV stable or lower, ship it. If Verified Viewings are flat but total leads drop, reject it.

**This Week's Actions**
1.  **Instrumentation:** Define and tag the "Viewing Scheduled" event in your analytics immediately. Ensure this event links back to the specific form variant via a stable, pseudonymous user ID.
2.  **Baseline Calculation:** Query the last 4 weeks of data to establish the current baseline for "Verified Viewings Per Week." Do not use industry benchmarks; use only PropFind's historical performance.
3.  **Test Setup:** Configure the A/B test to run for a fixed duration based on your current traffic (approx. 500 leads/week) to reach statistical significance on the *Verified Viewing* count, not just form submission.

**Note:** The sales team's qualitative feedback ("quality has not improved") is a critical guardrail; if quantitative views rise but qualitative scores fall, the metric definition may need to include a post-viewing sales rating.
Ensure all user IDs used for cohort tracking are pseudonymized and routed to privacy compliance protocols before analysis.
