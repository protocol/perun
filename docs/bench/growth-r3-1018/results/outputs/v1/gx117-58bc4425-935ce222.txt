**Subject:** Recommendation on Homepage Headline A/B Test Results

**Executive Decision: Do Not Adopt**
Based on the data from the November 1–10 test, we **cannot** permanently adopt the emotional headline style. The observed 25% increase in click-through rate (CTR) is statistically **unverified** as a causal result of the headline design. The external news cycle created a "mix shift" that contaminated the experiment, rendering the raw lift numbers unreliable for decision-making.

**Why the Results Are Contaminated**
Our experiment relied on the assumption that randomization would balance user behavior across both groups. However, the 200% traffic surge driven by breaking news violated this assumption. During high-intensity news cycles, user intent shifts dramatically; visitors are often seeking immediate confirmation rather than engaging with content depth.

The data suggests a **composition bias**: the treatment group inadvertently captured a higher proportion of these high-arousal, click-prone users compared to the control group. Consequently, the 25% lift likely reflects the *type* of user who arrived during the surge, not the *effectiveness* of the emotional headlines. In statistical terms, the external environment acted as a confounding variable that correlates with both the treatment assignment (due to random chance in a skewed sample) and the outcome (CTR). We cannot isolate the headline's impact from the news cycle's impact.

**Salvaging Insights vs. Re-testing**
Attempting to mathematically adjust for this noise post-hoc is not viable. Because we did not pre-commit to a model accounting for hourly traffic volatility or user intent segments, any regression adjustment we apply now would be speculative and prone to further bias.

**Recommended Path Forward**
1.  **Immediate Action:** Maintain the current headline strategy. Do not roll out emotional headlines globally based on this test.
2.  **Re-test Protocol:** Schedule a follow-up A/B test during a period of stable traffic (non-election, non-breaking news window).
    *   **Design:** Pre-commit to a fixed sample size calculated for normal traffic variance.
    *   **Guardrail:** Monitor "bounce rate" and "time on page" alongside CTR. If CTR rises but engagement time drops, it confirms the "clickbait" effect rather than genuine value.
    *   **Duration:** Run until the pre-committed sample is reached, regardless of daily fluctuations, to avoid "peeking" bias.

**Timeline**
We can draft the re-test design by next Tuesday and aim to have valid results within 14 days of launching during a calm news cycle. We will present a definitive recommendation once we have data isolated from external shocks.
