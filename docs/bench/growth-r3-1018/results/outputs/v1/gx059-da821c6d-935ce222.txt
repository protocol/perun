**Subject:** Metric Framework for Contract Review Module ROI Calculator

**Primary North Star Metric: "Verified Risk-Adjusted Throughput"**
Do not track "time saved" or "contracts processed" as your primary metric. For UK law firms, speed without certainty is a liability, not an asset. Instead, measure **the number of contracts reaching a "Senior Review Ready" state per lawyer per week where zero critical errors were flagged post-processing.**

This metric combines volume (throughput) with the non-negotiable constraint of accuracy (risk-adjusted). It directly answers the partner's fear: "Does this tool let my team handle more work without increasing my malpractice exposure?" If this number rises, the firm is genuinely more productive. If "contracts processed" rises but "Verified Risk-Adjusted Throughput" stays flat, the tool is creating hidden rework costs.

**Secondary Input Metrics (The Drivers)**
To explain *how* the North Star moves in your ROI calculator, track these three inputs:

1.  **First-Pass Acceptance Rate (Activation):** The percentage of drafted contracts that pass automated checks and require *no* substantive legal rewrites by the senior associate.
    *   *Why:* This measures the "aha" moment. If lawyers still have to rewrite the core logic, the tool is merely a typewriter, not an automator.
2.  **Critical Exception Density (Risk Guardrail):** The count of high-severity issues (e.g., missing indemnity clauses, jurisdiction errors) caught by the system per 100 pages.
    *   *Why:* This quantifies risk mitigation. Even if the lawyer catches these later, proving the system *found* them validates the safety net.
3.  **Cycle Time Variance (Efficiency Context):** Track the *standard deviation* of review time, not just the average.
    *   *Why:* Law firms bill by the hour but hate unpredictable delays. Reducing the variance (making the 4-hour manual task consistently 1.5 hours) is often more valuable to operations than a volatile average.

**ROI Calculator Logic for Enterprise Clients**
When building the model for clients, do not use industry benchmarks for "average time saved." Those are `UNVERIFIED` and will be rejected by skeptical partners. Instead, structure the calculator to ingest the client's own baseline:
*   **Input:** Client's current average manual hours per contract (your pilot data suggests 4 hours).
*   **Input:** Client's fully loaded cost per hour for the specific lawyer grade.
*   **Projection:** Apply the *First-Pass Acceptance Rate* to estimate hours reclaimed.
*   **Risk Adjustment:** Explicitly model the cost of a "missed error" (even if probability is low) to show that the tool pays for itself by preventing a single significant oversight.
