**Recommendation: Do not ship Variant B as the default flow; adopt a risk-tiered configuration.**

While Variant B achieves a critical security win (90% reduction in takeovers), the 25-point drop in legitimate recovery success (85% → 60%) represents a catastrophic failure in user value for a messaging app where account access equals communication access. A 40% failure rate for legitimate users is not an "acceptable trade-off" without further validation; it implies that for every 100 locked-out users, 40 are permanently lost or forced into high-cost support channels. This violates the principle of measuring real customer value: security that locks out the owner is a feature failure, not a success.

**Decision Framework for the Steering Committee:**

1.  **Reject the Binary Choice:** The data presents a false dichotomy between "secure but inaccessible" and "accessible but vulnerable." Shipping Variant B globally risks massive churn and brand damage among loyal users who cannot recover accounts.
2.  **Immediate Action (The "This-Week" Step):** Instrument a **cohort analysis** on the 40% failure group from the test period. Specifically, track:
    *   % who contact support within 24 hours.
    *   % who attempt recovery again via a different device/method.
    *   % who churn (no activity) after 7 days.
    *   *Why:* You cannot assess "permanent churn" or "trust impact" without knowing if these users recovered via support or abandoned the product. Until this is known, the true cost of Variant B is `UNVERIFIED`.
3.  **Proposed Configuration:** Implement **Variant B only for high-risk signals** (e.g., new device, unusual location, rapid repeated attempts) and retain the original flow (or a lighter friction variant) for low-risk recognized devices. This targets the 90% takeover reduction where it matters most while preserving the 85% success rate for trusted contexts.
4.  **Guardrail Metric:** Define a hard floor for legitimate recovery success (e.g., >75%) tied to support ticket volume. If the risk-tiered approach causes support volume to spike >20%, the friction is too high even for high-risk cases.

**Final Call:** Extend the experiment design. Do not decide on a global rollout based on aggregate numbers that hide the segment-specific pain. Run a follow-up test specifically on the **risk-tiered logic** for 14 days, pre-committing to a sample size that allows detection of a 5% lift in support costs. If the risk-tiered approach maintains >80% legitimate success while still blocking >80% of takeovers, ship that. If not, iterate on the friction mechanism (e.g., push notification approval instead of email) before forcing a 60% success rate on your user base.

*Note: The 90% takeover reduction must be validated against the absolute volume of attacks during the test window; a 90% drop on 10 attacks is less significant than a 50% drop on 10,000.*
*Ensure all user identifiers used for this cohort tracking are pseudonymized and routed to privacy compliance review before analysis.*
