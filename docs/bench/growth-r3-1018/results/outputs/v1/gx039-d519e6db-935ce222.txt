**Decision Framework: Tooltip Experiment Read-Out**

**Status:** `UNVERIFIED` (Causal link to Retention missing; Guardrail breached).
**Call:** Do not ship globally. Extend analysis to validate the retention hypothesis against the satisfaction guardrail before iterating copy.

**1. Reconcile the Conflict**
You have a primary metric lift (+25% click-through) but a failed guardrail (Satisfaction dropped 0.6 points). Since your North Star is **Retention**, clicks are merely an input metric. The critical failure is assuming clicks equal value. If the tooltips drive "angry clicks" or accidental interactions that do not lead to core value, they will increase noise without moving retention, while actively degrading sentiment for power users.

**2. Required Behavioral Analysis (The "So What?" Data)**
To determine if the lift is real value or vanity, segment the 20,000 participants into **Treatment-Exposed** vs. **Control** and analyze these specific cohorts over the next 14–28 days:

*   **Feature Retention Curve:** Do users who clicked via the tooltip return to use that specific feature again in Week 2 without the prompt? If repeat usage is flat, the tooltip is generating one-off noise, not adoption.
*   **Core Action Retention:** Compare the Day-7 and Day-14 retention rates of the Treatment group against the Control group. If overall retention is flat or lower despite higher clicks, the intrusiveness is causing churn that offsets any feature adoption gains.
*   **Power User Segmentation:** Isolate users with >6 months tenure. If their satisfaction drop is driving a measurable dip in their session frequency or core action completion, the cost of annoying your most valuable segment outweighs the gain from new users.

**3. Weighing Quantitative Lift vs. Qualitative Feedback**
Do not average the scores. The drop from 4.2 to 3.6 suggests a polarized reaction, likely concentrated among power users who find the guidance patronizing.
*   **The Trade-off:** A 25% lift in clicks is irrelevant if it correlates with a net negative in Lifetime Value (LTV) due to churn.
*   **The Verdict Logic:** If the **Feature Retention Curve** (above) shows no significant uplift in repeat usage, the quantitative lift is vanity. The qualitative feedback then becomes the deciding factor: remove or heavily restrict the feature to preserve power user sentiment.

**4. This-Week Steps**
1.  **Query Cohort Retention:** Run a query comparing Day-14 retention of "Tooltip Clickers" vs. "Control Group." If the difference is <2%, the feature does not move the North Star.
2.  **Segment Satisfaction:** Break down the satisfaction score by user tenure (New vs. Power Users). If Power Users drove >50% of the score drop, limit the tooltip logic to exclude users with >3 months activity immediately.
3.  **Define the Tripwire:** Set a hard rule: Ship only if "Repeat Feature Usage" increases by >10% *and* Power User satisfaction recovers to >4.0 in a follow-up micro-survey. If not, kill the experiment.
