**Executive Recommendation: Hold prices steady for Q2; do not proceed with a moderate increase yet.**

The data from the recent test indicates a critical failure in our price elasticity assumptions for Germany. While we hypothesized lower sensitivity, the 30% price hike triggered a 25% conversion drop, significantly outperforming the negative reaction in Spain (10% drop). More critically, the spike in cancellation rates just four days post-notification signals immediate churn risk rather than gradual attrition. This suggests the price point crossed a psychological or value-perception threshold specific to the German market, potentially exacerbated by the holiday timing but too severe to ignore.

**Analysis of Volume Loss vs. Revenue Gain**
To determine if a price increase is viable, the percentage gain in price must mathematically exceed the percentage loss in volume.
*   **The 30% Hike Scenario:** A 30% price increase coupled with a 25% volume drop results in a net revenue *decrease*. You are selling significantly fewer units at a higher price, but the volume erosion outweighs the margin gain.
*   **The "Moderate" Increase Risk:** Given the sharp cliff at 30%, the demand curve in Germany appears non-linear. A "moderate" increase (e.g., 15-20%) carries a high probability of triggering a disproportionate drop in conversion that erodes total revenue. The four-day cancellation spike confirms that existing customers are also highly reactive to price changes, threatening Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR) stability.

**Confounding Factors**
The holiday timing is a valid confounder. Purchasing behavior during holidays can be erratic, and price sensitivity may fluctuate due to seasonal spending caps or gift-giving dynamics. However, relying on "holiday noise" to explain a 25% drop is a dangerous gamble for Q2 planning. We cannot assume the reaction will be milder in a standard month without verification. The fundamental misunderstanding of German price sensitivity is the more likely root cause, requiring a reset of our baseline assumptions.

**Decision Framework**
Proceeding with a moderate increase now violates the principle of validating the demand curve before scaling. The cost of a failed price rollout (churn, brand trust erosion, and the operational cost of reverting prices) exceeds the opportunity cost of delaying revenue optimization by one quarter.

**This-Week Steps**
1.  **Segment the Holiday Cohort:** Isolate the conversion and cancellation data for users who signed up or were notified strictly during the holiday window versus those outside it. If the 25% drop persists in the non-holiday segment, the price sensitivity is structural, not seasonal.
2.  **Calculate the Break-Even Elasticity:** Determine the exact conversion drop percentage that would neutralize a 10% and 20% price hike respectively. Compare these thresholds against the observed 25% drop to quantify the revenue risk of a moderate increase.
3.  **Design a Fixed-Horizon Re-Test:** Plan a new A/B test for late Q2 with a pre-committed sample size and duration (no peeking), testing only a 5% and 10% increase against a control, with "Day-7 Retention" as a primary guardrail metric alongside conversion.
