**Recommendation: Proceed with a Desktop-Only Rollout, Hold Mobile**

Based on the three-week experiment data (N=25,000), the evidence supports an immediate rollout to desktop users while maintaining the current experience for mobile users. The divergence is statistically clear: desktop engagement time rose 18% with +2.5 pages/session, indicating validated value delivery. Conversely, mobile engagement dropped 10% with a significant bounce rate increase, signaling a broken user experience on that surface.

**The Narrative for Stakeholders**
Frame this not as a "partial success" but as a **device-specific validation**. The hypothesis that "responsive design ensures consistency" was falsified; the data proves the layout interacts differently with touch inputs, screen real estate, or network constraints. Rolling out to all users would knowingly degrade the experience for the mobile cohort, violating the core principle of retaining existing value. A segmented rollout allows AquaFinance to capture the 18% desktop efficiency gain immediately without incurring the mobile retention penalty.

**Risk vs. Opportunity Analysis**
*   **Opportunity Cost of Delay:** Holding back the desktop release delays value realization for the majority of engaged users. Every week of delay forfeits the compounded benefit of higher engagement and potential conversion lifts associated with the +2.5 pages/session metric.
*   **Risk of Partial Rollout:** The primary risk is operational complexity (maintaining two code paths) and potential user confusion if devices switch frequently. However, these are manageable engineering costs compared to the certainty of mobile churn if the feature forces a 10% engagement drop. There is no evidence suggesting the desktop win is dependent on mobile parity.

**Immediate Next Steps (This Week)**
1.  **Execute Segmented Release:** Deploy the new layout to 100% of desktop traffic and 0% of mobile traffic by Thursday.
2.  **Isolate Mobile Diagnostics:** Launch a targeted investigation on mobile only. Instrument specific events to distinguish between *load performance* (time-to-interactive on cellular), *interaction friction* (tap targets vs. click areas), and *rendering issues* (chart complexity on small viewports).
3.  **Define Mobile Success Criteria:** Do not attempt a mobile fix until a new hypothesis is formed. The next mobile experiment must pre-commit to a primary metric (e.g., "mobile engagement time") and a guardrail (e.g., "bounce rate") with a fixed sample size, avoiding the "peeking" error that might have obscured early mobile signals in this test.

**Note:** This decision relies on the user's own baseline data; no external benchmarks were applied. The mobile hold is a protective measure until a new, device-specific hypothesis is tested with rigorous pre-commitment.
