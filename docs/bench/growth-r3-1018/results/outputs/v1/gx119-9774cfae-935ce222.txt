# Recommendation Engine Experiment: Impact Assessment & Decision Framework

**Executive Summary**
The four-week experiment (30,000 users) successfully increased cart volume (+5% items per cart) but failed to increase total value, resulting in a 3% decrease in Average Order Value (AOV). This indicates a shift toward lower-priced, impulse-buy titles rather than premium relevance. Compounding this, the return rate for recommended items rose by 2%. The current data confirms a negative revenue trend; the decision to ship, tweak, or stop depends entirely on the uncalculated net profit impact.

**Conflicting Outcomes Analysis**
*   **Volume vs. Value:** The engine is optimizing for quantity over quality. Users are adding more books, but they are cheaper books. The 5% volume gain did not offset the 3% price drop, implying the average price of added items fell significantly.
*   **Quality Signal:** The 2% increase in return rates suggests the "relevance" hypothesis is flawed. Users are buying impulsively and regretting it, or the recommendations are mismatched to long-term satisfaction.
*   **Margin Risk:** If the cheaper books have lower absolute margins, and returns are increasing (incurring reverse logistics and restocking costs), the experiment may be actively destroying profit despite higher engagement.

**Required Data Points for Net Profit Calculation**
To answer the director's question on whether volume compensates for margin erosion, we must calculate the **Net Profit Per Visitor (NPPV)** delta. Please provide or query the following specific figures from our analytics and finance systems:

1.  **Baseline vs. Treatment AOV:** Exact dollar values to quantify the revenue loss per order.
2.  **Category Margin Mix:** The average gross margin % of the "impulse" books added vs. the "premium" books previously purchased.
3.  **Return Cost Structure:** The fully loaded cost of a return (shipping + processing + depreciation) for the specific titles involved.
4.  **Conversion Rate Delta:** Did the 5% increase in items per cart come from more users converting, or the same users adding more? (This affects total transaction fee costs).

**Decision Logic & Next Steps**
Do not decide based on "relevance" vs. "price" yet. Decide based on the **Break-Even Volume Threshold**.
*   *Calculation:* Determine the exact increase in unit volume required to offset the combined loss of lower AOV and higher return costs.
*   *Verdict Rule:* If the observed 5% volume lift is below this calculated threshold, the result is a **NO-GO**. The algorithm is cannibalizing margin.
