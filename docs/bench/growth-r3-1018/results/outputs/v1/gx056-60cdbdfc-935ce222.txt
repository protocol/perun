**Subject:** Recommendation on Gamified Dashboard Experiment (Feb 1–14)

**Decision: Do not ship. Revert to control or iterate on Day-2+ engagement before re-testing.**

**Executive Summary**
The experiment indicates a **pure novelty effect**, not a structural improvement in habit formation. While Day-1 retention spiked 25%, the convergence of treatment and control groups at Day-7 (both at the 18% baseline) confirms the new dashboard drives curiosity but fails to alter the core value loop. There is no statistical evidence of "latent value" appearing later; if the design improved the underlying habit mechanism, the retention curves would have remained separated or converged at a higher floor.

**Analysis of Conflicting Signals**
1.  **The Spike (Day-1):** The 25% lift represents users reacting to visual novelty. This is a "look" metric, not a "stick" metric.
2.  **The Convergence (Day-7):** Retention is the ultimate validator of product-market fit for this feature. Since both groups settled at the historical 18% baseline by Day-7, the treatment provided zero incremental long-term value.
3.  **The Horizon Fallacy:** Extending the test duration will not reveal hidden value here. In habit-forming products, if a feature does not improve retention by Day-7, it rarely generates a delayed lift at Day-14 or Day-30 without further product changes. The "signal" has already flatlined against the baseline.

**Recommended Next Steps (Actionable by Wednesday)**

**Option A: Revert (Recommended)**
Roll back the gamified dashboard immediately. The cost of maintaining a complex UI that yields no long-term retention gain outweighs the temporary engagement spike.
*   *Trigger:* Confirmed Day-7 convergence at baseline.

**Option B: Isolate and Iterate (If design resources are available)**
If the team believes specific gamification elements (e.g., badges, progress bars) have merit, do not A/B test the whole dashboard again. Instead:
1.  **Instrument Specific Events:** Define events for "Gamification Interaction" (e.g., `badge_viewed`, `streak_checked`).
2.  **Segment Analysis:** Filter the treatment group to users who *actually* engaged with the gamification elements. Did *those* specific users retain better at Day-7?
    *   *If Yes:* The mechanic works, but the UI placement failed to drive adoption. Redesign for visibility.
    *   *If No:* The mechanic itself is irrelevant to retention. Kill the feature entirely.

**This Week's Critical Actions**
1.  **Query:** Run a cohort query comparing Day-7 retention of "Treatment Users who clicked gamification elements" vs. "Treatment Users who ignored them."
2.  **Decision Rule:** If the "engaged" subgroup does not show >20% Day-7 retention (a meaningful lift over the 18% baseline), terminate the project.
3.  **Guardrail Check:** Verify if the new dashboard increased load times or error rates (technical debt) which might be suppressing long-term gains. If technical overhead exists with zero retention lift, the revert is urgent.

**Conclusion**
The data speaks clearly: the new design is a "sugar rush," not a vitamin. We cannot ship a feature based on Day-1 vanity metrics when Day-7 reality matches the status quo. Reverting preserves engineering velocity for features that actually move the North Star.
