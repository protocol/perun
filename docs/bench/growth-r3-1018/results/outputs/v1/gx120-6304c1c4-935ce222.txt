### Executive Summary: Do Not Ship Based on December Data
The December test result (1% relative lift) is **inconclusive**, not a failure. The massive seasonal baseline shift (40% surge) likely compressed the variance in user behavior, making it statistically difficult to detect the treatment effect you expected. Because the test was likely not powered for this specific high-volume, low-variance holiday context, and because "holiday urgency" acts as a confounder that overrides onboarding friction, you cannot trust this data to prove the new flow is ineffective.

**Recommendation:** Revert to the old flow for now to ensure stability, but immediately launch a **re-test in January** with a pre-committed design to validate the true impact. Do not make a permanent decision based on the December read.

### Arguments: Why the December Data is Unreliable

**Against Trusting the Data (The Confounder Argument):**
1.  **Baseline Compression:** When external motivation (holiday gifting) is extremely high, users activate regardless of UX friction. This "ceiling effect" masks the value of a smoother onboarding flow. If 90% of users activate due to urgency, even a perfect flow can only improve activation by a maximum of 10 percentage points, shrinking the observable lift.
2.  **Noise vs. Signal:** The 40% surge indicates a regime change. Comparing December performance to November baselines violates the assumption of a stable environment required for valid A/B testing. The 1% difference is statistically indistinguishable from noise in this volatile period.
3.  **Risk of False Negative:** Concluding the flow is "ineffective" based on this test risks discarding a feature that works well in normal conditions. This is a Type II error caused by seasonal masking.

**For Trusting the Data (The Stress Test Argument):**
1.  **Robustness Check:** If the new flow was significantly *worse* than the old one, it might have shown a negative lift even during the holiday surge. The fact that it held parity (1% lift) suggests it is at least not broken.
2.  **Simplicity:** If the lift is truly only 1% in a high-traffic month, the ROI of maintaining a new complex flow might be too low to justify the engineering cost, regardless of seasonality.

### Validation Plan: The January Re-Test
To disentangle the seasonal effect from the treatment effect, you must run a new test in January when behavior normalizes.

1.  **Pre-commit the Design:** Before launching, calculate the required sample size based on a **normal January baseline** (not the inflated December numbers). Define the Minimum Detectable Effect (MDE) you care about (e.g., 5% lift).
2.  **Fixed Horizon or Sequential Rule:** Set a strict end date or use a sequential testing method (like mSPRT) to avoid "peeking" as data comes in. Do not stop early if you see a spike.
3.  **Guardrail Metrics:** Monitor cancellation rates or support tickets to ensure the new flow doesn't introduce friction that leads to churn later, even if activation looks good.
4.  **Cohort Analysis:** Compare January activation retention (Week 2 and Week 4) between control and variant. True value is shown in retained activation, not just the initial click.
