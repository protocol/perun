**1. The Economic Trade-off: Quantifying the Cost**
To decide by end-of-day, compare the daily cost of friction against the daily value of fraud prevented. Do not use industry benchmarks; use your specific operational costs.

*   **Cost of Friction (Daily):** Calculate `(300 tickets / 3 days) × Cost Per Ticket`.

    *   *Current Estimate:* If support is $15/ticket, friction costs **$1,500/day** purely in labor, excluding reputational damage.
*   **Value of Fraud Prevented (Daily):** The $50,000 saved occurred over 3 days.
    *   *Current Rate:* **~$16,667/day**.
*   **The Verdict Logic:** If the daily fraud prevention value ($16.6k) significantly exceeds the daily friction cost ($1.5k + churn risk), the model is economically viable *provided* the friction is fixable. If the friction causes high-value customer churn exceeding $15k/day, revert immediately.

**2. Actionable Segmentation for Tuning**
Your assumption regarding "unusual spending locations" must be verified before tuning. Segment the 100 daily false positives by these three dimensions to isolate the leak:

*   **Segment A: Trigger Attribute:** Break down false positives by the specific rule fired (e.g., "Location Mismatch," "Velocity Check," "Merchant Category").
    *   *Decision Rule:* If >60% of false positives stem from a single attribute (e.g., Location), tune only that threshold rather than reverting the whole model.
*   **Segment B: Customer Tenure:** Split false positives between "New Users (<30 days)" and "Established Users."
    *   *Decision Rule:* If established users are disproportionately flagged, the risk to retention is critical; consider a "whitelist" logic for long-tenure accounts immediately.
*   **Segment C: Transaction Value:** Group false positives into buckets (<$50, $50–$200, >$200).
    *   *Decision Rule:* If the model is flagging low-value transactions with high frequency, the cost of review outweighs the risk. Raise the threshold for low-value bands.
