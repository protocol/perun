**Primary Metric: 4-Week Repeat Booking Rate by Cohort**

For your advisory board and upcoming seed round, reject Gross Merchandise Value (GMV) as your primary health indicator. In a two-sided marketplace like PawsSchedule, GMV is a vanity metric in the early stages; it can be inflated by heavy marketing spend or one-off transactions while the underlying product fails to retain users. If you pour acquisition budget into a leaky bucket, GMV rises temporarily while the business rots.

Your assumption is correct: **Repeat Booking Rate** is the true North Star. It directly measures the core value proposition—trust and reliability in pet care. Investors in seed-stage marketplaces prioritize evidence of retention over raw volume because retention proves product-market fit and predicts sustainable unit economics.

**The Data Story for Your Pitch:**
1.  **Define the Core Action:** A "successful booking" where the service was completed without dispute.
2.  **Measure Retention, Not Cumulative Volume:** Report the percentage of users who book a second time within 30 days of their first booking. Break this down by **weekly cohorts** (e.g., "Of the 100 users who joined in Week 1, 35% booked again in Weeks 2–5").
3.  **The Narrative:** "While our GMV is growing, our repeat booking rate of [X]% demonstrates that once pet owners trust us, they stay. This validates our supply quality and reduces our long-term customer acquisition costs."

**Addressing the Unknowns (Seasonality):**
Do not normalize your data yet. With only 1,200 users and no prior summer data, any normalization factor would be an invented benchmark, which undermines credibility. Instead, treat the upcoming summer as a live experiment.
*   **Action:** Establish your current baseline repeat rate immediately.
*   **Monitor:** Track if the rate dips in June/July. A dip due to holidays is expected and explainable; a dip due to poor service is fatal.
*   **Pitch Angle:** Tell investors, "We are establishing our baseline now to explicitly measure the seasonality impact this summer, allowing us to model accurate LTV adjustments for our growth phase."

**Input Metrics to Move the North Star:**
To improve the Repeat Booking Rate, track these specific inputs:
*   **Walker Response Time:** Time from request to acceptance.
*   **First-Booking Success Rate:** Percentage of first bookings that are completed without cancellation.
*   **Post-Service Rating:** Average score immediately following the first walk.

**Decision Framework for the Advisory Board:**
*   **If Repeat Rate > 30%:** Double down on acquisition; the bucket is not leaking.
*   **If Repeat Rate < 15%:** Pause aggressive marketing. Investigate walker quality or pricing friction before spending more on ads.
*   **If GMV is up but Repeat Rate is flat/down:** You are buying temporary revenue, not building a business.
