**Executive Summary: Re-analysis Strategy for Referral Bonus Test**

The reported 25% lift is **UNVERIFIED** as a causal effect of the incentive due to confirmed network interference (spillover) and geographic clustering. The current read conflates the bonus efficacy with local density effects. Do not expand globally based on this data.

**Immediate Re-analysis Plan**

1.  **Redefine the Unit of Analysis (Cluster-Level Aggregation)**
    Stop analyzing at the individual user level. Aggregate data by **community clusters** (e.g., barangay or zip-code level) rather than individual assignment.
    *   *Action:* Classify each cluster as "High Treatment Density" (>50% treated), "Mixed," or "Control Dominant."
    *   *Metric:* Compare average transactions *per cluster* between High Density and Control Dominant groups. This isolates whether the *environment* drives growth, not just the individual incentive.

2.  **Quantify Spillover via Interference Modeling**
    To estimate the true causal impact, you must measure the "contamination" of the control group.
    *   *Action:* Segment the original **Control Group** into two sub-cohorts:
        *   **Exposed Control:** Control users with >X treated friends/neighbors.
        *   **Pure Control:** Control users with zero or minimal treated connections.
    *   *Read:* If "Exposed Control" outperforms "Pure Control," you have quantified the spillover. The true incremental lift of the bonus is likely lower than 25% because the control baseline was artificially inflated by nearby treated users.

3.  **Guardrail Check: Unit Economics by Density**
    Before deciding on global vs. urban rollout, verify margin impact.
    *   *Action:* Calculate **Cost Per Incremental Transaction** separately for Manila (high density) and dispersed regions.
    *   *Decision Rule:* If the cost per incremental transaction in dispersed areas exceeds the lifetime value (LTV) margin, restrict the program to high-density urban markets. Do not subsidize low-density referrals where the network effect cannot sustain itself.

**Decision Framework for Head of Growth**

*   **Scenario A (Spillover Confirmed):** If "Exposed Control" shows significant lift, the bonus works primarily via network effects.
    *   *Call:* **Restrict to High-Density Markets.** Global rollout to dispersed areas will fail to trigger the feedback loop and will burn cash without organic amplification.
*   **Scenario B (No Spillover, Pure Lift):** If "Pure Control" and "Exposed Control" perform identically, and the lift is purely within the treated group.
    *   *Call:* **Proceed with Caution.** The incentive works individually, but re-run a geo-stratified test to confirm scalability before global commitment.
