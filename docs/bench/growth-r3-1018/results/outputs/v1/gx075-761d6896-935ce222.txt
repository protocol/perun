### Experiment Read-Out: Tooltip Frequency Test (Feb 10–24)

**Verdict: Do not ship globally. Extend analysis to define a "Power User" guardrail before the March 1st decision.**

The current result is **inconclusive for a global launch** because a critical guardrail metric (power user satisfaction/retention) has failed, even though the primary metric (support ticket volume) improved. A "win" on support costs that risks churn in your top 10% revenue segment is a net loss if the lifetime value of those users exceeds the saved support cost.

**1. Recompute the Trade-Off (The Money Question)**
Before measuring further, quantify the break-even point using your own data:
*   **Gain:** Calculate the exact cost saved from the drop in support tickets during the test window.
*   **Risk:** Estimate the revenue at risk if even 5% of the complaining power users churn.
*   **Decision Rule:** If the potential revenue loss from a 5% churn of power users exceeds the annualized support savings, the global change is financially unsafe. Given that power users are "most valuable," the threshold for annoyance is likely lower than the aggregate average suggests.

**2. Required Segmentation Analysis**
You cannot analyze this on aggregate. You must split the data immediately:
*   **Cohort A (Power Users):** Define this not just by self-reporting, but by behavior (e.g., sessions >30 mins, usage of advanced features, or historical ticket volume).
*   **Cohort B (Novice/Standard Users):** The remaining 90%.
*   **Metric to Watch:** Compare the *change* in "Core Action Completion Time" and "Session Duration" between Cohort A and Cohort B.
    *   If Cohort A's completion time increased significantly while Cohort B's decreased or stayed flat, you have confirmed the friction is isolated to experts.
    *   If *both* cohorts slowed down, the feature is universally inefficient, and the support drop might be due to users giving up rather than learning.

**3. The Tipping Point Metrics**
To find the annoyance threshold, instrument these specific events for the next iteration:
*   **"Tooltip Dismiss Rate":** Track how quickly users close the tooltip. A sub-200ms dismiss indicates immediate recognition of noise.
*   **"Hover-to-Click Ratio":** If users hover over an icon but *do not click* after a tooltip appears (where they previously did), the tooltip is interrupting flow.
*   **"Feature Adoption Lag":** For power users, measure the time to first use of a complex feature post-login. An increase here signals workflow disruption.

**4. Recommended Path Forward (By March 1st)**
Do not choose between "all on" or "all off." The data supports a segmented rollout:
*   **Action 1:** Implement a logic gate that suppresses tooltips for users meeting "Power User" criteria (e.g., >50 hours lifetime usage or specific feature flags).
*   **Action 2:** Run a follow-up A/B test *only* on the novice segment to optimize frequency without harming experts.
*   **Action 3:** Set a hard guardrail: If power user retention drops by >1% in any weekly cohort after launch, the feature auto-reverts.
