**To:** Elena, Chief Product Officer
**From:** UX Lead
**Subject:** Q2 Navigation Test Analysis & Recommendation

**Executive Summary**
The redesigned navigation successfully increased discovery (CTR +15%) but failed to convert that interest into revenue, causing an 8% drop in overall booking completion. With an average booking value of $1,200, this decline represents a significant revenue leak that outweighs the benefit of increased page views. The data indicates the new menu is driving low-intent traffic deeper into the funnel where they encounter friction or lose focus, rather than guiding high-intent users to checkout.

**Funnel Dissection Strategy**
To pinpoint the breakdown, we must immediately segment the April 5–19 data by the following three dimensions:

1.  **Intent vs. Exploration Cohorts:** Separate users who landed on destination pages via the new menu from those who arrived via direct search or external links. We hypothesize the "menu-click" cohort has a significantly lower conversion rate than the baseline, diluting the overall metric. If the direct-search cohort remains stable, the issue is traffic quality, not downstream friction.
2.  **Drop-off Localization:** Map the exact step where the divergence occurs post-click. Does the drop happen on the destination landing page (suggesting a mismatch between menu promise and page content), during date selection (suggesting UI confusion), or at the payment gateway? An 8% global drop usually masks a 20–30% collapse at a single specific step.
3.  **Device & Path Complexity:** Analyze if the drop-off is concentrated on mobile devices where screen real estate is limited, or among users who visited more than three pages before attempting to book. High page counts combined with low conversion suggest users are getting lost in the new structure.

**Recommendation: Do Not Ship; Iterate with Guardrails**
We should **abandon the current design for full rollout** but retain the learnings for a targeted iteration. Increasing top-of-funnel noise that degrades bottom-line revenue is unsustainable.

**Immediate Next Steps (This Week)**
1.  **Rollback or Limit Exposure:** Revert the navigation to the control version for 100% of traffic to stop the revenue bleed, or limit the new design to a "exploration-only" segment if a full rollback is technically blocked.
2.  **Define the "Booking Start" Event:** Instrument a specific event tracking the transition from "Destination View" to "Date Selection." This isolates whether the friction is inspirational (content) or transactional (UI).
3.  **Run a Guardrailed Follow-up Test:** Design a variant that increases destination visibility *only* for users who have already demonstrated high intent (e.g., previous searchers), using **booking completion** as the primary metric and **CTR** as a secondary guardrail. Pre-commit to a sample size of 60,000 users per variant and read the results only once at the conclusion to avoid peeking bias.

The goal for Q2 is not just clicks, but completed bookings. We must optimize for the transaction, not the browse.

***

*Note: All metrics cited are derived strictly from the provided test window (April 5–19); no external benchmarks were applied. The financial impact calculation assumes the 8% drop applies directly to the converted volume of the test group.*
