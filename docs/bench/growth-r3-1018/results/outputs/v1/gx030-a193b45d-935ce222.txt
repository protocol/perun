**Recommendation: Ship Variant B as the Default; Route "Quick-Action" Users to Variant A Logic**

**Decision:** Prioritize **Variant B** as the primary homepage experience. Despite the 30-second reduction in engagement time, the **8% lift in booking completions** is the only metric that directly correlates to revenue and Customer Lifetime Value (CLV). The 12% CTR increase in Variant A is a vanity metric in this context; it drives users to the search bar but fails to convert them, indicating a "leaky bucket" where initial interest does not translate to value. Optimizing solely for the click (Variant A) risks inflating acquisition costs while depressing actual business growth.

**The Hybrid Strategy (Personalization):**
Do not alienate the "inspiration" seekers who thrived on Variant A's layout. Instead of a static A/B winner, implement a **segmented entry point**:
1.  **Default State:** Serve Variant B to all traffic to capture the aggregate 8% revenue lift.
2.  **Intent Signal:** Detect "quick answer" intent via entry source (e.g., direct URL with specific destination params, returning users with recent search history, or paid search ads with high-intent keywords).
3.  **Dynamic Swap:** For these high-intent segments, serve the Variant A layout (prominent search bar) immediately.

**Weighing the Trade-offs:**
*   **Engagement Time vs. Revenue:** The 30-second drop in Variant B is likely a efficiency gain, not a loss. Users are finding what they need faster and booking. In travel, frictionless booking often reduces "browsing" time. Do not optimize for time-on-site unless it correlates with conversion; here, it inversely correlates.
*   **CLV Impact:** Optimizing for the initial click (Variant A) creates a false positive. Users click, get overwhelmed or distracted in the search flow, and drop off. This damages CLV by training users that the site is hard to book on. Variant B's focus on completion builds trust and habit.
