**Subject:** Q2 Inventory Strategy – Churn Root Cause Analysis

**Recommendation:** Segment churn by **cancellation feedback category** first, then overlay **customer tenure**. Do not rely on tenure alone. Tenure identifies *who* left (e.g., "holiday one-timers"), but feedback categories identify *why* they left (price vs. quality). Only the "why" dictates whether you adjust inventory purchasing (product fix) or marketing expectations (seasonal fix).

**Analysis Plan:**
1.  **Isolate the "Quality" Signal:** Filter the 14% cancellation rate for users selecting "product quality," "missing items," or "didn't like snacks."
    *   If this specific segment spiked significantly above the November baseline (e.g., from 1% to 5%), the issue is **product curation**. The "gift edition" likely set an unsustainable expectation, or January SKUs were genuinely weaker.
    *   *Action for Q2:* Adjust inventory purchasing immediately. Reduce volume of low-rated January SKUs and re-evaluate supplier quality before committing to Q2 orders.

2.  **Isolate the "Budget" Signal:** Filter for users selecting "too expensive," "financial reasons," or "pausing subscription."
    *   If this segment accounts for the majority of the increase while quality complaints remain flat at baseline levels, the issue is **seasonal budget tightening**.
    *   *Action for Q2:* Maintain current inventory levels but adjust marketing. Prepare retention offers (discounts/pauses) for next January rather than cutting product costs.

3.  **The Tenure Overlay:** Once segmented by reason, split by tenure (Pre-Holiday vs. Holiday Acquisition).
    *   If "Budget" churn is concentrated in **Holiday Acquisition** users, your assumption is correct: these were low-intent leads.
    *   If "Quality" churn is high among **Pre-Holiday** loyalists, you have a critical retention crisis unrelated to seasonality. These users have a baseline for comparison and are voting with their wallets on product merit.

**Decision Matrix for Q2 Inventory:**
*   **Scenario A (Product Failure):** Quality-related churn > 4% (approx. 1/3 of total churn). **Verdict:** Fix product. Do not cut inventory volume yet, but swap out underperforming SKUs identified in feedback. Risk of cutting inventory here is starving a fixable product.
*   **Scenario B (Seasonal/Budget):** Budget-related churn > 8% AND Quality churn remains at baseline (~1%). **Verdict:** Adjust marketing expectations. Proceed with Q2 inventory planning based on standard non-holiday trends; do not over-correct purchasing based on price-sensitive churn.
*   **Scenario C (Mixed):** Both segments elevated. **Verdict:** Conservative purchasing. Reduce Q2 order volume by the percentage of churn attributed to budget, but mandate a quality review for the remaining inventory mix.
