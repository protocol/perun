**Recommendation to Product Council: Ship with Guardrails, Do Not Extend**

**Decision:** Ship the collaboration feature to 100% of users immediately. Do not extend the A/B test duration.

**Rationale:**
Extending the test is not a viable strategy. With a plateaued user base and only 400 enrolled users over six weeks, achieving statistical significance (p < 0.05) for a 12% lift would require a sample size likely exceeding your total addressable market or an unrealistic timeframe. The opportunity cost of delaying the roadmap for another month to chase a p-value that may never arrive outweighs the risk of shipping a neutral feature.

While the frequentist result is "inconclusive" (p=0.14), the observed 12% lift represents your best current estimate of reality. In a low-traffic environment, waiting for "certainty" guarantees stagnation. The risk of shipping a feature that provides zero value is low (assuming no negative performance impact), whereas the risk of *not* shipping a feature that actually improves task completion by 12% is a guaranteed loss of potential retention and revenue.

**Reframing the Trade-offs:**
*   **Risk of Shipping:** You deploy a feature that users ignore. Cost: Minor development maintenance and potential UI clutter. Mitigation: The feature can be hidden or removed in a future sprint if adoption metrics flatline post-launch.
*   **Risk of Waiting:** You delay the roadmap for a month+ to potentially confirm a 12% gain that you already suspect exists. Cost: Lost opportunity to iterate on other high-value features, demoralized team, and stalled growth.

**Using Bayesian Thinking (Conceptual):**
Instead of asking "Is this result statistically significant?", ask "What is the probability the lift is greater than 0%?" Given a 12% observed lift, even with wide confidence intervals, the probability that the true effect is positive is likely high (e.g., >80%). Bayesian analysis favors acting on the most probable outcome when the cost of being wrong is low and the cost of waiting is high. You do not need complex modeling to see that the weight of evidence leans toward a positive effect.

**Integrating Qualitative Feedback:**
Since quantitative power is low, triangulate with qualitative data before the meeting:
1.  **User Interviews:** Did the 400 test users who saw the feature mention it in support tickets or interviews? Did they describe it as "helpful"?
2.  **Usage Depth:** Even if the overall completion rate lift isn't significant, did the *subset* of users who actually clicked the collaboration button show higher retention? If the "activated" users love it, the problem is discovery, not value.

**Implementation Plan & Guardrails:**
1.  **Ship Now:** Release to all users.
2.  **Define Success Post-Launch:** Monitor the **Task Completion Rate** and **Weekly Active Users** for the next 4 weeks.
3.  **Kill Switch (Guardrail):** If the global task completion rate drops by >2% compared to the pre-launch baseline after 4 weeks, roll back the feature. This protects against the small chance the 12% lift was noise and the true effect is negative.
4.  **Iterate:** If the metric holds or improves, invest in better onboarding to drive adoption of the new feature.

**Conclusion:**
In low-traffic environments, "statistical significance" is often a luxury you cannot afford. Trust the direction of the signal (12% up), mitigate the downside with a rollback plan, and prioritize speed of learning over false precision.

*Note: Ensure the "12% improvement" is calculated on the core action (task completion) and not a vanity metric like pageviews. If the feature introduces significant load latency, that becomes a hard guardrail against shipping regardless of the lift.*
