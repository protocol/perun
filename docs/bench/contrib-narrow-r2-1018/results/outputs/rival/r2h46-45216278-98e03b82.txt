Do not disclose your employment, Helios Technologies' sponsorship, or your company's specific infrastructure dependence in the pull request description.

In open-source norms, the merit of a contribution is judged strictly on the code quality, the correctness of the fix, and the robustness of the accompanying tests. Introducing corporate identity or financial status into a technical PR is generally viewed as irrelevant noise at best, and as an attempt to exert undue influence or "buy" priority at worst. Maintainers prioritize bugs based on severity and reproducibility, not the employer of the contributor. Since the project's `CONTRIBUTING.md` does not require this disclosure, adding it voluntarily violates the principle of minimal scope and introduces non-technical variables into a technical review.

Your manager's advice to maintain a low profile aligns with standard operational security practices. Revealing that your entire infrastructure relies on a specific library, or that you are a major sponsor, can inadvertently signal attack vectors or create expectations of private support channels that do not exist. The fact that you tested this in a staging environment is sufficient evidence of validity; the specific identity of the environment owner is not required for the maintainer to trust the fix.

If you wish to acknowledge Helios Technologies' support, do so through the appropriate non-code channels, such as the project's official sponsorship page, a separate discussion thread about community support, or by ensuring your company's logo is listed on the project website if that is part of your sponsorship agreement. Keep the pull request focused entirely on the bug fix: the problem (log rotation failure on full disk), the solution (the code diff), and the verification (test results). This approach ensures the PR is evaluated on its technical merits while respecting your company's security posture and adhering to open-source etiquette.

Note: If the maintainers explicitly ask about the testing environment during review, you may state it was tested in a "production-like staging environment" without naming the company.
