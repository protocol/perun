Instead, reply to the maintainers immediately with the following message:

> "Thank you for the approval. Regarding the CLA: My contribution is made on behalf of my employer, OmniSoft. Our internal policy requires legal review of specific CLA terms before I can formally declare our corporate identity in your system. I have initiated this review internally. In the interim, can I sign the CLA as an individual contributor for this specific change, or should I pause the merge process until our legal team provides clearance? I want to ensure full compliance with both your project's requirements and my employer's policies."

Most well-maintained open-source projects have a mechanism for "Individual" vs. "Corporate" CLAs. If the project allows you to sign as an individual, you can often proceed immediately, provided the change is small enough that you personally own the intellectual property (or your employment contract assigns minor contributions to you without needing a specific corporate signature per project). However, if the project strictly requires a corporate CLA for any employee contribution, you must wait. Do not attempt to bypass the form by using a personal name if your employment contract states that all work product belongs to OmniSoft; this could constitute fraud or a breach of your employment agreement.

The safest path is transparency: inform the maintainers of the internal review requirement. Maintainers generally prefer a short delay over accepting a contribution with invalid or unauthorized legal assertions. If the legal review takes too long, you can always ask the maintainers if they would accept the fix as a "patch" from you personally (if legally permissible under your contract) while the corporate process finishes, though this depends entirely on the project's specific licensing strictness.

Note: Check your OmniSoft employment contract specifically for clauses regarding "open source contributions" to see if you have pre-authorized rights for minor fixes.
