### Recommended Action Plan

**1. Do Not Submit a PR Yet**
Adhere strictly to the project's `CONTRIBUTING.md`. Submitting a PR without a linked issue when the rules explicitly forbid it will likely result in an immediate closure, damaging your credibility and reducing the chances of future engagement. The rule exists to force discussion before code; bypassing it signals that you do not respect the maintainer's workflow.

**2. Send a Neutral, Time-Bound Follow-up Comment**
Post a new comment on the existing issue. Do not mention "production stability," "DataFlow Systems," or "audit trail gaps." Instead, frame the follow-up around technical clarity and willingness to help. Use this template:

> "Hi team, following up on this after a month. We've encountered this behavior in a high-throughput setup and have a minimal fix ready that aligns with the project's architecture.
>
> Before I open a PR, could you confirm if this issue is still on your roadmap or if you need any additional reproduction steps from me? I want to ensure my proposed fix aligns with your current priorities before spending cycle time on formal submission. Thanks!"

**Why this works:** It respects the "issue-first" rule, offers value (a ready fix), and asks a specific question that requires a yes/no answer, making it easier for a busy maintainer to reply.

**3. Escalate via Alternative Channels (If no response in 7 days)**
If the follow-up yields no result, look for the project's communication channels listed in their README or website (e.g., Slack, Discord, Matrix, or a mailing list).
*   Join the channel.
*   Post a brief message: "Hi, I opened issue #<Number> regarding log drops in high-throughput scenarios. I have a fix ready but wanted to check if there's any context I'm missing before submitting the PR. Is there a specific maintainer I should tag?"
*   This moves the conversation to a real-time medium without revealing proprietary details.

**4. The "Fork-and-Maintain" Contingency**
If silence persists after the alternative channel attempt, you must prioritize your own production stability.
*   Fork the repository.
*   Apply your fix in your private fork.
*   Deploy the fix from your fork to your infrastructure.
*   Continue to monitor the upstream project. If they eventually respond, you can then offer your PR. If they never respond, you maintain your own patched version until the upstream project becomes responsive or you migrate to a different solution.

**Confidentiality Note:** At no point should you disclose *how* you are currently mitigating the issue internally or the specific business impact. Keep the narrative focused on the technical bug and the desire to contribute a standard fix.

**Note:** Maintainer silence often indicates resource constraints, not rejection. Patience combined with a low-friction offer of help is usually the most effective strategy.
**Note:** If the project remains unresponsive for another month while you rely on a forked fix, consider evaluating alternative logging frameworks that offer active maintenance guarantees.
