Do not force push to the main repository. Force pushing to a shared remote where you lack explicit maintainer rights is a severe violation of open-source norms, effectively an act of aggression that will likely get your access revoked and your contribution rejected. It does not "get attention"; it destroys trust.

Since the maintainers are on an indefinite break and the project appears abandoned, you must treat it as such. The professional path forward involves these steps:

1.  **Fork Immediately:** Create a fork under your company's organization or your own namespace. This is the standard, respectful mechanism for continuing work on inactive projects. It does not violate licensing; standard open-source licenses (MIT, Apache, BSD) explicitly permit forking and private use. If your licensing constraint refers to *copyleft* concerns (e.g., GPL), consult your legal team, but forking itself is rarely the legal blocker—distribution is.
2.  **Apply and Document the Fix:** Apply your null-pointer check in the fork. Ensure your commit message is generic and privacy-safe. Do not mention "Acme Corp," "Northwind," specific data structures, or your Tuesday deadline. Instead, write: "Fix null pointer exception when processing legacy data files with missing fields."
3.  **Leave a Clear Signal Upstream:** Open a single, polite Issue on the original repository (do not open a PR if they are unresponsive to issues). State clearly: "We have encountered a critical bug blocking production use. As there has been no activity for six months, we have forked the project to apply a fix internally. We are happy to upstream this change if maintenance resumes." This respects the community norms by announcing your intent without demanding immediate action.
4.  **Justify the Fork to Management:** Explain to management that forking is the industry-standard solution for abandoned dependencies. It isolates your critical fix, ensures release stability, and protects the company from future upstream breakage. Maintaining a private version of a small, stable library is often less costly than the risk of a blocked release.
5.  **Plan for Long-Term Maintenance:** If this library is critical, your team effectively becomes the maintainer. Consider renaming the package (e.g., `northwind-analytics-active`) if you intend to publish it, to avoid confusion with the original abandoned artifact.

**Note:** Never include internal deadlines or customer names in public artifacts; treat all external communication as permanent and public. If the license is strictly copyleft and you cannot distribute your changes, you may need to isolate the fix in a proprietary wrapper rather than modifying the library directly.
