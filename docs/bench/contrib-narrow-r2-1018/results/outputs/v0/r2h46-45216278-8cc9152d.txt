You should disclose your affiliation with Helios Technologies in the pull request description, but you can do so in a way that balances transparency with operational security.

**Recommended Approach:**
State clearly that you are an engineer at Helios Technologies and that your team uses OpenLog in production. You may optionally mention that Helios is a financial sponsor if that fact is already public knowledge (e.g., listed on the project's website or sponsor page). However, you should **not** disclose specific details about your infrastructure, the criticality of the fix to your operations, or internal testing environments beyond stating that the fix was validated in a staging environment.

**Why this is the norm:**
1.  **Transparency builds trust:** Maintainers appreciate knowing the context of a contribution. Knowing a fix comes from a production environment adds weight to the testing claims. Hiding corporate affiliation can sometimes backfire if discovered later, appearing as an attempt to mask influence rather than exercise discretion.
2.  **Sponsorship is not "buying influence":** If Helios is already a public sponsor, mentioning it is simply stating a fact. It signals that your company has a vested interest in the project's health, which aligns with the maintainers' goals. It does not obligate them to merge your code; the technical merit of the fix remains the primary criterion.
3.  **Operational Security (OpSec):** Your manager's concern about revealing "heavy dependence" is valid regarding *how* you use the tool, not *that* you use it. You can say, "We use OpenLog for our infrastructure monitoring," without detailing stack architecture, load volumes, or specific failure scenarios that might expose vulnerabilities. The fact that a disk-full condition causes a crash is a general software behavior, not a secret about Helios specifically.

**Draft Statement for your PR:**
> "I am a software engineer at Helios Technologies, where we rely on OpenLog for our infrastructure monitoring. [Optional: Helios is a financial sponsor of this project.] This fix addresses a log rotation failure when disks are nearly full, a scenario we encountered and validated in our staging environment. We hope this contribution benefits the wider community as well."

This approach satisfies the ethical norm of disclosure without compromising your specific operational details. It frames the contribution as a community improvement derived from real-world use, rather than a transactional demand based on sponsorship.

Note: If Helios's sponsorship is not publicly listed anywhere, you might choose to omit the specific mention of "financial sponsor" and simply state your employment and usage, as the act of contributing code is itself a form of support.
