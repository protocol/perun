**Recommended Initial Step: Private Disclosure via Security Email**

The safest and most responsible initial step is to report the issue privately to the maintainers using the security email address listed in the readme. Do not open a public issue yet.

**Reasoning:**
1.  **Risk Mitigation:** Since the issue involves cryptographic weakness ("weak output"), there is a non-zero probability it is a critical vulnerability. Public disclosure before a fix is available exposes all users to potential exploitation. Private disclosure eliminates this risk during the assessment phase.
2.  **Standard Practice:** In the security industry, any ambiguity regarding severity defaults to private reporting. Maintainers prefer to triage potential vulnerabilities privately rather than have them exposed publicly prematurely.
3.  **Intellectual Property Protection:** Reporting privately allows you to share the necessary technical details with the maintainers under an implicit expectation of confidentiality, without broadcasting your audit methods or specific findings to competitors before the maintainers have acknowledged the report.

**How to Proceed Without Revealing Confidential Details:**

Draft a concise email to the security contact that includes:
*   **Subject Line:** "Potential Security Vulnerability in SecureHash - [Your Name/Organization]"
*   **The Component:** Specify the library name and version.
*   **The Trigger:** Mention that a specific, rarely used configuration (128-bit key size in a specific mode) produces unexpected results.
*   **The Impact:** State that preliminary analysis suggests the output may be weaker than expected, but do not include the mathematical proof, the specific nature of the weakness, or your audit methodology.
*   **The Request:** Ask for a secure channel (such as PGP encryption or a private repository invitation) to share the full proof-of-concept and technical details for their triage.

This approach allows the maintainers to assess the severity. If they determine it is a low-severity bug, they can invite you to move the discussion to a public tracker. If they confirm it is a vulnerability, you have already initiated the responsible disclosure process, protecting your clients and allowing time for a patch.

**Note:** Withhold the specific cryptographic flaw and your internal audit process until a secure channel is established.
**Note:** If the maintainers do not respond within a reasonable timeframe (e.g., 7 days), a follow-up email is appropriate before considering other actions.
