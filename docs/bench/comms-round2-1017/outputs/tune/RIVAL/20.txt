**Executive Summary: Warehouse Automation Pilot Recommendation**

The Q3 pilot at the Columbus center confirms the Acme Robotics system delivers significant operational gains but carries financial risk if volume targets are missed. Throughput increased 62.7% to 231 picks/hour, error rates dropped to 0.3%, and per-pick costs fell 33%. However, system uptime reached only 94.2%, missing the 98% target due to mechanical jams and integration errors.

The financial case hinges entirely on sales performance. Without volume growth, the full $5.48M rollout yields a weak 9.1-year payback based on labor savings alone. If the system enables a 15% increase in order capacity as projected, incremental revenue of ~$1.8M/year accelerates payback to 2.7 years.

**Recommendations**

1.  **Do not approve full rollout yet.** The uptime shortfall and unverified volume growth assumption make a $4.3M additional commitment premature.
2.  **Adopt Option 2 (Phased Rollout).** Deploy to two additional zones in Q1 2025. This limits exposure while validating whether mechanical reliability scales and if sales can generate the required 15% volume lift.
3.  **Decline the 3-year vendor agreement for now.** While the $320k discount is attractive, the mandatory three-year maintenance term creates unacceptable risk given the current 94.2% uptime and proprietary lock-in concerns. Negotiate a one-year term aligned with the phased evaluation.

**Decision Required:** Approve the phased rollout (2 zones) and authorize legal to negotiate a short-term vendor contract. Re-evaluate full deployment in Q2 2025 based on verified uptime data and actual order growth.

Note: Union contract renewal in Q2 2025 requires early engagement regarding automation provisions.
The $1.2M pilot investment is sunk; the decision rests on the marginal return of the next $4.3M.
