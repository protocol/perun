**You cannot claim "zero harm" or "completely fair," and you must not print an error rate of "effectively zero" on your landing page.** Doing so would be a fabrication of metrics you have not measured and a certification of safety that no test can prove. Given your reliance on flawed social graph data and the high stakes of denying credit to unbanked residents, proceeding with full automation as planned creates an unacceptable risk of systematic bias against tight-knit communities.

**Immediate Action Plan (Within Your Constraints):**

1.  **Stop the "Zero Harm" Claim:** Immediately discard the goal of certifying the system as unbiased. No amount of internal testing with three engineers in two weeks can prove a negative (zero error). Instead, prepare a transparency statement acknowledging the system is experimental, intended for pilot learning, and subject to human review.
2.  **Implement a Human-in-the-Loop Gate:** You cannot afford a full development cycle, but you can afford to change the deployment logic. **Do not auto-reject.** Modify the system so that any application flagged for rejection based on "irregular social graph patterns" is routed to a manual review queue or requires explicit senior engineer confirmation before a final decision is sent. Automated *approval* for low-risk profiles may proceed, but automated *rejection* of high-stakes applications (denying capital) must be gated. This prevents irreversible harm while you gather data.
3.  **Create a Minimal Harm Inventory:** Before launch, document the specific risk: "The model may systematically reject qualified applicants from tight-knit communities due to flawed social graph data." 4.  **Run a Targeted Red-Team Eval:** With your three engineers, spend two days constructing a specific test set:

    *   **Negative Controls:** Known unqualified applicants.
    *   **Probe:** Run these through the current logic. If the system rejects your positive controls, you have empirical proof of the bias.
