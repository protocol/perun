### Immediate Safety Assessment Strategy for Automated Claims System

**1. Harm Inventory (MAP)**
Before processing any real claims, formally document the specific risks your system introduces:
*   **Bias/Unfair Outcomes:** The model systematically denies valid claims for homeowners in regions experiencing new climate patterns not present in historical training data.
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** The system presents a denial as an objective "fact" based on outdated criteria, masking the uncertainty caused by climate shifts.
*   **Unsafe High-Stakes Automation:** Fully autonomous denial of financial payouts without human review constitutes an irreversible action with severe customer impact.
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Customers are not informed that an AI model, potentially limited by historical data, made the initial determination.

**2. Measurement Plan (MEASURE)**
Do not assert safety; test for failure immediately using your existing data and team:
*   **Adversarial Probe Set:** Construct a test batch of 50–100 synthetic claims representing "new type" storm scenarios (e.g., unprecedented rainfall intensity, novel wind patterns) that are *known* to be valid losses but likely mismatch your historical model.
*   **Controls:** Include known-good (clearly valid legacy storms) and known-bad (clear fraud) cases to baseline performance.
*   **Metric & Threshold:** Measure the **False Denial Rate** specifically on the "new storm" subset. Given the high stakes, propose a **zero-tolerance threshold** for autonomous denials in this category until verified.
*   **Stop Condition:** If the model denies >0% of the synthetic "new storm" valid claims, **block autonomous launch** for those claim types.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Draft a preliminary **Model Card** documenting the intended use, the limitation of historical weather data, and the specific "UNVERIFIED" status of performance on recent climate shifts. Do not fabricate an accuracy percentage.

**3. Risk Management & Mitigation (MANAGE)**
Given the regulatory deadline and the identified risk of wrongful denials, implement these controls immediately:
*   **Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Gate (Critical):** **Do not auto-deny any claim** flagged by the model as "climate-disaster related" or any claim where the storm type falls outside the historical training window. Route these 100% to human adjusters for final review. This satisfies the requirement to prevent irreversible harm while meeting your filing deadline.
*   **Defined Uncertainty Tiers:** Instead of showing customers a raw model score, classify decisions as **"Confirmed"** (matches historical patterns) or **"Under Review"** (novel patterns). Explicitly define these tiers internally so staff know which claims require manual intervention.
*   **Disclosure:** Include a clear statement in denial letters: *"This initial determination was assisted by an automated model trained on historical data. Due to evolving climate patterns, your claim has been flagged for mandatory human review."*
*   **Ownership:** Assign a specific team member as the owner of this risk. They must re-evaluate the model's performance weekly as new real-world data arrives.

**4. Governance & Legal Routing**
*   **Regulatory Duty:** Whether this automated decision-making triggers specific disclosure requirements under Florida insurance law or emerging AI regulations (e.g., EU AI Act implications if operating globally) is a legal determination. **Route this immediately to counsel** alongside your Model Card draft. Do not assume compliance; ask counsel to verify the duty.
*   **No Certification:** This strategy does not certify the system as "safe" or "unbiased." It provides a controlled rollout plan that isolates the highest risk (wrongful denial due to outdated data) behind a human gate.

**Note:** The tight deadline necessitates the HITL gate; attempting to retrain the model on new climate data before launch is likely infeasible and risky.
**Note:** Ensure your "Model Card" explicitly marks performance on recent climate shifts as "UNVERIFIED" rather than inventing a confidence metric.
