I cannot confirm that your current threshold settings guarantee the system is "completely fair" or that it will not wrongly block innocent drivers. No test or threshold can prove a system is unbiased or error-free, and asserting such a guarantee would be a fabrication.

Instead, here is the immediate plan to manage this risk within your three-week constraint and legacy infrastructure limits:

**1. Harm Inventory (MAP)**
*   **Bias/Unfair Outcomes:** The model may disproportionately flag drivers from specific São Paulo neighborhoods or shifts due to historical data gaps, not actual fraud.
*   **Unsafe High-Stakes Automation:** Automatically blocking an account destroys a driver's daily income. This is an irreversible, high-cost action.
*   **Over-reliance:** Operations staff may trust the "suspicious" label without investigating context.

**2. Measurement Plan (MEASURE)**
You must run these evaluations on your legacy database before launch; do not rely on aggregate accuracy alone:
*   **Probe Set:** Slice your beta data by neighborhood (e.g., comparing periphery vs. central zones) and time of day.
*   **Controls:** Inject known-good driver patterns (negative controls) and known-fraud rings (positive controls) across these slices.
*   **Metric:** Measure the false-positive rate *per subgroup*, not just overall.
*   **Threshold:** Set a stop condition: If the false-positive rate for any specific neighborhood exceeds a defined tolerance (e.g., 2x the baseline), the automatic blocking feature must not launch.
*   **Status:** Until these tests run, your fairness metric is **UNVERIFIED**.

**3. Risk Management (MANAGE)**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Critical):** Given the high stakes (loss of livelihood), you cannot fully automate account blocking at launch. The system should flag accounts as "Suspicious – Review Required" rather than "Blocked." A human operator must verify the pattern before the block executes. Full autonomous blocking of income-generating accounts is the failure mode this review prevents.
*   **Uncertainty Disclosure:** Do not show drivers a raw confidence score (e.g., "87% fraud risk"). Instead, use defined tiers like "Confirmed Anomaly" (multiple signals) vs. "Single Signal – Unverified."
*   **Appeal Mechanism:** Ensure an immediate, low-friction path for drivers to contest a block and recover earnings if the review was erroneous.

**4. Governance**
*   **Owner:** Assign one person responsible for reviewing blocked accounts and the bias metrics weekly.
*   **Transparency:** Draft a simple "Model Card" for your internal team detailing intended use (fraud detection), limitations (potential bias in specific zones), and the fact that human review is required.

**Constraint Adherence:**
This plan requires no cloud migration. It uses your existing database for slicing and testing. It meets the three-week deadline by prioritizing the human gate and targeted subgroup testing over a full retraining pipeline. If you cannot implement the human review gate in time, the recommendation is to delay the automatic blocking feature, not to remove the gate.

The decision to ship with residual risk rests with you, but it must be an informed decision based on measured subgroup performance, not an unproven claim of fairness.
