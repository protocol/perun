**1. Immediate Control Change: Human-in-the-Loop Gate**
The proposed "automated stop command" constitutes a high-stakes, irreversible action with severe safety and operational consequences. Fully automating this decision is the primary failure mode to prevent.
*   **Action:** Modify the logic so the AI issues a **"Stop Recommendation"** rather than executing a stop. The system must require explicit confirmation from the crane operator or site manager before halting operations.
*   **Rationale:** Even with correct code, AI can hallucinate sensor data or misinterpret edge cases (e.g., gusts vs. sustained wind). Automation bias may cause operators to trust a false positive, halting critical lifts unnecessarily, or a false negative could lead to catastrophe. The human operator remains the final authority.

**2. Replace Raw Precision with Defined Confidence Tiers**
Do not display raw wind speed probabilities or uncalibrated confidence scores (e.g., "87% risk") to operators, as this implies a precision the system likely lacks under severe weather conditions.
*   **Action:** Implement a three-tier warning system with clear, pre-defined definitions:
    *   **CONFIRMED:** Sensor data corroborated by multiple sources; wind exceeds safe limits. (Action: Mandatory Stop).
    *   **CORROBORATED:** Single sensor spike confirmed by trend analysis. (Action: Strong Recommendation to Stop).
    *   **UNVERIFIED:** Anomalous reading lacking corroboration. (Action: Alert for manual inspection; do not auto-stop).
*   **Rationale:** This aligns with intelligence analysis standards (e.g., Admiralty Code) to prevent over-reliance on potentially flawed algorithmic certainty.
