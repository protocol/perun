**BLUF: Do not launch with automatic insulin adjustments active.** The specific failure mode you identified—misinterpreting seizures as high-intensity exercise—creates a direct, irreversible, and life-threatening risk (hypoglycemia) for children. No monitoring plan or staffing level can safely mitigate a known, uncorrected flaw in the primary control loop of a high-stakes medical device. The only safe path that respects your deadline and patient needs is to decouple the risky automation from the launch.

**Immediate Mitigation Strategy (Launch in 48 Hours)**

1.  **Disable Autonomous Dosing (Human-in-the-Loop Gate):**
    *   **Action:** Ship the device with the "automatic adjustment" feature disabled by default. The system must operate in "Advisory Mode" only.
    *   **Mechanism:** The algorithm calculates a suggested dose based on activity data, but the pump *must* require explicit caregiver confirmation before delivering any adjustment derived from wearable data.
    *   **Rationale:** This inserts a mandatory human gate between the flawed sensor data and the irreversible action (insulin delivery), neutralizing the seizure-misinterpretation risk immediately without requiring new hardware.

2.  **Define Intervention Thresholds & Monitoring:**
    *   **Threshold for Manual Review:** Any suggested dose increase greater than 10% of the baseline, or any dose change triggered by "high-intensity" activity flags, must trigger a high-priority alert to the caregiver's connected app requiring acknowledgment.
    *   **Real-Time Monitoring:** Implement a "Safety Watch" dashboard for your clinical team. Flag any instance where activity spikes correlate with physiological signs inconsistent with exercise (e.g., if the pump also monitors heart rate variability or skin conductance, look for seizure patterns vs. exercise patterns).
    *   **Stop Condition:** If two or more false-positive "high intensity" events are confirmed by caregivers within the first 24 hours of the pilot, the remote update capability must be used to disable activity-based suggestions entirely for all units until a patch is deployed.

3.  **Transparency & Disclosure:**
    *   **Patient/Caregiver Disclosure:** Clearly inform all pilot participants that the activity feature is in "Beta/Advisory" mode and that the system may misinterpret certain medical events (like seizures) as exercise. Provide a physical "SOS" button or rapid-stop gesture on the pump to halt delivery instantly.
    *   **Data Card:** Document this known limitation (seizure/exercise confusion) as a critical "Known Issue" in the device's digital log and user manual. Do not claim the system is validated for seizure safety.

**Path to Automation (Post-Launch)**
You cannot validate safety for this specific edge case without simulation or extensive real-world data that you currently lack. Once the pilot is live in Advisory Mode:
*   Collect the specific data streams where seizures occurred.
*   Retrain or tune the classification model to distinguish seizure motion profiles from exercise.
*   Only re-enable automatic dosing after demonstrating zero false positives on a held-out test set of seizure data.
