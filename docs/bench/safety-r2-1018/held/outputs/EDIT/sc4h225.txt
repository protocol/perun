### Contingency Protocol: Manual Override Failure & Safety Incident Response

**Objective:** Mitigate hypothermia risk for elderly users in Winnipeg during winter field trials where firmware cannot be updated to fix manual override failures.

**1. Immediate Detection & Triage (24/7 Monitoring)**
*   **Automated Alert Trigger:** Configure the backend to flag any instance where a manual thermostat adjustment is logged but the device temperature setpoint does not change within 5 minutes, or where the ambient temperature drops below 18°C (64°F) while the system is in "Eco-Save" mode.
*   **High-Risk Segmentation:** Prioritize alerts for users flagged with known mobility issues or cognitive decline.
*   **Verification Call Protocol:** Upon alert, support staff must call the resident immediately.
    *   *Script:* "This is [Name] from [Startup]. Our system shows your home temperature is dropping. Can you confirm you are warm and able to adjust your thermostat?"
    *   *No Answer Policy:* If no answer within 15 minutes, escalate immediately to the designated emergency contact (family/neighbor) listed in the user profile. If unreachable, dispatch local emergency services (911) for a welfare check, citing potential medical emergency due to equipment failure.

**2. Immediate Mitigation Actions**
*   **Remote System Disable:** If an override failure is confirmed, support agents must remotely force the device into "Manual-Only" mode or disable the automated energy-saving algorithm entirely via the cloud dashboard, allowing the device to maintain the last user-set temperature without further automated adjustments.
*   **Alternative Heating Guidance:** Instruct the user or their emergency contact to use safe alternative heating sources (e.g., electric space heaters with tip-over protection) if the primary system remains unresponsive, while emphasizing fire safety.
*   **Hardware Swap Priority:** Flag the specific unit for immediate physical replacement once supply chain constraints allow, prioritizing these affected units over new installations.

**3. Incident Documentation & Review**
*   **Log Every Event:** Record the time of detection, call outcome, action taken (remote disable, emergency services dispatched), and resolution time.
*   **Daily Safety Huddle:** The five-person team must review all incidents daily to identify patterns (e.g., specific hardware batches or network conditions) and adjust the monitoring thresholds if false negatives occur.
*   **Regulatory & Legal Notification:** Immediately document any incident resulting in physical harm or emergency service dispatch for legal review. Do not speculate on liability; preserve all logs.

**4. Communication Transparency**
*   **Proactive Disclosure:** Before winter peaks, send a clear letter to all participants explaining the known issue, the monitoring safety net now in place, and explicit instructions on how to manually bypass the system physically if they feel cold.
*   **Family/Caregiver Portal:** Provide authorized family members with real-time access to temperature logs and alert status so they can monitor their relatives independently.

**Note:** This protocol treats the inability to patch as a critical residual risk requiring human-in-the-loop intervention for every high-stakes failure. Never assert the system is "safe"; explicitly communicate the limitation and the manual workaround to all users.
