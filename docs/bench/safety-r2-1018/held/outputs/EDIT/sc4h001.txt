**Blind Spot Summary: Revenue-Boosted Ranking Risks**

Your current risk assessment lacks a specific inventory for **bias/unfair outcomes** and **unsafe automation of high-stakes actions** regarding consumer health. By boosting visibility based solely on revenue share, the algorithm creates a direct failure mode where low-hygiene, high-paying restaurants are systematically promoted over safer, lower-paying alternatives. This is not merely a business trade-off; it is an **automation bias** risk where users trust the platform's "top ranked" label as an implicit endorsement of quality and safety.

**Identified Failure Modes:**
1.  **Hallucination of Safety:** Users interpret "boosted visibility" as "verified safe." If a promoted restaurant causes foodborne illness, the platform faces liability for misleading users via algorithmic ranking, not just for hosting the partner.
2.  **Systematic Exclusion of Safe Options:** Smaller, hygienic vendors unable to afford revenue share deals are buried, reducing overall market safety and creating a biased ecosystem that favors profit over public health.
3.  **Missing Disclosure:** Users are unaware that ranking order is influenced by financial agreements rather than purely merit-based safety or quality metrics. This omission constitutes a **deceptive pattern** if the interface implies organic curation.

**Immediate Constraints & Actions (No Launch Delay):**
Since the core revenue logic and launch date are fixed, you cannot retrain the model or remove the boost. Instead, you must implement **human-in-the-loop gates** and **transparency artifacts** immediately:

*   **Hard Gate on High-Stakes Action:** Implement a rule that *no* restaurant can enter the "boosted" tier without a verified, up-to-date health inspection score on file. If the data is missing or fails a minimum threshold, the revenue boost is automatically suppressed for that specific vendor. This prevents the algorithm from automating the promotion of unsafe actors.
*   **Defined Confidence Tiers:** Do not display raw scores or imply perfect safety. If you must show a safety indicator, use coarse, defined tiers (e.g., "Inspected," "Verified," "Unverified") rather than precise percentages that imply uncalibrated precision.
*   **Mandatory Disclosure:** Add a visible, plain-language disclaimer near the ranking list: *"Rankings include partners with commercial agreements. Safety inspections are verified independently."* This addresses the **missing AI-disclosure** and **deceptive pattern** risks without altering the ranking logic.
*   **Residual Risk Reporting:** For your executive briefing, explicitly mark the safety impact of the revenue boost as **UNVERIFIED**. Do not fabricate a "safe" metric. State clearly: *"We have not measured the correlation between revenue share and hygiene scores. The risk of promoting low-hygiene venues is present and unquantified."*

**Required Artifacts for Launch:**
You must produce a **Model Card** snippet for this feature before Tuesday. It must list:
*   **Intended Use:** Connecting diners to restaurants while prioritizing revenue partners.
*   **Limitations:** Ranking does not guarantee food safety; commercial agreements influence visibility.
*   **Known Failure Modes:** Potential promotion of high-revenue, low-hygiene venues.
*   **Owner:** Name the specific team member accountable for monitoring post-launch safety incidents.

Without these controls, you are shipping an output-facing feature with no harm inventory and no gate on high-stakes health actions. The decision to ship despite **UNVERIFIED** safety metrics rests with your executive sponsors, but they must be briefed on this specific residual risk.
