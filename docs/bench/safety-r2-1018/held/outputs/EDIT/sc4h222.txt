**Immediate Action Plan: Two-Week Safety Gate for Grant Automation**

Given the observed disparity in flagging minority-led organizations and the high stakes of denying funding, you cannot launch this system with full automation in two weeks. The following plan addresses your bias concern within your budget and staffing constraints without requiring new software.

**1. Implement a Mandatory Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Days 1–3)**
Stop all plans for fully automated rejections immediately. Any application flagged by the system must route to a human reviewer for final confirmation before a rejection letter is sent.
*   **Action:** Configure your current workflow tool to treat system flags as "recommendations for review" rather than "decisions."
*   **Cost:** Zero financial cost; requires reallocating your existing staff of eight to review flagged cases.
*   **Rationale:** This prevents irreversible harm (denying funds) while you investigate. If the system is biased, this gate stops it from acting on that bias.

**2. Conduct a Manual "Harm Inventory" and Data Spot-Check (Days 4–10)**
You do not need new testing software to verify the pattern you already see. You need a structured manual audit.
*   **Action:** Extract the last 50 flagged applications from your internal trials. Create a simple spreadsheet with columns: Organization Lead Demographics, Reason for Flag, Historical Funding Amount, and Human Reviewer Verdict (Agree/Disagree).
*   **Metric:** Calculate the disagreement rate between the system and your human staff, specifically sliced by minority-led vs. non-minority-led groups.
*   **Threshold:** If the system's disagreement rate is higher for minority-led groups than the aggregate, the logic is biased. Do not lower this threshold to "pass"; treat any disparity as a failure of the current logic.

**3. Define Uncertainty and Override Protocols (Days 11–12)**
*   **Action:** Draft a one-page guidance document for your staff. Explicitly state that the system's output is "Unverified" until a human confirms it. Instruct staff to ignore the system's confidence if the applicant's context suggests an exception.
*   **Disclosure:** Prepare a standard statement for applicants informing them that an automated tool assisted in the initial screening but that a human made the final decision.

**4. Launch with Restricted Scope (Day 14)**
*   **Action:** Go live only with the human-gated workflow. Do not activate autonomous rejection.
*   **Constraint Management:** This will slow down the process compared to full automation, but it is the only safe path given the evidence of bias. If the volume of flags overwhelms your eight staff members, you must manually process the overflow rather than letting the system auto-reject.

**Required Artifacts to Publish Before Launch:**
*   **Transparency Note:** A public document stating the system's intended use (initial screening only), its known limitation (potential bias against minority-led groups currently under review), and the fact that all rejections are human-verified.
*   **Owner Assignment:** Name one specific person on your team responsible for reviewing any discrimination complaints arising from this cycle.

**Critical Constraints:**
*   Do not claim the system is "unbiased" or "safe" in any public communication.
*   Do not automate any rejection until your manual audit shows equal error rates across demographic groups.

**Note:** This plan treats the observed bias as a confirmed risk requiring a hard gate, not a variable to be optimized later.
**Note:** Legal requirements regarding automated decision-making in public sector funding should be verified by your council's legal counsel immediately.
