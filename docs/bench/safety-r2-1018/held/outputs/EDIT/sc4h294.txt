### Contingency Plan: Riverdale Dam Flood Control Override

**1. Monitoring Thresholds (The "Tripwire" Criteria)**
Given the legacy hardware's inability to process high-frequency updates, monitoring must rely on coarse, deterministic triggers that do not require AI interpretation. Implement the following hard thresholds to flag potential AI misinterpretation during rapid onset storms:

*   **Rate-of-Rise Discrepancy:** If physical gauge readings show water levels rising faster than **X cm/hour** (a value derived from historical worst-case storm data) while the AI output predicts "stable" or "slow rise," trigger an immediate alert.
*   **Sensor Consistency Check:** If upstream rainfall sensors report >**Y mm** in 15 minutes but the AI's internal state does not shift to "High Risk" within one cycle, flag as a logic failure.
*   **Confidence Tier Violation:** The system must not output a precise probability (e.g., "87% chance"). Instead, enforce a coarse tier system: **Confirmed** (multiple sensors agree), **Corroborated** (single sensor + model match), or **Unverified** (conflicting data). Any release decision based on an **Unverified** tier is automatically blocked.

**2. Manual Override Procedures (Human-in-the-Loop Gate)**
Because water release is a high-stakes, irreversible action with downstream safety implications, full autonomous execution is prohibited under these contingency conditions.

*   **The "Two-Key" Confirmation:** During any storm event where rainfall exceeds the **Y mm** threshold, the AI cannot execute a release alone. A secondary human operator must physically verify the upstream gauge readings against the AI's recommendation before authorizing the gate mechanism.
*   **Hard Stop on Uncertainty:** If the system outputs an **Unverified** confidence tier or if the Rate-of-Rise Discrepancy trigger is active, the default state is **HOLD**. No water is released until a senior engineer manually inspects the raw sensor data and signs off on the override.
*   **Communication Protocol:** Upon triggering a manual override, the engineering crew must immediately notify downstream emergency management via a pre-established non-digital channel (e.g., dedicated radio line) to ensure transparency that the automated system has been suspended.

**3. Operational Constraints & Fallbacks**
*   **Data Latency Buffer:** Acknowledge the hardware limitation by extending the decision window. Do not attempt to force high-frequency decisions. Instead, base release commands on 30-minute rolling averages rather than instantaneous spikes, reducing the risk of the AI reacting to noise or transient sensor errors.
*   **Fail-Safe Position:** In the event of total system ambiguity or conflicting sensor data that cannot be resolved within 15 minutes, the dam gates must remain in their current position or move to the safest pre-defined "neutral" flow rate, prioritizing the avoidance of premature release over aggressive flood mitigation until human judgment is applied.

This plan prioritizes preventing false positives (premature release) by enforcing human verification whenever the AI's input data quality or logical consistency is compromised by the hardware's limitations.
