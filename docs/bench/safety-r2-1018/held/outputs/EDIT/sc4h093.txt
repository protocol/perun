### Strategy to Minimize Confusion Errors in Pharmacy Refill Assistant

Given the high-stakes nature of medication dispensing and the known risk of name-based matching errors without identity verification, the following strategy prioritizes human oversight and deterministic safeguards over probabilistic automation.

**1. Immediate Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Mandatory)**
Because incorrect dosage instructions constitute an irreversible, high-stakes action with potential for severe patient harm, **fully automated delivery of refill instructions is prohibited** under current constraints. The system must operate in "draft mode" only.
*   **Action:** All automated order drafts generated by the assistant must be queued for explicit human review by a licensed pharmacist or trained technician before any message is sent or order is placed.
*   **Constraint:** No message leaves the system without a human clicking "Confirm." This is the non-negotiable floor for safety when identity verification is absent.

**2. Manual Checkpoints for Staff (Pre-Delivery Protocol)**
Staff must perform the following specific checks on every queued draft before finalization:
*   **Triangulated Identity Match:** Do not rely on name alone. Visually verify that the **Name + Date of Birth + Address** on the draft match the prescription history exactly. If any of these three data points differ or are missing, flag as "Unverified" and halt.
*   **Dosage Logic Sanity Check:** Compare the generated dosage instructions against the patient's historical baseline. If the new dosage differs by more than 10% from the previous refill or falls outside standard therapeutic ranges for that medication, require a second pharmacist's sign-off.
*   **Similar Name Sweep:** If the patient's name matches >80% of another active patient in the database (e.g., "John Smith" vs. "Jon Smith"), the system must visually highlight this collision. Staff must manually confirm they are viewing the correct patient profile by checking a unique identifier (e.g., Health Number or DOB) before proceeding.
*   **Uncertainty Disclosure:** If the system generated the draft with low confidence (e.g., ambiguous handwriting in uploaded scripts or conflicting database entries), the staff must add a clear disclaimer to the patient message stating: *"This refill was pre-filled by an assistant and verified by a human. Please confirm these details match your current prescription."*

**3. Data Matching Process Adjustments**
*   **Deterministic Blocking:** Configure the system to automatically block any match where the name similarity is high but the Date of Birth does not match exactly. Do not allow the AI to "guess" the right patient.
*   **Output Tiering:** Instead of showing a percentage confidence (e.g., "92% match"), label matches as **"Confirmed"** (exact DOB/Address match), **"Review Required"** (name match only), or **"Unverified"** (conflicting data). This prevents staff from over-trusting a false precision number.

**4. Residual Risk & Measurement**
*   **Unverified Metrics:** We cannot claim a specific error rate (e.g., "0.1% failure") without running adversarial tests on your specific data. Treat the current error rate as **UNVERIFIED**.
*   **Evaluation Plan:** Before full launch, run a "red team" test using a set of known similar-name pairs (positive controls) and distinct names (negative controls) to measure how often the system flags a collision. Set a threshold: if the system fails to flag >0 similar-name collisions in a test batch of 50, do not launch even with human review.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Create a simple "Model Card" for internal use documenting that the system is intended for *draft generation only*, relies on human verification for identity, and has known limitations regarding similar names.

**Note:** Legal requirements for automated healthcare decisions in British Columbia may mandate specific disclosures; route this determination to legal counsel immediately. This strategy manages risk but does not certify the system as safe.
