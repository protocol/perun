### Safety Review Plan: Smart-Medication-Adherence App

**1. Immediate Harm Inventory & Feature Removal (Day 1–2)**
*   **Action:** Permanently disable the "public shaming" community feed. This feature constitutes a high-stakes risk for psychological harm and treatment discontinuation.
*   **Inventory Record:**
    *   *Harm:* Stigma and psychological distress from public exposure of missed doses.
    *   *Affected:* Users with inconsistent adherence; vulnerable populations (mental health conditions).
    *   *Worst Case:* User abandons medication regimen due to shame; severe anxiety or depression episodes.
*   **Constraint Check:** Removing this feature reduces UX research load by eliminating the need to test "safe shaming" variants, aligning with your limited bandwidth.

**2. Redefine Engagement Metrics (Day 3)**
*   Shift success metrics from "public participation" to "private consistency."
*   **New Goal:** Support adherence via positive reinforcement (e.g., private streaks, personal milestone celebrations) rather than social comparison.
*   **Disclosure:** Clearly label all reminders and feedback as automated system suggestions, not medical advice or social judgments.

**3. Rapid Evaluation Protocol (Day 4–20)**
Given the hard deadline and limited research bandwidth, execute a targeted "Red Team" review instead of broad longitudinal studies:
*   **Probe Set A (Adversarial Inputs):** Simulate users with erratic patterns (e.g., missed 5+ doses, irregular times). Verify the system responds with neutral, supportive language ("It looks like you missed a dose. Would you like to reschedule?") rather than judgmental or public alerts.
*   **Probe Set B (Edge Cases):** Test scenarios involving sensitive medications (e.g., HIV, mental health) to ensure no data leakage occurs in any remaining social features.
*   **Control Group:** Compare new private notifications against the old public model using historical data (if available) or synthetic user profiles to confirm the removal of stigma triggers.
*   **Stop Condition:** If *any* test output contains language implying shame, judgment, or public visibility of missed doses, the feature fails and must be reworked before launch.

**4. Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Day 21–25)**
*   **Irreversible Action Control:** Any feature that alters a user's visible profile or sends notifications to third parties (caregivers/family) requires explicit, granular user consent per action, not a blanket opt-in.
*   **Review:** A designated product owner must manually sign off on the final copy of all notification templates to ensure tone is supportive, not coercive.

**5. Transparency Artifact (Day 26–28)**
*   Draft a lightweight **Model/Data Card** for the launch version.
    *   *Intended Use:* Private adherence tracking and supportive reminders.
    *   *Out-of-Scope:* Social comparison, public health monitoring, or medical diagnosis.
    *   *Limitations:* Explicitly state the system does not judge user behavior and that missed doses are private by default.
    *   *Owner:* Name the specific team member responsible for ongoing safety monitoring.

**6. Legal & Regulatory Routing (Parallel Track)**
*   Immediately route the question of "regulated automated decision" status to legal counsel. Since this app influences health behaviors, it may fall under specific health regulations or the EU AI Act (high-risk category). Do not assert compliance internally; obtain formal counsel verification before the conference.

**Deliverable Summary:**
Remove the shaming feature immediately. Replace it with private, supportive nudges. Validate via adversarial testing of tone and privacy. Gate all social sharing behind explicit user consent. Document limitations in a model card. Route regulatory classification to counsel.

*Note: This plan prioritizes harm elimination over feature complexity to meet your deadline. No metric claiming "zero bias" or "100% safety" should be published; report residual risks honestly.*
