### Pre-Launch Safety Review: Credit-Limit Augmenter

**Critical Finding:** Your current plan to fully automate high-stakes financial decisions for a vulnerable population without external auditing or a human-in-the-loop gate constitutes an unsafe launch condition. Fully autonomous execution of irreversible actions (granting debt that cannot be instantly undone) is the primary failure mode this review identifies. You cannot "audit" safety post-launch if the harm (unsustainable debt) has already occurred.

#### 1. Harm Inventory (MAP)
Before tracking metrics, acknowledge these specific risks to your low-income user base:
*   **Bias/Unfair Outcomes:** "Social connectivity data" may systematically disadvantage isolated individuals or specific communities, correlating poverty with lower limits regardless of repayment ability.
*   **Over-Reliance:** Users may treat an automated limit increase as a verified endorsement of their financial health, borrowing beyond actual means.
*   **Unsafe Automation:** Granting credit is a high-stakes action. If the model hallucinates a positive signal in transaction history, the user incurs real debt immediately.
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Users must know this decision was made by an algorithm, not a human underwriter, especially given the use of non-traditional data (social connectivity).

#### 2. Immediate Mitigation Strategies (MANAGE)
Given your two-week timeline and fixed budget, you cannot build a new auditing team. You must alter the product behavior:
*   **Implement a Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Gate:** Do not auto-approve limits for the lowest credit-score decile or the highest requested increases. Route these specific cases to a human reviewer or require explicit, multi-step user confirmation that details the repayment math. **Full automation of high-impact debt is the failure; gating is the floor.**
*   **Staged Rollout with Hard Caps:** Launch to only 5% of users initially. Set a hard global cap on total exposure for this new cohort. If missed payments spike in this 5%, the system must automatically pause further increases (kill switch).
*   **Defined Confidence Tiers:** Do not show users a raw probability score (e.g., "87% chance of repayment"). Instead, use coarse, defined tiers for internal routing (e.g., "Verified," "Corroborated," "Unverified"). If the model's confidence is "Unverified," default to no increase or a manual review.
*   **Mandatory Disclosure:** Clearly state on the offer screen: "This limit increase was generated by an automated system based on your transaction and social data. It is not a guarantee of financial health."

#### 3. Post-Launch Metrics Checklist (MEASURE)
Do not track aggregate averages alone; they hide harm to subgroups. Track these daily, sliced by income bracket and user tenure:
*   **Early Delinquency Rate (Day 30):** Percentage of new limit users who miss their first payment. *Stop Condition:* If this exceeds your baseline control group by >10%, halt the feature.
*   **Utilization Spike:** Percentage of users who max out the new limit within 48 hours. High velocity suggests desperation or misunderstanding of the limit.
*   **Hardship Contact Rate:** Volume of support tickets mentioning "cannot pay," "debt," or "mistake" from the new cohort.
*   **Subgroup Disparity:** Compare approval rates and delinquency rates across different regions or social graph densities. If one group has significantly higher delinquency, the model is biased against them.
*   **Reversal/Complaint Rate:** How many users try to return the limit or dispute the charge immediately after acceptance.
