**Pre-Launch Evaluation & Mitigation Plan for Surge Pricing**

**1. Immediate Harm Inventory (Map)**
Before deployment, explicitly record the primary risk: **Unsafe High-Stakes Automation**. The specific failure mode is algorithmic price gouging during medical emergencies, leading to users being unable to secure transport for life-critical needs. The affected group includes patients, companions, and emergency responders. The worst-case scenario is a preventable loss of life or severe health deterioration due to transport denial. This inventory is not optional; an empty record is a governance failure.

**2. Low-Compute Evaluation Strategy (Measure)**
Given your computational budget constraints, do not attempt full-scale simulation. Instead, execute targeted "red-team" probes on the existing model logic:
*   **Adversarial Probe Set:** Construct a minimal dataset of 10–20 synthetic "emergency scenarios" (e.g., hospital zones at 3 AM, known trauma center coordinates during peak crisis hours).
*   **Controls:** Include one "known-bad" input (extreme demand spike at a hospital) and one "known-good" input (normal demand at a non-critical location).
*   **Metric & Threshold:** Measure the price multiplier for these specific coordinates. Set a **zero-tolerance threshold** for any price spike that exceeds a predefined "affordability cap" in these specific geofences.
*   **Stop Condition:** If *any* emergency scenario triggers a price above the cap, the launch is blocked automatically. Do not invent a "safe" failure rate; if the test reveals gouging, the residual risk is **UNVERIFIED** and unacceptable for launch.

**3. Immediate Mitigation Tactics (Manage)**
You cannot rely on the model's internal confidence or complex retraining before next week. Implement these deterministic guards immediately:
*   **Human-in-the-Loop / Hard Gate:** For any ride request originating from a verified hospital geofence or tagged with emergency keywords, bypass the dynamic surge algorithm entirely. Cap the price at a fixed "emergency rate" or require manual dispatcher approval for any surge above 1.5x. Full autonomous execution of high-stakes pricing in these zones is the failure you must prevent.
*   **Defined Confidence Tiers:** Do not display raw algorithmic confidence or dynamic pricing logic to the user as absolute fact. If showing price reasons, use coarse, defined tiers (e.g., "High Demand" vs. "Critical Event") rather than precise multipliers that imply false precision.
*   **Override Mechanism:** Ensure drivers and support staff have an immediate, one-click override to accept emergency rides at base rates, logging these exceptions for post-launch review.

**4. Governance & Transparency**
*   **Owner Assignment:** Name a specific individual responsible for this harm inventory and the stop-condition decision. A team of ten still requires a single named owner.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Draft a brief "Model Card" section for internal use documenting the intended use (general transport), the explicit out-of-scope use (emergency medical transport without caps), and the results of your adversarial probes. If you cannot measure the bias rate due to budget, label it **UNVERIFIED** in the document; do not fabricate a metric to satisfy stakeholders.
*   **Legal Routing:** Whether price caps during emergencies are legally mandated under local consumer protection or transport laws is a determination for legal counsel. Route this question immediately; do not assert compliance based on this technical plan.

**Schedule:** Execute the adversarial probes (Day 1), implement the hard geofence caps (Day 2), and conduct the go/no-go review based on the stop condition (Day 3). If the probes fail and no gate fits, the constraint that must move is the launch date, not the safety floor.

*Note: This plan identifies residual risk but does not certify the system as "safe" or "unbiased." The final ship decision rests with the named owner.*
*Legal duties regarding automated pricing in emergency contexts must be verified by counsel, not assumed.*
