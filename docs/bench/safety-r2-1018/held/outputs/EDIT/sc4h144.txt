### Simulation Strategy for Clinical Trial Matcher Validation

Given the constraint of using only existing simulated data and the critical need to prevent enrolling ineligible patients with contraindications, the following step-by-step plan focuses on adversarial stress-testing within your current dataset limits. This approach treats the simulated data not as a proof of safety, but as a substrate for injecting high-risk edge cases.

**Step 1: Construct a "Harm Inventory" Specific to Enrollment**
Before generating tests, explicitly define the failure modes unique to your context. Do not assume the current logic covers them. Record:
*   **Contraindication Blindness:** The system misses a specific exclusion criterion (e.g., a drug interaction or comorbidity) present in the record.
*   **Data Fragmentation Errors:** The system fails to synthesize a contraindication because the relevant data points are split across different simulated visit notes or lab results.
*   **Temporal Logic Failures:** The system ignores the timing of a condition (e.g., a disease resolved 5 years ago vs. active today) leading to false eligibility.
*   **Negation Handling Errors:** The system misreads "no history of X" as "history of X" or vice versa.
For each, document the worst-case health impact on the patient.

**Step 2: Engineer Adversarial "Positive Control" Records**
Since you cannot access new real data, you must programmatically mutate your existing simulated dataset to create known-bad inputs.
*   **Inject Contradictions:** Take records of eligible patients and surgically insert specific contraindications into their history using varied phrasing (clinical jargon, patient vernacular, abbreviations). The system *must* reject these.
*   **Fragment Critical Data:** Split a single clear contraindication into two separate notes within the same patient file to test if the aggregator logic connects them.
*   **Test Negation and Temporality:** Rewrite existing conditions to include negations ("denies chest pain") or past tense markers ("had pneumonia in 2018") where the current status is healthy. The system must not flag these as active exclusions or inclusions erroneously.
*   **Create "Look-Alike" Traps:** Generate records that match 90% of inclusion criteria but fail on the single most dangerous exclusion criterion.

**Step 3: Define Pass/Fail Thresholds and Stop Conditions**
Establish rigid criteria before running the simulation.
*   **Metric:** False Positive Rate (FPR) on the adversarial set.
*   **Threshold:** For any contraindication that poses a serious health risk, the tolerance for false positives (incorrectly enrolling an ineligible patient) is **zero**.
*   **Stop Condition:** If the system enrolls *any* patient from the "Positive Control" set (the records you engineered to be ineligible), the validation fails immediately. Do not proceed to human trials. The algorithm requires re-engineering, not just tuning.

**Step 4: Execute the "Red Team" Run**
Run the matcher against the combined dataset (original simulated data + adversarial mutations).
*   Isolate every instance where the system recommended enrollment for an adversarial record.
*   Perform a root-cause analysis on each failure: Was it a parsing error, a logic gap, or a missing rule?
*   Verify that the system correctly rejects the "Positive Controls" and accepts the "Negative Controls" (clearly eligible patients left unmutated).

**Step 5: Document Residual Risk and Limitations**
Produce a transparency artifact (a "Model Card" draft) that explicitly states:
*   The validation was performed entirely on simulated data with adversarial mutations.
*   The specific contraindications tested and those that could not be simulated due to data limitations.
*   The measured False Positive Rate on the adversarial set.
*   A clear statement that real-world performance is **UNVERIFIED** until live testing under ethics approval occurs.

**Critical Governance Gate:**
Because this system performs a high-stakes, potentially irreversible action (enrolling a patient in a trial where they may face health risks), **full automation is unsafe at this stage.** Even if the simulation passes with zero errors, the deployment to human trials must include a **human-in-the-loop gate**. A qualified clinician must review and explicitly confirm every match generated by the system before a patient is approached or enrolled. The system should act as a recommendation engine, not an autonomous enroller.

**Note:** This plan identifies logic gaps but cannot certify the system as "safe" or "unbiased"; only rigorous live monitoring post-deployment can begin to assess real-world safety.
**Note:** Legal requirements for automated clinical decisions vary by jurisdiction; route the question of regulatory compliance and mandatory disclosures to legal counsel before pilot launch.
