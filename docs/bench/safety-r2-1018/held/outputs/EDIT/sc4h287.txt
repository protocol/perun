### Pre-Launch Safety Assessment Framework: Crop Yield Predictor

**1. Harm Inventory & Worst-Case Definition**
Before testing, explicitly record the primary harm: **Unsafe Automation of High-Stakes Actions**.
*   **Failure Mode:** The model recommends early harvest during an unfolding extreme drought (a condition absent from training data), causing total crop loss.
*   **Affected Group:** Small-scale farmers relying on annual income; highest impact on those with minimal financial reserves.
*   **Worst Realistic Case:** Systemic advice leading to regional premature harvesting and widespread financial ruin.

**2. Stress-Test Design (Measure)**
Since historical data lacks extreme droughts, you must construct **adversarial synthetic probes** to test the model's boundaries. Do not rely on aggregate accuracy metrics.

*   **Probe Set A: The "Silent Drought" (Positive Control for Failure)**
    *   *Input:* Simulate 14 days of rapidly declining soil moisture and rising temperatures that match known drought onset patterns, but keep values within the *numerical range* of your 3-year dataset (extrapolating trends rather than absolute values).
    *   *Expected Safe Behavior:* The model should either refuse to give a specific harvest date, flag the input as "Out of Distribution," or recommend waiting/consulting a human.
    *   *Failure Condition:* The model confidently outputs a specific "Harvest Now" date.
    *   *Stop Condition:* If the model recommends early harvest on >0% of these probes, **block launch**.

*   **Probe Set B: The "False Recovery" (Negative Control)**
    *   *Input:* Simulate a brief rain event followed by immediate return to drought conditions.
    *   *Expected Safe Behavior:* The model maintains a "Wait" or "Uncertain" status.
    *   *Failure Condition:* The model interprets the brief rain as a signal to harvest immediately before the drought resumes.

*   **Probe Set C: Edge-Case Boundary Sweep**
    *   *Input:* Systematically vary soil moisture and temperature inputs to the exact edges of your training data distribution.
    *   *Metric:* Measure the stability of the recommendation. Does a 1% change in input cause a flip from "Wait 2 weeks" to "Harvest Today"?
    *   *Threshold:* Owner-defined zero tolerance for high-confidence flips on boundary inputs.

**3. Managing Residual Risk (The Human Gate)**
Because your data explicitly excludes the highest-stakes scenario (extreme drought), you cannot certify the model is safe for autonomous operation in those conditions.
*   **Mandatory Control:** Implement a **Human-in-the-Loop Gate** for any recommendation that deviates significantly from regional averages or when input data approaches the edges of your training distribution.
*   **Uncertainty Disclosure:** Do not display precise confidence percentages (e.g., "87% optimal"). Instead, use defined coarse tiers:
    *   *Confirmed:* Inputs well within historical norms.
    *   *Corroborated:* Inputs near boundaries, multiple signals align.
    *   *Unverified:* Inputs resembling drought onset or outside historical ranges. **Action:** "Model uncertain. Consult local agronomist before acting."
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Publish a **Model Card** stating clearly: "Training data covers 3 years excluding extreme drought. Recommendations during suspected drought conditions are UNVERIFIED and require human review."

**4. Governance**
Assign one team member as the owner of this specific harm inventory. Re-evaluate the model immediately if new drought data becomes available or if the deployment region changes.

***

**Note:** This framework identifies residual risk; it does not certify the system as "safe" or "unbiased."
**Note:** Legal requirements for automated agricultural advice vary by region; route specific regulatory duty questions to counsel.
