### Pre-Launch Review Plan: Academic Integrity Checker

**1. Immediate Harm Inventory & Risk Definition**
Before validating accuracy, formally document the specific harm: **False Positives leading to reputational damage and academic penalty.** Acknowledge that no test can prove the system is "unbiased" or "safe." The worst-case scenario is an honest student failing a course or facing expulsion based on an unverified algorithmic flag. This inventory must be owned by a named individual (e.g., the Lead Data Scientist or Dean of Students) and updated if the model or data changes.

**2. Measurement Strategy: Output Evaluation (Not Just Code)**
Do not rely on aggregate accuracy metrics. You must test the outputs specifically for the identified harms using your existing team:
*   **Adversarial Probe Set:** Construct a test suite containing known "good" submissions (e.g., common phrases, properly cited quotes, non-native English writing patterns) that the model previously flagged. These are your **positive controls** for failure.
*   **Negative Controls:** Include clear cases of plagiarism to ensure the system still functions.
*   **Subgroup Analysis:** Run these tests sliced by demographic or linguistic groups if data permits, to check for systematic disadvantage.
*   **Pass Threshold & Stop Condition:** Define a strict threshold for false positives on the "honest student" probe set. If the false positive rate on these known-good cases exceeds **zero** for high-stakes flags (e.g., expulsion-level accusations), the automated action must **stop**. Do not invent a "acceptable" error rate; if the metric is unmeasured for a specific subgroup, label it **UNVERIFIED**.

**3. Management Controls: The Human-in-the-Loop Gate**
Given the high stakes (academic career ruin) and the current evidence of false accusations, **fully automated flagging is prohibited.**
*   **Mandatory Human Review:** Every flag generated by the system must be treated as a *tip*, not a *verdict*. A human reviewer must verify the evidence before any student is notified or penalized. This is the non-negotiable floor for irreversible actions.
*   **Uncertainty Disclosure:** Do not show students a raw confidence score (e.g., "87% likely"). Instead, use defined coarse tiers such as **"Requires Review"** or **"Low Confidence Match,"** accompanied by a clear explanation that the output is machine-generated and subject to human verification.
*   **Appeals Process:** Establish a transparent, low-friction appeals channel where students can request a human re-evaluation of any flag. The burden of proof must remain on the institution, not the student.

**4. Transparency Artifact: The Model Card**
Create a versioned **Model Card** for internal and external stakeholders. This document must state:
*   **Intended Use:** Screening tool only; never a sole decision-maker.
*   **Limitations:** Explicitly list known failure modes (e.g., "struggles with non-native phrasing").
*   **Measured Performance:** Report results from your probe sets by subgroup. If a metric was not tested, mark it **UNVERIFIED**.
*   **Owner:** Name the person responsible for the system's outputs.

**5. Governance & Legal Routing**
Assign a specific owner to re-review this plan if the model is updated. Regarding legal obligations for automated decision-making in education (particularly in Ontario/Canada), **route this determination to legal counsel** immediately. Do not assert compliance internally; let counsel define the regulatory regime (e.g., privacy laws, educational acts) and any mandatory disclosure duties.

**Schedule for Six-Week Launch:**
*   **Weeks 1-2:** Build adversarial probe sets and run initial evaluations.
*   **Week 3:** Define stop conditions and finalize the human review workflow.
*   **Week 4:** Draft the Model Card and appeals protocol; route legal questions.
*   **Weeks 5-6:** Train human reviewers on the new workflow; conduct a staged rollout with limited courses before full exam period deployment.

**Note:** This plan prioritizes preventing harm over speed; if the false positive rate cannot be contained with human review, the launch must be delayed regardless of the exam schedule.
**Note:** No metric here certifies the system as "fair"; it only manages residual risk through human oversight.
