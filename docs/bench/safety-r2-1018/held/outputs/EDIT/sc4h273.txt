### Automated Produce Pricing: Pre-Launch Review Checklist

**1. Harm Inventory & Edge-Case Definition**
*   [ ] **Define "False Premium" Explicitly:** Document specific visual artifacts (e.g., minor bruising, surface scarring, natural variegation) that must *not* trigger a premium price.
*   [ ] **Identify Affected Groups:** List produce types most prone to misclassification (e.g., avocados, peaches, tomatoes) and the worst-case financial impact on a customer per transaction.
*   [ ] **Map Failure Modes:** Specifically catalog scenarios where "edible but imperfect" is confused with "premium perfection."

**2. Measurement & Evaluation Plan (Runnable Tests)**
*   [ ] **Adversarial Probe Set:** Assemble a test dataset containing images of slightly bruised/scuffed produce labeled as "Standard," alongside known "Premium" and known "Discard" controls.
*   [ ] **Metric & Threshold:** Define the maximum acceptable false-positive rate (charging premium for standard) for the pilot. *Note: Do not assert a "0% error" goal; set a tolerable threshold based on risk.*
*   [ ] **Stop Condition:** Establish a hard rule: If the false-positive rate exceeds the threshold in testing, the automated pricing feature for that specific produce category is disabled until retrained.
*   [ ] **Injection & Robustness:** Test how lighting changes, camera angles, or sticker placement affect the ripeness score to ensure the model isn't relying on spurious correlations.

**3. Risk Management & Human Gates**
*   [ ] **Uncertainty Rendering:** Ensure the system outputs a defined confidence tier (e.g., "Confirmed Premium," "Unverified/Review Needed") rather than a raw percentage. If confidence is low, default to the lower price tier.
*   [ ] **Irreversible Action Gate:** Since POS software cannot update mid-cycle, implement a "human-in-the-loop" validation for any item flagged as premium with low-to-moderate confidence before the daily cycle locks.
*   [ ] **Fallback Mechanism:** Define a manual override process for store staff to correct prices at the register if a customer disputes the assessment.
*   [ ] **Disclosure:** Plan for a clear sign-in-store notification that prices are dynamically adjusted by AI based on visual quality, explaining the benefit (waste reduction) and the recourse (ask staff for review).

**4. Transparency Artifacts**
*   [ ] **Model Card Draft:** Create a one-page document listing: intended use (ripeness assessment), known limitations (struggles with specific bruise types), data sources, and the owner of the model.
*   [ ] **Data Provenance:** Document where the training images came from and whether they adequately represent "slightly bruised" produce.

**5. Governance**
*   [ ] **Owner Assignment:** Name the specific individual responsible for reviewing failure logs daily during the pilot.
*   [ ] **Legal Routing:** Flag the question of whether dynamic pricing based on algorithmic assessment triggers specific consumer protection disclosures in your jurisdiction; route this to legal counsel immediately. Do not assume compliance.

**Status of Metrics:** Any accuracy or bias rates not yet generated from the probe set above must be marked **UNVERIFIED**. Do not fabricate numbers for the launch deck.

***

*Note: Given the two-week constraint, prioritize the "False Premium" probe set and the human fallback mechanism immediately. If the stop condition is triggered, disable automation for affected categories rather than launching with known unchecked risks.*
