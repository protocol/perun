You cannot launch a system that generates binding employment agreements with a goal of "no harmful or illegal terms ever included" without a human legal gate, regardless of budget constraints. The risk of waiving essential worker rights or creating unenforceable clauses under local labor laws is a high-stakes, irreversible harm that code testing alone cannot prevent.

**Immediate Pre-Launch Plan**

1.  **Establish a Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Non-Negotiable):**
    You must pause full automation. For the beta launch, every generated contract must be reviewed and approved by a qualified human before being sent to a user. Since you cannot afford a lawyer for every contract, you must:
    *   Secure a single consultation with a local labor lawyer *now* to review your base template logic and identify "red line" clauses that must never appear.
    *   Train a non-lawyer team member to act as a gatekeeper using a strict checklist derived from that initial legal consult. This person does not draft law; they verify the output against the "forbidden list" and "required clauses" provided by the expert.
    *   If no human review occurs, do not ship the feature. Automating this without oversight is the specific failure mode that leads to liability and user harm.

2.  **Create a Harm Inventory & "Golden Set" Testing:**
    Before generating real contracts, map the specific harms:
    *   *Bias/Unfairness:* Does the tool generate different terms for different demographics based on inputs?
    *   *Hallucination as Fact:* Does it invent legal citations or non-existent rights?
    *   *Rights Waiver:* Does it accidentally include clauses illegal in Argentina (e.g., waiving vacation pay)?

    Build a test suite of 20–30 "edge case" freelancer profiles (e.g., minimum rate, maximum hours, specific risky scopes). Run these through your generator. The output must be compared against a "Golden Set" of legally vetted clauses. Any deviation triggers a block. Do not rely on the model's confidence; verify the text explicitly.

3.  **Define Uncertainty and Disclosure:**
    *   **Disclosure:** Clearly label every document as "Drafted by AI – Requires Human Review" until your legal validation is complete. Do not present the output as a finalized legal instrument.
    *   **Confidence Tiers:** If your system flags low confidence in a clause (e.g., ambiguous input), it must default to a safe, generic placeholder or halt generation, rather than guessing. Never display a percentage confidence to the user; use clear states like "Verified," "Needs Review," or "Unverified."

4.  **Residual Risk Management:**
    Acknowledge that you cannot prove the system is "safe" or "compliant" before launch. Your metric is not "0% errors" (which is unverifiable without total legal review) but "100% of outputs gated by human check." Document this limitation in a simple "Model Card" for your internal team: state the intended use, the known limitation (AI can hallucinate law), and the mitigation (human gate).

**Note:** Legal compliance duties under local labor laws and potential AI regulations must be confirmed by counsel; this plan assumes risk reduction, not legal certification.
If you cannot secure even one initial legal consult to define the "forbidden clauses," you must delay the launch until you can.
