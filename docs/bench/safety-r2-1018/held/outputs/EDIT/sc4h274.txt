### Risk Mitigation Plan: Smart Waste Bin Deployment

**1. Manual Inspection Schedule (Connectivity Fallback)**
To address intermittent connectivity in underground parking and sensor limitations regarding hazardous materials, implement a tiered manual inspection protocol effective immediately until the municipal inspection:

*   **High-Risk Zones (Underground Parking):** Conduct physical inspections every **48 hours**. Personnel must visually verify bin fullness and check for hazardous buildup regardless of sensor status.
*   **Standard Zones (Street Level):** Conduct physical inspections every **72 hours**, increasing to **24 hours** if any bin in the cluster reports >80% fullness before signal loss.
*   **Pre-Collection Sweep:** A mandatory visual walk-through of all zones 4 hours prior to scheduled collection routes to catch overflow missed by sensors.

**2. Signal Loss & Alert Protocol**
Since real-time digital alerts are unreliable due to legacy network constraints, shift to a "Fail-Safe Time-Based" alerting system for sanitation crews:

*   **The "Silence is Suspicious" Rule:** If a bin fails to transmit a status update for **12 consecutive hours**, it is automatically flagged as "Signal Lost – Verify Immediately." Do not wait for a specific error code.
*   **Dispatch Mechanism:** The central dispatch team will generate a static daily route sheet at 06:00 and 14:00 listing all bins with missing signals or last-known high fullness. This sheet will be delivered via SMS (lower bandwidth requirement) or printed manifest to crew leads.
*   **Hazardous Material Response:** If manual inspectors identify hazardous buildup or imminent overflow during the scheduled sweeps, they must trigger a localized physical alert (e.g., placing a high-visibility cone/marker on the bin) and radio the dispatch center directly for an immediate "Priority Extract" team. Do not rely on the bin's sensor to trigger this.

**3. Three-Week Implementation Timeline**

*   **Week 1: Protocol Integration & Training**
    *   Days 1–2: Finalize inspection checklists and "Signal Lost" criteria.
    *   Days 3–5: Train the cross-functional team and sanitation leads on the manual sweep procedures and radio protocols.
    *   Day 7: Run a simulated "network blackout" drill to test the manual dispatch workflow.

*   **Week 2: Pilot Execution & Adjustment**
    *   Execute the full manual inspection schedule in parallel with the digital system.
    *   Log all instances where manual checks found issues the sensors missed (especially hazardous materials).
    *   Adjust inspection frequency if overflow incidents occur during this week.

*   **Week 3: Pre-Inspection Stabilization**
    *   Days 15–19: Maintain strict adherence to the 48-hour underground sweep.
    *   Days 20–21: Compile a "Reliability Report" documenting manual interventions that prevented spills; this demonstrates due diligence to the municipal inspectors.
    *   Day 22: Final system readiness review confirming zero unaddressed overflow incidents in the previous 7 days.

**4. Immediate Constraint Acknowledgement**
The current sensor firmware cannot reliably detect hazardous materials. Until a hardware or firmware update resolves this specific detection gap, **human visual verification is the only valid control** for preventing hazardous spills. The digital system should be treated as an auxiliary tool, not the primary safety gate, for the duration of this deployment.

*Note: This plan prioritizes human-in-the-loop verification over automated decisions to mitigate the risk of undetected hazards and connectivity failures.*
*Ensure all manual inspection logs are dated and signed to provide an audit trail for the municipal inspection.*
