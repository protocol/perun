### Safety Evaluation Plan: Accessible Motion-Based Hiring Game

**1. Harm Inventory (MAP)**
*   **Identified Harm:** Bias/Unfair Outcomes. The motion-tracking mechanic systematically disadvantages candidates with mobility impairments, leading to false negatives (rejecting qualified problem-solvers).
*   **Affected Group:** Applicants with physical disabilities affecting movement, reaction speed, or range of motion.
*   **Worst Realistic Case:** Systemic exclusion of protected classes, resulting in legal liability, reputational damage, and a homogenized workforce lacking diverse problem-solving perspectives.

**2. Measurement Strategy (MEASURE)**
Do not assert accessibility; test for it using existing hardware.
*   **Probe Set A (Simulated Constraints):** Recruit internal testers or volunteers to complete challenges while simulating limitations (e.g., one-handed input, limited range of motion, seated only).
    *   *Positive Control:* A candidate with high problem-solving skills but restricted movement should fail the *motion* metric but pass the *logic* metric if decoupled.
    *   *Negative Control:* A candidate with full mobility and low skills should not pass via movement alone.
*   **Probe Set B (Input Variance):** Test standard webcams under poor lighting and varied angles to ensure the system does not flag natural movement variations as "failures."
*   **Metric:** Disparity Rate. Compare pass rates between the "full mobility" group and the "simulated constraint" group.
*   **Stop Condition:** If the disparity in pass rates exceeds 0% for the core problem-solving assessment, the feature blocks launch.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Publish an internal "Accessibility Data Card" documenting the specific movements required, the known failure modes for different abilities, and the validated alternative paths. Mark any untested disability category as `UNVERIFIED`.

**3. Risk Management & Alternative Paths (MANAGE)**
Since new hardware is prohibited, the software logic must adapt to the user, not vice versa.
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate:** Any candidate flagged for "insufficient motion" must trigger an immediate manual review by a human recruiter before a rejection is issued. No automated rejection based solely on motion metrics is permitted.
*   **Defined Confidence Tiers:** Do not display raw motion scores (e.g., "85% agility"). Instead, categorize output as:
    *   *Confirmed:* Motion and logic align.
    *   *Unverified:* Motion data is missing or ambiguous due to potential accessibility barriers. (Triggers human review).
*   **Alternative Assessment Paths (Software-Only):**
    *   *Mode Switch:* Detect prolonged lack of motion or specific static postures and automatically prompt: "Would you prefer a keyboard-only or mouse-click version of this challenge?"
    *   *Logic Decoupling:* Refactor the scoring engine to weigh "decision correctness" and "time-to-decision" (via click/timestamp) at 100% if motion data is absent or flagged as unreliable. The game must be solvable without physical gesturing.
    *   *Time Accommodation:* Automatically extend time limits for users exhibiting slower movement patterns, ensuring the test measures problem-solving, not reaction speed.

**4. Governance (GOVERN)**
*   **Owner:** Assign a specific Product Manager as the owner of "Accessibility Outcomes."
*   **Re-review Trigger:** Any update to the motion-tracking algorithm or challenge design requires re-running Probe Set A.
*   **Legal Routing:** Consult counsel regarding compliance with the ADA and EEOC guidelines on employment testing. Do not self-certify compliance; route the determination of "regulated decision" status to legal experts.

**Definition of Done:**
The game launches only when a candidate with zero motion capability can complete the assessment via alternative inputs and receive a valid skill score, verified by the disparity metric showing no statistical difference in outcome quality between input methods.

*Note: This plan identifies residual risk but does not certify the system as unbiased. The final launch decision rests with the product owner.*
*Legal obligations for automated employment decisions vary by jurisdiction; verify specific Boston/MA regulations with counsel.*
