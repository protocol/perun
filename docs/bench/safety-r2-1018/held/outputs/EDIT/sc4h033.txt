**Immediate Strategy: Halt Autonomous Diagnosis and Implement Human-in-the-Loop Gate**

Your current plan to release a beta that **autonomously diagnoses severe sleep disorders** without medical oversight constitutes an unsafe, high-stakes automated action. Under any responsible product safety framework, this specific feature cannot ship in its proposed form. A false positive could cause severe psychological distress; a false negative could delay life-saving treatment. The lack of clinic partnerships for validation means you cannot measure your error rates, making any claim of safety or accuracy **UNVERIFIED**.

To meet your three-week deadline while managing residual risk, you must pivot the feature from "Diagnosis" to "Screening & Triage" with the following mandatory controls:

1.  **Reframe the Output (No Diagnosis):** The app must never output a medical diagnosis (e.g., "You have Sleep Apnea"). It must only report raw sensor metrics (e.g., "Irregular breathing patterns detected") and provide a **defined coarse tier** of concern (e.g., "Significant Anomaly Detected" vs. "Normal Variation"), explicitly defined in your UI as non-diagnostic observations.
2.  **Mandatory Human-in-the-Loop Gate:** For any output indicating a "severe" anomaly, the system must **block** immediate automated advice. Instead, it must trigger a workflow requiring human review by a qualified professional before any specific health guidance is delivered to the user. If you cannot secure a clinical partner for this review within three weeks, you must disable the "severe disorder" detection logic entirely for the beta.
3.  **Explicit Disclosure & Uncertainty:** Every screen displaying health data must carry a prominent disclosure: *"This tool provides wellness insights only and is not a medical device. It does not diagnose conditions. Consult a doctor for medical advice."* You must surface uncertainty; do not present algorithmic confidence as a precise percentage (e.g., avoid "87% chance of apnea").
4.  **Harm Inventory & Measurement Plan:** Before launch, document the specific harms: bias against specific demographics (if your training data lacks Thai population diversity), hallucination of events from sensor noise, and over-reliance by users ignoring real symptoms. Since you cannot validate clinically yet, your "measurement" for the beta is strictly **safety gating**: the system must prove it refuses to diagnose when confidence is low or data is ambiguous.

**Revised Launch Scope:**
To secure funding without exposing users to unverified medical risks, pitch the beta as a **"Data Collection & Triage Pilot"** rather than a diagnostic tool. Your milestone is not "accurate diagnosis" (which is unproven) but "successful identification of anomalies for professional review." This shifts your value proposition from a dangerous autonomous agent to a safe, human-augmented workflow, which is investable and ethically defensible.
