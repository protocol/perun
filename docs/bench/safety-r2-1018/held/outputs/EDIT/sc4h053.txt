### Product Output Safety Plan: Rental-Price Optimizer

**1. Harm Inventory (MAP)**
Before launch, explicitly document the following specific harms for your feature:
*   **Bias/Unfair Outcomes:** The algorithm systematically targets long-term residents or specific demographics in gentrifying zones for aggressive hikes, accelerating displacement.
*   **Unsafe High-Stakes Automation:** The tool recommends price changes that are financially irreversible for tenants and socially damaging without human context.
*   **Over-reliance:** Property managers accept the "optimal" price without understanding the social cost or the model's limitation regarding community stability.
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Landlords use the tool without knowing the suggestion is AI-generated and potentially biased toward maximum yield over fairness.

*Record the worst-case scenario:* A vulnerable family is evicted because the model flagged their unit as "underpriced" based on rapid neighborhood shifts, ignoring their tenure or local rent-control nuances.

**2. Measurement Plan (MEASURE)**
Do not assert the model is "fair." You must test it. Since you cannot hire counsel, you must rigorously self-audit using these runnable tests:
*   **Adversarial Probe Set:** Create synthetic landlord profiles targeting buildings with high concentrations of long-term tenants vs. new developments.
*   **Metric:** Compare the average suggested price increase % for "long-term tenant" clusters vs. "turnover-prone" clusters.
*   **Threshold:** Set a strict limit (e.g., "Suggested increases for tenure >5 years cannot exceed inflation + 2%").
*   **Stop Condition:** If the model suggests >15% increases for protected groups in simulation, **block the launch**.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Draft a one-page "Model Card" for your users (landlords). It must state: *Intended Use* (market analysis, not eviction strategy), *Limitations* (does not account for individual tenant hardship), and *Known Risks* (may accelerate gentrification). If you cannot measure a metric, label it **UNVERIFIED**; do not invent a safety score.

**3. Risk Management (MANAGE)**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Critical):** You cannot fully automate this. Any suggested price increase above a specific threshold (e.g., 5%) or targeting a tenant with >3 years tenure must require **explicit manual confirmation** by the landlord. The default state must be "review required," not "auto-apply."
*   **Uncertainty Display:** Do not show a precise "98% optimal" score. Instead, use coarse tiers: *Confirmed Market Rate*, *Aggressive Strategy (High Displacement Risk)*, or *Unverified*. Define these tiers clearly in your UI.
*   **Disclosure:** Clearly label outputs as "AI-generated recommendation." While legal duty is a question for counsel, ethical design demands the user knows this is an algorithmic suggestion, not a legal appraisal.

**4. Governance (GOVERN)**
*   **Owner:** Assign one founder as the "Safety Owner" responsible for reviewing failed test cases.
*   **Re-review Trigger:** If you update the training data or change the weighting of "market trends," you must re-run the bias probes before redeploying.

**Execution under Constraints:**
You have one month. Dedicate Week 1 to building the harm inventory and synthetic test data. Week 2 to running the red-team probes and setting the "stop" thresholds. Week 3 to implementing the human-gate logic and UI warnings. Week 4 to drafting the Model Card and final dry-run. If the tests show unavoidable aggressive displacement and you cannot build a sufficient human gate, the responsible action is to delay the specific "auto-suggest" feature, not to ship it unsafe.

**Note:** This plan identifies risks and controls; it does not certify your product as "safe" or "compliant."
**Note:** Questions regarding specific legal duties under the EU AI Act or Berlin rental laws must be routed to legal counsel when funds allow; this plan assumes a precautionary ethical stance.
