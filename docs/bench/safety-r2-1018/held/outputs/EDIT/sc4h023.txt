### Flood-Alert Risk Mitigation Protocol

**1. Harm Inventory & Worst-Case Definition**
*   **Harm:** Systemic exclusion of informal settlements due to incomplete open-source map data.
*   **Affected Group:** Families in unmapped zones within the coastal city.
*   **Worst Realistic Case:** Rising water levels go undetected in specific zones, resulting in zero automated alerts for those communities while neighboring mapped areas receive warnings, leading to preventable injury or loss of life.

**2. Immediate Mitigation Strategy (Pre-Launch)**
Since the data gap cannot be fixed before the monsoon, the system must shift from "automated detection" to "hybrid verification" for high-risk zones.

*   **Implement a "Human-in-the-Loop" Gate for High-Stakes Silence:**
    Do not rely solely on the algorithm to declare an area "safe." For any zone identified as "data incomplete" or "low confidence," the system must default to a **manual verification status**.
    *   **Action:** Establish a rapid-response team (utilizing your existing 10-person team) to monitor weather radar and river gauge trends manually for these specific unmapped clusters during heavy rain events.
    *   **Protocol:** If regional water levels rise above a conservative threshold, trigger a **blanket alert** for the entire coastal sector, including unmapped zones, rather than waiting for localized sensor confirmation. It is safer to over-warn than to miss a threat in an unmapped area.

*   **Define Uncertainty Tiers (No False Precision):**
    Do not display specific water level predictions for unmapped zones. Instead, use coarse, defined tiers for public communication:
    *   **Confirmed:** Sensor data exists and validates rising levels.
    *   **Corroborated:** Regional trends suggest high risk; manual verification active.
    *   **Unverified (High Risk):** No sensor coverage; assume risk matches regional worst-case.
    *   *Constraint:* Never show a percentage probability (e.g., "87% chance of flooding") for these areas, as the underlying data does not support such precision.

*   **Community-Based Fallback (The "Last Mile" Fix):**
    Since digital maps are incomplete, leverage physical presence.
    *   **Action:** Identify community leaders or volunteers in the informal settlements immediately. Equip them with simple communication channels (SMS groups, radio, or messengers).
    *   **Protocol:** When the system triggers a regional "Corroborated" or "Unverified" alert, these human nodes are tasked with visual verification and sounding local alarms (whistles, sirens) independent of the digital system.

**3. Measurement & Transparency Plan**
*   **Eval Plan:** Before launch, run a simulation using historical flood data. Intentionally "hide" known flood zones in the map data to test if the "blanket alert" protocol triggers correctly when local sensors are missing.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Publish a **Data Card** with the system launch. Explicitly state: "Coverage is incomplete in informal settlements. Alerts in these zones rely on regional proxies and human verification, not direct sensor data." Do not claim full coverage.
*   **Residual Risk:** Acknowledge that false negatives remain possible in unmapped zones until better data is acquired. This risk is `UNVERIFIED` regarding specific location accuracy but `MANAGED` via the blanket alert protocol.

**4. Governance**
*   **Owner:** Assign one team member as the "Coverage Gap Owner" responsible for updating the map data and refining the manual verification list post-monsoon.
*   **Review Trigger:** The protocol must be reviewed immediately after the first major rain event or if any community reports a missed alert.

**Note:** This plan treats the lack of data as a known failure mode and manages it with human oversight rather than pretending the algorithm is sufficient. Legal counsel should review the wording of public disclosures regarding coverage limitations.
