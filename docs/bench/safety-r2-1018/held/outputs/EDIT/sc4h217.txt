**Pre-Launch Evaluation Plan: High-Risk Clause Recall**

Given the high stakes of missed liability shifts in merger agreements, you cannot certify the tool as "safe" or claim a specific recall rate without empirical evidence. Since you lack diverse historical data, you must construct a targeted adversarial evaluation rather than relying on aggregate performance metrics.

**1. Construct a "Golden Set" of Adversarial Probes**
Do not test on random contracts. Build a curated dataset of 20–30 complex merger agreement excerpts specifically engineered to contain the subtle liability shifts your tool currently misses.
*   **Positive Controls:** Insert known, difficult-to-detect liability shift clauses (e.g., nested indemnity caps, obscure change-of-control definitions) into standard text. You know these *must* be flagged.
*   **Negative Controls:** Include standard, non-critical clauses that resemble high-risk language but are harmless. The tool must *not* flag these to prevent alert fatigue.
*   **Diversity Simulation:** Since your historical data is limited, manually vary the phrasing, jurisdiction-specific terminology, and sentence structure in your positive controls to simulate the diversity you lack in training data.

**2. Define the Metric and Threshold**
*   **Metric:** Measure **Recall** (True Positives / Total Actual High-Risk Clauses) specifically for the "liability shift" category. Precision is secondary to safety here; false positives are annoying, but false negatives are malpractice.
*   **Threshold:** Set a **zero-tolerance stop condition** for any high-risk clause type where the tool fails to flag a positive control in the Golden Set. If the tool misses even one known liability shift in your test suite, it fails the gate for autonomous influence on final advice.

**3. Implement a Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Gate**
Because the tool has a known failure mode (missing subtle shifts) and the consequence is severe client harm, **do not allow fully automated final advice**.
*   **Mandatory Workflow:** The tool acts only as a "first pass" highlighter. Every document processed by the AI must undergo mandatory human review by a senior associate before any advice is finalized.
*   **Uncertainty Rendering:** If the tool outputs a confidence score, do not display raw percentages (e.g., "87% confident") to users, as this implies unverified precision. Instead, map outputs to defined coarse tiers: **"Confirmed Match," "Potential Match – Review Required,"** or **"No Critical Clauses Detected – Spot Check Recommended."**

**4. Execution and Reporting**
Run the Golden Set through the system. Record exactly which specific clauses were missed.
*   **Result Reporting:** Report the findings as **"UNVERIFIED"** for any metric not explicitly tested. Do not invent a global accuracy number.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Create a one-page "Model Card" for internal use stating:
    *   *Intended Use:* Drafting assistance for junior associates only.
    *   *Limitations:* Known difficulty with subtle liability shifts; limited training diversity.
    *   *Measured Performance:* Recall rate on the specific Golden Set (e.g., "Flagged 18/20 adversarial probes").
    *   *Owner:* Name the specific legal technologist responsible for re-evaluation if the model or contract types change.

**5. Legal and Regulatory Routing**
Whether this tool constitutes a "regulated automated decision" under the EU AI Act or requires specific client disclosures is a legal determination. Route this question immediately to your firm's counsel or compliance officer; do not assume the current workflow is compliant based solely on technical testing.

**Note:** This plan measures residual risk; it does not guarantee the absence of future errors.
**Note:** If the tool fails to flag any positive control in your Golden Set, the deployment must be halted until the model is retrained or the HITL gate is strengthened.
