### VR Motion Sickness & Safety Testing Protocol

**Objective:** Identify specific visual triggers causing motion sickness and falls within fixed hardware constraints to prevent physical harm before deployment.

**Phase 1: Harm Inventory & Risk Mapping**
Before testing, document the specific failure modes:
*   **Physical Injury:** Falls resulting in fractures or head trauma.
*   **Severe Physiological Distress:** Vomiting, vertigo, or migraines requiring medical attention.
*   **Exclusion:** Systematic inability for workers with specific vestibular sensitivities to complete training.
*   **Worst Case:** A trainee falls from height or into machinery due to disorientation, resulting in permanent disability.

**Phase 2: Controlled Trigger Isolation (The "One Variable" Rule)**
Do not test the full simulation initially. Build isolated "micro-scenes" that isolate single visual variables while keeping all others constant. Test these sequentially:
1.  **Locomotion Mechanics:** Test teleportation vs. smooth movement vs. vignette-tunneling.
2.  **Visual Flow:** Test static horizons vs. moving peripheral textures (e.g., floors moving while the user stands still).
3.  **Frame Stability:** Test camera bob/sway effects vs. locked camera positions.
4.  **Contrast & Flicker:** Test high-contrast patterns and rapid lighting changes known to trigger seizures or nausea.

**Phase 3: The Measurement Plan (Runnable Test Rows)**
Execute the following specific test rows. Each row includes a positive control (known bad) and negative control (known good).

| Harm Trigger | Probe Set (Input) | Positive Control (Known Bad) | Negative Control (Known Good) | Metric | Pass Threshold | Stop Condition |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Smooth Locomotion** | 2-min warehouse walk | No vignette, 60° FOV | Teleport only | Simulator Sickness Questionnaire (SSQ) Score | SSQ Increase < 15 pts | **HALT** if >2 users vomit or request immediate exit |
| **Peripheral Flow** | Standing on moving conveyor | Full peripheral motion | Static floor, moving target only | Time-to-onset of dizziness (seconds) | Median > 120s | **HALT** if any user loses balance/stances |
| **Camera Bob** | Walking animation | 1:1 head bob ratio | No head bob | User stability rating (1-5) | Avg Score > 4 | **HALT** if >1 user reports "severe" nausea |
| **Flicker/Contrast** | Flashing warning lights | 15Hz strobe | Steady light | Incident report of headache/eye strain | 0 Incidents | **HALT** on any seizure-like symptom |

**Phase 4: Human-in-the-Loop Safety Gates**
*   **Physical Tethering:** No testing occurs without a physical spotter and a soft-mat environment. This is non-negotiable for fall prevention.
*   **Immediate Abort:** Any participant reporting "moderate" nausea or dizziness must stop immediately. Do not push through symptoms.
*   **Uncertainty Disclosure:** Clearly label the experience as "Beta – High Risk of Motion Sickness" to all testers. Do not present it as a finished, safe product.

**Phase 5: Residual Risk Reporting**
*   Do not certify the system as "safe" or "nausea-free."
*   Report findings as: "Trigger X causes severe sickness in Y% of users under Z conditions."
*   If a trigger cannot be removed due to hardware limits, the feature using that trigger must be disabled or made optional (e.g., force teleportation only).

**Deliverable:** A finalized "Go/No-Go" matrix based strictly on the Stop Conditions above. If any Stop Condition is met, the feature is blocked from deployment until the specific visual trigger is redesigned or removed.

*Note: This protocol measures observed harm; it does not guarantee zero incidents in the wider population. Legal counsel should review liability waivers for industrial testing.*
