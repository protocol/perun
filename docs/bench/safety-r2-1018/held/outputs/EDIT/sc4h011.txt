**Immediate Safety Plan: Extreme Heat Wave Lockout Feature**

**1. Harm Inventory (MAP)**
Before proceeding, we must explicitly name the risks your feature introduces to users:
*   **Unsafe High-Stakes Automation:** The system executes an irreversible action (locking out manual control) that could directly endanger life (e.g., users with medical equipment requiring specific temperatures, elderly vulnerable to heat stress).
*   **Over-Reliance/Automation Bias:** The design assumes the grid signal is always correct and the user's local context is secondary, removing human agency during a crisis.
*   **Missing Uncertainty Disclosure:** Users are not informed of the confidence level of the "extreme heat" declaration or the specific criteria triggering the lockout.
*   **Bias/Unfair Outcomes:** The blanket rule may disproportionately harm vulnerable groups (elderly, disabled) who cannot relocate or tolerate temperature shifts, while having little impact on others.

**2. Measurement Plan (MEASURE)**
You cannot certify safety without data. Since you lack real-world data, you must simulate the failure modes adversarially:
*   **Adversarial Probe Set:** Create test scenarios where the "extreme heat" signal is triggered erroneously (false positive) or during a localized medical emergency. Include inputs representing users with documented medical dependencies.
*   **Metric & Threshold:** Measure the system's ability to *detect* and *allow* an override in these specific edge cases.
    *   *Pass Condition:* The system must allow a manual override in 100% of simulated medical emergency scenarios.
    *   *Stop Condition:* If the system successfully locks out a user in any simulated medical emergency scenario, launch is blocked.
*   **Residual Risk Reporting:** Do not claim "0% risk." Report: "Risk of harm to vulnerable populations remains UNVERIFIED due to lack of real-world data on medical dependencies. Mitigation relies entirely on the human-in-the-loop gate below."

**3. Risk Management (MANAGE)**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Mandatory):** You cannot fully automate this high-stakes action.
    *   *Implementation:* The "lockout" must be a *recommendation* or a *soft limit* by default, requiring explicit, verified human confirmation (e.g., a secondary approval step via a mobile app notification to the user or a designated caregiver) before becoming a hard lockout.
    *   *Emergency Override:* Implement a hardcoded, local physical or digital "panic button" that bypasses all grid signals and restores manual control immediately, no questions asked. This is non-negotiable for life-safety systems.
*   **Transparency Artifact (Model/Data Card):** Draft a one-page document for regulators and users stating:
    *   *Intended Use:* Grid load balancing during declared emergencies.
    *   *Limitations:* Does not account for individual medical needs; relies on external grid signals.
    *   *Known Failure Modes:* False positives in heat declarations; inability to detect user medical status.
    *   *Owner:* [Name of Lead Engineer].
*   **Disclosure:** Clearly inform users *before* activation that the system may restrict manual control during grid emergencies and provide the exact method to override it.

**4. Governance (GOVERN)**
*   Assign a single owner (e.g., CTO or Lead Product Manager) responsible for reviewing any changes to the heat-wave trigger logic or the override mechanism.
*   Schedule a re-review immediately after the first real-world heat event, regardless of outcome.

**Note to Regulators:** Present the harm inventory, the adversarial test results (even if simulated), the mandatory human-in-the-loop gate, and the emergency override mechanism as your primary safety controls. Explicitly state that full autonomy was rejected due to unverified risks to vulnerable populations.

Do not assert that the system is "safe" or "unbiased." State that residual risks are managed via the human gate and override, and that real-world impact on vulnerable groups remains UNVERIFIED pending post-launch monitoring.
