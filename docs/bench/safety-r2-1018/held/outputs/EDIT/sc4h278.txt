**Harm Inventory**
Your system performs **unsafe automation of high-stakes actions**. Automatically releasing gases and managing ventilation involves irreversible physical changes that could destroy crops, damage property, or harm human health if concentrations become toxic or oxygen is displaced. The constraint of using only a single sensor per greenhouse creates a **single point of failure**; if that sensor drifts, fails, or is spoofed, the system may fail to vent dangerous accumulations or falsely vent essential gases. This architecture lacks redundancy for a high-stakes physical actuation.

You cannot certify safety without testing the output behavior under failure conditions. You must run adversarial probes simulating sensor drift, total sensor failure, and conflicting data states. **Risk Management & Human Gate**
Because this system executes high-stakes, potentially irreversible physical actions (gas release) with a known single point of failure (one sensor), it requires a **human-in-the-loop gate** or a deterministic, hardware-level safety interlock that operates independently of the AI control logic. Fully autonomous execution of gas release based on a single data source is the failure mode this review identifies. Given your fixed deadline and budget, you cannot add sensors, but you must implement a non-AI, hard-wired mechanical or electronic override that forces ventilation if concentrations exceed a hard threshold, regardless of the controller's decision.
