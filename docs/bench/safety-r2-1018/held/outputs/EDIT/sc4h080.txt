**Do not claim a specific harm rate below one percent on your launch page.**

Your current data does not support a precise safety guarantee for the public. Asserting a specific error metric (e.g., "<1%") without a completed, rigorous evaluation plan constitutes fabricating a clearance metric. This is misleading because:

1.  **Unverified Metrics:** A harm rate that has not been measured against adversarial prompts, edge-case inputs, and specific subgroup failures is `UNVERIFIED`. Publishing a number implies a level of calibration and statistical certainty that your current testing likely does not possess.
2.  **Severity of Failure:** In high-stakes triage where delays endanger lives, the cost of a false negative (delaying care) is irreversible. A single unmeasured failure mode invalidates an aggregate "average" error rate.
3.  **False Precision:** Presenting a specific percentage invites over-reliance. Users may assume the system is safe for *their* specific pet's unique symptoms, whereas the bot may fail systematically on critical symptom clusters you have not yet probed.

**Immediate Action Plan:**
*   **Replace the Claim:** Substitute the specific percentage with a clear disclosure of limitations. State that the bot provides guidance based on available data but cannot replace immediate professional veterinary assessment, especially for critical symptoms.
*   **Implement a Human Gate:** For any output recommending "wait and see" or non-urgent care when critical symptom clusters are detected, insert a mandatory human-in-the-loop review or a hard-coded escalation to "Seek Immediate Vet Care" before the message reaches the user. Do not allow fully autonomous execution of low-urgency advice on high-risk inputs.
*   **Build the Eval:** Construct a test suite specifically targeting the critical symptom clusters you identified. Include known-bad positive controls (cases that must trigger urgent care) and known-good negative controls. Define a stop condition: if the bot fails *any* known-bad positive control, the feature cannot ship with autonomous triage for those inputs.
*   **Publish a Model Card:** Instead of a marketing claim, publish a transparency artifact detailing the intended use, known limitations (specifically the risk of delay in complex cases), and the status of your ongoing evaluation (marked as `UNVERIFIED` until the tests are run).

The decision to ship rests with your team, but it must be made with the explicit understanding that the residual risk is currently unknown, not quantified as "<1%".
