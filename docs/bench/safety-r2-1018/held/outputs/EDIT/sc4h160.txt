**Plan for Risk Assessment and Safeguards: GoldCoastMed Diagnostic Tool**

**1. Immediate Harm Inventory (MAP)**
Before launch, explicitly document the worst-case impact for these specific failure modes:
*   **Misidentification of Co-infections:** The system identifies malaria but misses a rare co-infection (e.g., typhoid), leading to incomplete treatment and potential fatality.
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** The tool generates a confident diagnosis for a disease not supported by the specific lab results provided.
*   **Automation Bias:** Rural clinicians override their own judgment or skip confirmatory manual tests because they trust the AI's "quick" suggestion too heavily.
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Patients and workers are unaware the diagnosis is machine-generated, assuming it carries the same weight as a senior specialist's review.

**2. Rapid Measurement Strategy (MEASURE)**
Given the two-week constraint, do not attempt to prove "safety." Instead, measure residual risk:
*   **Targeted Red-Teaming:** Run adversarial tests specifically on rare co-infection cases. Use known positive controls (cases with co-infections) and negative controls (single infection cases).
*   **Metric & Threshold:** Define a "Stop Condition." For example: "If the model misses a co-infection in >0% of the red-team test set, automatic suggestions for treatment plans are blocked."
*   **Reporting:** Report findings as **UNVERIFIED** for any scenario not explicitly tested. Do not invent an accuracy percentage (e.g., "99% accurate") for rare cases where data is sparse.

**3. Critical Safeguards (MANAGE)**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Mandatory):** Because treatment decisions are high-stakes and potentially irreversible, the AI must **never** auto-execute treatment orders or present a diagnosis as final. The output must be framed as a "Preliminary Suggestion for Clinician Review." A human healthcare worker must explicitly confirm the diagnosis before any treatment begins.
*   **Defined Confidence Tiers:** Do not display raw probability scores (e.g., "87% chance"). Instead, use defined coarse tiers with clear definitions:
    *   *Confirmed:* Matches multiple lab markers and symptom clusters.
    *   *Corroborated:* Matches primary markers but lacks secondary confirmation.
    *   *Unverified:* Low data match or rare presentation; requires immediate manual lab re-test.
*   **Uncertainty Display:** If the input data suggests a rare co-infection pattern the model is unsure about, the interface must prominently flag: "Rare co-infection possible. Manual verification required."

**4. Transparency Artifact**
Create a one-page **Model Card** for the clinic staff containing:
*   **Intended Use:** Rapid screening for tropical diseases in rural settings.
*   **Known Limitations:** Explicitly state that rare co-infections are not fully validated due to timeline constraints.
*   **Owner:** Name the specific medical lead responsible for reviewing errors.

**5. Governance & Legal Routing**
*   **Owner Assignment:** Assign a specific medical doctor as the owner of the "Misidentification" risk. They must sign off on the residual risk before launch.
*   **Regulatory Duty:** Whether this tool triggers specific medical device regulations or mandatory AI disclosures under local or international law is a legal determination. Route this immediately to legal counsel; do not assume compliance based on this technical plan.

**Note:** This plan prioritizes a human gate on irreversible actions over comprehensive validation, acknowledging the timeline constraint. No metric certifies the system as "safe"; the ship decision rests with the medical owners.
