### Pre-Deployment Validation Plan for SafeSip Water Monitoring

**1. Harm Inventory & Worst-Case Definition**
Before testing, explicitly record the primary harm: **False Negative Detection**.
*   **Failure Mode:** Sensors fail to trigger an alert when lead is present at levels harmful to children, specifically in older pipe configurations.
*   **Affected Group:** Students and staff in schools with aging infrastructure.
*   **Worst Realistic Case:** Chronic exposure to undetected lead causing developmental health issues, with liability falling on the district and SafeSip due to reliance on automated "all-clear" signals.

**2. Adversarial & Edge-Case Testing (The "Red Team" Phase)**
Do not rely on manufacturer specs alone. Construct a physical test bed mimicking the district's oldest infrastructure.
*   **Probe Set A (Low-Level Stress):** Introduce water samples with lead concentrations just below the EPA action level (e.g., 10–14 ppb) into pipes of varying ages and materials. Verify if the chemical reaction triggers the alert threshold consistently.
*   **Probe Set B (Interference Controls):** Run samples containing common interferents (chlorine, iron, sediment) without lead to ensure the system does not generate false positives that cause alert fatigue, nor false negatives due to signal masking.
*   **Probe Set C (Environmental Variance):** Test sensor performance under temperature and flow-rate extremes typical of school summer shutdowns and morning rush hours.

**3. The Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Irreversible Action Control)**
**Critical Constraint:** Do not enable fully automated "All Clear" declarations for the first semester.
*   **Initial Deployment Mode:** Configure the system to "Alert Only." Any positive detection triggers an immediate human verification protocol (manual water sampling by a certified technician) before any public notification or pipe-shutdown action is taken.
*   **Negative Result Protocol:** For the first 30 days of operation, treat automated "No Lead Detected" logs as **UNVERIFIED**. Require random manual spot-checks (e.g., 10% of sensors weekly) to validate the negative readings. Only transition to full automation if the manual audit shows zero discrepancies.

**4. Transparency & Uncertainty Reporting**
*   **Confidence Tiers:** Do not display raw sensor confidence percentages to administrators. Instead, report status as:
    *   **Confirmed Safe:** Automated pass + recent manual spot-check validation.
    *   **Unverified:** Automated pass without recent manual validation (default state for new installs).
    *   **Alert Triggered:** Requires immediate human investigation.
*   **Limitation Disclosure:** Clearly document in the administrator dashboard that the current hardware has a known detection floor and that "No Alert" does not guarantee zero lead, only "No lead detected above sensor threshold."

**5. Stop Conditions & Rollback**
Define strict launch abort criteria:
*   If the system fails to detect lead in **any** of the Probe Set A trials during validation, deployment is halted.
*   If manual spot-checks during the first month reveal any lead presence where the system reported "Unverified/Safe," revert to manual-only testing immediately.

**6. Ownership & Governance**
Assign a specific individual (e.g., "Lead Safety Officer") as the owner of this validation data. This person must sign off on the residual risk before the school year starts. If the fixed hardware budget prevents meeting the minimum detection sensitivity required for student safety in the oldest pipes, the plan must recommend delaying deployment or seeking emergency funding rather than shipping a known gap.

***

**Note:** This plan treats the automated alert as a decision-support tool, not a final safety certifier, until statistical reliability is proven via the manual audit loop.
**Note:** Legal counsel should review the specific wording of the "Limitation Disclosure" to ensure it meets state education and health code requirements.
