**Fail-Safe Mechanism: "Human-Confirmed Thermal Hold"**

Given the high-stakes nature of this feature (irreversible health risk to vulnerable populations) and your constraint of a tight integration window, full automation of heating shutoffs is unsafe. The system must default to **maintaining heat** unless a human explicitly confirms a shutoff.

1.  **Default State:** The predictor's output to "shut off" heating is treated as a **recommendation only**, not a command. The building management system (BMS) ignores automatic shutoff signals during cold snaps.
2.  **The Gate:** When the model predicts a shutoff is safe, the system triggers an alert to a designated facility manager or on-call engineer.
3.  **Explicit Confirmation:** The heating only shuts off if the human operator reviews the prediction, verifies current indoor/outdoor temperatures and resident status, and manually authorizes the action via a secure interface.
4.  **Hard Override:** Implement a physical or software "Thermal Hold" switch that bypasses the AI entirely, forcing heating to remain on regardless of algorithmic output. This must be easily accessible to staff.

**Pre-Launch Verification Checklist**

Do not launch until these specific checks are complete. Do not rely on aggregate accuracy metrics; focus on failure modes.

*   **[ ] Harm Inventory Completed:** Document specific scenarios where the model fails (e.g., rapid temperature drops, sensor malfunctions, specific building insulation types). Identify the worst-case health outcome for each.
*   **[ ] Adversarial "Cold Snap" Testing:** Run the model against historical data from extreme cold events where shutoffs would be catastrophic. Verify the system *refuses* to auto-shutoff in these cases without human input.
*   **[ ] False Positive Probe:** Inject known "bad" inputs (e.g., faulty sensor data suggesting warmth) to ensure the system defaults to "heat on" rather than trusting the erroneous data.
*   **[ ] Human-in-the-Loop Drill:** Simulate a shutoff recommendation. Measure the time it takes for a human to receive the alert and make a decision. Ensure the workflow is feasible within your operational reality.
*   **[ ] Uncertainty Display:** If the model shows confidence levels to operators, ensure they are presented as defined tiers (e.g., "Low Confidence – Verify Manually") rather than precise percentages (e.g., "87%"), which can induce over-reliance.
*   **[ ] Rollback Plan:** Define the exact steps to disable the AI module instantly if erroneous behavior is detected post-launch. Test this switch.
*   **[ ] Transparency Artifact:** Draft a one-page "Model Card" for your internal team and regulators stating: intended use (energy optimization only), known limitations (fails in rapid cold snaps), and the mandatory human gate. Do not claim the system is "safe" or "unbiased."

**Critical Note:** No metric can certify this system as "safe" or "unbiased." If you cannot measure the failure rate for specific vulnerable subgroups, mark that risk as **UNVERIFIED** and maintain the human gate indefinitely.
