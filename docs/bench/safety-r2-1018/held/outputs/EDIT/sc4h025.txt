### Harm Inventory & Critical Findings
Before testing, we must name the specific harms this feature introduces:
*   **Unsafe High-Stakes Automation:** Remotely locking a door is an irreversible, high-stakes action with immediate physical safety consequences. Automating this based on billing status without a human-in-the-loop gate violates the fundamental safety floor.
*   **Bias & Unfair Outcomes:** Automated enforcement disproportionately impacts vulnerable groups who may rely on caregivers for payments or lack digital literacy to resolve billing errors quickly.
*   **Over-Reliance:** The system invites property managers to trust an automated "debt collection" tool over human judgment regarding tenant safety.
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Tenants may not be aware that non-payment triggers immediate physical lockdown rather than standard legal eviction processes.

### Why Testing Cannot Fix This
You requested a testing strategy to ensure vulnerable populations are "never accidentally locked out." **This guarantee is impossible to verify.**
*   **False Negatives:** No amount of red-teaming or adversarial prompting can prove a negative ("never") in a complex real-world environment involving legacy hardware and human behavior.
*   **The Legacy Constraint:** The inability to support two-factor authentication (2FA) for override commands removes the primary technical control for preventing accidental or malicious lockouts. Without 2FA, there is no secure way for a human to override the system in an emergency if the automation fails or acts on bad data.
*   **Regulatory Reality:** In Germany, and under broader EU frameworks (such as the EU AI Act), automated decisions that significantly affect individuals' physical safety and access to housing are subject to strict scrutiny. Locking someone out of their home via an algorithm during a billing dispute likely violates tenant protection laws and product safety directives, irrespective of software correctness.

### Required Management Controls (The Only Safe Path)
To manage residual risk, you must implement a **Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Gate** as a hard floor:
1.  **Stop Automation:** The system must **not** automatically disable doors. It should only flag accounts for review.
2.  **Human Verification:** A trained human operator must verify the debt, confirm no vulnerable persons are inside (via contact or building staff), and manually authorize any restriction.
3.  **Physical Override:** Since 2FA is impossible on legacy hardware, you must rely on mechanical keys or local physical overrides that cannot be disabled remotely, ensuring egress is always possible from the inside.
4.  **Disclosure:** Clearly inform tenants in their contract that remote disabling is a manual, last-resort measure, not an automated penalty.

### Conclusion
There is no "safe" version of an automated lockout feature for billing disputes on legacy hardware without 2FA. The only compliant and ethical path is to remove the automation entirely. The engineering team should pivot to building a **notification and manual review workflow**, not a remote deactivation switch. Any attempt to ship the current design exposes the company to severe liability and potential harm to residents.
