**Pre-Launch Safety Verification Plan: Smart-Crutch Locking Mechanism**

**Immediate Action: Implement a Mandatory Human-in-the-Loop Gate**
Given the risk of immediate, irreversible physical injury (falls) from a locking failure, **do not proceed with an autonomous pilot launch.** The "floor" for high-stakes automation involving physical safety is a human gate. For the two-day pilot, the device must operate in a "confirmed-lock" mode where a secondary verification (either a distinct audible/visual confirmation signal verified by a supervisor or a manual override check by a therapist) occurs before the user bears full weight. Fully autonomous locking without this gate is the failure mode to prevent, not the feature to ship.

**Harm Inventory & Worst-Case Scenario**
*   **Harm:** Unsafe automation of a high-stakes action (locking mechanism failure).
*   **Affected:** Elderly users with limited mobility.
*   **Worst Case:** Instantaneous lock failure during weight transfer results in a fall, causing hip fractures, head trauma, or loss of confidence leading to permanent immobility.

**Measurement Plan (Runnable Tests for Remaining 48 Hours)**
Since physical lab access is limited, focus testing on the specific "sudden weight bearing" failure mode using the following structured approach. Do not assert a "0% failure rate"; report residual risk based on these probes.

1.  **Probe Set A: Simulated Sudden Load (Edge Case)**
    *   **Input:** Drop a calibrated weight (equivalent to 120% of max user weight) onto the sensor from a height of 5cm to simulate a stumble or rapid weight shift, rather than a slow press.
    *   **Positive Control:** A known-failing sensor unit (if available) or a simulated delay injection in the firmware to ensure the test detects latency.
    *   **Negative Control:** A unit previously validated on a slow, steady press.
    *   **Metric:** Time-to-lock (milliseconds) from initial contact to full mechanical engagement.
    *   **Pass Threshold:** Owner-defined (e.g., <50ms). **Stop Condition:** Any single instance where time-to-lock exceeds the threshold or mechanical slip is observed triggers an immediate halt to pilot preparations.
    *   **Budget:** Test 100% of pilot units (8 devices) with 5 drops each.

2.  **Probe Set B: Sensor Noise & False Negatives**
    *   **Input:** Apply weight while introducing vibration or uneven surface angles (simulating real-world floor conditions).
    *   **Metric:** Binary pass/fail of lock engagement.
    *   **Stop Condition:** Any failure to engage.

3.  **Probe Set C: Injection & Agency Check**
    *   **Input:** Verify the system cannot be tricked into unlocking while weight is present (e.g., rapid lift-and-replace maneuvers).
    *   **Metric:** Lock state integrity.

**Transparency & Residual Risk Reporting**
*   **Confidence Tiering:** Do not display raw confidence percentages (e.g., "99% safe") to users or stakeholders. Instead, define coarse tiers for the pilot status:
    *   *Verified:* Passed all sudden-load probes with zero slips.
    *   *Unverified:* Any probe not executed due to time/resource constraints.
    *   *Failed:* Any stop condition triggered.
*   **Artifact:** Create a one-page "Safety Data Card" for the pilot listing the intended use (supervised mobility aid), the specific limitation (tested only on simulated sudden loads, not real falls), and the owner of the risk decision. Mark any untested scenarios as **UNVERIFIED**.

**Governance**
*   **Owner:** Name one specific individual responsible for the "Go/No-Go" decision based on the test results.
*   **Decision Rule:** If any unit fails the sudden-load probe, the pilot is paused. No amount of schedule pressure justifies bypassing the stop condition for a fall-risk device.

**Note:** This plan addresses product output safety; legal obligations regarding medical device disclosure must be routed to counsel immediately.
**Note:** Do not fabricate a safety metric; if testing cannot be completed in 48 hours, the residual risk remains "Unverified."
