**Prioritized Mitigation Plan (48-Hour Window)**

**1. Immediate Logic Change: "Fail-Safe Open" with Alert (Priority: Critical)**
*   **Action:** Reconfigure the barrier control logic immediately. If the license plate recognition (LPR) confidence score falls below a high threshold (e.g., due to dirt or unusual fonts), the system must **default to raising the barrier** rather than lowering it or remaining closed.
*   **Implementation:** Modify the decision engine to treat "uncertain read" as "authorized entry pending verification" rather than "unauthorized/deny."
*   **Safety Outcome:** This eliminates the risk of the barrier slamming down on an approaching vehicle (physical damage) or trapping a pedestrian. A false negative becomes a minor operational inconvenience (a car entering without a logged plate) rather than a safety hazard.
*   **Compensation:** Trigger a high-priority alert to the on-site attendant's mobile device or control room screen with a snapshot of the "uncertain" vehicle for manual logging.

**2. Deploy Human-in-the-Loop for Low-Confidence Events (Priority: High)**
*   **Action:** Mandate human verification for any entry event where the LPR confidence is not "High."
*   **Implementation:** Utilize existing staff. When the system flags a low-confidence read (which now triggers the "Fail-Safe Open"), the attendant must visually verify the plate against the captured image before the session is closed in the database.
*   **Safety Outcome:** Ensures that while traffic flows safely, the data integrity is maintained post-entry by a human, preventing revenue loss without risking physical safety.

**3. Dynamic Speed Reduction via Signage (Priority: Medium)**
*   **Action:** Since hardware sensors cannot be added, use existing digital signage or temporary physical signs at the entry lane.
*   **Implementation:** Display "Camera Verification Ahead – Reduce Speed" or place temporary cones to force a slower approach angle.
*   **Safety Outcome:** Slower vehicle approach speeds reduce the kinetic energy involved if a mechanical failure occurs and give the camera system more frames to capture a clear image of dirty or angled plates, naturally improving recognition rates without code changes.

**4. Aggressive Logging and "Watch List" Creation (Priority: Medium)**
*   **Action:** Configure the system to log every "Fail-Safe Open" event with timestamp, image, and reason code.
*   **Implementation:** Create a real-time dashboard for the engineering team to monitor the frequency of these events during the first 24 hours.
*   **Safety Outcome:** Allows rapid identification of specific font types or dirt patterns causing issues, enabling targeted manual overrides or future model updates.

**5. Pedestrian Lane Segregation Protocol (Priority: High)**
*   **Action:** If the entry lane shares space with pedestrian access, institute a strict procedural rule.
*   **Implementation:** Staff must physically stand at the pedestrian crossing point during peak entry times to manually halt foot traffic whenever a vehicle is approaching, regardless of barrier status.
*   **Safety Outcome:** Adds a redundant human layer specifically for pedestrian safety, decoupling it from the unreliable automated sensor logic.

**Note:** This plan prioritizes physical safety and damage prevention over perfect data capture; revenue reconciliation for "Fail-Safe Open" events must be handled manually post-entry. Do not attempt to retrain the AI model within 48 hours; focus entirely on control logic and operational procedures.
