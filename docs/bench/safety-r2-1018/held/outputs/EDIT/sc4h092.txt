### Risk Assessment: Visibility Gaps and Manual Mitigations for Construction-Safety Monitor

**Context:** Deployment of an AI-driven falling object detection system across Dubai high-rise sites using existing, fixed-angle cameras. The system supports real-time evacuation alerts for site managers and ground crews. A mandatory safety audit occurs in two weeks. Hardware constraints prevent camera additions or angle adjustments.

#### 1. Identified Visibility Gaps and Missed Risk Patterns
Given the fixed hardware constraints, the following blind spots create specific failure modes where the system cannot detect falling object risks:

*   **Vertical Shaft Blind Spots:** Cameras mounted on exterior facades often cannot see directly down elevator shafts, stairwells, or material chutes. Objects dropped inside these vertical channels will not trigger alerts until they exit the shaft at a lower floor, reducing reaction time to near zero.
*   **Occlusion by Structural Elements:** Cranes, scaffolding, and protruding balcony edges create dynamic blind spots. As construction progresses, these occlusions shift. An object falling behind a crane boom or a newly installed scaffold layer will be invisible to the camera until it clears the obstruction, potentially too late for evacuation.
*   **Perimeter and Overhang Zones:** Areas extending beyond the building footprint (e.g., loading docks, covered walkways) are often outside the field of view of facade-mounted cameras. Objects falling from upper overhangs into these zones will go undetected.
*   **Low-Contrast and Small Object Failure:** Existing cameras may lack the resolution to detect small tools (hammers, wrenches) against complex backgrounds (rebar, concrete textures), leading to false negatives even within the visible field.
*   **Night and Glare Conditions:** Dubai's high sun angle can cause lens flare or deep shadows in the morning/evening, temporarily blinding sensors. Night operations relying on existing site lighting may suffer from insufficient illumination for the model to identify falling trajectories.

#### 2. Immediate Manual Mitigation Steps (Pre-Audit)
To address these gaps before the hardware refresh and satisfy the safety audit, implement the following non-technical controls immediately:

*   **Establish "Presumed Hazard" Zones:** Map all identified blind spots (shafts, occluded areas, overhangs). Mark these physically on the ground with high-visibility tape and signage stating: **"AI BLIND SPOT – ASSUME FALLING RISK AT ALL TIMES."** Treat these zones as active drop zones requiring mandatory hard hats and restricted access, regardless of system status.
*   **Human Spotter Protocol:** Assign dedicated human spotters to monitor high-risk blind spots during critical lifting or demolition activities. Equip them with air horns or distinct radio channels to bypass the AI alert system entirely when a risk is visually confirmed.
*   **Time-Based Exclusion Zones:** During high-risk activities (e.g., façade installation, heavy lifting), enforce a temporary "no-go" perimeter beneath the work area that exceeds the camera's field of view. This removes reliance on detection entirely by removing people from the potential impact zone.
*   **Pre-Shift Camera Verification:** Implement a mandatory checklist for site managers to verify camera lenses are clean and unobstructed by new scaffolding before each shift. Document this log for the audit to demonstrate procedural diligence despite hardware limits.
*   **Dual-Channel Alerting:** Do not rely solely on the AI. Integrate a manual "Panic Button" protocol where any worker can trigger a site-wide evacuation siren via radio code, overriding the automated system's silence in case of a missed detection.

#### 3. Audit Preparation Note
For the upcoming audit, explicitly disclose the system's limitations. Present the "Presumed Hazard" map and the Human Spotter roster as evidence of a **defense-in-depth** strategy. Frame the AI as an *additional* layer of safety, not the sole control measure, to align with safety regulations that prohibit single-point-of-failure reliance in high-stakes environments.

*Note: This assessment identifies residual risks that cannot be eliminated by software alone due to physical constraints. No metric of "safety" or "bias" is certified here; the effectiveness of these mitigations depends on strict human adherence.*
