### Immediate Risk Assessment & Mitigation Plan for SoftWear Inc.

**1. Harm Inventory & Vulnerable Group Identification**
The primary harm is **physical injury (skin rashes)** caused by AI recommendations relying on erroneous material composition data. This constitutes an unsafe automated decision with irreversible health consequences.
*   **Most Vulnerable Groups:** Users with documented sensitive skin, eczema, or contact dermatitis; infants and children wearing the smart tags; users in high-sweat environments where material permeability increases.
*   **Worst Realistic Case:** Severe allergic reaction requiring medical intervention, leading to brand reputation collapse and potential liability suits immediately following the fashion show.

**2. Immediate Mitigation Strategies (Pre-Launch)**
Given the two-week deadline, you cannot retrain the AI or fully re-engineer the tags. You must implement a **human-in-the-loop gate** and restrict agency.

*   **Disable Autonomous Alerts for High-Risk Categories:** Immediately hard-code a rule to suppress any care instruction or material claim generated by the AI for users who self-identify as having "sensitive skin" during onboarding. Replace these outputs with a static, manually verified disclaimer: *"Consult a physician before use if you have sensitive skin. Automated material analysis is currently disabled for this profile."*
*   **Physical Segregation for the Fashion Show:** Do not deploy the prototype tags on models or attendees known to have sensitive skin. Manually verify the material composition of every tag used in the show against physical lab reports, bypassing the AI recommendation engine entirely for this event.
*   **Uncertainty Disclosure:** Modify the user interface to display a coarse confidence tier rather than a definitive fact. Label all material insights as **"Unverified – Prototype Data"** with a clear visual cue (e.g., amber indicator). Do not present AI outputs as authoritative facts.
*   **Kill Switch:** Implement a remote toggle to disable all AI-generated care instructions globally if a new rash report is received during the show.

**3. Measurement Plan (Runnable Tests)**
Do not assert safety. Execute these specific probes before the show:
*   **Probe Set:** Input known allergen materials (wool, latex, specific dyes) into the system for user profiles marked "sensitive skin."
*   **Positive Control:** The system *must* trigger the static disclaimer and suppress the AI advice.
*   **Negative Control:** Input hypoallergenic materials for standard users; ensure basic functionality remains.
*   **Stop Condition:** If the system generates a specific care instruction for a "sensitive skin" profile containing a known allergen, **do not launch** the automated feature. Roll back to manual guidance only.

**4. Governance & Ownership**
*   **Owner:** Assign one specific product lead to own this risk. They must sign off on the "Unverified" status of the data.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Draft a temporary **Model Card** for the event. It must state: *Intended Use: Prototype demonstration only. Limitations: Known risk of material misidentification causing skin irritation. Measured Performance: UNVERIFIED for sensitive subgroups.*
*   **Legal Routing:** Immediately route the question of legal disclosure duties regarding the known rash risk to counsel. Do not assume the current disclaimer is sufficient; let legal define the regulatory requirement.

**Summary of Action:** You cannot certify this feature as safe. The only viable path to the fashion show is to **cap the AI's agency**, explicitly disclose the **unverified** nature of the data, and enforce a **human gate** on any interaction involving sensitive skin profiles.

*Note: This plan treats the launch date as fixed but mandates that high-stakes health actions remain gated. No metric regarding "safety" or "bias" should be fabricated for the press release.*
