**Preparation Plan: Pet Health Alert Validation**

**1. Harm Inventory & Risk Definition**
Before testing, explicitly define the two primary failure modes:
*   **False Negative (Missed Detection):** The algorithm fails to alert on a life-threatening event (e.g., arrhythmia, respiratory distress) during sleep. *Worst Case:* Preventable pet death.
*   **False Positive (Excessive Alarm):** The system alerts on normal sleep variations. *Worst Case:* Owner anxiety, "alert fatigue" leading to ignored real warnings, and unnecessary vet visits.

**2. Testing Strategy: The "Golden Set" Evaluation**
Do not rely solely on live field tests yet. Construct a static, labeled dataset ("Golden Set") to run against your algorithm repeatedly as you tune it.
*   **Data Composition:** Aggregate historical veterinary data and simulated signals representing:
    *   *Critical Events:* Known patterns of cardiac arrest, severe bradycardia, and apnea in elderly dogs.
    *   *Normal Variance:* Deep sleep cycles, dreaming (REM), and movement artifacts common in older dogs.
    *   *Edge Cases:* Low battery signal noise, loose collar fit simulations, and extreme ambient temperatures.
*   **Controls:** Ensure every test batch includes known "bad" inputs (critical events that must trigger) and "good" inputs (normal sleep that must not trigger).

**3. Metric Definition & Thresholds**
Define pass/fail criteria before running tests. Do not optimize for a single metric.
*   **Sensitivity (Recall):** Target near 100% for critical events. A single missed life-threatening event in the Golden Set is a block condition.
*   **Specificity:** Measure the false alarm rate per 24-hour cycle. Set a maximum threshold (e.g., <1 false alarm per week per dog) based on owner tolerance interviews.
*   **Latency:** Measure time from event onset to alert generation, accounting for your battery-saving transmission intervals.

**4. Battery-Constrained Simulation**
Since you cannot transmit continuously, simulate the "duty cycle" in software:
*   Run the algorithm on high-frequency data, but only allow "alerts" to pass through at your planned transmission intervals.
*   Test if critical events occurring *between* transmission windows are still detected once the device wakes up, or if the smoothing logic accidentally filters them out as noise.

**5. Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Launch Condition)**
*   **Irreversible Action Control:** Until the False Negative rate is proven zero on the Golden Set, the device must not operate in "Silent/Auto-Only" mode.
*   **Staged Rollout:** Launch initially to a beta group of 20–50 users with a mandatory "Human Review" step where ambiguous alerts are flagged as "Check Manually" rather than "Emergency," or ensure immediate customer support availability to triage incoming alerts before they reach the owner.

**6. Transparency Artifact (Model Card)**
Draft a one-page "Performance & Limitations" document for customers and vets.
*   State clearly: "Designed to detect X, Y, Z; known limitations include [specific sleep states or conditions]."
*   List the sensitivity/specificity results from your Golden Set test. If a metric is unverified for a specific breed or weight class, label it "UNVERIFIED" rather than estimating.

**7. Governance**
Assign one team member as the "Safety Owner" responsible for reviewing any missed alerts or false positives during the beta. No algorithm update ships without re-running the full Golden Set.

**Note:** This plan identifies risks and measurement methods; it does not certify the device as safe. Legal requirements for medical/veterinary device disclosures must be routed to counsel.
