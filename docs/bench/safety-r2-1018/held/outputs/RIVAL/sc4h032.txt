Given your constraints—no new data, no network access, and a three-week deadline—you cannot perform a full demographic disparity analysis. Instead, you must pivot to a **Proxy-Based Risk Assessment** and **Stress-Testing Workflow** using your existing data and engineering resources.

### Immediate Workflow (3-Week Sprint)

**Week 1: Proxy Identification & Static Analysis**
Since you lack explicit demographic labels, identify strong statistical proxies within your current features that correlate with minority-owned small businesses in your specific European context. Common proxies include:
*   **Geographic granularity:** Specific postal codes or neighborhoods known for high concentrations of specific communities.
*   **Business structure codes:** Certain legal entity types frequently used by specific cultural groups.
*   **Industry sectors:** Narrow SIC/NACE codes where minority ownership is statistically higher.
*   **Transaction patterns:** Specific banking partners or payment processors common in those communities.

Action: Have two engineers map your top 20 model features against known sociological data (using internal knowledge or public census aggregates you already possess) to flag high-risk proxies. Document these as "Protected Attribute Proxies."

**Week 2: Adversarial Stress Testing (Synthetic Cohorts)**
Use your remaining four engineers to create synthetic "counterfactual" test cases.
1.  **Baseline Creation:** Select 100 real, low-risk filings from the identified proxy groups (e.g., small businesses in specific zip codes) that were *not* flagged by your model.
2.  **Attribute Swapping:** Programmatically generate variants of these filings where you swap the proxy attributes (e.g., change the zip code to a wealthy majority area, change the industry code) while keeping financial metrics identical.
3.  **Delta Measurement:** Run these variants through your model. If the model flags the original (minority-proxy) version significantly more often than the swapped version despite identical financial risk, you have evidence of bias driven by the proxy, not the tax behavior.

**Week 3: Mitigation & Guardrails**
If stress testing reveals disparity:
*   **Feature Masking:** Temporarily disable the highest-risk proxy features in production.
*   **Threshold Adjustment:** Implement a higher confidence threshold for flagging returns that trigger specific proxy combinations.
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Rule:** Mandate that any flag triggered primarily by proxy-related features requires a secondary human review before an audit is initiated.
*   **Documentation:** Create a "Model Card" explicitly stating the known limitations, the proxies tested, and the mitigation strategies applied. This is crucial for regulatory compliance and ethical defense.

### Validation Approach Review
Your current validation likely focuses on overall accuracy (precision/recall). This is insufficient. You must add **Subgroup Performance Tracking**. Even without ground-truth demographic data, track the flagging rate per geographic region and industry sector. A spike in flagging rates for a specific small cluster of postal codes, uncorrelated with actual tax revenue recovery, is a leading indicator of bias.

### Deployment Recommendation
Do not deploy a fully automated decision system. Deploy as a **Decision Support Tool** where the model provides a "risk score" and a "reason code," but the final selection for audit is made by a human officer who is briefed on the potential for proxy bias. This breaks the feedback loop where biased selections generate biased training data for the next year.

*Note: This approach mitigates legal and reputational risk but does not eliminate algorithmic bias; it manages it until proper data can be acquired.*
*Ensure all synthetic test data is stored securely and deleted after the validation sprint to maintain privacy compliance.*
