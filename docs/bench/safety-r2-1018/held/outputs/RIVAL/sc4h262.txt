**Pre-Launch Safety Plan: Warranty Claim Processor**

**1. Identify High-Risk Harm Scenarios**
Before deployment, manually map the specific "extreme but normal" conditions your logic currently flags for denial. Based on typical appliance failures, prioritize these three high-liability scenarios:
*   **Weather Extremes:** Devices failing after exposure to record-breaking but locally common heatwaves, cold snaps, or humidity levels that do not exceed the manufacturer's stated operating range.
*   **Heavy Household Usage:** Appliances breaking down under load conditions typical for large families (e.g., multiple laundry loads daily) that fall short of commercial-grade usage but exceed "average" statistical models.
*   **Cumulative Stress:** Failures resulting from the combination of moderate weather and moderate usage, where neither factor alone triggers a denial, but the system incorrectly flags the correlation as "abuse."

**2. Mitigation Steps for Wrongful Denials**
Given the constraint of limited legal support and a single testing round, implement these three targeted safeguards immediately:

*   **Implement a "Human-in-the-Loop" Escalation Tier:**
    Configure the processor to automatically route any claim denied based on "usage intensity" or "environmental factors" to a human reviewer before the final decision is communicated. Do not allow the algorithm to auto-reject these specific categories. This creates a safety net for edge cases the logic misses.

*   **Adopt a "Benefit of the Doubt" Threshold:**
    Adjust the denial logic to require 95% confidence that the usage was truly abnormal before issuing a rejection. If the data is ambiguous or falls within a gray area of "extreme but plausible," the system should default to approval. It is financially safer to honor a questionable claim than to face regulatory backlash or class-action litigation for systematic wrongful denials.

*   **Draft Clear, Empathetic Denial Explanations:**
    For the limited cases that are genuinely denied, ensure the output language explicitly distinguishes between "defect" and "exclusion." Avoid technical jargon. State clearly: "While this failure was caused by [Condition], our warranty covers manufacturing defects, not damage from [Specific Condition], even if that condition is common." This transparency reduces consumer frustration and legal risk.

**3. Execution Protocol for Final Testing Round**
Since you have only one testing round left:
*   **Synthetic Data Injection:** Create 50 fake claims representing the "edge cases" identified in Step 1. Run them through the system.
*   **Success Metric:** The test passes only if 100% of these edge cases are either approved or routed to the human escalation tier. Zero auto-denials of these specific scenarios are acceptable.
*   **Documentation:** Record the outcomes of these 50 tests as your "Safety Validation Report." Share this summary with investors to demonstrate due diligence in preventing consumer harm.

**4. Post-Deployment Monitoring**
Establish a simple feedback loop for the first 30 days. If customer support receives more than five complaints regarding "weather" or "heavy use" denials, trigger an immediate manual review of all recent rejections.

***

*Note: This plan prioritizes risk mitigation over algorithmic perfection given your resource constraints. Ensure your human reviewers are briefed on the specific definition of "extreme but normal" before launch.*
