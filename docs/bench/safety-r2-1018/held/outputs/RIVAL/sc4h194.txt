### Pre-Launch Safety Validation Framework for Skin Diagnosis Widget

**Objective:** Validate diagnostic accuracy and safety of the skin cancer identification model across all skin types, with specific emphasis on closing the data gap for darker skin tones before the eight-week regulatory deadline.

#### Phase 1: Strategic Data Augmentation (Weeks 1-3)
Before testing can begin, the evaluation dataset must be rebalanced to reflect the target population.
*   **Synthetic Data Generation:** Partner with medical imaging institutions to access de-identified dermatological images of Fitzpatrick skin types IV–VI. Where real patient data is unavailable, utilize validated generative adversarial networks (GANs) trained on diverse dermatological datasets to create high-fidelity synthetic lesions on darker skin backgrounds.
*   **External Collaboration:** Establish immediate data-sharing agreements with diverse clinics in London and international partners to acquire real-world validation images.
*   **Dataset Composition:** Construct a "Challenge Set" comprising 40% darker skin tones (Fitzpatrick IV–VI), ensuring representation of various lesion types (melanoma, basal cell carcinoma, benign nevi) and anatomical locations.

#### Phase 2: Stratified Performance Benchmarking (Weeks 4-5)
Execute a blinded evaluation using the rebalanced dataset to quantify performance disparities.
*   **Stratified Metrics:** Do not rely on overall accuracy. Calculate sensitivity (recall), specificity, and F1-scores separately for each Fitzpatrick skin type.
*   **Disparity Thresholds:** Define strict acceptance criteria. For example, the difference in sensitivity between the lightest and darkest skin groups must not exceed 5%. Any condition where the model fails to identify cancer in darker skin at a rate higher than in lighter skin constitutes a critical failure.
*   **False Negative Analysis:** Prioritize the analysis of false negatives (missed cancers) in underrepresented groups. A single missed melanoma in the test set for darker skin requires immediate model retraining.

#### Phase 3: Adversarial and Edge-Case Testing (Weeks 6-7)
Simulate real-world mobile conditions that may disproportionately affect image quality for darker skin.
*   **Lighting Variance:** Test images under varying lighting conditions (low light, harsh flash, indoor tungsten) to ensure the model does not misinterpret shadow or melanin concentration as pathology.
*   **Device Diversity:** Run the widget on a range of mobile devices with different camera sensors and default image processing algorithms (e.g., skin smoothing features) to verify consistent performance.
*   **Confounding Factors:** Include images with common benign conditions prevalent in darker skin (e.g., dermatosis papulosa nigra) to ensure the model does not flag these as malignant.

#### Phase 4: Regulatory Documentation and Go/No-Go Decision (Week 8)
*   **Bias Audit Report:** Compile a comprehensive report detailing the dataset composition, stratified performance metrics, and results of adversarial testing. This document is essential for regulatory compliance.
*   **Risk Mitigation Plan:** If disparities persist, implement a "human-in-the-loop" fallback where cases involving darker skin tones with low confidence scores are automatically routed to a human dermatologist rather than providing an automated result.
*   **Final Gate:** The release is contingent upon meeting the predefined disparity thresholds. If the model cannot demonstrate equitable safety, the launch must be delayed or the feature restricted until retraining is successful.

**Note:** This framework prioritizes patient safety over the deadline; releasing with known biases poses significant legal and ethical risks. Immediate engagement with dermatological experts is required to validate the synthetic data quality.
