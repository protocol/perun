### Risk Assessment: Battery-Recycling Bot Misclassification

**1. Hazard Identification**
The primary risk is the bot instructing users to dispose of lithium-ion batteries in regular household waste. This creates an immediate fire hazard in waste collection trucks and sorting facilities, violates Berlin's strict electronic waste regulations (ElektroG), and poses severe reputational and legal liability for the organization.

**2. Root Cause Analysis (Hypothetical based on logs)**
Given the inability to update the CMS, the error likely stems from:
*   **Ambiguous User Input:** Users describing devices in colloquial terms (e.g., "old phone battery") that the bot's current logic maps incorrectly to general waste categories.
*   **Logic Gaps:** Missing specific conditional checks for lithium chemistry in the bot's decision tree for certain device categories.
*   **Training Data Bias:** If AI-driven, the model may be over-indexing on general "small waste" patterns without sufficient weighting for hazardous material exceptions.

**3. Impact Severity**
*   **Safety:** Critical. Potential for property damage, injury, and emergency service deployment.
*   **Compliance:** High. Violation of German circular economy laws could result in fines and operational shutdown.
*   **Trust:** High. Environmentally conscious users in Berlin will lose trust rapidly if the bot contradicts known safety norms.

**4. Risk Mitigation Strategies (Pre-Launch)**
Since the CMS is locked, mitigation must occur at the interface and operational levels:
*   **Hard-Coded Interface Interstitials:** Implement a mandatory, non-skippable warning screen in the mobile app frontend (independent of the CMS) that appears before any disposal instruction is shown. This screen should explicitly state: *"Never place lithium batteries in regular trash. If you are unsure, select 'Unsure' below."*
*   **"Fail-Safe" Default Logic:** Modify the bot's routing logic (if code-deployable separate from CMS) to default any ambiguous query regarding batteries to "Hazardous Waste Collection Point" rather than "Regular Trash."
*   **Input Constraint:** Restrict free-text input for battery types. Replace with a strict multiple-choice menu of known safe/unsafe items to reduce classification ambiguity.

### Contingency Communication Plan

**Objective:** Immediately inform users of potential errors and provide a verified safe path for disposal while the bot is active.

**1. Immediate In-App Notification (Day 0)**
*   **Mechanism:** Push notification and banner within the mobile app.
*   **Message:** "Important Safety Update: Our new recycling bot is in a testing phase. If you are disposing of lithium batteries (phones, laptops, e-bikes), please ignore automated suggestions that mention regular trash. Always take these to a dedicated recycling bin or a 'Sondermüll' collection point. We are correcting our data."

**2. Public Statement & Social Media (Day 0-1)**
*   **Channels:** Twitter/X, local Berlin community forums, partner environmental groups.
*   **Key Message:** Transparency about the "pre-release validation" status. Acknowledge the log anomalies proactively.
*   **Call to Action:** Provide a direct, static link to the official Berlin city waste management map for hazardous waste, bypassing the bot entirely for high-risk items.

**3. User Feedback Loop**
*   **Action:** Add a prominent "Report Wrong Advice" button in the chat interface.
*   **Process:** Route these reports directly to the engineering team for manual review and immediate patching of the decision logic, even if the CMS remains locked.

**4. Escalation Protocol**
*   If a user reports a fire incident or near-miss linked to the bot's advice, immediately suspend the bot's public availability via a feature flag and issue a press release coordinating with Berlin fire safety authorities.

**Note:** This plan assumes the mobile app binary can be updated or configured independently of the locked CMS. If the app itself cannot be changed, the launch must be delayed regardless of the maintenance window.
