**Pre-Launch Legal Validation Plan for Buenos Aires Co-op**

Given your constraints (five-person team, minimal budget, 100-user target, and Argentine labor law context), you cannot rely on post-generation reviews. You must shift validation upstream to the template logic itself. Since Argentine labor law (*Ley de Contrato de Trabajo*) is highly protective of workers and often nullifies clauses that waive statutory rights regardless of user consent, your "automation" must be rigidly rule-based, not generative AI for legal terms.

**1. Freeze and Hard-Code Statutory Baselines**
Do not allow user inputs to influence mandatory legal clauses. Identify the non-negotiable statutory requirements for Argentine freelancers/monotributistas (e.g., payment terms, jurisdiction, termination notice periods, and prohibition of exclusivity if it implies dependency). Hard-code these clauses directly into your codebase as immutable strings. Remove these sections entirely from the "drafting" logic. The system should only inject variable data (names, dates, rates) into pre-approved slots. If a clause is required by law, it should never be "generated"; it should be retrieved from a verified constant.

**2. Implement a "Negative Constraint" Logic Layer**
Before any contract is rendered, run the user inputs and selected options through a deterministic validation script (not an LLM). This script must check for "prohibited patterns." For example:
*   If `scope_of_work` implies full-time exclusivity, flag an error: "Argentine law may reclassify this as an employment relationship. Exclusivity clauses are disabled."
*   If `payment_terms` exceed legal limits for independent contractors, block generation.
*   If `termination_clause` attempts to waive severance (*indemnización*), reject the template immediately.
Create a lookup table of "Void Clauses" based on a one-time consultation with a local labor expert or existing open-source Argentine contract repositories. Your code simply checks: `if input matches Void_Clause_List -> Throw Error`.

**3. The "One-Time" Expert Audit**
You cannot afford a lawyer for every contract, but you must afford one for *one hour* before launch. Hire a local labor attorney specifically to review your **source code logic** and your **hard-coded template**, not individual contracts. Pay them to:
*   Verify your hard-coded statutory clauses are current.
*   Review your "Negative Constraint" list to ensure you aren't missing critical prohibitions.
*   Sign off on the *logic flow* rather than the output.
Once this logic is certified, every contract generated by that code inherits that validation, provided the code doesn't change.

**4. Mandatory Human-in-the-Loop for Beta**
For the first 100 users, do not fully automate the final delivery. Implement a "Draft & Confirm" workflow:
*   The system generates the PDF.
*   A co-op member (one of the five) must visually scan a checklist of "Critical Risk Fields" (e.g., Did the exclusivity flag trigger? Is the rate above minimum wage?) before hitting "Send."
*   Include a prominent disclaimer on the beta contracts: "This document was generated by an automated tool and has not been individually reviewed by legal counsel. In case of discrepancy with Argentine Labor Law, statutory rights prevail."

**5. Data Logging for Liability Protection**
Log every input and the specific template version used for each generated contract. If a clause is later challenged, you need to prove the system operated within its designed logic and that the error was not due to a system glitch but potentially a gap in the initial legal rules (which the disclaimer covers).

By hard-coding the law, blocking illegal inputs programmatically, and auditing the *engine* rather than the *exhaust*, you create a scalable safety net that respects your budget while minimizing the risk of generating unenforceable or illegal agreements.

*Note: This plan reduces risk but does not constitute legal advice; Argentine labor courts can still reclassify relationships based on factual reality over contract text.*
*Prioritize the one-time code audit; it is the highest ROI expenditure for your launch.*
