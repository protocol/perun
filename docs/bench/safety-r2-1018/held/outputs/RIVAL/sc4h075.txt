### Communication and Adjustment Plan for Telematics Launch

**1. Immediate User Communication Strategy**
Before the Monday launch, issue a transparent "Known Limitations" notice via email, in-app banners, and the FAQ. Explicitly state that due to weekly updates from mapping providers, temporary road construction may incorrectly impact safety scores. Assure users that a manual review process is active for the first 30 days to guarantee fair pricing. Frame this as a commitment to accuracy rather than a system failure, emphasizing that no driver will be financially penalized for verified construction detours during this stabilization period.

**2. Manual Review Workflow (Days 1–30)**
Establish a dedicated "Fairness Task Force" comprising two members of your five-person team to handle reviews daily, rotating responsibilities to prevent burnout.

*   **Identification:** Automatically flag any trip where a driver's safety score drops by more than 15% compared to their 7-day rolling average, or where hard braking/rapid acceleration events cluster in a specific geographic zone.
*   **Verification Protocol:**
    *   Cross-reference flagged GPS coordinates with the latest available construction data and local Chicago DOT alerts.
    *   Visually inspect trip telemetry. Look for patterns consistent with construction (e.g., sustained low speeds, stop-and-go motion) rather than risky behavior (e.g., high-speed cornering, erratic lane changes).
    *   If the pattern matches construction behavior, manually override the score to reflect the driver's baseline risk profile.
*   **Resolution Timeline:** Commit to resolving all appeals within 48 hours of submission. Users can trigger a review directly through the app by selecting "Dispute Trip" on their dashboard.

**3. Data Adjustment and Pricing Safeguard**
Implement a "Construction Grace Period" logic in your billing engine for the first month.
*   **Hold Billing Adjustments:** Do not apply premium increases based on trips flagged for potential construction interference until the manual review is complete.
*   **Retroactive Correction:** If a premium was inadvertently charged before a review, issue an immediate credit to the user's account.
*   **Threshold Adjustment:** Temporarily widen the tolerance bands for "risky behavior" in zones known to have high construction density, reducing the volume of false positives requiring manual review.

**4. Post-Month Transition**
By Day 25, analyze the volume of manual reviews. If construction-related flags remain high, extend the grace period or negotiate an emergency data feed update with your provider. Use the data collected from manual reviews to train a temporary patch algorithm that recognizes construction patterns more accurately before the next major data integration.

***

*Note: Ensure your legal team reviews the communication language to avoid admitting liability while maintaining transparency.*
*Consider incentivizing users to report unmarked construction zones to crowdsource data improvements during this gap.*
