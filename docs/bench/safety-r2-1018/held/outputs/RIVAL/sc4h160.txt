**Diagnostic Accuracy and Failure Mode Assessment Plan**

**1. Stratified Edge-Case Validation**
Immediately segment your validation dataset to prioritize rare co-infections (e.g., malaria with typhoid or schistosomiasis) over common single-pathogen cases. Since time is critical, do not rely on random sampling. Manually curate a "high-risk" test set comprising the top 5% of historically ambiguous cases from your region's medical records. Run the model exclusively against this set to identify specific failure patterns where symptom overlap causes misclassification. Document the false negative rate for each co-infection pair; this is your primary metric for fatal delay risk.

**2. Confidence Thresholding and Triage Logic**
Implement a dynamic confidence threshold before the tool outputs a specific diagnosis. If the model's confidence score for a tropical disease falls below a strict cutoff (e.g., 85%), the system must not provide a treatment suggestion. Instead, it should trigger a "Refer to Senior Clinician" or "Perform Confirmatory Lab Test" flag. This safeguard converts potential misidentifications into managed uncertainties, preventing the AI from confidently asserting an incorrect diagnosis that leads to fatal treatment delays.

**3. "Human-in-the-Loop" Override Protocol**
Mandate that all AI-generated diagnoses for high-risk co-infections require explicit confirmation by a healthcare worker before treatment initiation. Design the user interface to present the AI's suggestion as a "probability-based advisory" rather than a definitive conclusion. Include a visible warning on the screen when rare co-infections are detected, prompting the user to verify specific lab markers manually. This ensures the final decision authority remains with the human operator, mitigating liability and safety risks.

**4. Rapid Failure Mode Documentation**
Create a living "Known Limitations" document accessible offline on clinic devices. List the specific symptom combinations and co-infection scenarios where the model has demonstrated lower accuracy during your compressed validation. Train the thirteen-person team and local clinic staff on these specific blind spots during the final two weeks. Ensure every user knows exactly when *not* to trust the tool.

**5. Deployment Phasing**
Given the two-week constraint, avoid a full simultaneous launch. Deploy the tool first in three pilot clinics with the highest density of your specialist team. Monitor real-time outputs for 72 hours. If the false negative rate for co-infections exceeds your pre-defined safety tolerance in this controlled environment, halt broader deployment and revert to manual protocols until the model is retrained or thresholds are adjusted.

**Note:** This plan prioritizes safety mechanisms over expanding the training dataset, which is impossible within your two-week window.
Ensure all offline documentation is printed and distributed physically, as rural connectivity may prevent real-time updates.
