### Safety Review Framework for Automated Fitness Coaching

**1. Define Explicit Safety Boundaries (The "Never" List)**
Before testing, codify the medical constraints into strict rules the AI must never violate. Based on your pilot with elderly users, these boundaries should include:
*   **No High-Impact Movements:** Prohibit jumping, running, or rapid direction changes.
*   **No Unsupported Balance Challenges:** Ban exercises requiring standing on one leg without a specified stable support (e.g., chair or wall).
*   **No Heavy Loading:** Exclude free weights or resistance bands above a defined low threshold unless explicitly cleared by a user's profile.
*   **Pain Ignorance:** The AI must never advise a user to "push through" sharp pain or dizziness.

**2. Implement a Tiered Human-in-the-Loop Audit**
Since full physiotherapist review is not budget-feasible, adopt a risk-based sampling strategy:
*   **100% Automated Pre-Screen:** Every generated plan runs against the "Never" list. Any flag triggers an immediate hold and a generic "consult your doctor" message.
*   **Randomized Expert Sampling:** A physiotherapist reviews a random 10% of approved plans weekly to catch subtle errors (e.g., inappropriate progression speed).
*   **Edge-Case Mandatory Review:** Any plan generated for a user reporting "low" mobility or recent injury is flagged for mandatory human review before delivery.

**3. Establish a "Safe Fallback" Protocol**
Define exactly what the AI should do when it lacks sufficient information or detects risk:
*   If a user's mobility report is ambiguous, the AI must default to the safest possible routine (e.g., seated stretches) rather than guessing.
*   If a user reports pain during a suggested exercise in a follow-up, the system must immediately cease that specific movement category and alert the human oversight team.

**4. Create a Feedback Loop for Continuous Learning**
Use the pilot phase to refine the safety net:
*   **Incident Logging:** Record every instance where the automated screen blocked a plan or a human reviewer corrected an AI suggestion.
*   **Pattern Analysis:** Weekly, analyze these logs to identify specific prompts or mobility descriptions that confuse the AI. Use these findings to update the "Never" list and the system instructions.
*   **User Reporting:** Provide a simple mechanism for users or center staff to report "unsafe feeling" exercises, treating these reports as high-priority data points.

**5. Documentation and Consent**
Ensure transparency with your participants:
*   Clearly state in user consent forms that the plans are AI-generated and not a substitute for professional medical advice.
*   Provide users with a printed "Safety Checklist" to self-verify their environment (e.g., "Is my chair stable?") before starting any routine.

This framework prioritizes harm reduction through strict automated guardrails, targeted human oversight, and rapid iteration based on real-world pilot data.

*Note: This framework is a procedural guide and does not constitute medical advice; final safety protocols should be validated by a qualified healthcare professional.*
*Ensure all data collection regarding user health status complies with GDPR regulations given your location in Italy.*
