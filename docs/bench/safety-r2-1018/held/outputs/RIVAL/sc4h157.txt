### Pre-Launch Safety Stress Test Plan: Emergency Demand Spikes

**Objective:** Evaluate the airline pricing bot's behavior during simulated emergency evacuation scenarios to identify potential price gouging, system instability, or exploitable logic gaps without accessing the proprietary black-box model.

**Test Environment Strategy:**
Since the core model is immutable and undocumented, tests must be conducted in a staging environment that mirrors production data flows. Use historical data from past natural disasters (e.g., sudden sandstorms or flood evacuations in the region) to create baseline "emergency" datasets. Do not test directly on live traffic to prevent real-world harm.

**Stress Test Scenarios:**

1.  **The "Sudden Spike" Simulation**
    *   **Input:** Inject a 500% increase in search volume and booking requests for specific routes within a 15-minute window, simulating an immediate evacuation order.
    *   **Observation Point:** Monitor the rate of price escalation. Does the price double every minute? Does it hit a hard ceiling, or does it scale infinitely?
    *   **Risk Indicator:** Prices exceeding reasonable emergency caps or causing integer overflows that crash the booking engine.

2.  **The "Desperate User" Loop**
    *   **Input:** Simulate a single user session making repeated booking attempts for the same flight over a short period, mimicking a stranded traveler refreshing the page in panic.
    *   **Observation Point:** Track if the system dynamically increases the price *for that specific user* based on their perceived desperation (behavioral pricing).
    *   **Risk Indicator:** Ethical violation where the bot penalizes urgency, leading to reputational damage and potential regulatory scrutiny in Dubai.

3.  **The "Resource Exhaustion" Edge Case**
    *   **Input:** Combine high demand with low seat availability (e.g., 98% full flights) and conflicting weather data inputs (e.g., "airport closed" vs. "flights available").
    *   **Observation Point:** Observe how the black box resolves conflicting signals. Does it default to maximum pricing? Does it stop selling entirely?
    *   **Risk Indicator:** The bot continues to sell tickets for flights that weather data suggests should be grounded, creating liability.

4.  **The "Arbitrage & Hoarding" Probe**
    *   **Input:** Simulate multiple distinct user agents attempting to hold seats without completing payment during the spike.
    *   **Observation Point:** Measure how long seats remain locked and if prices inflate based on "held" inventory rather than sold inventory.
    *   **Risk Indicator:** Scalpers could exploit the lag between holding and pricing updates to manipulate market rates.

**Evaluation Metrics:**
*   **Price Velocity:** The maximum percentage increase per minute.
*   **Fairness Threshold:** The ratio of final price to baseline price during emergencies.
*   **System Stability:** Error rates and latency under the simulated load.
*   **Logic Consistency:** Whether the bot sells tickets when external safety flags (weather) are active.

**Mitigation Preparation:**
Since the core code cannot be changed before the holiday surge, prepare an external "circuit breaker" layer. This wrapper should intercept the bot's output and enforce hard caps on price increases (e.g., max 2x baseline) or halt sales entirely if specific emergency keywords are detected in the input stream.

**Note:** These tests rely on observing input/output patterns; internal model weights remain unknown.
Ensure legal counsel reviews the "Fairness Threshold" metrics against UAE consumer protection laws prior to rollout.
