**Internal Bias Review Plan for AI Hiring Platform**

**1. Data Inventory and Segmentation**
Since external auditing is prohibited, begin by isolating your current training dataset. Create a secure, internal copy of the data used to train or fine-tune your ranking model. Segment this data by gender (specifically isolating female applicants for engineering roles) and compare it against the male counterpart dataset. You must verify if the volume of historical hiring data for women in engineering is significantly lower, which can cause the model to undervalue patterns common in female resumes.

**2. Feature Importance Analysis**
Conduct an internal statistical review of the features your model weights most heavily. Look for proxies that correlate with gender rather than technical skill. Common examples include:
*   **Graduation Years:** Gaps may correlate with career breaks often taken by women.
*   **Specific Keywords:** Terms like "rockstar," "ninja," or specific fraternity/sorority names may appear more frequently in one demographic's historical data.
*   **University Names:** If your training data favors institutions with historically lower female enrollment in engineering, the model may inherit this bias.
Remove or down-weight any feature that does not have a direct, provable correlation to job performance.

**3. Synthetic Resume Testing (Counterfactual Evaluation)**
Generate pairs of synthetic resumes that are identical in skills, experience, and education, differing only in gender-indicative signals (e.g., names, pronouns, or membership in gender-specific organizations). Run these pairs through your current ranking logic.
*   **Metric:** Calculate the "Disparate Impact Ratio." If the ranking score for the female-identified resume is consistently lower than the male-identified resume by more than 20%, your logic contains bias.
*   **Action:** If a gap exists, retrain or adjust the scoring weights until the scores for identical qualifications converge.

**4. Historical Outcome Audit**
Analyze past hiring decisions made by your human team that were used as ground truth for training. If your historical data reflects a bias where qualified women were rejected or ranked lower by human recruiters, the AI will learn to replicate this. You must manually flag and exclude these historical instances from the training set or apply re-weighting techniques to ensure the model learns from an equitable distribution of successful outcomes.

**5. Mitigation and Documentation**
*   **Pre-processing:** Clean training data to remove direct identifiers and known proxies before model ingestion.
*   **In-processing:** Apply constraints during model training that penalize the model when prediction accuracy diverges significantly between demographic groups.
*   **Post-processing:** Adjust final ranking scores to ensure the top percentage of candidates reflects a diverse pool, provided qualifications are equivalent.
*   **Documentation:** Maintain a detailed internal log of all tests, identified biases, and mitigation steps taken. This serves as your compliance evidence under data sovereignty laws, demonstrating due diligence without exporting data.

**6. Continuous Monitoring Plan**
Establish a weekly internal review cycle for the beta phase. Track the demographic breakdown of the top 10% of ranked candidates. If the representation of women in engineering drops below a defined threshold relative to the applicant pool, trigger an immediate manual review of the ranking logic before further candidates are processed.

*Note: Ensure all synthetic data generation and testing occurs strictly within your local Riyadh-based infrastructure to maintain data sovereignty. This plan relies on statistical comparison and does not require external data sharing.*
