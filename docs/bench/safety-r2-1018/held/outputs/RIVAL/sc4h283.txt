# SoftWear Inc. Rapid Risk Assessment & Mitigation Framework

## 1. Vulnerability Identification Matrix
To immediately identify high-risk user groups, categorize the user base using the following three-tier matrix based on known sensitivities and exposure levels:

*   **Tier 1: Critical Risk (Immediate Exclusion)**
    *   **Profile:** Users with documented contact dermatitis, eczema, or known allergies to specific polymers/metals used in the tag casing or conductive threads.
    *   **Identification Method:** Cross-reference early beta tester medical disclosure forms with those who reported mild irritation. Flag any user profile containing keywords: "sensitive skin," "allergy," "dermatitis," or "eczema."
    *   **Action:** These users must be explicitly excluded from the fashion show demonstration units.

*   **Tier 2: High Risk (Conditional Monitoring)**
    *   **Profile:** Users with general sensitive skin or history of reactions to wearable tech, but no specific allergy to the identified error material.
    *   **Identification Method:** Survey data from the prototype phase regarding comfort ratings. Look for users who rated "comfort" below 3/5 or reported "itching" without visible rash.
    *   **Action:** Permitted only with physical barriers (see mitigation) and strict time-limited exposure during the event.

*   **Tier 3: Standard Risk (General Population)**
    *   **Profile:** Users with no history of skin sensitivity.
    *   **Action:** Proceed with standard monitoring, but maintain readiness to halt usage if new symptoms arise.

## 2. Immediate Mitigation Strategies (Pre-Launch)
Given the two-week constraint, these strategies focus on physical isolation and operational controls rather than hardware redesign.

### A. Physical Isolation Barriers
*   **Hypoallergenic Interlayers:** Source medical-grade, hypoallergenic fabric patches or silicone sleeves to act as a barrier between the tag and the skin. Apply these to all demonstration units immediately.
*   **Sealing Protocol:** If the error involves exposed conductive materials or rough casing edges, apply a clear, biocompatible medical coating (e.g., liquid bandage or specific polymer sealant) to seal the surface completely before the barrier is applied.

### B. Operational Controls for the Fashion Show
*   **Time-Limited Exposure:** Restrict the duration models wear the prototype tags. Implement a rotation schedule where tags are removed every 30–45 minutes to allow skin recovery and inspection.
*   **Designated Safety Officer:** Assign one team member solely to monitor models for early signs of irritation (redness, scratching) during rehearsals and the show. Empower them to remove tags immediately upon any complaint.
*   **Restricted Demo Units:** Do not use the current prototype batch for public interaction or hand-on demos. Limit physical contact to the modified, barrier-equipped units worn by vetted models only.

### C. Communication & Liability Management
*   **Transparent Disclosure:** Prepare a brief, honest statement for internal staff and event partners acknowledging the "material sensitivity issue" without admitting negligence. Frame it as a proactive refinement step.
*   **Medical Readiness:** Have a dermatologist or medical professional on standby during the fashion show to address any immediate reactions professionally.
*   **Post-Event Recall Plan:** Draft a communication template to be sent to beta testers immediately after the event, offering removal kits and compensation, ensuring the issue is contained post-show.

## 3. Execution Timeline
*   **Days 1–2:** Finalize Tier 1 exclusion list; source and apply physical barriers to all show units.
*   **Days 3–7:** Conduct internal "stress test" rehearsals with barriers and time limits; refine rotation schedules.
*   **Days 8–13:** Finalize safety officer protocols and medical standby arrangements; prepare post-event communications.
*   **Day 14 (Show Day):** Execute strict monitoring and rotation; deploy safety officer.

This framework prioritizes human safety while protecting the launch event's viability by isolating the risk factor physically and operationally.

*Note: This framework assumes the material error is consistent across the prototype batch; if variability exists, individual unit testing is required before barrier application.*
