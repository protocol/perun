### Risk Mitigation Plan: Smart Waste Bin Deployment

**1. Manual Inspection Schedule**
Given the intermittent connectivity in underground parking areas and the sensor's inability to detect hazardous buildup, a tiered manual inspection protocol is required immediately until the firmware is stabilized or network coverage improves.

*   **High-Risk Zones (Underground Parking):**
    *   **Frequency:** Twice daily (06:00 and 14:00).
    *   **Scope:** Visual check for fullness, structural integrity, and signs of hazardous leakage (unusual odors, discoloration, steam).
    *   **Personnel:** Assigned facility maintenance staff or dedicated sanitation scouts.
    *   **Action:** If bin is >75% full or shows hazard signs, tag for immediate emptying regardless of digital status.

*   **Standard Zones (Surface/Public Walkways):**
    *   **Frequency:** Once daily (07:00).
    *   **Scope:** Visual confirmation of sensor housing integrity and overflow signs.
    *   **Action:** Immediate escalation if physical overflow is detected despite "empty" digital readings.

*   **Pre-Inspection Surge (3 Days Prior to Municipal Inspection):**
    *   **Frequency:** Every 4 hours across all zones.
    *   **Scope:** Comprehensive audit of every unit to ensure zero incidents during the official visit.

**2. Signal Loss & Alert Protocol**
Since digital signals cannot be relied upon for real-time alerts in dead zones, the protocol shifts from "automated alerting" to "presumed failure" logic.

*   **Step 1: The "Dark Bin" Rule**
    *   Any bin located in a known low-connectivity zone that has not transmitted a heartbeat in over 12 hours is automatically classified as "Critical/Unknown."
    *   **Action:** This status triggers an automatic dispatch ticket for a physical check within 4 hours. Do not wait for a "full" alert; the lack of signal is the trigger.

*   **Step 2: Hardline Escalation Chain**
    *   Since cellular data is unreliable, alerts to sanitation crews must utilize redundant channels.
    *   **Primary:** SMS text blast to zone-specific crew leads (SMS often penetrates where data fails).
    *   **Secondary:** Voice call tree. If a "Hazard Suspected" manual report is filed, the supervisor must verbally confirm receipt with the crew lead within 15 minutes.
    *   **Tertiary:** Physical signage. If a bin is suspected of hazard but cannot be emptied immediately, place high-visibility "Do Not Use – Maintenance Required" barriers around the unit.

*   **Step 3: Hazardous Material Specifics**
    *   If a manual inspector suspects hazardous buildup (chemical smell, heat, unknown substances):
        1.  Do not compact or touch the contents.
        2.  Isolate the area (2-meter radius).
        3.  Contact the specialized HazMat response team directly via voice line, bypassing standard sanitation dispatch.
        4.  Log the incident manually on a physical clipboard attached to the inspection route sheet to ensure record-keeping survives digital outages.

**3. Implementation Timeline**
*   **Week 1:** Train all maintenance and sanitation staff on the new visual inspection criteria and the "Dark Bin" rule. Distribute physical logbooks for dead zones.
*   **Week 2:** Run a simulation drill where connectivity is artificially severed to test the SMS/Voice escalation speed.
*   **Week 3:** Execute the Pre-Inspection Surge schedule and compile manual logs to demonstrate due diligence to municipal inspectors.

***

*Note: This plan assumes immediate availability of maintenance staff for the proposed frequency; adjust shift rosters accordingly.*
*Ensure all manual inspectors are equipped with personal protective equipment (PPE) suitable for potential hazardous waste exposure.*
