# Internal Fairness Evaluation Strategy for FinFlow Bankruptcy Model

Given your constraints of limited budget, no external tools, and the need to rely on internal simulations, the following strategy focuses on rigorous data segmentation and statistical analysis using your existing development environment. This approach targets the root causes of false positives in hospitality and retail without requiring new infrastructure.

### Phase 1: Construct a Stratified "Golden" Test Set
Since you cannot run live A/B tests with banks yet, you must build a high-fidelity simulation dataset from historical records.
1.  **Data Collection:** Aggregate historical loan application data for the hospitality and retail sectors, specifically isolating businesses that remained solvent but were flagged as high-risk by your current model (false positives).
2.  **Stratification:** Divide this dataset into granular cohorts based on:
    *   **Business Size:** Micro (<10 employees), Small (10–50), and Medium (50–200).
    *   **Sub-Sector:** e.g., Quick Service Restaurants vs. Fine Dining; Brick-and-Mortar Retail vs. E-commerce.
    *   **Geography:** Regional economic indicators relevant to your two beta bank partners.
3.  **Ground Truth Labeling:** Manually verify the solvency status of these cases using actual historical outcomes (e.g., tax filings, credit bureau archives) to ensure your "false positive" label is accurate. This becomes your baseline for fairness.

### Phase 2: Feature Importance and Bias Auditing
Run your existing model against this stratified dataset to identify which features drive the erroneous high-risk scores.
1.  **Disaggregated Performance Metrics:** Do not look only at overall accuracy. Calculate the False Positive Rate (FPR) separately for each cohort defined in Phase 1. If the FPR for "Small Hospitality Businesses" is significantly higher than for "Medium Retail Businesses," you have identified a disparate impact.
2.  **Proxy Variable Analysis:** Investigate if specific input features act as proxies for industry volatility rather than actual creditworthiness. For example, check if "cash flow variance" or "seasonal revenue dips" are weighted too heavily for hospitality, penalizing normal seasonal business cycles as signs of distress.
3.  **Threshold Sensitivity Analysis:** Simulate different decision thresholds for each sector. Determine if a single global threshold is causing the harm. You may find that lowering the risk threshold specifically for retail reduces false positives without significantly increasing risk exposure.

### Phase 3: Counterfactual Simulation
Since you cannot test on live users, use your simulation environment to run counterfactuals.
1.  **Synthetic Perturbation:** Take a flagged "false positive" application from the hospitality sector. Systematically alter non-financial attributes (e.g., change the business structure or location code) while keeping financial health constant. If the risk score changes drastically, the model is relying on unstable or biased correlates.
2.  **Stress Testing:** Inject controlled noise into the financial inputs of solvent companies to see at what point the model flips to "high risk." Compare this tipping point across industries. If the model breaks under minor fluctuations for retail but remains stable for tech, the model is overfit to noise in the retail training data.

### Phase 4: Mitigation and Documentation
1.  **Sector-Specific Calibration:** Based on findings, propose recalibrating the model weights or applying sector-specific bias correction factors before the beta launch.
2.  **Human-in-the-Loop Protocol:** For the beta launch, mandate that any application flagged as high-risk in the identified vulnerable sectors (hospitality/retail) triggers an automatic manual review by a loan officer, rather than an auto-denial.
3.  **Audit Trail:** Document the disparity metrics found during this internal simulation. This serves as your initial fairness report for the regional banks, demonstrating proactive risk management despite the lack of external audit.

**Note:** Ensure all synthetic data generation strictly adheres to privacy regulations by anonymizing any real business identifiers used in the simulation. Prioritize the "Human-in-the-Loop" protocol immediately for the beta launch to prevent immediate financial harm while long-term model retraining occurs.
