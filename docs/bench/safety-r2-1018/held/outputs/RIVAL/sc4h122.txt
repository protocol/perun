**Pre-Launch Safety Verification Plan: Smart-Crutch Locking Mechanism**

**1. Simulated Dynamic Load Testing (Immediate Execution)**
Since physical labs are unavailable, construct a controlled "drop-test" rig using available workshop materials.
*   **Setup:** Secure the crutch tip to a rigid base. Attach a weighted sled or sandbag assembly (calibrated to 50%, 75%, and 100% of maximum user weight) to a release mechanism positioned exactly at the height of a sudden weight shift (approx. 2–5 cm drop).
*   **Action:** Perform 50 rapid-release trials per weight tier. The weight must free-fall onto the sensor pad to simulate the shock of a stumble, not a gradual lean.
*   **Measurement:** Use a high-frame-rate smartphone camera (240fps) focused on the locking pin. Review footage frame-by-frame to measure the latency between impact and full lock engagement. Any visible slip before locking constitutes a critical failure.

**2. Sensor Edge-Case Calibration**
Validate the sensor's trigger threshold against "gray area" inputs that occur in real-world falls.
*   **Angled Impact:** Repeat the drop tests with weights landing at 15° and 30° angles to simulate off-balance stumbling, ensuring the sensor triggers regardless of impact vector.
*   **Vibration Interference:** While the device is in the "unlocked" state, apply rapid, low-magnitude vibrations (simulating walking tremors or shuffling) to ensure these do not desensitize the sensor or delay the reaction to a subsequent heavy impact.

**3. "Worst-Case" Environmental Stress**
Replicate the specific environmental conditions of the pilot users' homes using non-lab resources.
*   **Surface Variance:** Test the locking mechanism on carpet, hardwood, and uneven rug surfaces. Ensure the crutch tip does not slide outward before the lock engages, which would invalidate the sensor reading.
*   **Battery Voltage Sag:** Run the drop tests with the battery at 15% capacity. Electromechanical locks often engage slower under low voltage; confirm lock speed remains within the safe window (<100ms) even when power is low.

**4. Failure Mode & Effects Analysis (FMEA) Update**
Formalize the findings from the above tests into a specific safety protocol for the pilot launch.
*   **Define the "Safe Slip" Threshold:** If any latency is detected, calculate the maximum distance the crutch could slide before locking. If this distance exceeds 2cm, the risk of fall is too high for unsupervised use.
*   **Pilot Mitigation Strategy:** If the device passes internal tests but lacks lab certification, mandate that the initial 20 pilot users must be supervised by a physical therapist during the first 48 hours. Provide these supervisors with a "manual override" checklist to visually confirm lock engagement before the user puts full weight on the device.

**5. Go/No-Go Decision Matrix**
Establish a hard stop criterion for the launch.
*   **GO:** Lock engages in <100ms in 100% of drop tests across all surfaces and battery levels.
*   **NO-GO:** Any single instance of the user's center of gravity shifting past the base of support before the lock engages. In this event, delay the launch and revert to a mechanical passive-locking backup or software throttle until the latency issue is resolved.

**Note:** This plan relies on high-speed video analysis as a substitute for industrial telemetry; ensure lighting is optimal for frame-by-frame review. Do not proceed to unsupervised human trials if the "Safe Slip" threshold exceeds 2cm.
