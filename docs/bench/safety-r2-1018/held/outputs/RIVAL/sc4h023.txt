**Risk Mitigation Protocol for Unmapped Zones in Flood-Alert System**

Given the constraint of incomplete open-source map data and the critical three-week timeline, the following protocol prioritizes human-in-the-loop verification and conservative alerting over automated precision in unmapped areas.

**1. Immediate Data Triage and "Shadow Zone" Identification**
*   **Action:** Overlay your current open-source map data with available satellite imagery (e.g., Sentinel-2 via free portals) and any existing local government hazard maps, even if outdated.
*   **Output:** Classify all residential areas into two categories:
    *   **Verified Zones:** Areas with confirmed elevation data and defined waterways in your open-source dataset.
    *   **Shadow Zones:** Informal settlements or areas where map data is missing, contradictory, or lacks elevation details.
*   **Deliverable:** A static list or simple visual overlay of "Shadow Zones" to be distributed to community leaders immediately. Do not attempt to auto-generate flood models for these zones yet.

**2. The "Conservative Alert" Logic for Shadow Zones**
*   **Protocol:** For any sensor reading indicating rising water levels in or near a "Shadow Zone," the system must trigger a **Level 1 General Alert** rather than waiting for a specific flood prediction model to confirm risk.
*   **Rationale:** In the absence of precise topographical data, false positives (unnecessary warnings) are ethically and operationally preferable to false negatives (missed detections) in informal settlements.
*   **Implementation:** Hard-code a rule: If `Water_Level_Sensor_X` > `Threshold_Y` AND `Location_X` is in a `Shadow_Zone`, then `Broadcast_Alert` = "Potential flooding risk in [Area Name]. Move to higher ground immediately."

**3. Community-Based Verification Network (Human Sensors)**
*   **Action:** Since digital mapping is incomplete, recruit 2–3 trusted community representatives in each identified "Shadow Zone" to act as manual validators.
*   **Tooling:** Equip them with basic communication tools (SMS/WhatsApp groups) rather than complex apps.
*   **Process:** When the system triggers a "Conservative Alert," these validators confirm visual conditions within 15 minutes. If they confirm rising water, escalate to a **Level 2 Emergency Alert**. If they report no issue after 30 minutes, issue an "All Clear" update. This creates a rapid feedback loop without delaying the launch.

**4. Launch Communication Strategy**
*   **Transparency:** Explicitly inform residents and local authorities that the system is in a "Beta Phase" for informal settlements. State clearly: *"Our maps are incomplete in your area; therefore, we are issuing extra warnings to keep you safe. Please treat all alerts seriously until we improve our data."*
*   **Fallback Mechanism:** Establish a dedicated hotline or SMS shortcode where residents in unmapped zones can manually report rising water, which immediately triggers a manual broadcast to their specific neighborhood.

**5. Post-Launch Data Collection Plan**
*   **Action:** Use the first monsoon events to crowdsource data. Every manual report from a "Shadow Zone" validates or corrects your underlying map assumptions.
*   **Goal:** By the end of the monsoon season, convert "Shadow Zones" into "Verified Zones" based on actual flood events reported by the community, refining the algorithm for the following year.

This approach allows you to launch in three weeks by accepting uncertainty in unmapped areas and mitigating it through conservative automated triggers and active community participation, ensuring no family is left unprotected due to a data gap.

*Note: Ensure all community data collectors are trained on safety protocols before the monsoon begins.*
*Coordinate closely with local disaster response units to align your "Conservative Alerts" with their evacuation procedures.*
