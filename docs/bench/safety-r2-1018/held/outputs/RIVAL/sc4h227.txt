### Safety Assessment Strategy for Climate-Disaster Claims Models

**1. Immediate Gap Analysis (Days 1–3)**
*   **Data Recency Audit:** Compare the cutoff date of your historical weather training data against the timeline of major climate shifts in the Southeast US (e.g., intensification of hurricane rainfall rates, rapid intensification events, and compound flooding scenarios post-2018). Identify specific storm types absent from your training set.
*   **Feature Drift Review:** Analyze whether your model's input features (e.g., wind speed thresholds, flood zone maps) align with current NOAA and FEMA updated standards. Outdated zip-code-level risk scores are a primary source of false denials.
*   **Edge Case Inventory:** Catalog recent claims from the last 24 months that were manually overridden or appealed. These represent known failures of the legacy logic and should be used as immediate test cases.

**2. Targeted Stress Testing (Days 4–10)**
*   **Synthetic "New Climate" Scenarios:** Generate synthetic claim profiles representing emerging risk patterns (e.g., inland flooding from stalled tropical systems, hail storms in previously low-risk zones) that violate historical distributions but match recent meteorological reality. Run these through the current model to quantify the false denial rate.
*   **Adversarial Boundary Testing:** Systematically vary input parameters near the decision threshold (e.g., wind speed ±2 mph, water height ±1 inch) to identify "cliff effects" where minor data variances cause total denial. This highlights fragility in the model's confidence scoring.
*   **Demographic & Geographic Bias Check:** Cross-reference denial rates against updated census and flood map data to ensure the model isn't systematically penalizing specific neighborhoods that have recently been reclassified as high-risk due to climate changes.

**3. Immediate Mitigation Steps (Pre-Launch)**
*   **Implement a "Human-in-the-Loop" Safety Valve:** Configure the system to automatically flag and route any claim involving a "novel" storm event (defined by date or type not in the training set) or any claim falling within a 10% confidence margin of the denial threshold to a senior human adjuster. Do not allow auto-denial for these categories.
*   **Dynamic Threshold Adjustment:** Temporarily lower the automated denial threshold (increase the burden of proof for denial) for categories identified as high-risk for false negatives during stress testing. It is financially safer to manually review a potentially fraudulent claim than to illegally deny a valid one under regulatory scrutiny.
*   **Explainability Overlay:** Deploy a post-hoc explainability tool (e.g., SHAP values) on every denied claim to generate a plain-language reason code. If the primary driver for denial is an outdated data point (e.g., "flood zone X" when the area was rezoned to "A" in 2023), the system should block the auto-denial.
*   **Regulatory "Safe Harbor" Documentation:** Draft a formal disclosure for regulators detailing the known limitations of the historical data, the specific stress tests performed, and the active human-oversight protocols implemented to mitigate these gaps. This demonstrates good faith and proactive risk management ahead of the filing deadline.

**4. Continuous Monitoring Plan**
*   Establish a daily review loop for the first 30 days of live processing, focusing exclusively on denied claims. Track appeal success rates; if appeals on climate-related denials exceed 5%, trigger an immediate model freeze and manual review of all pending denials.

*Note: Prioritize the "Human-in-the-Loop" implementation above all else; it is your most effective regulatory shield against systemic model error.*
*Ensure all synthetic test data used in stress testing is documented as part of your regulatory filing evidence.*
