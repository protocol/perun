# Contingency Plan: Manual Identification and Service of Unmapped Locations

**Objective:** Ensure equitable supply distribution to families in remote, unmapped Haitian villages when automated routing systems fail to identify these locations due to outdated map data.

### Phase 1: Rapid Community Intelligence Gathering
Since satellite imagery is unavailable, rely on human intelligence (HUMINT) already present in the region.
*   **Leverage Local Leadership:** Immediately convene with local *Chef de Section*, community elders, and leaders of local faith-based organizations. These individuals possess mental maps of all settlements, including informal or newly formed clusters of homes.
*   **Mobilize Community Health Workers (CHWs):** Deploy existing CHWs and local teachers as scouts. They routinely visit remote households and can provide GPS coordinates (via simple smartphone apps) or descriptive landmarks for unmapped villages.
*   **Establish "Check-In" Points:** Set up temporary registration desks at known major intersections or market squares. Allow residents from unmapped areas to self-register their location and household size in exchange for initial ration cards.

### Phase 2: Low-Tech Mapping and Verification
Create a functional, immediate map without digital dependencies.
*   **Participatory Sketch Mapping:** Conduct rapid workshops with local guides. Have them draw village layouts, access roads, and household clusters on large printed sheets of the current (outdated) maps or blank paper. Overlay this hand-drawn data onto the digital system manually.
*   **Landmark-Based Navigation Protocols:** Instead of relying on street names or GPS pins which may be missing, create route instructions based on immutable local landmarks (e.g., "the large mango tree near the river bend," "the destroyed church," "the blue water tank").
*   **Verification Loops:** Pair a logistics coordinator with a local guide to physically verify the top three highest-priority unmapped clusters identified by community leaders before full-scale dispatch.

### Phase 3: Adaptive Distribution Logistics
Modify delivery operations to accommodate non-digital targets.
*   **Hub-and-Spoke Manual Delivery:** Designate a primary truck drop-off point at the nearest *mapped* location accessible by large vehicles. From there, utilize smaller, agile transport (motorcycles, donkeys, or porters) guided by local scouts to reach the final unmapped households.
*   **Scheduled Community Collection:** If door-to-door delivery is too risky or slow, communicate specific times and neutral ground locations where trucks will wait for residents of unmapped villages to collect supplies. Use local radio and word-of-mouth chains to broadcast these schedules.
*   **Token-Based Accountability:** Issue physical, numbered tokens to households in unmapped areas during the registration phase. Track distribution manually via ledger books against these tokens to prevent duplication and ensure coverage, rather than relying on digital scan-in/scan-out systems tied to map coordinates.

### Phase 4: Feedback and Iteration
*   **Daily Debriefs:** At the end of each distribution day, hold a 15-minute stand-up with drivers and local guides. Record any new villages discovered or access routes blocked by storm debris.
*   **Dynamic Route Adjustment:** Update the manual sketch maps daily. If a previously unmapped village is successfully serviced, mark it clearly for future runs until the digital map can be updated post-emergency.

**Critical Success Factor:** Trust local knowledge over incomplete digital data. The community knows where they live; the system must adapt to them, not vice versa.

*Note: Ensure all local scouts are compensated fairly and provided with safety gear. Prioritize the safety of personnel over speed of delivery in unstable terrain.*
