# Surge Pricing Safety Review Checklist: Preventing User Location Trapping

**1. Algorithmic Feedback Loop Analysis**
*   **Verify Exit Incentives:** Confirm that the pricing model includes a "decay factor" or time-based override that forces prices down in high-risk zones after a set duration (e.g., 15 minutes), regardless of demand, to explicitly incentivize driver exit.
*   **Check Supply Elasticity:** Ensure the algorithm distinguishes between "high demand/low supply" (normal surge) and "no supply due to risk" (dangerous trap). If driver acceptance rates drop to near zero in a specific geo-fence, the system must flag this as a safety anomaly, not just a supply shortage.
*   **Cap Surge Multipliers in Risk Zones:** Implement a hard ceiling on surge multipliers for areas historically flagged for safety issues. Artificially high prices are currently discouraging drivers; capping them prevents the "price wall" that traps users.

**2. Data Pipeline & Risk Integration Workarounds**
*   **Static Risk Heatmaps:** Since real-time crime data is unavailable, overlay a static, pre-calculated "high-risk" heatmap based on historical data (last 12 months) and known festival crowd densities. Hard-code these zones into the pricing logic as "Safety Priority" areas.
*   **Driver Behavior as Proxy Data:** Use real-time driver acceptance/cancellation rates as a proxy for safety. If cancellations spike in a specific zone, automatically trigger a "Safety Alert" and suspend dynamic pricing increases in that immediate radius.
*   **Festival Crowd Density Overrides:** Pre-define festival routes and venues. Apply a "Festival Mode" logic that prioritizes throughput over maximum revenue, ensuring prices do not spike so high that drivers avoid entering or leaving the festival perimeter.

**3. User Experience & Emergency Overrides**
*   **"Stuck User" Detection:** Create a logic check for users who have been waiting >10 minutes in a high-surge zone with no available drivers. Automatically trigger a manual dispatch override or a subsidized flat-rate fare to the nearest safe zone.
*   **Transparent Safety Messaging:** If a user is in a high-risk zone with low supply, display a message: "High demand and limited availability in this area. Consider moving to [Nearby Safe Location] for faster pickup." Do not just show a high price; explain the scarcity.
*   **Emergency Exit Routing:** Ensure the app suggests pickup points slightly outside the immediate high-risk/surge zone if the current location has been inactive for too long.

**4. Manual Monitoring & Incident Response**
*   **War Room Dashboard:** Set up a real-time dashboard specifically monitoring the ratio of *Price Surge* vs. *Driver Acceptance* in the festival zone. If Price is High and Acceptance is Low, manual intervention is required immediately.
*   **Driver Hotline Feedback:** Establish a direct channel for drivers to report "unsafe to enter/exit" zones during the festival. Empower the operations team to manually zero-out surge pricing in reported zones instantly.
*   **Post-Trip Safety Check:** For trips originating in identified high-risk zones during peak surge, trigger an automated safety check-in message to the rider 5 minutes after trip start.

**5. Pre-Launch Simulation (Mental Walkthrough)**
*   **Scenario Test:** Simulate a user stuck in a high-crime alley during peak festival hours. Does the app encourage a driver to come (by lowering price/subsidizing) or push them away (by raising price)? If the latter, the logic must be inverted immediately.

*Note: Given the legacy pipeline constraints, rely heavily on driver cancellation rates as your real-time safety signal. Prioritize user evacuation over revenue maximization for the first 48 hours.*
