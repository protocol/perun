### Safety Review and Refinement Plan for Mental-Wellness Bot

**Phase 1: Retrospective Data Audit (Weeks 1–2)**
Since new data labeling is not possible, you must manually audit your existing dataset using a targeted "harm-focused" sampling strategy rather than a full review.
1.  **Keyword Filtering:** Extract all conversation logs containing high-risk keywords associated with isolation (e.g., "alone," "hide," "avoid," "nobody," "sleep all day") and depression markers.
2.  **Contextual Triage:** Have your five team members manually review these filtered logs. Tag instances where the bot's response validates withdrawal (e.g., "It's okay to stay in bed") without balancing it with a gentle nudge toward connection or professional help.
3.  **Pattern Identification:** Categorize the failures. Determine if the bot is misinterpreting user intent (e.g., treating "I want to be alone" as a permanent solution rather than a temporary feeling) or lacking a specific safety constraint in its system instructions.

**Phase 2: Logic and Prompt Refinement (Week 3)**
Based on the audit findings, update the bot's core response generation logic. Since you cannot retrain the model on new data, you must rely on **system prompt engineering** and **post-processing rules**.
1.  **System Instruction Update:** Explicitly rewrite the system prompt to prioritize "balanced coping." Add a strict directive: "When a user expresses a desire to isolate, acknowledge their feelings but gently suggest one small, low-pressure social step or professional resource. Never validate complete withdrawal as a long-term strategy."
2.  **Negative Constraints:** Add negative constraints to prevent specific harmful phrases identified in your audit (e.g., "Do not say 'stay away from people'").
3.  **Rule-Based Guardrails:** Implement a lightweight post-processing layer. If the generated response contains high-risk isolation language, trigger a fallback template that offers a standardized, clinically reviewed message encouraging connection or providing helpline information.

**Phase 3: Simulation and Validation (Week 4)**
Before launch, verify the changes using your existing data as a test bed.
1.  **Re-play Testing:** Run the identified "failure cases" from Phase 1 through the updated bot logic. Confirm that the new responses now offer balanced advice.
2.  **Adversarial Spot-Checks:** Have team members role-play difficult scenarios not explicitly in the logs but plausible based on your findings (e.g., a user refusing all help). Ensure the bot remains empathetic but firm on safety boundaries.
3.  **Final Go/No-Go:** If any simulated run produces harmful isolation advice, revert to the fallback guardrail immediately. Do not launch until 100% of the re-played failure cases are resolved.

**Deployment Strategy**
Launch with a "human-in-the-loop" monitoring plan for the first two weeks. Flag any user session containing high-risk keywords for immediate manual review by your team to catch edge cases the current dataset missed.

***

*Note: This plan relies entirely on your existing logs and manual team review due to the budget freeze.*
*Ensure all team members reviewing logs have access to mental health support, as exposure to distressing content can be impactful.*
