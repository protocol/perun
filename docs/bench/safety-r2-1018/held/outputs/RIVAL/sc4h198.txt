### Proxy-Based Evaluation Roadmap for Institutional Bias

**Phase 1: Synthetic Resume Generation (The Control Group)**
Create pairs of synthetic resumes that are identical in every measurable merit factor except for the educational institution.
*   **Variables to Hold Constant:** Years of experience, specific technical skills, project descriptions, GitHub activity summaries, and soft skill phrasing.
*   **Variables to Manipulate:** Replace the university name across a spectrum:
    *   *Tier A:* The specific "prestigious" institutions your tool currently highlights.
    *   *Tier B:* Accredited state universities with strong engineering programs.
    *   *Tier C:* Lesser-known private colleges or international institutions with equivalent accreditation.
*   **Volume:** Generate at least 50 unique pairs per tier combination to ensure statistical significance. Ensure names and demographic indicators are neutral or randomized evenly across all tiers to prevent confounding variables.

**Phase 2: Blind Ranking Execution**
Feed these synthetic resumes into the AI assistant in a randomized order.
*   **Isolation:** Run each resume individually or in small, shuffled batches to prevent the model from comparing them directly against each other in a single context window, which might trigger anomaly detection.
*   **Data Capture:** Record the raw output for each candidate: the ranking score, the qualitative summary provided by the AI, and any specific "highlighted" strengths mentioned.

**Phase 3: Proxy Metric Analysis**
Since you cannot measure demographic impact directly, use "Institutional Prestige" as the proxy for potential systemic bias.
*   **Score Delta Calculation:** For every identical resume pair, calculate the difference in ranking scores based solely on the university attended. A consistent positive delta for Tier A indicates institutional bias.
*   **Language Sentiment Analysis:** Analyze the qualitative feedback. Does the AI use more enthusiastic or definitive language (e.g., "exceptional potential," "top-tier background") for prestigious schools while using hedging language (e.g., "adequate," "promising despite...") for others, even when skills are identical?
*   **Threshold Testing:** Determine if candidates from non-prestigious schools need significantly higher skill scores to achieve the same ranking as those from prestigious schools.

**Phase 4: Iterative Remediation**
*   **Prompt Engineering Adjustment:** If bias is detected, modify the system instructions to explicitly weight "demonstrated skills" and "project outcomes" over "institutional reputation."
*   **Re-testing:** Re-run the synthetic suite after every configuration change. The goal is to reduce the Score Delta to near zero.
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Audit:** Before full rollout, have human recruiters review the top 10% of ranked synthetic candidates from Tier B and C schools. If humans agree with the AI's lower ranking despite identical skills, the bias may be in the training data's definition of "merit," requiring a broader policy review.

**Deliverable:** A final report detailing the "Bias Coefficient" (average score difference per tier) and a go/no-go recommendation based on whether the tool meets a predefined fairness threshold (e.g., <5% variance attributable to school name).

*Note: This strategy relies on the assumption that the synthetic resumes accurately reflect real-world candidate diversity in skills.*
*Ensure all synthetic data is clearly marked and excluded from actual hiring pipelines to prevent accidental processing.*
