# Pre-Launch Safety Review Plan: Extreme Heat Wave Lockout Feature

## 1. Immediate Risk Identification (Human-Centric)
Based on your description, the primary risks are not technical failures but human consequences:
*   **Medical Dependency:** Users relying on specific temperatures for medical equipment (e.g., insulin storage, respiratory conditions) or physiological regulation (elderly, infants).
*   **Psychological Distress:** Loss of agency causing panic or anxiety, particularly in vulnerable demographics who may not understand *why* the lockout occurred.
*   **Misinterpretation of Status:** Users assuming the system is broken rather than actively restricting them, leading to dangerous attempts to bypass the thermostat physically.

## 2. Low-Cost Mitigation Strategies (Implementable Now)
Since you cannot test with real vulnerable populations or buy new hardware, focus on **communication overrides** and **procedural safeguards** within the existing software stack.

### A. The "Emergency Override" Protocol
Do not rely on a hard lockout. Implement a software-based "Break Glass" mechanism immediately:
*   **Mechanism:** Allow a manual override if the user completes a specific, deliberate action sequence (e.g., hold two buttons for 5 seconds) or answers a safety confirmation prompt on the app: *"I acknowledge this may impact grid stability. I require this adjustment for medical/safety reasons."*
*   **Logging:** Every override must be logged with a timestamp and user ID for your regulatory report. This proves the system allows human priority over grid priority.

### B. Enhanced User Communication
The interface must clearly distinguish between a **malfunction** and a **safety restriction**.
*   **Display Message:** Update the UI text immediately. Instead of "Adjustment Disabled," use: *"Grid Safety Mode Active due to Extreme Heat. Manual changes restricted to prevent blackout. [Button: Emergency Medical Override]."*
*   **Proactive Notification:** If your device connects to Wi-Fi, push a notification 15 minutes *before* the lockout engages, explaining the duration and the override method.

### C. "Wizard of Oz" Simulation for Regulatory Evidence
You lack real-world data, but you can generate **qualitative evidence** for regulators using your internal team:
*   **Role-Playing Exercise:** Have team members simulate specific personas (e.g., "80-year-old with heart condition," "parent of an infant").
*   **Task:** Attempt to change the temperature during a simulated lockout.
*   **Data Collection:** Record time-to-override, confusion levels, and success rates of the "Emergency Override" path.
*   **Output:** Present these findings as "Usability Safety Testing" showing that while the default is restrictive, the escape hatch is discoverable and functional under stress.

## 3. Regulatory Presentation Structure
Organize your submission to highlight **responsiveness** rather than just restriction:

1.  **Intent Statement:** Clearly define the grid safety goal.
2.  **Risk Acknowledgement:** Explicitly list the identified human risks (medical, psychological).
3.  **Mitigation Architecture:** Detail the "Emergency Override" logic and the enhanced UI messaging.
4.  **Simulation Evidence:** Include the results of the internal role-playing exercise, emphasizing that no user is ever truly "locked out" in a life-safety scenario.
5.  **Post-Launch Monitoring Plan:** Commit to reviewing override logs weekly for the first month to identify patterns requiring further adjustment.

## 4. Immediate Next Steps
*   **Day 1-2:** Deploy the "Emergency Override" logic and updated UI strings to your staging environment.
*   **Day 3-4:** Conduct the internal role-playing simulations and document results.
*   **Day 5:** Compile the safety report integrating the simulation data and mitigation architecture.

By shifting the narrative from "preventing user action" to "managing grid safety with a guaranteed human escape hatch," you address the regulator's primary concern: that life safety always supersedes grid stability.

*Note: Ensure your legal team reviews the specific wording of the "Emergency Override" acknowledgment to limit liability while maintaining safety.*
*This plan relies entirely on software updates and internal process; no external budget or hardware is required.*
