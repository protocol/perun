# Risk Assessment: Camera Coverage Gaps and Manual Mitigations
**Project:** DesertBuild Construction-Safety Monitor
**Location:** Dubai High-Rise Sites
**Context:** Pre-Audit Preparation (2 Weeks Remaining)

## 1. Identified Visibility Gaps
Given the constraint of fixed camera angles and no new hardware, the following blind spots present critical risks for falling object detection:

*   **Vertical Shaft Blind Zones:** Cameras positioned at ground level or mid-floors often cannot track objects accelerating down elevator shafts, stairwells, or utility chases. An object dropped here may gain lethal velocity before entering any camera's field of view (FOV), resulting in zero alert time.
*   **Perimeter Overhangs and Corners:** Wide-angle lenses typically distort or exclude the immediate edges of the building facade. Materials stored on scaffolding just outside the frame, or objects knocked off cornices, will not trigger detection until they are already too low for effective evacuation.
*   **Occlusion by Structural Elements:** Cranes, temporary hoarding, and dense rebar structures create dynamic shadows in the video feed. Small tools or debris falling behind these elements remain invisible to the algorithm until they emerge, potentially too late for an alert.
*   **Low-Contrast Environments:** In high-glare Dubai sunlight or dusty conditions, light-colored objects (e.g., plastic fittings, wood offcuts) falling against concrete backgrounds may fail pixel-differentiation thresholds, causing false negatives even within the visible frame.

## 2. Immediate Manual Mitigation Steps
To satisfy the upcoming safety audit and protect personnel without hardware changes, implement the following procedural controls immediately:

*   **Establish "Presumed Risk" Exclusion Zones:** Map all identified blind spots (shafts, corners, occluded areas) on site plans. Mark the ground radius below these zones as **Red Zones**. Access is prohibited unless a dedicated human spotter is positioned with a direct line of sight to the specific blind spot.
*   **Deploy Human Spotters for High-Risk Activities:** During lifting operations, formwork stripping, or material handling near identified gaps, assign a certified safety officer with a radio dedicated solely to visual monitoring of the blind area. They act as the primary sensor where the camera fails.
*   **Mandatory Tethering Protocol:** Enforce a strict rule that all tools and loose materials used near edges or shafts must be tethered to the worker or the structure. This eliminates the "falling" variable entirely in high-risk zones, rendering camera coverage less critical for those specific items.
*   **Time-Based Work Restrictions:** Prohibit work involving loose materials near blind spots during peak glare hours (11:00–14:00) or during high-wind sandstorm warnings, when camera efficacy is lowest. Schedule these tasks for optimal lighting conditions only.
*   **Physical Debris Netting and Toe Boards:** Install high-visibility netting and reinforced toe boards on all scaffolding and open edges. While this does not improve detection, it physically contains falling objects, mitigating the consequence of a missed detection.
*   **Audit Documentation Strategy:** For the audit, present a "Hybrid Safety Model" diagram. Clearly document that the AI system is a *secondary* layer of defense, with primary reliance on physical containment (netting) and human verification (spotters) in known gap areas. This demonstrates due diligence despite hardware limitations.

## 3. Conclusion
The current system cannot guarantee 100% detection of falling objects due to inherent geometric and environmental blind spots. Safety compliance for the audit must rely on documenting these limitations explicitly and proving that robust manual protocols (exclusion zones, spotters, and tethering) effectively cover the gaps the technology cannot see.

*Note: This assessment is based solely on the operational constraints provided; no site inspection or software analysis was performed.*
