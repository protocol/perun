**Bias Audit Plan for Gig Worker Credit Scoring Model**

**1. Objective**
To identify and quantify systematic exclusion of valid gig workers caused by training data biases favoring formal employment patterns, ensuring fair access across diverse gig economy sectors before production launch.

**2. Scope and Constraints**
*   **Target Population:** Gig workers in Jakarta lacking traditional credit histories.
*   **Sectors to Test:** Ride-hailing, food delivery, freelance digital services, domestic work, and informal retail.
*   **Data Constraint:** Audit must rely solely on available open-source datasets and synthetic data generation to simulate underrepresented demographics.
*   **Timeline:** Execution within 2 weeks to allow 2 weeks for remediation before full production.

**3. Methodology**

*   **Phase A: Data Gap Analysis (Days 1-3)**
    *   Map current open-source training features against known income drivers in the Jakarta gig economy (e.g., cash tips, platform bonuses, multi-apping).
    *   Identify "proxy variables" that may inadvertently penalize informal work patterns (e.g., irregular transaction timestamps, lack of fixed employer ID).
    *   Create a "Shadow Dataset" by augmenting open-source data with synthetic profiles representing valid but non-traditional income streams typical of excluded sectors.

*   **Phase B: Disparate Impact Testing (Days 4-10)**
    *   **Segmentation:** Group test applicants by gig sector, income volatility, and geographic zone within Jakarta.
    *   **Metric Calculation:** Calculate the approval rate ratio between the most favored group (likely formal-like patterns) and the least favored group (irregular gig patterns).
    *   **Threshold:** Flag any segment where the approval rate is less than 80% of the highest-performing segment (the "Four-Fifths Rule") as a potential bias failure.
    *   **False Negative Analysis:** Manually review rejected synthetic profiles with strong repayment indicators (e.g., consistent platform ratings, long tenure) to determine if the model rejects valid risks due to data structure rather than risk reality.

*   **Phase C: Stress Testing Edge Cases (Days 11-14)**
    *   Test "Multi-apping" scenarios: Workers with fragmented income across three+ platforms vs. single-platform workers.
    *   Test "Seasonality" scenarios: Workers with high income variance due to religious holidays or weather patterns common in Jakarta.
    *   Evaluate if the model interprets volatility as risk or as normal gig behavior.

**4. Deliverables**
*   **Bias Heatmap:** Visual matrix showing approval rates by sector and income pattern.
*   **Exclusion Report:** List of specific model features causing the highest rejection rates for valid gig workers.
*   **Remediation Recommendations:** Specific adjustments to feature weighting or threshold tuning to reduce disparate impact without compromising overall risk management.

**5. Success Criteria**
The audit is successful if disparate impact ratios exceed 0.8 across all defined gig sectors and if a clear path is established to approve valid workers previously flagged as "high risk" solely due to informal data patterns.

*Note: Given the one-month timeline, prioritize remediation of high-impact exclusionary features over complete model retraining.*
*Ensure all synthetic data used in testing is documented for investor transparency regarding the audit's limitations.*
