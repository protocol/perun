### Step-by-Step Verification Process for Acoustic Classification

**Phase 1: Synthetic Data Stratification & Segmentation**
Divide your limited city center dataset into three distinct strata before processing:
1.  **Ground Truth Quiet:** Residential streets with verified low decibel readings and no industrial activity.
2.  **Ground Truth Noisy:** Known industrial zones or high-traffic corridors.
3.  **Edge Cases:** Mixed-use areas or residential streets near construction sites.
*Action:* Tag these segments in your internal cloud database. Do not run the full model yet. Process only 5% of the "Ground Truth Quiet" segment to establish a baseline false-positive rate without exhausting cluster resources.

**Phase 2: Iterative Batch Processing with Resource Throttling**
Given your processing constraints, avoid full-city simulations. Instead, execute rolling batch jobs:
1.  **Batch Size:** Process data in small chunks (e.g., one neighborhood block per job) to prevent cluster overload.
2.  **Priority Queue:** Always prioritize "Ground Truth Quiet" batches. If the model flags any of these as "Industrial," halt the pipeline immediately.
3.  **Resource Monitoring:** Set automatic job termination if CPU/Memory usage exceeds 80% to ensure stability for the seven-person team.

**Phase 3: The "Silence Audit" (False Positive Detection)**
For every batch processed, run an automated comparison script against your Ground Truth tags:
*   **Metric:** Calculate the False Positive Rate (FPR) specifically for residential zones.
*   **Threshold:** Define a strict tolerance (e.g., <1% of quiet streets misclassified).
*   **Action:** If FPR exceeds the threshold, flag the specific acoustic features causing the error (e.g., specific frequency ranges mistaken for machinery) and retrain only on those edge cases before the next batch.

**Phase 4: Evidence Compilation for Appeals**
To defend against homeowner appeals, generate a "Classification Dossier" for every flagged zone containing:
1.  **Raw Acoustic Snapshot:** The specific decibel levels and frequency spectra recorded at the time of classification.
2.  **Model Confidence Score:** The probability percentage the model assigned to the "Industrial" label. Low confidence scores on residential flags indicate model uncertainty.
3.  **Neighbor Comparison:** Data showing how adjacent blocks with similar profiles were classified (consistency check).
4.  **Human-in-the-Loop Log:** A record of which team member reviewed the flag and the specific reason for overriding or upholding the classification.

**Phase 5: Final Validation Run**
Once the False Positive Rate stabilizes below your threshold across all stratified batches:
1.  Run a final integration test on a "holdout" set of data never seen by the model during tuning.
2.  Document the final error rates and the specific mitigation steps taken for any remaining edge cases.
3.  Freeze the model version used for this validation to ensure reproducibility if legal challenges arise.

This approach maximizes your limited compute by focusing strictly on the risk of false positives, ensuring that quiet neighborhoods are not erroneously taxed while building a defensible audit trail.

*Note: Ensure all synthetic data used in Phase 1 strictly mirrors real-world variance to avoid biased results. Maintain immutable logs of all batch results for legal discovery.*
