**Immediate Risk Assessment and Mitigation Strategy**

Your current framework contains a critical failure: claiming to "diagnose" severe sleep disorders without medical oversight or validation is not a manageable risk; it is a regulatory violation and a direct threat to patient safety. In Thailand, the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) strictly regulates medical devices. Software that claims to diagnose conditions is classified as a medical device (SaMD). Releasing this beta without registration, clinical validation, or professional oversight exposes DreamStream Health to immediate legal action, forced shutdown, and severe liability if a user suffers harm due to a false negative or positive.

You cannot "manage" the danger of incorrect diagnoses while maintaining the claim of diagnosis. The only practical strategy to secure funding without endangering users or violating the law is to fundamentally pivot the beta's value proposition from **diagnosis** to **wellness monitoring**.

**1. Immediate Product Pivot (Pre-Launch)**
*   **Remove Diagnostic Claims:** Strip all language suggesting the app "diagnoses," "detects," or "identifies" specific medical disorders (e.g., sleep apnea, insomnia disorder). Replace these with wellness-oriented metrics such as "sleep quality scores," "restlessness indicators," or "pattern tracking."
*   **Mandatory Disclaimers:** Implement prominent, unavoidable disclaimers stating: "This app is for wellness and informational purposes only. It does not provide medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. Consult a qualified healthcare professional for any health concerns."
*   **Disable "Immediate Insights" on Severity:** Do not provide immediate alerts regarding "severe" conditions. Instead, offer trend data over time, encouraging users to share these reports with a doctor if they feel unwell.

**2. Validation Strategy Without Clinic Partnerships**
Since you lack clinic partnerships for clinical validation, you cannot claim medical accuracy. Instead, pivot your validation narrative for investors:
*   **Technical Validation:** Focus on the reliability of the sensor data capture and the consistency of the algorithm in tracking *patterns*, not medical outcomes.
*   **User Experience (UX) Validation:** Use the three weeks to test user engagement, interface usability, and data visualization clarity.
*   **Pathway to Clinical Study:** Present a clear roadmap to investors showing that the *next* phase (post-funding) involves formal clinical trials with Thai medical partners to seek FDA approval. Investors in health-tech understand that regulatory clearance is a milestone, not a day-one requirement for a wellness-focused beta.

**3. Liability Shielding**
*   **Terms of Service:** Update your ToS to explicitly exclude liability for health outcomes derived from app usage.
*   **Data Privacy:** Ensure strict compliance with Thailand's Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA), especially regarding sensitive health-related data, even if framed as wellness data.

**Conclusion**
Attempting to launch a diagnostic tool without validation is a existential threat to your startup. By repositioning the beta as a high-fidelity wellness tracker that *empowers* users to seek professional help rather than replacing it, you mitigate legal risk, protect users from harmful misinformation, and present a responsible, scalable path to investors. Funding is more likely to follow a compliant, ethical roadmap than a reckless attempt to bypass medical standards.
