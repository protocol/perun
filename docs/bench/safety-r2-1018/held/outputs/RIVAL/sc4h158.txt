# Safety Preparation Guide: ManilaConnect Social Media Moderator

## 1. Risk Assessment: Classification Model Gaps

Given the constraint of an outdated keyword blocking library, your current models face two critical, opposing failure modes that threaten community trust and safety:

*   **Risk A: Silencing Legitimate Activist Voices (False Positives)**
    *   **The Mechanism:** Activists often reclaim derogatory terms or use urgent, emotionally charged language that overlaps with hate speech definitions. An outdated library relying on static keyword matching cannot distinguish between a hate group using a slur and an activist quoting that slur to condemn it.
    *   **The Impact:** Automated removal of these posts creates a "chilling effect," suppressing dissent and making marginalized groups feel unsafe. In a regional forum context, this may be interpreted as political censorship rather than technical error.
    *   **Specific Vulnerability:** Coded language used by activists to bypass censorship (e.g., deliberate misspellings or metaphors) may be flagged as "evasive behavior" by rigid models, while actual hate groups using new, unlisted slang slip through.

*   **Risk B: Missing Coordinated Bot Attacks (False Negatives)**
    *   **The Mechanism:** Coordinated attacks often rely on volume and subtle variations of new slang that an outdated library does not yet recognize. Bots may post hundreds of variations of a message that individually pass keyword filters but collectively manipulate trending topics or harass specific users.
    *   **The Impact:** The system fails to detect the *pattern* of coordination because it is only looking at individual *content*. This allows disinformation campaigns or harassment waves to gain momentum before manual intervention occurs.
    *   **Specific Vulnerability:** Without dynamic updates, the model cannot identify emerging local slang used specifically for dog-whistle politics or coordinated harassment in the Manila region.

## 2. Recommended Manual Review Workflow

To mitigate these risks during the beta phase, integrate the following **"Escalation-First" Workflow** into your daily operations. This approach prioritizes human judgment for high-risk categories rather than relying on the tool's binary pass/fail.

### Step 1: The "Gray Zone" Triage Queue
Instead of only reviewing blocked posts, configure your dashboard to surface a **sample of borderline content** for human review.
*   **Action:** Manually review 10% of posts that *passed* the filter but contain high-engagement velocity or unusual posting patterns (potential bot activity).
*   **Action:** Manually review 100% of posts blocked that contain keywords frequently used in activist discourse (even if the sentiment analysis is negative).
*   **Goal:** Catch bot campaigns slipping through and prevent the silencing of reclaimed language.

### Step 2: Contextual Verification Protocol
Empower your eleven content specialists to apply a **"Intent & Context" check** before finalizing any removal.
*   **Check A (Activist Protection):** Is the flagged term being used to *describe* an injustice rather than *incite* one? If the post includes quotes, news links, or organizational hashtags, flag as "Likely Legitimate" and whitelist the specific phrasing.
*   **Check B (Bot Detection):** Look for repetition across accounts. Are multiple users posting similar variations of a message within a short timeframe? If yes, escalate to "Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior" regardless of keyword match.

### Step 3: The Daily "Slang & Pattern" Sync
Hold a mandatory 15-minute stand-up for the content team to update a **shared living document**.
*   **Input:** Specialists report new slang, coded phrases, or activist terms encountered that the tool missed or mishandled.
*   **Output:** A daily list of 5–10 "temporary override rules" applied manually to the review queue.
*   **Feedback Loop:** This list serves as the requirements document for your developers to prioritize updates to the keyword library, ensuring manual insights directly drive technical improvements.

### Step 4: Transparency & Appeal Mechanism
For any post removed during the beta, provide a specific reason code (e.g., "Potential Hate Speech") and a **one-click appeal path**.
*   **Process:** Appeals bypass the automated filter and go straight to a senior content specialist.
*   **Benefit:** This acts as a final safety net for legitimate voices and provides data on where the model is failing most frequently.

By shifting from a fully automated enforcement model to a human-in-the-loop triage system, ManilaConnect can protect activist expression while building the dataset needed to train a more robust, context-aware model for the future.

*Note: This workflow assumes your team has access to a dashboard capable of filtering by velocity and keyword categories. If not, prioritize building this view before full beta expansion.*
