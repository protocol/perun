# Pre-Launch Safety Plan: Equitable Traffic Optimization

## 1. Immediate Algorithmic Mitigation
Before further simulation runs, the optimization objective function must be rewritten to include a hard constraint for emergency vehicle throughput. Currently, the system optimizes for "overall congestion," which inadvertently penalizes low-income districts with fewer arterial roads.
*   **Action:** Implement a "Priority Weighting Factor" for emergency routes. The algorithm must treat one minute of ambulance delay as equivalent to $N$ minutes of general commuter delay (where $N$ is a high multiplier, e.g., 50x).
*   **Action:** Establish a "Minimum Throughput Floor." No signal timing plan generated by the AI can result in average emergency vehicle speeds below a specific threshold (e.g., 30 km/h) in any designated district, regardless of overall network efficiency gains.

## 2. Simulation Validation Strategy
Since live testing is prohibited, the simulation environment must be stressed to prove safety margins. The current "happy path" simulations are insufficient.
*   **Adversarial Scenario Generation:** Run 1,000+ Monte Carlo simulations focusing exclusively on worst-case scenarios for low-income districts. Variables should include: peak festival traffic, road closures due to processions, and simultaneous multiple ambulance dispatches in non-arterial zones.
*   **Baseline Comparison:** Compare the AI's performance against two baselines:
    1.  The current static timing plans used by the city.
    2.  A "Emergency-First" heuristic model that ignores commuter congestion entirely.
    *   *Success Criterion:* The AI must perform within 5% of the "Emergency-First" model regarding ambulance transit times, while still offering net benefits to general traffic.
*   **Spatial Equity Audit:** Disaggregate simulation results by neighborhood income data. Generate a "Delay Equity Map" visualizing the ratio of ambulance delay in low-income vs. high-income zones. If the ratio exceeds 1.1 (10% disparity), the model fails validation.

## 3. Synthetic Data & Edge Cases
*   **Data Augmentation:** Inject synthetic "ghost" ambulance trips into historical traffic logs used for training/validation. These trips should originate from deep within low-income grids, not just main roads, to test the system's ability to clear paths from non-optimized nodes.
*   **Failure Mode Analysis:** Specifically simulate sensor failures or communication latency in low-income areas (which may have older infrastructure). Ensure the AI defaults to a safe, pre-calculated "emergency corridor" timing pattern rather than attempting real-time optimization with incomplete data.

## 4. Governance and "Go/No-Go" Gates
*   **Independent Review Panel:** Before the six-week deadline, convene a review board including city planners, representatives from local health services, and community leaders from the affected low-income districts. Present the "Delay Equity Map" and worst-case simulation logs.
*   **Hard Stop Criteria:** Define explicit failure conditions that prevent certification request. Examples:
    *   Any simulation run where ambulance response time increases by >15% compared to current static systems.
    *   Any identified scenario where the AI creates a "gridlock trap" preventing emergency egress from a specific zone for >5 minutes.
*   **Documentation for Certification:** Compile a "Safety Case" dossier. This must not only show average improvements but explicitly document how the system behaves under stress in vulnerable areas. Include the mathematical proof of the priority weighting constraints.

## 5. Deployment Safeguards (Post-Certification Prep)
Although live testing is currently banned, prepare the operational framework for when certification is granted:
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Override:** Ensure traffic control center operators have a one-click "Emergency Override" to freeze signals in a green-wave pattern for specific corridors, bypassing the AI instantly.
*   **Real-Time Monitoring Dashboard:** Build a dashboard that tracks the "Equity Ratio" in real-time. If live data ever shows the disparity widening beyond simulation predictions, the system should automatically revert to static timing.

By shifting the optimization goal from "global efficiency" to "constrained equity," and rigorously stressing the simulation with adversarial scenarios, the team can demonstrate due diligence regarding public safety and social justice before seeking official certification.

*Note: This plan relies entirely on the fidelity of your simulation engine; if the simulator does not accurately model low-income road geometry, these tests will yield false positives.*
*Ensure all simulation parameters and weighting factors are version-controlled for auditability during the certification process.*
