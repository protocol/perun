### Targeted Safety Term Review Workflow for CairoTranslate

**Phase 1: Critical Term Extraction & Prioritization**
Before translation begins, extract a "Golden List" of high-risk safety terms from the source manuals. This list should include imperative verbs (e.g., "Disconnect," "Lockout"), hazard identifiers (e.g., "High Voltage," "Crush Zone"), and conditional warnings (e.g., "Only if," "Never while"). Prioritize these terms based on severity: any error resulting in immediate physical harm receives "Critical" status, while operational errors receive "High" status. This list serves as the anchor for all subsequent checks, ensuring the twenty-language model is evaluated specifically on its weakest potential points rather than general fluency.

**Phase 2: Automated Flagging via Back-Translation & Consistency Checks**
Run the source manuals through your neural model to generate drafts in all twenty languages. Immediately subject these drafts to an automated filtering process:
1.  **Back-Translation:** Automatically translate the output back into the source language. Flag any segment where the back-translated meaning deviates significantly from the original source, specifically looking for negation errors (e.g., "Do not touch" becoming "Touch") or modality shifts (e.g., "Must" becoming "Should").
2.  **Keyword Presence Check:** Script a scan to ensure every term from the "Golden List" appears in the target output in some form. If a critical source term has no corresponding detected entity in the target text, flag the entire paragraph for human review.
3.  **Cross-Language Consistency:** Since you are using a single multi-lingual model, compare how specific ambiguous source terms are rendered across different languages. If the model translates a term inconsistently in similar contexts within the same language, flag those instances.

**Phase 3: The "Linguist-Engineer" Dual Verification Loop**
Divide your team of nine into paired units, each assigned a subset of the 20 languages based on proficiency. For every segment flagged in Phase 2:
*   **The Linguist** verifies semantic accuracy, ensuring the tone is imperative and unambiguous in the target culture.
*   **The Engineer** verifies technical validity, confirming that the translated instruction aligns with the physical machinery's actual safety mechanisms.
*   **Resolution:** If either party flags an issue, the segment is marked "Blocked." The pair must collaboratively rewrite the translation until both sign off. Do not proceed to printing until all "Critical" and "High" flags are resolved.

**Phase 4: Contextual Spot-Checking**
Even if a specific term translates correctly in isolation, it may fail in context. Randomly select 5% of non-flagged pages containing high-density safety instructions. Have a native-speaking engineer (who was not part of the initial pairing) read the translated manual *without* looking at the source. Ask them to explain the safety procedure back to you. If their understanding of the risk differs from the intended design, the entire section undergoes re-verification.

**Phase 5: Final Freeze and Audit Trail**
Once all flags are cleared, generate a final report listing every critical term, its translation in all twenty languages, and the names of the linguist and engineer who approved it. This document serves as your audit trail. Lock the translation files immediately after this sign-off. Any subsequent changes to the manual, no matter how small, must trigger a re-run of Phase 2 for the affected sections only.

*Note: Maintain a living "False Positive" log to refine your automated flagging scripts over time, reducing manual workload for future batches.*
*Note: Ensure your team has access to physical photos or diagrams of the machinery during Phase 3 to ground abstract terms in physical reality.*
