**Immediate Action Plan: Bias Review and Mitigation**

**1. Halt Automated Rejections Immediately**
Do not activate the system for public use or issue any automated rejections until this review is complete. Given the observed disparity affecting minority-led organizations, proceeding poses a significant legal and ethical risk to the council. Manually revert to your existing human-led review process for the upcoming quarterly cycle, even if it requires staff overtime. The cost of manual review is lower than the cost of discriminatory outcomes and potential litigation.

**2. Conduct a "Human-in-the-Loop" Disparity Audit**
Since you cannot purchase new software, use your current staff to perform a targeted manual audit of the internal trial data:
*   **Sample Selection:** Extract all applications flagged for rejection by the system during trials. Create two groups: those led by minority-owned organizations and those led by non-minority organizations.
*   **Blind Review:** Assign your eight staff members to review these flagged applications *without* seeing the system's decision or the applicant's demographic data. They should apply the written grant criteria strictly.
*   **Comparison:** Compare the human decisions against the system's decisions. Calculate the "false positive" rate for each group. If the system rejects applications that humans would approve significantly more often for minority-led groups, your historical training data likely contains embedded biases (e.g., past funding patterns that favored certain demographics).

**3. Scrutinize Data Proxies and Criteria**
Bias often hides in variables that seem neutral but correlate with race or geography:
*   **Review Input Variables:** Examine every data point the system uses. Does it consider "past funding history," "neighborhood zip code," "organizational budget size," or "language complexity"? These often act as proxies for race or socioeconomic status in London.
*   **Historical Data Check:** If the system was trained on historical funding data, acknowledge that this data reflects past human biases. If previous reviewers systematically underfunded minority groups, the AI has learned to replicate that discrimination. You must decouple the model from historical outcomes and re-align it strictly with current written policy.

**4. Implement Simple Mitigation Strategies**
Without new tools, you can adjust the system's logic or usage:
*   **Remove High-Risk Variables:** If specific data points (like zip code or past funding amounts) drive the disparity, disable them in the decision logic immediately.
*   **Change the System's Role:** Repurpose the tool from a "decision-maker" to a "triage assistant." Instead of auto-rejecting, have the system flag applications for *priority human review* only. Ensure no application is rejected without a human signature.
*   **Threshold Adjustment:** If the system uses a confidence score to reject, raise the threshold significantly so only the most clearly ineligible applications are flagged, reducing the risk of edge-case bias affecting vulnerable groups.

**5. Document and Communicate**
*   **Transparency:** Document the disparity you found, the steps taken to investigate, and the decision to delay automation. This demonstrates due diligence.
*   **Stakeholder Notification:** Inform leadership and relevant community stakeholders that the council is pausing automation to ensure fairness. This builds trust rather than eroding it.

**6. Long-Term Data Hygiene**
Before the next cycle, commit to cleaning your training data. This involves manually labeling a dataset based on *current* policy (not past decisions) to retrain or recalibrate the logic. If internal expertise is lacking, seek pro-bono assistance from local universities or tech-for-good nonprofits in London who specialize in algorithmic auditing.
