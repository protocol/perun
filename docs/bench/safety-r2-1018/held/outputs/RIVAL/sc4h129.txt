### Pre-Launch Bias Examination Plan

**1. Construct Synthetic "Mirror" Profiles (Days 1–3)**
Since you cannot acquire new datasets, use your existing internal data to create controlled test cases. Identify 10–15 "golden" profiles from your historical data that were successfully hired and performed well, specifically those with non-traditional backgrounds (e.g., bootcamp graduates, career switchers, gap years).
*   **Action:** Create paired synthetic resumes for each golden profile. Keep skills, experience duration, and achievements identical.
*   **Variable:** Systematically alter only the demographic proxies or non-traditional markers (e.g., change university names to lesser-known institutions, remove prestigious brand names, alter graduation years to create gaps, or modify phrasing typical of non-native speakers).
*   **Volume:** With a team of eight, each member can generate 5–10 paired variations, yielding 40–80 controlled test cases without external data.

**2. Execute Blind A/B Screening (Days 4–7)**
Run your current dataset and the new synthetic mirror profiles through the screener in a "blind" mode where possible, or manually record the outputs if the system logs are opaque.
*   **Metric:** Compare the pass/fail decision and ranking score between the "traditional" version and the "non-traditional" version of the *same* candidate.
*   **Threshold:** Flag any instance where the non-traditional variant is rejected or ranked significantly lower (>20% score drop) than its traditional mirror. This isolates bias from qualification differences.

**3. Heuristic Keyword & Pattern Audit (Days 8–10)**
Manually inspect the system's logic or prompt instructions (if accessible) for rigid filters that disproportionately affect non-traditional paths.
*   **Focus Areas:** Look for hard filters on specific university accreditations, strict date ranges for employment gaps, or requirements for specific job titles that might differ across industries (e.g., "Ninja" vs. "Developer").
*   **Team Task:** Assign two team members to trace the decision path of the flagged synthetic profiles from Step 2 to identify exactly which rule triggered the rejection.

**4. Adversarial "Red Team" Review (Days 11–14)**
Utilize the remaining team capacity to attempt to "break" the fairness of the system.
*   **Scenario Testing:** Have team members write resumes for highly qualified candidates using only non-standard phrasing, foreign-language degree titles (translated), or unconventional formatting.
*   **Goal:** Determine if the system fails to parse valid experience due to formatting or linguistic bias rather than actual lack of skill.

**5. Mitigation and Calibration (Days 15–21)**
*   **Adjust Thresholds:** If the system uses a scoring threshold, analyze if lowering it slightly recovers high-quality non-traditional candidates without flooding the pipeline with unqualified ones.
*   **Rule Relaxation:** Replace hard "knock-out" questions regarding education or gaps with soft scoring penalties, allowing human review for borderline cases.
*   **Human-in-the-Loop:** Mandate that any candidate rejected by the AI who possesses a specific set of core skills (identified in Step 3) must be routed to a human recruiter for a secondary check before final dismissal.

**Deliverable:** A report listing specific rules or keywords causing disparate impact, supported by the pass/fail delta of your synthetic mirror profiles, and a configured "safe mode" for launch that routes edge cases to humans.

*Note: This plan relies entirely on internal data manipulation and team hours, requiring zero external spend. Prioritize fixing "hard filters" first, as they cause the most immediate exclusion.*
