### Equitable Routing Review Framework for QuickEats

**Objective:** Systematically identify and mitigate discriminatory patterns in the new routing algorithm using existing simulation data, ensuring equitable service across all neighborhoods prior to launch.

#### Phase 1: Data Stratification & Baseline Definition
Before analyzing results, segment the simulation data to ensure fair comparison.
*   **Geographic Segmentation:** Map all delivery zones using census tract data. Tag each zone with demographic indicators (specifically racial composition) and socioeconomic status.
*   **Control Group Establishment:** Define "baseline performance" using historical data from the current algorithm or industry standards for similar density areas.
*   **Variable Isolation:** Ensure simulation scenarios vary only the routing logic while holding constant order volume, driver availability, and traffic conditions to isolate algorithmic impact.

#### Phase 2: Metric Definition (The Equity Scorecard)
Move beyond average efficiency metrics. Evaluate the algorithm using these specific equity-focused KPIs:
*   **Service Availability Rate:** Percentage of orders accepted vs. rejected by neighborhood. Flag any zone with rejection rates >10% above the city average.
*   **Estimated Wait Time Disparity:** Compare average predicted delivery times in predominantly Black neighborhoods versus demographically similar white neighborhoods with comparable distance from restaurants.
*   **Dynamic Pricing/Surge Bias:** Analyze if the algorithm triggers surge pricing or "unavailable" status more frequently in specific demographic zones during peak times.
*   **Driver Allocation Efficiency:** Measure the ratio of drivers assigned to orders per square mile in target neighborhoods compared to control neighborhoods.

#### Phase 3: Simulation Stress Testing
Run targeted simulation scenarios designed to expose edge cases where bias often hides:
*   **The "Food Desert" Test:** Simulate low-order volume periods. Does the algorithm completely withdraw service from specific areas while maintaining thin coverage in others?
*   **The "Boundary" Test:** Analyze behavior at the borders of predominantly Black neighborhoods. Does service quality drop sharply upon crossing a specific street line?
*   **The "Restaurant Density" Test:** Normalize results by restaurant count. If an area has fewer restaurants, does the algorithm penalize wait times disproportionately compared to areas with similar restaurant density but different demographics?

#### Phase 4: Thresholds & Go/No-Go Criteria
Establish hard limits based on the analysis. The launch should be paused if:
*   Any predominantly Black neighborhood shows a service availability rate <90% of the citywide average.
*   Average wait times in target zones exceed the citywide average by >15% without a corresponding distance-based justification.
*   Simulation shows a correlation coefficient >0.6 between racial composition and order rejection rates.

#### Phase 5: Mitigation & Iteration
If thresholds are breached:
*   **Constraint Injection:** Modify the optimization function to include a "fairness constraint" that mandates minimum service levels per zone, even at a slight cost to total global efficiency.
*   **Weight Adjustment:** Recalibrate the weight of "distance" vs. "neighborhood priority" in the scoring logic.
*   **Re-simulation:** Re-run the stress tests immediately after adjustment. Do not proceed until all Go/No-Go criteria are met.

#### Reporting & Governance
*   **Documentation:** Create a "Bias Audit Report" summarizing findings, metrics, and adjustments made.
*   **Stakeholder Review:** The four-person product team must sign off on the Equity Scorecard results.
*   **Post-Launch Monitoring Plan:** Define a lightweight protocol to monitor these same KPIs using real-world data for the first 30 days post-launch, triggering an immediate rollback if live data mirrors simulation failures.

*Note: This framework relies entirely on the integrity of your input simulation data; ensure the underlying traffic and demand models do not themselves contain historical biases.*
