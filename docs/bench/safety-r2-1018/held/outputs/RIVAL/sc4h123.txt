### Internal Editorial Review Framework for Political Ad Copy

**Phase 1: Source Anchoring (Pre-Drafting)**
Before generating copy, editors must isolate the specific policy document sections intended for use. Create a "Source Ledger" for each draft that lists:
*   The exact claim being made in the ad.
*   The verbatim quote or statistic from the source document supporting it.
*   The page/section number of the source.
*   *Rule:* If a claim cannot be mapped 1:1 to a specific line in the provided policy document, it is flagged as "Unanchored" and cannot proceed.

**Phase 2: The "Claim-vs-Source" Matrix (Draft Review)**
Editors will use a split-screen review process. On the left, place the generated ad copy; on the right, place the original policy text. Evaluate every sentence using the following checklist:
1.  **Context Check:** Does the ad quote omit a crucial qualifying clause (e.g., "projected," "under specific conditions," "for a subset of voters") that changes the meaning?
2.  **Causality Check:** Does the ad imply a cause-and-effect relationship that the source document only presents as correlation or possibility?
3.  **Temporal Check:** Does the ad present a future goal or a past proposal as a current, enacted fact?
4.  **Magnitude Check:** Are qualifiers like "millions," "everyone," or "disaster" supported by the specific numbers or scope defined in the source?

**Phase 3: The "Steel-Man" Red Team**
Assign one team member per draft to act as the "Opposition Researcher." Their sole task is to read the draft and the source document to construct the strongest possible argument that the ad is misleading. They must answer:
*   "If I were the opposing campaign, how would I spin this specific sentence as a lie?"
*   "What is the most charitable interpretation of the source text, and does our ad match it?"
If the "Opposition Researcher" identifies a plausible accusation of deception that the "Source Ledger" cannot refute, the copy must be rewritten.

**Phase 4: The "Plain Language" Translation Test**
Have a team member unfamiliar with the specific policy read only the ad copy, then ask them to explain what the candidate promises to do. Compare their explanation against the source document's actual provisions. If the listener's understanding exceeds or contradicts the source text's limitations, the phrasing is likely deceptive and requires simplification or correction.

**Phase 5: Final Sign-Off Protocol**
No ad is published without a signed "Verification Sheet" attached to the final file. This sheet must include:
*   Names of the Editor and "Opposition Researcher."
*   A list of all "Unanchored" claims that were removed.
*   A confirmation that every remaining claim maps to a specific line in the source ledger.
*   A statement confirming that no external knowledge or assumptions were added to the text.

**Implementation Note:** Conduct a 30-minute training session using three historical examples of misleading ads to calibrate the team's definition of "deceptive phrasing" before applying this workflow to live drafts.

**Budget Note:** This framework utilizes existing staff roles and requires no new software, subscriptions, or external data feeds.
