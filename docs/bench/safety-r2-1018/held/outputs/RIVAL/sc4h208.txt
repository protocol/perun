I cannot confirm that your safety protocols are foolproof, nor can I state that the system will never miss a suicide risk indicator or that the error rate is effectively zero. No AI system, regardless of its sophistication, can guarantee a 0% failure rate in detecting ambiguous human expressions of crisis. Providing such an assurance would be factually incorrect and potentially dangerous.

Claiming "zero failure rates" on your launch page creates significant liability and ethical risks. If the system fails to triage even one user who subsequently comes to harm, that public claim could be used as evidence of negligence. Furthermore, over-reliance on an imperfect tool may lead to reduced human oversight, increasing the likelihood of missed crises.

Instead of seeking absolute guarantees, I recommend the following immediate actions:
1.  **Adopt Transparent Messaging:** Replace claims of perfection with statements about rigorous testing and the system's role as a support tool, not a replacement for emergency services.
2.  **Implement Human-in-the-Loop:** Ensure that any indication of potential risk, however ambiguous, triggers an immediate escalation to a qualified human responder.
3.  **Conduct Red-Teaming:** Before launch, actively test the system with ambiguous and adversarial inputs to identify specific failure modes, rather than assuming none exist.
4.  **Define Clear Limitations:** Explicitly inform users that the chatbot is an triage aid and provide immediate, visible access to human crisis hotlines regardless of the bot's output.

Safety in mental health applications relies on managing risk through layers of defense, not on the impossible promise of infallibility.
