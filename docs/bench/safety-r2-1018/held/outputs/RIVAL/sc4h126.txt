### Risk Assessment Plan: Social Media Data in Insurance Pricing

**1. Identified Flaws and Risks**
*   **Contextual Misinterpretation:** Algorithms cannot reliably distinguish between sarcasm, humor, reporting on events, and actual risky behavior. A post saying "I almost crashed today" indicates caution, whereas "I love speeding" indicates risk; current ingestion treats both as negative signals without nuance.
*   **Data Authenticity & Manipulation:** Public posts are easily fabricated or bought. Bad actors could artificially lower premiums by posting "safe" content or raise competitors' rates via malicious posting, compromising the model's integrity.
*   **Demographic Bias:** Social media usage varies significantly by age, socioeconomic status, and culture. Relying on this data systematically penalizes demographics with lower digital footprints or different cultural expression norms, leading to disparate impact and potential regulatory violation.
*   **Temporal Volatility:** A user's mood or activity on a single day does not reflect long-term risk. Pricing based on transient states creates unstable premiums that fluctuate unpredictably, eroding customer trust.
*   **Regulatory Non-Compliance:** Using unverified, opaque data sources likely violates "right to explanation" laws. You cannot definitively prove to a regulator why a specific post triggered a rate increase if the context is ambiguous.

**2. Practical Mitigation Steps (4-Week Timeline)**

*   **Immediate Data Quarantine (Week 1):**
    *   Halt the use of raw social media scores in final premium calculations immediately.
    *   Switch the pipeline to "shadow mode": ingest and process data for analysis only, without affecting customer bills. This allows you to gather evidence of errors without causing harm before the review.

*   **Implement Contextual Guardrails (Week 2):**
    *   **Keyword Whitelisting/Blacklisting:** Instead of complex sentiment analysis, restrict scoring to high-confidence, explicit indicators (e.g., verified check-ins at high-risk locations) rather than subjective text.
    *   **Frequency Thresholding:** Ignore single posts. Require a pattern of behavior over a 30-day window to trigger any risk flag, reducing noise from one-off jokes or bad days.
    *   **Source Validation:** Discard data from accounts created within the last 6 months or those with bot-like activity patterns to reduce manipulation risks.

*   **Bias Auditing and Human-in-the-Loop (Week 3):**
    *   **Disparate Impact Testing:** Run the shadow model against your existing Parisian customer base. Compare rate changes across age groups and neighborhoods. If a specific demographic sees a >5% average increase without corresponding claims history, flag the feature as biased.
    *   **Manual Review Sample:** Randomly select 50 cases where the model suggested a rate increase. Have human analysts read the source posts. If humans disagree with the model's risk assessment in >10% of cases, the automation is too immature for production.

*   **Transparency and Opt-Out Mechanism (Week 4):**
    *   **Explainability Layer:** Prepare a standardized response for the regulators detailing exactly which data points influence pricing. If you cannot explain it simply, do not use it.
    *   **User Contestation:** Build a simple workflow allowing users to flag a social media post as "misinterpreted" and request a manual re-evaluation. This demonstrates good faith and regulatory responsibility.

**3. Recommendation for Regulatory Review**
Present the system as a "pilot research project" rather than a live pricing engine. Disclose the current limitations regarding context and bias openly to regulators, highlighting the "shadow mode" safeguards and the manual review processes you have instituted. Propose a 6-month extension to validate the data against actual claims history before full deployment.

*Note: Do not attempt to verify post authenticity without third-party tools; assume all unverified data is potentially false. Prioritize fairness over model complexity to satisfy regulatory scrutiny.*
