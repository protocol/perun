**Safety-First VR Evaluation Framework**

**1. Pre-Deployment Static Analysis**
Before any human interaction, conduct a rigorous review of the simulation assets against known comfort heuristics:
*   **Frame Rate Stability:** Verify that the application maintains a locked frame rate matching the headset's refresh rate (e.g., 72Hz or 90Hz) under worst-case load scenarios using profiler tools. Any dropped frames must be resolved before testing.
*   **Locomotion Mechanics:** Audit movement systems. Replace artificial locomotion (joystick walking) with teleportation or room-scale movement where possible. If artificial movement is required, ensure a static cockpit or horizon reference is permanently visible.
*   **Acceleration Profiles:** Ensure all camera movements use smooth acceleration and deceleration curves. Instantaneous starts or stops are primary triggers for vestibular conflict.
*   **Field of View (FOV) Management:** Implement dynamic FOV restriction (vignetting) that automatically activates during any user-controlled or scripted movement.

**2. Controlled Pilot Observation (Non-Provocative)**
If human observation is necessary, it must be conducted under strict medical supervision with immediate termination criteria, not as a stress test:
*   **Participant Selection:** Use only volunteers with prior VR experience and no history of vestibular disorders.
*   **Session Limits:** Limit exposure to 5-minute intervals with mandatory 15-minute breaks.
*   **Passive Monitoring:** Observe for early signs of discomfort (pallor, sweating, postural instability) rather than waiting for reported sickness. The test ends immediately upon the first sign of distress.
*   **Data Collection:** Record subjective comfort scores (Simulator Sickness Questionnaire) pre- and post-session, but do not attempt to "break" the user to find limits.

**3. Iterative Refinement Strategy**
*   **Component Isolation:** Test individual visual elements (e.g., particle effects, lighting changes, movement speeds) in isolation at low intensity, gradually increasing only if zero discomfort is reported.
*   **Hardware Verification:** Confirm that the fixed hardware specification is sufficient for the scene complexity. If the hardware cannot maintain performance without dropped frames, the content complexity must be reduced to match the hardware, not the other way around.

**4. Deployment Gate**
Widespread deployment should only occur after a sustained period of pilot testing yields zero reports of moderate-to-severe sickness and zero incidents of loss of balance. If the current build causes falls, it is fundamentally unsafe for industrial use and requires architectural redesign rather than parameter tuning.
