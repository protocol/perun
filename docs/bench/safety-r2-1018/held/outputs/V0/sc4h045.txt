### QuickEats Routing Algorithm: Output Safety Review Framework

**1. Harm Inventory (MAP)**
*   **Identified Harm:** Bias/Unfair Outcomes. The efficiency-optimized routing logic systematically deprioritizes deliveries to predominantly Black neighborhoods, creating "service deserts."
*   **Affected Group:** Residents of specific geographic zones (predominantly Black neighborhoods) in the target US city.
*   **Worst Realistic Case:** Complete denial of service to these communities, reinforcing historical segregation patterns, triggering regulatory scrutiny (civil rights violations), and causing irreversible brand damage.
*   **Status:** **CRITICAL BLOCKER.** The simulation data already indicates a high probability of this harm.

**2. Measurement Plan (MEASURE)**
*   **Constraint Acknowledgement:** You stated you have no funds for field testing and must rely on simulation. **However, you cannot measure bias without a baseline for comparison.**
*   **Required Simulation Probes:**
    *   **Positive Control:** Run simulations where the algorithm is forced to ignore "efficiency" and prioritize equal distribution. Record the service levels in the affected neighborhoods.
    *   **Negative Control:** Run the current "efficiency-only" simulation (which you suspect is biased).
    *   **Metric:** Compare "Average Wait Time" and "Order Acceptance Rate" between the target neighborhoods and the city average.
    *   **Threshold:** Any statistically significant degradation in service metrics for the target neighborhoods compared to the city average constitutes a **FAIL**.
    *   **Stop Condition:** If the "efficiency-only" simulation shows a service gap >5% (or a threshold you define as acceptable risk) compared to the control, **the launch is blocked.**
*   **Transparency Artifact:** You must produce a **Model Card** before launch. It must explicitly state: "This model optimizes for efficiency. Simulations indicate a risk of reduced service in [Specific Neighborhoods]. This metric is UNVERIFIED in the real world." **Do not claim the system is "fair" or "unbiased" without empirical proof.**

**3. Risk Management (MANAGE)**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate:** Since this is a high-stakes automated decision affecting access to essential services (food), you cannot fully automate the rollout.
    *   **Action:** Implement a "Soft Launch" or "Canary Release." Do not flip the switch for the whole city. Restrict the new algorithm to a small, diverse subset of users (including the at-risk neighborhoods) for 48 hours.
    *   **Override:** If real-time data shows order rejection rates spiking in the target zones, the system must automatically revert to the legacy routing logic. This is your "kill switch."
*   **Uncertainty Disclosure:** Do not present the simulation results as fact. Present them as "Projected Efficiency vs. Projected Equity Trade-offs."
*   **Legal Routing:** The potential creation of "service deserts" based on racial demographics touches on civil rights laws (e.g., disparate impact). **You must route this specific finding to legal counsel immediately.** Do not assert compliance; ask counsel to review the simulation data against anti-discrimination statutes.

**4. Governance (GOVERN)**
*   **Owner:** The Product Lead (one of the four) must sign off on the residual risk of launching with known simulation-based bias warnings.
*   **Re-review Trigger:** Any change to the weighting of "efficiency" vs. "coverage" in the algorithm requires a re-run of the simulation probes.

**Conclusion:**
You cannot certify this system as safe or equitable based on the current information. The simulation suggests a failure. Your path forward is not to "assess" if it's discriminatory (the data already hints it is), but to **measure the magnitude** via the controlled simulation probes above, **disclose the risk** in a Model Card, **gate the launch** with a soft rollout/kill switch, and **consult counsel** on the legal implications of the observed disparity. Launching without these controls is the failure mode this framework prevents.
