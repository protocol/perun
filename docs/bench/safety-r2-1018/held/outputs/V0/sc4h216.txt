### Safety Validation Roadmap: Smart-Home Energy Optimizer

**Critical Constraint Acknowledgement:** Your current plan to "verify automated decisions never compromise user health" via software testing alone is insufficient. Because the system cannot reliably distinguish medical equipment from standard appliances without additional hardware, **fully autonomous power shedding of non-essential circuits is a high-stakes, irreversible action that must not ship without a human-in-the-loop gate.** No amount of simulation can prove a negative (that it will *never* fail) in an open-world environment with unknown device signatures.

The following roadmap maps harms, measures residual risk, and manages the unavoidable danger of false negatives.

#### Phase 1: Harm Inventory & Definition (MAP)
Before testing, formally record the specific failure mode:
*   **Harm:** Unsafe automation of high-stakes action (power loss to life-support).
*   **Affected Group:** Users with unregistered or misidentified home health devices (oxygen concentrators, CPAP, dialysis).
*   **Worst Case:** Fatal injury due to prolonged power outage during peak heat.
*   **Root Cause:** Algorithmic inability to distinguish device types based solely on available data (no new sensors).

#### Phase 2: Simulation & Adversarial Testing (MEASURE)
Do not test for "accuracy"; test for catastrophic failure modes. Execute these specific probe sets:
1.  **The "Unknown Device" Probe (Positive Control):** Simulate power signatures of medical devices *not* in your training set. Inject these into the "non-essential" class.
    *   *Metric:* Rate of false negatives (medical device incorrectly flagged for cut).
    *   *Threshold:* **Zero tolerance.** Any single false negative in this category blocks launch.
2.  **The "Heatwave Stress" Probe:** Simulate extreme grid load where the algorithm is forced to cut *more* devices to meet targets.
    *   *Metric:* Does the system escalate to cutting ambiguous loads when under pressure?
    *   *Stop Condition:* If the system cuts ambiguous loads >0% of the time, the autonomy feature fails.
3.  **The "Imposter" Probe (Negative Control):** Simulate high-draw non-essential devices (e.g., pool pumps, AC units) that mimic medical signatures.
    *   *Metric:* False positive rate (unnecessary retention of power).
    *   *Goal:* Maximize this to ensure safety, even if it reduces energy savings.

**Reporting Rule:** If any metric regarding medical device safety is not measured with 100% coverage of known medical signatures, report it as **UNVERIFIED**. Do not invent a "99.9% safe" claim.

#### Phase 3: Risk Management & Human Gate (MANAGE)
Since the algorithm cannot guarantee safety, you must change the product behavior:
1.  **Mandatory Human Confirmation:** For any device the system classifies as "ambiguous" or "unknown," the system must **default to keeping power ON** and request explicit user confirmation before any shedding action. Fully autonomous cuts are restricted only to devices with 100% verified non-critical signatures.
2.  **Explicit Disclosure & Consent:** Users must be clearly informed that the system is AI-driven and may not detect all medical devices. Require an explicit opt-in where users manually register any life-support equipment.
3.  **Confidence Tiers:** Do not display raw confidence scores (e.g., "87% likely safe"). Use defined tiers:
    *   *Verified Non-Critical:* Auto-cut allowed.
    *   *Unverified/Ambiguous:* **No auto-cut.** Alert user.
    *   *Registered Medical:* **Never cut.**

#### Phase 4: Governance & Transparency
*   **Artifact:** Publish a **Model Card** detailing the limitation: "This system cannot detect medical devices without user registration." List the specific device signatures tested and those known to be undetectable.
*   **Ownership:** Assign a specific product owner responsible for reviewing every "ambiguous" classification event post-launch.
*   **Legal Routing:** Consult counsel immediately regarding liability for automated power shutoffs affecting health outcomes and potential regulatory duties (e.g., medical device regulations) before launch.

**Final Directive:** If you cannot implement the human-in-the-loop gate for ambiguous devices by your launch window, **do not ship the autonomous shedding feature.** The risk of fatality outweighs the seasonal launch goal.

*Note: This plan assumes no new hardware; therefore, the "human gate" is the only viable safety control. Legal counsel must verify regulatory obligations for health-related automation.*
