### Risk Assessment and Mitigation Plan: Social Media-Based Premium Calculator

**1. Harm Inventory (MAP)**
Before the regulatory review, we must explicitly name the risks inherent in ingesting unverified social data for pricing:
*   **Bias/Unfair Outcomes:** Algorithms may systematically penalize specific demographics whose online expression differs culturally or linguistically, leading to discriminatory pricing.
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** The system may interpret sarcasm, jokes, or shared news articles as the user's actual behavior or risk profile, presenting fabricated risk scores as objective truth.
*   **Missing Context/Authenticity:** Public posts lack verification of authorship (impersonation) or context (e.g., a post about a friend's accident vs. the user's own), leading to false positives in risk assessment.
*   **Over-reliance:** Stakeholders may trust the automated score without understanding the low reliability of the underlying unverified data.
*   **Unsafe High-Stakes Automation:** Automatically adjusting insurance premiums based on this noisy data constitutes a high-stakes, financially damaging action without a human gate.

**2. Measurement Strategy (MEASURE)**
We cannot certify the system as "unbiased" or "accurate" without validation. Instead, we will treat all current metrics as **UNVERIFIED**.
*   **Adversarial Testing:** Construct a probe set containing known false positives (e.g., sarcastic posts, news sharing, impersonated profiles) and known negatives.
*   **Subgroup Analysis:** Run the current pipeline against data sliced by demographic proxies to detect disparate impact.
*   **Stop Condition:** If the error rate on the "known false positive" set exceeds 0%, or if disparate impact is detected in subgroup testing, the automated pricing feature must not launch.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Draft a preliminary **Model Card** stating clearly: "Intended use is limited; data provenance is unverified public social media; performance by subgroup is currently UNVERIFIED." This document must accompany the regulatory submission to avoid claims of fabricated accuracy.

**3. Risk Management Controls (MANAGE)**
Given the four-week timeline and zero budget for new verification services, we must implement strict procedural guards:
*   **Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Gate:** **Mandatory.** No premium adjustment based on social media data shall be executed automatically. Any flag raised by the system must trigger a manual review by a human underwriter who can verify context and authenticity before a price change is finalized. Full automation of this high-stakes action is the failure mode we must prevent.
*   **Defined Confidence Tiers:** Do not display raw probability scores (e.g., "87% risk") to users or underwriters, as this implies false precision. Instead, use coarse, defined tiers such as "Unverified Signal," "Corroborated Signal," or "Confirmed Risk," accompanied by the specific post content for human judgment.
*   **Explicit Disclosure:** Users must be informed that their public social media activity is being used as a *signal* for review, not as a definitive fact for pricing.
*   **Legal Routing:** Immediately route the question of whether this specific use of automated decision-making triggers specific regulatory duties (e.g., right to explanation, prohibition on certain data types) to legal counsel. Do not assert compliance internally; name the regime (e.g., EU AI Act) and let counsel determine binding obligations.

**4. Immediate Next Steps**
1.  Freeze the pipeline to prevent any live auto-pricing.
2.  Build the adversarial test set using existing team knowledge of common social media ambiguities.
3.  Draft the Model Card marking all accuracy claims as UNVERIFIED.
4.  Implement the HITL workflow as the primary launch feature.

*Note: This plan identifies residual risk and mandates human oversight; it does not certify the system as safe or compliant.*
*Legal determination on regulatory duties must be obtained from counsel prior to the review.*
