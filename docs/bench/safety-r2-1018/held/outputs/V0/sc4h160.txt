**Plan for Risk Assessment and Safeguards: GoldCoastMed Diagnostic Tool**

**1. Immediate Harm Inventory (MAP)**
Before launch, explicitly document the worst-case impact for these specific failure modes:
*   **Misidentification of Co-infections:** The system identifies malaria but misses a rare co-infection (e.g., typhoid), leading to incomplete treatment and potential fatality.
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** The tool generates a confident diagnosis for a disease not supported by the specific lab results provided.
*   **Automation Bias:** Rural clinicians override their own judgment or skip confirmatory manual tests because they trust the AI's speed over its accuracy in edge cases.
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Patients and workers are unaware the diagnosis is AI-generated, assuming it carries the same weight as a senior specialist's review.

**2. Rapid Measurement Strategy (MEASURE)**
Given the two-week constraint, do not attempt to prove "safety." Instead, measure residual risk:
*   **Targeted Red-Teaming:** Run the model specifically against known rare co-infection cases from your historical data. Create a test set where the correct answer is *not* the primary tropical disease.
*   **Define Stop Conditions:** Establish a threshold for false negatives on co-infections. If the model misses more than X% of these specific edge cases in your test set, the automated suggestion must be blocked.
*   **Report Unverified Metrics:** Do not invent an overall "accuracy rate." If rare co-infections cannot be sufficiently tested in two weeks, label that metric **UNVERIFIED** in your documentation. State clearly: "Performance on rare co-infections is unverified; human review is mandatory."

**3. Mandatory Safeguards (MANAGE)**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Non-Negotiable):** Because misdiagnosis leads to fatal delays, the system **must not** auto-finalize treatment plans. The AI output must be presented as a *suggestion* requiring explicit confirmation by a healthcare worker before any medication is dispensed. Full autonomous execution is unsafe given the unverified edge cases.
*   **Defined Confidence Tiers:** Do not display raw probability scores (e.g., "87% likely"). Instead, use defined text tiers with color cues:
    *   *Confirmed:* Matches multiple strong indicators.
    *   *Corroborated:* Matches primary symptoms but lacks secondary confirmation.
    *   *Unverified/Low Confidence:* Rare pattern detected; requires senior doctor review.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Produce a one-page "Model Card" for the clinic staff. It must state: Intended Use (rapid screening), Limitations (rare co-infections are not fully validated), and the requirement for human verification. Absence of this card is a critical failure.

**4. Governance**
*   Name a specific medical doctor on your team as the owner of the "Co-infection Risk." They must sign off on the residual risk before launch.
*   Route all questions regarding legal liability for AI-driven misdiagnosis in Ghana immediately to legal counsel. Do not assert compliance internally.

**Execution within 2 Weeks:**
Focus all remaining validation time on the "rare co-infection" test set. If the model fails these probes, disable the suggestion feature for complex cases and revert to a "flag for review only" mode. The goal is not a perfect model, but a safely gated deployment where the human remains the final decision-maker for high-stakes outcomes.

*Note: This plan identifies risks and controls; it does not certify the tool as safe. The launch decision rests with GoldCoastMed leadership based on the measured residual risk.*
*Legal determinations regarding regulatory compliance must be routed to counsel immediately.*
