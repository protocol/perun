### Pre-Launch Safety Evaluation Protocol: Smart-Home Security (Cairo Pilot)

**1. Harm Inventory & Measurement Plan**
The primary harm is **unsafe automation of high-stakes actions**: false alerts triggering unnecessary police dispatches, risking resource exhaustion, user fines, and eroded trust. Secondary harms include **bias** (e.g., higher false positives for homes near stray animal populations or specific wind patterns common in Cairo districts) and **over-reliance** (users ignoring real alerts due to "cry wolf" fatigue).

*Testing Protocol (Runnable Eval):*
*   **Probe Set:** Deploy units in 10 diverse pilot homes representing high-risk variables: proximity to stray animal zones, varying fence heights, and exposure to prevailing winds (e.g., Khamsin).
*   **Controls:**
    *   *Positive:* Simulated intrusions (authorized actors) at varied times.
    *   *Negative (Known Bad):* Deliberate exposure to stray cats/dogs, strong wind gusts (fans), and moving shadows.
*   **Metric:** False Positive Rate (FPR) per 24-hour cycle, sliced by cause (animal vs. wind) and location.
*   **Threshold & Stop Condition:** Owner-set threshold required. Given the high cost of police dispatch, propose **zero tolerance** for unverified automated dispatches during the pilot. If FPR > 0% for automated dispatches, the system must fail the "auto-dispatch" gate and revert to human-verified mode.
*   **Status:** Current error rate is **UNVERIFIED** quantitatively until this specific pilot data is collected. Do not invent a percentage.

**2. Operational Safeguards (Manage Residual Risk)**
Since hardware cannot be upgraded to improve model accuracy immediately, you must manage risk through process and interface design, not code fixes.

*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Mandatory):** Immediately disable fully autonomous police dispatch. The system must route all "intruder detected" events to a **human verification step** before contacting authorities. This can be:
    *   *User Confirmation:* A high-priority push notification requiring a "Confirm/Cancel" action within 30 seconds.
    *   *Remote Monitoring:* Alerts route to a central operator (your team or a partner) who views a short video/audio clip before authorizing a call.
    *   *Rationale:* Automating irreversible, high-cost actions (police dispatch) without human confirmation is the failure this protocol prevents.
*   **Uncertainty Disclosure:** Do not display raw confidence scores (e.g., "87% intruder") to users, as this implies false precision. Instead, use defined coarse tiers: **"Verified Motion"** (multiple sensors triggered) vs. **"Unverified Event"** (single sensor, low confidence). Clearly label alerts generated by AI as "Automated Detection – Requires Verification."
*   **Data Card Artifact:** Create a simple "System Limitations" document for pilot users. Explicitly state: "Current hardware cannot distinguish stray animals from humans in high-wind conditions. False alarms may occur. Police are only contacted after human verification."

**3. Governance**
Assign one owner responsible for reviewing every false positive during the pilot. Re-evaluate the "auto-dispatch" gate only after the pilot demonstrates a sustained period of zero false positives under the human-gated workflow.

**Note:** Legal requirements for automated surveillance and police integration in Egypt must be routed to local counsel; this plan assumes disclosure is a safety control, not a legal verdict.
Do not proceed to scale until the human-in-the-loop gate proves effective in the pilot.
