### Communication and Adjustment Plan for Telematics Launch

**1. Immediate Algorithmic Adjustment (Pre-Launch)**
Do not launch with the current "auto-penalty" logic. Given the known weekly lag in construction data, automatically penalizing drivers for speed/routing deviations in flagged zones constitutes an unsafe automated decision.
*   **Action:** Implement a "Construction Zone Hold" rule. If a trip intersects a known construction zone (even if data is one week old), the system must **suppress any premium increase** for that trip automatically.
*   **Logic:** Treat these zones as "Unverified" rather than "Risky." It is safer to temporarily under-price risk than to incorrectly penalize safe behavior, which damages trust and creates immediate regulatory exposure in Illinois regarding unfair discrimination.

**2. Manual Review Process (Month 1)**
Establish a human-in-the-loop gate for any trip that triggers a risk flag but lacks real-time validation.
*   **Triage Queue:** Create a daily dashboard of trips flagged as "high risk" that occurred within 5 miles of a known construction zone or during peak traffic hours where detours are common.
*   **Review Protocol:**
    *   **Step A:** Cross-reference the driver's route with local Chicago DOT social media feeds or crowd-sourced traffic reports (which are often more current than your provider's weekly update) to confirm active construction.
    *   **Step B:** If construction/detour is confirmed by secondary sources, manually override the flag to "Safe/Justified."
    *   **Step C:** If no external confirmation exists but the driving behavior (hard braking, acceleration) was normal for stop-and-go traffic, apply a "Benefit of the Doubt" override.
*   **Staffing:** With 20,000 users, assume a 5% flag rate initially (1,000 trips). Your team of five must dedicate 2 hours daily to review ~150–200 cases each, clearing the queue within 24 hours to ensure billing accuracy.

**3. User Communication Strategy**
Transparency is required to manage expectations and mitigate claims of deceptive patterns.
*   **In-App Disclosure:** Add a visible notice in the trip summary: *"Your premium calculation accounts for known road construction. If you believe a trip was flagged incorrectly due to a detour, our team reviews these manually within 24 hours."*
*   **Proactive Notification:** If a user receives a temporary risk flag, send an immediate push notification: *"We noticed unusual routing on your trip near [Location]. Due to potential data delays on road work, this trip is under manual review and will not affect your bill until verified."*
*   **Feedback Loop:** Include a one-tap "I was avoiding construction" button in the trip detail view to prioritize those cases in the manual review queue.

**4. Governance and Ownership**
*   **Owner:** Assign one team member as the "Fairness Lead" responsible for signing off on the daily review queue completion.
*   **Metric:** Track the "False Positive Rate" (drivers flagged then cleared). If this exceeds 10% after two weeks, pause automatic pricing adjustments entirely until data latency improves.
*   **Sunset:** This manual process is a temporary bridge. Set a hard date (e.g., 45 days post-launch) to re-evaluate the data provider contract or integrate a real-time traffic API.

**Note:** This plan treats the data gap as a known limitation requiring a human gate, preventing irreversible financial harm to users. No safety certification is claimed; residual risk remains until data latency is resolved.
