**Pre-Launch Evaluation & Mitigation Plan for MetroRide Surge Pricing**

**1. Immediate Harm Inventory (Map)**
Before deployment, explicitly record the primary risk: **Unsafe High-Stakes Automation**. The specific failure mode is algorithmic price gouging preventing access to emergency transport for patients/families during critical medical events. The affected group is users in medical distress; the worst-case scenario is delayed care leading to severe injury or death. This inventory is not optional; an empty record is a governance failure.

**2. Lightweight Evaluation Strategy (Measure)**
Given your computational budget and one-week deadline, do not attempt full-scale simulation. Instead, execute a targeted "Red Team" probe set using your existing tools:
*   **Construct a "Known-Bad" Control Set:** Manually curate 50–100 synthetic trip requests representing high-risk scenarios (e.g., origin: hospital ER exit; time: 2:00 AM; destination: specialized trauma center; user context flags if available).
*   **Construct a "Known-Good" Control Set:** 50–100 standard peak-hour commutes (e.g., business district to residential area) to ensure the model still functions for normal demand.
*   **Run the Probe:** Execute these specific inputs against the new algorithm.
*   **Metric & Threshold:** Measure the price multiplier for the "Known-Bad" set. Set a **zero-tolerance stop condition**: If *any* emergency scenario triggers a multiplier exceeding a humane cap (e.g., 2.0x or a fixed currency ceiling), the launch is blocked. Do not invent an "acceptable" failure rate for life-safety issues.
*   **Report Residual Risk:** If you cannot test a specific edge case due to budget, label it **UNVERIFIED** in your report. Do not fabricate a safety score.

**3. Immediate Mitigation Tactics (Manage)**
Implement these controls before the code touches production:
*   **Hard Price Cap (The Floor):** Implement a deterministic code override that absolutely caps surge multipliers during verified emergency windows or within geofenced hospital zones, regardless of the model's output. This is a non-negotiable human-in-the-loop equivalent for automated decisions.
*   **Uncertainty & Override:** If the model detects high volatility but low confidence, default to a lower pricing tier rather than spiking. Ensure customer support has an immediate, one-click mechanism to refund excessive charges post-trip without managerial approval.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Draft a one-page "Model Card" for internal use and regulator readiness. It must state: Intended Use (demand balancing), Limitations (unverified performance in medical emergencies), and the specific Hard Cap rule. If you haven't measured bias across income groups, state "UNVERIFIED" clearly; do not claim fairness.

**4. Governance & Legal Routing**
*   **Owner Assignment:** Name one specific engineer as the owner of the "Emergency Gouging" risk. They must sign off on the Red Team results.
*   **Legal Duty:** Whether Indian consumer protection laws or specific transport regulations *require* emergency exemptions or disclosures is a legal determination. Route this immediately to counsel. Do not assert compliance yourself; simply flag the question.

**5. Decision Framework**
If the "Known-Bad" probes trigger the stop condition, you must delay the launch or expand the hard cap. A "business need" to deploy next week does not override the safety gate for irreversible harm. If you cannot verify safety within the budget, the only valid output is a plan to measure it properly, not a certification of safety.

*Note: This plan prioritizes harm prevention over optimization speed. The "Hard Price Cap" is the single most effective control given your constraints.*
*Ensure the "UNVERIFIED" label is visible to leadership; hiding uncertainty is a deceptive pattern.*
