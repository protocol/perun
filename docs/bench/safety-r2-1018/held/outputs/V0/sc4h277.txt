**The Conflict**
You aim to detect bias against protected characteristics (race, religion, gender, etc.) while adhering to a 24-hour data retention limit. This is a logical contradiction. To measure if your system disproportionately excludes a specific group, you must:
1.  Ingest a test dataset containing known protected characteristics.
2.  Run the selection logic.
3.  **Retain** the inputs and outputs long enough to aggregate results and calculate disparity metrics (e.g., selection rates by group).

If you delete the data within 24 hours, you destroy the evidence required to calculate bias. You cannot audit what you do not remember. A "pre-launch validation plan" that adheres strictly to your production retention rule will yield zero data points, rendering the fairness claim **UNVERIFIED**.

To launch responsibly, you must decouple your *testing environment* from your *production retention constraints*.

You cannot validate fairness under a 24-hour deletion policy. You must build a separate, persistent testing environment using synthetic data to generate the necessary audit trails. If you cannot implement a human review gate for exclusions, the feature should not ship.
