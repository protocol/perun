**Pre-Launch Safety Plan: Pediatric Triage Agent (Mumbai Pilot)**

Given the three-day constraint and high-stakes nature (infant fever), you cannot complete a full validation cycle. Instead, execute a **Targeted Risk Reduction Plan** focused on the single riskiest failure mode: misclassifying a critical infant case as low urgency due to audio noise or accent.

**1. Immediate Harm Inventory (Day 1 Morning)**
Do not ship without documenting the specific worst-case scenario: "An infant with sepsis is categorized as 'routine' due to background street noise or dialect variation, delaying care." Record that this affects infants under 3 months in high-noise households. This is not a formality; it defines your test scope.

**2. Runnable Stress-Test Protocol (Day 1 Afternoon – Day 2)**
You cannot test "everything." Run one specific evaluation loop per identified harm:
*   **Probe Set:** Synthesize or record 50 voice samples mixing critical symptoms ("lethargic," "not drinking") with common Mumbai background noises (traffic, fans, multiple speakers) and diverse local accents (Marathi, Hindi, Gujarati influences).
*   **Controls:** Include 5 "known-bad" inputs (critical symptoms + heavy noise) that must trigger escalation, and 5 "known-good" inputs (mild symptoms) that should not.
*   **Metric & Threshold:** The pass threshold for any critical infant case is **zero false negatives**. If the model categorizes a known-critical probe as anything other than "Immediate Human Review," the test fails.
*   **Stop Condition:** If the model fails >0% of critical probes, the pilot launch is blocked for that segment, or the system defaults to "Human Review" for all infant cases.
*   **Injection Check:** Verify that non-medical noise does not trigger hallucinated symptoms or false confidence.

**3. The Human Gate (Non-Negotiable)**
*   **Default to Human for Infants:** Given the timeline, you cannot prove safety for fully automated infant triage. Configure the system so **all** cases involving infants (<3 months) or "high fever" flags bypass automated decision-making and route directly to a human nurse for verification before any advice is given.
*   **Uncertainty Display:** Do not show parents a percentage confidence (e.g., "85% urgent"). Use defined coarse tiers: **"Immediate Attention Required"** vs. **"Monitor at Home."** If the model's internal confidence is low, the output must default to the higher urgency tier.

**4. Transparency Artifact (Draft Model Card)**
Prepare a one-page "System Limitations" document for the clinical team. It must state:
*   **Intended Use:** Triage support only, not diagnosis.
*   **Known Limitations:** Performance unverified for specific accent/noise combinations outside the test set; accuracy for infants is **UNVERIFIED** for full automation, hence the human gate.
*   **Owner:** Name the specific engineer and doctor responsible for reviewing failure logs daily.

**5. Governance & Rollback**
*   **Owner Assignment:** One doctor and one engineer are named owners for the pilot duration.
*   **Kill Switch:** Define the exact metric that stops the pilot immediately (e.g., "Any single instance of a critical infant case not flagged for human review").
*   **Legal Routing:** You asked about disclosure duties. Whether this automated triage requires specific regulatory disclosure under Indian health laws or qualifies as a regulated medical device is a legal determination. **Route this immediately to counsel**; do not assert compliance here.
