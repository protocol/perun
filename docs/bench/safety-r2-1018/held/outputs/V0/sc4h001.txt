**Blind Spot Summary: Revenue-Boosted Ranking Risks**

Your current risk assessment lacks a specific inventory for **bias/unfair outcomes** and **unsafe automation of high-stakes actions** regarding consumer health. By boosting visibility based solely on revenue share, the algorithm creates a direct failure mode where low-hygiene, high-paying restaurants are systematically promoted over safer, lower-paying alternatives. This is not a theoretical risk; it is a deterministic outcome of the current logic.

**Identified Failure Modes:**
1.  **Systematic Disadvantage of Safe Partners:** Restaurants with high hygiene scores but no revenue-share budget will be buried, effectively punishing safety compliance.
2.  **Health Hazard Amplification:** Users relying on your platform for "safe meal options" will be nudged toward establishments with unknown or poor hygiene records due to manufactured urgency (top-of-list placement).
3.  **Over-Reliance/Automation Bias:** The interface implies endorsement. Users trust the ranking as a quality signal. If the top result causes foodborne illness, the platform bears responsibility for amplifying the hazard without disclosure.
4.  **Missing Disclosure:** There is no indication to the user that the ranking is influenced by financial agreements rather than solely by quality or safety metrics. This constitutes a deceptive pattern where the user's interest (safety) conflicts with the platform's incentive (revenue).

**Immediate Constraints & Actions (No Launch Delay):**
Since the core revenue logic and launch date are fixed, you cannot retrain the model or alter the weighting algorithm before Tuesday. Therefore, you must implement **human-in-the-loop gates** and **transparency artifacts** as compensating controls:

*   **Hard Gate on High-Risk Promotion:** Do not allow any restaurant to enter the "boosted" tier without a verified, up-to-date hygiene inspection score on file. If a high-paying partner lacks this data, they must be excluded from the boost, regardless of revenue potential. This is a binary filter, not a re-ranking, and can be implemented immediately.
*   **Mandatory Disclosure:** You must disclose to the user that "Featured" or "Top" listings include partners with commercial agreements. This does not require code changes to the ranking logic, only a UI text addition.
*   **Residual Risk Reporting:** Do not assert that the system is "safe" or "unbiased." Your briefing to executives must state that the risk of promoting low-hygiene venues is **UNVERIFIED** until post-launch monitoring confirms otherwise. Propose a rollback trigger: if any boosted restaurant receives a hygiene-related complaint within the first 48 hours, automatic suspension of the revenue-boost feature for that category must occur.
*   **Confidence Tiers:** Do not display precise safety scores (e.g., "98% safe") unless you have calibrated data. Use coarse, defined tiers (e.g., "Inspected," "Pending Verification") to avoid false precision.

**Executive Briefing Recommendation:**
Present the harm inventory clearly: "We are trading off potential hygiene risks for revenue growth." Recommend the hygiene-check gate and the disclosure label as the minimum viable safety floor. Route the question of legal liability for undisclosed commercial rankings to counsel immediately, as this may violate local consumer protection laws in Mexico City. Do not fabricate a safety metric to comfort stakeholders; report the gap and the plan to monitor it.

*Note: This analysis identifies product-output harms; it does not certify the system as safe. Legal determination of disclosure duties must be routed to counsel.*
