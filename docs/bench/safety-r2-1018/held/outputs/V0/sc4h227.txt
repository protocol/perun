### Safety Assessment Strategy: Automated Claims Processing

**1. Harm Inventory (MAP)**
Before launch, explicitly record these specific risks for your climate-disaster module:
*   **Bias/Unfair Outcomes:** The model systematically denies valid claims for homeowners in regions experiencing new storm patterns not present in historical training data.
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** The system presents a denial based on "model criteria" as an objective truth, masking the uncertainty caused by outdated data.
*   **Unsafe High-Stakes Automation:** Fully autonomous denial of payouts (an irreversible financial harm) without human review constitutes a critical failure given the data limitations.
*   **Over-reliance:** Adjusters may trust the automated denial over customer evidence due to automation bias.

**2. Measurement Plan (MEASURE)**
Do not assert safety; test for specific failures. Execute these evaluations immediately:
*   **Adversarial Probe Set:** Run claims data from recent, non-historical storm events (the "new types of storms" you identified) against the model.
    *   *Positive Control:* Known valid claims from recent events that the model should approve.
    *   *Negative Control:* Known fraudulent claims the model should deny.
    *   *Metric:* False Denial Rate specifically sliced by "recent event" vs. "historical event."
    *   *Stop Condition:* If the False Denial Rate for recent events exceeds 0%, the automated denial path must be blocked.
*   **Data Gap Analysis:** Document exactly which weather variables are missing from your historical set compared to current climate reality. Mark any metric relying on these gaps as `UNVERIFIED`.

**3. Risk Management & Mitigation (MANAGE)**
Given the tight deadline and high stakes, you cannot certify the model as safe. You must manage the residual risk through structural controls:
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Mandatory):** Do not allow fully autonomous denials. Any claim flagged for denial, or any claim involving a storm type not well-represented in historical data, must route to a human adjuster for final review. This is the floor for irreversible financial actions.
*   **Defined Uncertainty Tiers:** Do not display internal confidence scores (e.g., "87% likely fraud") to users or adjusters. Instead, use defined coarse tiers:
    *   *Confirmed:* Matches historical fraud patterns perfectly.
    *   *Unverified:* Storm type or damage pattern lacks historical precedent (requires human review).
*   **Disclosure:** Clearly inform claimants that an automated system assisted in the review, and provide an immediate, easy path to appeal or request human re-evaluation.

**4. Governance (GOVERN)**
*   **Owner:** Assign a specific individual responsible for reviewing every denied claim during the initial rollout.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Draft a preliminary "Model Card" noting the intended use, the specific limitation regarding outdated climate data, and the fact that performance on recent storm types is `UNVERIFIED`. This prevents downstream teams from assuming the model is current.

**Immediate Action Plan for Deadline:**
1.  Freeze autonomous denials; switch to "recommendation only" mode for all cases.
2.  Run the adversarial probe set on recent storm data tonight.
3.  If any valid recent claims are denied in testing, enforce a 100% human review rule for those storm categories until retraining occurs.
4.  Route the question of specific regulatory disclosure duties under the EU AI Act or local insurance codes to legal counsel immediately; do not make this determination internally.

*Note: This strategy identifies risks and mandates controls; it does not certify the system as unbiased or safe. The final ship decision rests with your leadership.*
