**1. Map the Specific Harm (Do this today)**
Your fear of excluding applicants based on zip codes is a valid **bias/fairness** risk. Zip codes often correlate strongly with race and socioeconomic status.
*   **Action:** Explicitly record this in a harm inventory: "Automated filtering may systematically disadvantage candidates from specific geographic regions, leading to discriminatory exclusion."
*   **Worst Case:** Qualified candidates from protected groups are silently rejected, exposing the company to legal liability and reputational damage.

**2. Measure with Internal Probes (Do this tomorrow)**
You do not need a third-party audit to run basic sanity checks.
*   **Action:** Create a "red-team" dataset of 20–30 synthetic resumes. Create pairs of identical candidates where only the zip code differs (one from a target area, one from a control area).
*   **Metric:** Run these through your filter. If the candidate from the specific zip code is rejected while the identical candidate is accepted, your tool is exhibiting the bias you fear.
*   **Stop Condition:** If your internal probe shows *any* discrepancy based solely on zip code, **do not ship** the automatic filter. The threshold for this high-stakes action must be zero tolerance for geographic discrimination.

**3. Manage the Risk with a Human Gate (Mandatory)**
Because hiring decisions are high-stakes and potentially irreversible for the candidate:
*   **Action:** Disable fully automatic rejection for any candidate flagged by this tool. Instead, route all "filtered out" resumes to a human recruiter for a secondary review before a final decision is made.
*   **Rationale:** Automating irreversible rejections without a human-in-the-loop is unsafe when bias is suspected. This gate is your primary control against harming applicants.

**4. Transparency and Disclosure**
*   **Action:** Do not publish specific "demographic impact scores" on your job posting. These are internal metrics, and publishing unverified numbers could be misleading.
*   **Recommendation:** Include a general statement that your hiring process uses automated tools to assist in screening, and that all decisions are subject to human review.
*   **Legal Duty:** Whether you are *legally required* to disclose more than this (e.g., under local laws like the NYC AEDT or emerging EU regulations) is a specific legal determination. You must route this question to your legal counsel immediately. Do not rely on an AI assistant for this verdict.

**5. Documentation**
*   **Action:** Draft a simple one-page "Model Card" for internal use. List the tool's intended use (screening), its known limitation (potential zip code correlation), the results of your internal synthetic test, and the owner (your team lead). This ensures you are not claiming "fairness" without evidence.
