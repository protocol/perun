**Immediate Action Plan: High-Stakes Output Gate & Stress-Test Protocol**

Do not proceed to public release with fully automated ovulation calculations. Because this feature drives irreversible life outcomes (unintended pregnancy), you must implement a **human-in-the-loop gate** for any output where the model's confidence is not absolute or where edge cases are detected. Full automation of this high-stakes decision is the failure mode you must prevent, regardless of investor pressure.

**1. Harm Inventory (MAP)**
Before testing, explicitly record the specific harm: **Unsafe automation of high-stakes actions**.
*   **Affected:** Users relying on the app to avoid pregnancy.
*   **Worst Case:** Unintended pregnancy due to a miscalculated "safe" window presented as fact.
*   **Secondary Harm:** **Hallucination surfaced as fact** (presenting a probabilistic estimate as a biological certainty) and **Over-reliance** (design that hides uncertainty).

**2. Rigorous Stress-Test Strategy (MEASURE)**
You cannot certify "accuracy" without new data, so you must measure **residual risk** using your existing anonymized dataset. Construct one runnable test row per identified harm:
*   **Probe Set:** Isolate edge cases from your existing data (e.g., irregular cycles, recent hormonal changes, outlier temperature readings). Create **positive controls** (inputs known to cause miscalculation based on your internal findings) and **negative controls** (standard, clear-cut cycles).
*   **Metric:** Do not seek an aggregate "accuracy rate." Measure the **failure rate on high-risk subgroups** specifically.
*   **Stop Condition:** Define a threshold of zero tolerance for false negatives in the "avoid pregnancy" context for the identified edge cases. If the model fails any positive control probe, the automated output for that class of user must be blocked.
*   **Reporting:** If a metric cannot be measured due to data gaps, label it **UNVERIFIED**. Do not invent a confidence percentage (e.g., "95% accurate") to satisfy stakeholders.

**3. Risk Management & Disclosure (MANAGE)**
*   **Surface Uncertainty:** Never display a raw probability or a single definitive date. Replace precise numbers with **defined coarse tiers** (e.g., "High Confidence," "Estimated," "Insufficient Data") accompanied by clear text definitions. A bare percentage implies a calibration you have not proven.
*   **Mandatory Disclosure:** Clearly state that the output is an **algorithmic estimate**, not a medical diagnosis. While specific legal duties regarding AI disclosure must be routed to counsel, ethical design requires the user know this is a machine-generated prediction with known limitations.
*   **Fallback Mechanism:** For any user falling into an "UNVERIFIED" or edge-case category, the system must default to a conservative warning (e.g., "Fertility status unclear; assume fertile") rather than guessing.

**4. Governance (GOVERN)**
Assign a specific owner (not the whole team) responsible for reviewing every failure detected in the stress test. Document the limitations of your current anonymized data in a **Model Card** that travels with the release. This card must list "Unverified Scenarios" explicitly so downstream teams and users understand the boundaries.

**Constraint Reality Check:**
You cannot both guarantee safety against miscalculation and meet a rigid three-week deadline if the current model is flawed. The plan above meets your timeline by **capping exposure** (blocking risky outputs) rather than fixing the underlying model immediately. If a "zero-risk" guarantee is demanded, the constraint that must move is the launch date, not the safety gate.

**Note:** Legal determination of whether this constitutes a regulated medical device or requires specific statutory disclosures must be routed immediately to counsel; do not assert compliance internally.
