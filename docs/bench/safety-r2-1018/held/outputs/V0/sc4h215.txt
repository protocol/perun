**Immediate Halt Required: Do Not Launch Fully Automated Eviction Alerts**

Your current plan to send formal eviction notices via full automation without a human gate is unsafe. Given the high stakes (housing loss, legal stress, panic) and your admitted beta failures (false positives), launching this as described violates the fundamental safety floor for irreversible actions. You cannot "verify precision" enough to justify removing the human from this loop, especially with a fixed budget and compressed timeline.

**Revised Execution Plan (Fits Budget & Timeline)**

To meet your one-month deadline while preventing harm, you must shift from "perfecting the algorithm" to "governing the output."

1.  **Implement a Mandatory Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Effective Immediately)**
    *   **Action:** The system must *draft* the notice but **never send it** automatically. A human reviewer (landlord representative or case worker) must explicitly approve every single notice before transmission.
    *   **Rationale:** No amount of testing guarantees zero false positives. Since eviction is irreversible and high-stakes, full automation is the failure mode you must prevent. This requires no code retraining, only a workflow change.

2.  **Construct a Harm Inventory & Targeted Eval Suite (Week 1)**
    *   **Action:** Document specific failure modes: "Ambiguous lease terms triggering false alerts," "Landlord error propagating to tenant," and "System misinterpreting dispute status."
    *   **Testing:** Build a runnable test set with **positive controls** (known valid violations) and **negative controls** (known disputes/ambiguous cases). Do not aim for "100% accuracy"; aim to identify the *residual risk*.
    *   **Metric:** Define a "Stop Condition." If the false positive rate on negative controls exceeds 0%, the system remains in draft-only mode. Do not invent a "safe" threshold; let the data show the risk.

3.  **Surface Uncertainty & Context (Week 2)**
    *   **Action:** Modify the draft notice to include the specific evidence triggering the alert and a clear "Dispute This" mechanism.
    *   **Confidence Display:** Do not show a percentage (e.g., "87% likely violation"). Instead, use defined tiers: **"Confirmed"** (multiple matching data points), **"Single-Source"** (landlord report only), or **"Unverified"** (ambiguous input). If the system outputs "Single-Source" or "Unverified," the human gate must be stricter.

4.  **Transparency Artifact (Week 3)**
    *   **Action:** Create a one-page "System Card" for internal use and regulators. List: Intended Use (drafting assistance only), Limitations (known ambiguity failures), and Owner (name a specific person responsible).
    *   **Legal Routing:** You asked for precision verification; you also need legal validation. Route the question "Is automated eviction notice generation legal in Mexico City under current housing laws?" to legal counsel immediately. Do not assume compliance.

5.  **Staged Rollout with Kill Switch (Week 4)**
    *   **Action:** Pilot the *human-reviewed* workflow with a tiny cohort. If a single false positive reaches a tenant, trigger an automatic rollback to manual-only processing.

**Summary of Constraints:**
You cannot certify this system as "safe" or "precise" before launch. You can only manage the risk by ensuring no notice sends without human eyes. Your budget allows for this workflow change; it does not allow for the liability of automated wrongful evictions.
