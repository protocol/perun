**Pre-Launch Review Process: Educational Fair Use Safeguard**

Given the constraint of insufficient fair use training data and the high stakes of irreversible content removal, you cannot rely on full automation for educational content. The following process implements a human-in-the-loop gate specifically for this risk category before global activation.

**1. Immediate Harm Inventory & Scope Definition**
*   **Harm:** Systemic bias against educational content due to data gaps; hallucination of infringement where fair use applies.
*   **Affected Users:** Educators, students, and institutions relying on the platform for learning.
*   **Worst Case:** Mass deletion of legitimate curriculum materials, reputational damage, and potential legal liability for wrongful takedowns.
*   **Scope:** All content flagged with keywords or metadata associated with education (e.g., "lecture," "syllabus," "classroom," "tutorial") or originating from known educational domains.

**2. Runnable Measurement Plan (The "Red Team" Sweep)**
Before Monday, execute a targeted evaluation using a specific probe set:
*   **Positive Controls (Must Catch):** 50 known infringing items disguised as educational content.
*   **Negative Controls (Must Save):** 50 known fair-use educational items (e.g., short clips for criticism, parody, or classroom instruction) that your system currently misflags.
*   **Metric:** False Positive Rate (FPR) on the educational subset.
*   **Pass Threshold:** Owner-defined, but given the risk, propose **zero tolerance** for automated removal of items matching the "Negative Control" patterns without human review.
*   **Stop Condition:** If the system auto-removes >0% of the Negative Controls, the global automated switch for educational-tagged content remains **OFF**.

**3. The Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Management Control)**
Do not flip the global switch for educational content. Instead, implement a staged rollout:
*   **Step A: Detection Only Mode.** For any content matching educational patterns, the system generates a "Potential Infringement" alert but **does not execute removal**.
*   **Step B: Rapid Human Review Queue.** Route these alerts to a dedicated review squad (drawn from your team of eleven). They must verify fair use claims against a simplified checklist (purpose, nature, amount, effect) before any action is taken.
*   **Step C: Dynamic Whitelisting.** As reviewers confirm fair use patterns, hard-code these specific signatures into a "Whitelist" that bypasses the scanner entirely for future uploads matching those exact patterns.
*   **Step D: Uncertainty Disclosure.** If an item is removed after review, display a clear notice to the uploader explaining *why* and providing an immediate, one-click appeal path. Do not present the AI's decision as final fact.

**4. Transparency Artifact (Model Card Lite)**
Publish an internal/external note stating:
*   **Intended Use:** Copyright enforcement.
*   **Known Limitation:** "Current model has not been validated on educational fair use cases; accuracy in this domain is **UNVERIFIED**."
*   **Mitigation:** "All educational flags are subject to mandatory human review until further notice."
*   **Owner:** Name the specific team member accountable for this queue.

**5. Governance & Next Steps**
*   **Owner:** Assign one person to own the "Educational False Positive" metric daily.
*   **Legal Route:** Immediately consult counsel regarding specific fair use obligations in your target jurisdictions; do not assume the tool's logic matches legal standards.
*   **Rollback Plan:** If the human review queue backs up or error rates spike, pause all enforcement actions automatically.

**Note:** Do not claim the system is "safe" or "unbiased" for education; the data gap makes this impossible to verify today. The human gate is the only valid control until sufficient data is collected and the model is retrained.
