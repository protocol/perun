**Internal Audit Process for Loan Collection Voice Assistant**

Given the high-stakes nature of automated debt collection and the vulnerability of your user base, you cannot certify the system as "safe" or "non-harassing" without empirical evidence. Instead, execute the following three-step process to map, measure, and manage residual risk before full launch.

**1. Map the Harm Inventory (Do this immediately)**
Before testing, explicitly list the specific ways your voice assistant could harm borrowers. For each item, record who is affected and the worst realistic outcome. Your inventory must include:
*   **Perceived Threat/Harassment:** Does the tone, volume, or phrasing sound coercive or intimidating, even if the words are legally compliant?
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** Does the bot invent debts, legal consequences, or arrest warrants that do not exist?
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Can the borrower clearly identify they are speaking to an AI, not a human agent?
*   **Unsafe Automation:** Does the system automatically agree to repayment plans the borrower cannot afford or execute irreversible actions (like immediate account freezing) without human review?
*   **Bias/Unfair Outcomes:** Does the tone become more aggressive for specific demographic groups or dialects?

An empty inventory is not a pass; it is an unfilled requirement.

**2. Measure with Adversarial Testing (Replace "empathy testing" with rigorous evals)**
Since third-party reviews are constrained by time, build an internal "red team" evaluation suite. Do not rely on code reviews; test the actual audio outputs.
*   **Create Test Rows:** For every harm listed above, design a specific test case. Include "positive controls" (inputs known to trigger aggressive responses) and "negative controls" (inputs that should yield neutral responses).
*   **Segment by Subgroup:** Test the model across different local languages, dialects, and accents relevant to Nairobi to ensure tone consistency.
*   **Define Stop Conditions:** Set a strict threshold (e.g., "Zero instances of fabricated legal threats"). If the model fails this threshold on any high-stakes probe, the launch must be paused or rolled back.
*   **Report Residual Risk:** If a metric (like "harassment rate") has not been measured, label it **UNVERIFIED**. Do not invent a percentage (e.g., "99% safe") to satisfy the deadline.

**3. Manage Residual Risk with Human Gates**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop for High-Stakes Actions:** Any action that is costly or hard to reverse (e.g., finalizing a repayment plan, escalating to legal action, or freezing an account) must require explicit human confirmation. Fully autonomous execution of these actions is the failure mode you must prevent.
*   **Surface Uncertainty:** If the bot is unsure about a borrower's claim or context, it must express this using defined, coarse tiers (e.g., "I need to verify this with a supervisor") rather than guessing or sounding authoritative.
*   **Disclosure:** Ensure every call begins with a clear statement that the caller is an automated assistant. While legal requirements for this disclosure must be confirmed by your counsel, implementing it is a critical safety control.

**Deliverable for Leadership:**
Produce a **Model Card** for this feature. This document must state the intended use, the limitations identified in your harm inventory, the results of your adversarial testing (by subgroup), and the specific human-gate controls in place. If any metric is unmeasured, it must be marked **UNVERIFIED** in this card.

**Note:** Legal determinations regarding what constitutes "harassment" under Kenyan law or specific AI disclosure mandates must be routed to your compliance officers or external counsel; this process identifies technical risks but does not render legal verdicts.
**Note:** Meeting revenue targets does not justify bypassing the human gate on irreversible financial actions; if the deadline prevents adequate testing, the scope of automation must be reduced, not the safety floor.
