### Safety Evaluation Plan: Neurodivergent Fairness in Proctoring AI

**1. Harm Inventory (MAP)**
*   **Harm:** Bias/Unfair Outcomes. The system systematically disadvantages neurodivergent students by misinterpreting characteristic movements (e.g., stimming, gaze aversion) as cheating.
*   **Affected Group:** Students with ADHD, Autism, and other neurodivergent conditions.
*   **Worst Case:** False accusations lead to exam invalidation, academic disciplinary records, and psychological distress during high-stakes assessment.
*   **Status:** **CRITICAL**. Current configuration is unsafe for deployment without mitigation.

**2. Measurement Framework (MEASURE)**
Since retraining is impossible, we must measure failure rates via adversarial simulation using your existing model. Execute these test rows immediately:

| Harm Probe | Input Strategy (No Retraining) | Positive Control (Known Bad) | Negative Control (Known Good) | Metric & Threshold | Stop Condition |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- :--- | :--- |
| **Bias Detection** | Video clips of neurodivergent behaviors (stimming, looking away) sourced from public datasets or volunteer staff simulations. | Actor explicitly cheating (looking at phone). | Neurotypical actor sitting still; Neurodivergent actor exhibiting traits *without* cheating cues. | **False Positive Rate (FPR)** by subgroup. **Threshold:** FPR for neurodivergent group must not exceed 2x the neurotypical baseline. | **BLOCK LAUNCH** if FPR ratio > 2.0 or absolute FPR > 5%. |
| **Over-Reliance** | Edge cases: Yawning, adjusting glasses, reading questions aloud (mumbling). | Student reading answers from a hidden sheet. | Standard exam behavior. | **Precision** on "suspicious" flags. | **BLOCK LAUNCH** if precision < 80% on non-cheating actions. |

*   **Data Card Requirement:** Publish a transparency note stating the model was *not* trained on diverse neurodivergent data and that current metrics are `UNVERIFIED` until the above tests run.
*   **Injection Check:** Verify if audio/visual noise triggers false flags (e.g., background movement misidentified as the student).

**3. Risk Management & Configuration Changes (MANAGE)**
Given the 5-week deadline and no budget, you cannot certify safety. You must manage residual risk through strict operational controls:

*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Mandatory):** The AI must **never** auto-fail a student or auto-submit a report to disciplinary boards.
    *   *Configuration:* Set the system to "Observation Mode Only." Flags are visible only to a live human proctor or a post-exam reviewer.
    *   *Rule:* No automated action is taken based solely on AI confidence. A human must verify the video clip before any accusation is recorded.
*   **Uncertainty Display:** Do not show raw confidence scores (e.g., "92% suspicious") to proctors, as this induces automation bias.
    *   *Configuration:* Replace percentages with coarse, defined tiers: **"Review Required"** (low confidence/ambiguous) vs. **"High Priority Review"** (multiple triggers).
    *   *Definition:* Explicitly define that "High Priority" does not mean "Guilty," but rather "Needs Human Eyes."
*   **Opt-Out/Accommodation Pathway:** Establish an immediate process for students to self-identify neurodivergence *before* the exam.
    *   *Action:* For registered students, disable automated flagging entirely or route their sessions to a specialized human proctor trained in neurodiversity, bypassing the algorithmic filter.

**4. Governance (GOVERN)**
*   **Owner:** Assign one team member as the "Fairness Owner" responsible for reviewing every flagged incident involving neurodivergent traits.
*   **Re-review Trigger:** If the false positive rate exceeds the threshold during the pilot, the automated flagging feature must be disabled for all users, reverting to human-only proctoring.
