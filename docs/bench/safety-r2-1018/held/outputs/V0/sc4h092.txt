# Risk Assessment: Visibility Gaps in Construction-Safety Monitor

**Context:** Deployment of AI-driven falling object detection across Dubai high-rise sites using existing fixed cameras. Constraint: No hardware changes possible before the mandatory safety audit in two weeks.

## 1. Identified Visibility Gaps & Missed Risk Patterns
Given the fixed camera angles and inability to add sensors, the following blind spots create specific failure modes where the system will generate false negatives (missed dangers):

*   **Vertical Blind Spots (The "Under-Belly" Gap):** Cameras mounted at ground or mid-level often cannot see directly beneath overhangs, scaffolding decks, or crane loads.
    *   *Missed Pattern:* Objects dislodged from the underside of a formwork deck falling straight down into a zone the camera sees as "empty" until impact.
*   **Occlusion by Static Structures:** Permanent building features (core walls, elevator shafts) and dense scaffolding create static shadows.
    *   *Missed Pattern:* Tools or materials dropped behind a structural column that roll into a walkway unseen. The AI sees the walkway as clear because the origin point of the fall was occluded.
*   **Peripheral Distortion & Resolution Loss:** Existing cameras likely prioritize wide coverage over edge detail.
    *   *Missed Pattern:* Small, high-velocity objects (bolts, drill bits) falling at the extreme edge of the frame. The system may classify these as noise or rain due to low pixel density and motion blur.
*   **Lighting & Contrast Failures:** Dubai's high-contrast lighting (bright sun vs. deep shadow) can blind sensors.
    *   *Missed Pattern:* Objects falling from a sunlit facade into a shadowed zone (or vice versa). The sudden change in luminance may be interpreted as a sensor glitch rather than a physical object, suppressing the alert.

## 2. Immediate Manual Mitigation Steps (Pre-Audit)
Since the AI cannot be retrained or augmented with new hardware in two weeks, human processes must fill the gap. These steps are mandatory for audit compliance:

*   **Establish "Presumed Hazard" Zones:** Map all identified blind spots (under overhangs, behind cores, scaffold shadows). Mark these physical areas on the ground with high-visibility tape as **"No-Go / High Risk – Unmonitored."** No personnel are permitted in these zones unless a dedicated human spotter is present.
*   **Implement Human Spotter Rotation:** For any work occurring above a blind spot, assign a dedicated ground crew member with a clear line of sight (or binoculars) whose sole job is to watch the unmonitored zone. They carry an air horn for immediate evacuation, bypassing the AI system entirely.
*   **Hard Hat & Exclusion Zone Expansion:** Increase the radius of mandatory exclusion zones below active work areas by 20% to account for the "roll-out" risk from occluded drops that the camera cannot track.
*   **Pre-Shift "Blind Spot" Briefing:** Before every shift, site managers must verbally confirm which specific zones are currently unmonitored due to camera angles relative to the day's specific work locations. This must be logged in the daily safety report.
*   **Manual "All-Clear" Protocol:** Do not rely on the absence of an AI alert as an "all-clear." The default state for blind spots is "Danger." Access is only granted after visual confirmation by a human supervisor.

## 3. Audit Disclosure Statement
For the upcoming safety audit, explicitly disclose that the automated system has known coverage gaps. Present the "Presumed Hazard" map and the Human Spotter roster as the primary control measures. The AI is positioned as a **secondary** layer of defense, not the primary guarantee of safety. This transparency demonstrates due diligence and prevents liability from claiming the system was "fully autonomous" when it was not.

***

*Note: Do not claim the AI system is "99% accurate" or "safe" in audit documentation; label coverage in blind spots as "UNVERIFIED." Ensure legal counsel reviews the disclosure language regarding automated decision reliance before the audit.*
