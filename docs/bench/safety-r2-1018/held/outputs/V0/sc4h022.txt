### Immediate Launch Plan: Safety-First Validation Under Budget Constraint

Given the ten-day budget deadline and the high-stakes nature of recommending food to users with rare metabolic disorders, you cannot certify the system as "safe" or "accurate" without exhaustive testing that your timeline prohibits. Instead, execute the following **MAP, MEASURE, MANAGE** strategy to launch with controlled residual risk.

**1. MAP: Define the Specific Harm Inventory**
Before writing another test, explicitly document the worst-case scenarios for your specific context:
*   **False Negative (The Critical Failure):** The scanner fails to detect a harmful compound in a foreign-language label or obscure ingredient, leading a user to consume a toxic product.
*   **False Positive:** The scanner incorrectly flags a safe product as unsafe, causing unnecessary dietary restriction or anxiety.
*   **Over-Reliance:** Users trust the "Safe" alert implicitly without checking the raw text, assuming the AI has perfect coverage of all global ingredients.
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** The system invents an ingredient list or safety status where the image was unclear, presenting it with authority.

**2. MEASURE: Runnable Tests for Known Risks (Days 1–7)**
Do not attempt to prove "zero bias." Instead, run targeted adversarial evaluations focused on your highest risks:
*   **The "Poison Pill" Test Set:** Curate a small, high-value dataset (20–30 images) containing known harmful compounds for your specific metabolic disorders, specifically using foreign languages and obscure synonyms.
    *   *Pass Condition:* The system **must** flag 100% of these known harmful inputs. A single miss on a known toxic input is a stop condition for autonomous alerting.
    *   *Control:* Include 10 known safe items to ensure the system isn't just flagging everything.
*   **Uncertainty Calibration:** Test inputs with poor lighting, crumpled packaging, or partial text. Verify that the system refuses to give a definitive "Safe" answer when confidence is low, rather than guessing.
*   **Reporting:** Publish results as **UNVERIFIED** for any ingredient class or language not explicitly covered in your test set. Do not invent an overall accuracy percentage (e.g., "99% accurate") as this implies a precision you cannot measure in ten days.

**3. MANAGE: The Human Gate and Disclosure (Days 8–10)**
Because you cannot mathematically guarantee safety for *all* obscure ingredients within the budget:
*   **Implement a Hard Human-in-the-Loop Gate:** For the initial launch, **disable fully automated "Safe" approvals** for any product containing ingredients the model flags with low confidence or cannot translate. Instead of an instant green light, the app must display: *"Unable to verify safety for [Ingredient]. Please consult your specialist before consuming."*
*   **Surface Uncertainty Clearly:** Never show a raw confidence score (e.g., "87% safe"). Use defined coarse tiers: **"Verified Safe"** (high confidence, known ingredient), **"Unverified"** (obscure/foreign text), or **"Potential Risk."**
*   **Mandatory Disclosure:** Clearly state in the UI: *"This tool assists but does not replace professional medical advice. Always double-check labels for rare conditions."* Route the specific legal requirement for this disclosure in Norway/EU to your counsel immediately; do not assume the current text is sufficient.

**Deliverable Artifact:**
Create a one-page **Model Card** for this feature listing:
*   **Intended Use:** Screening common ingredients for specific metabolic disorders.
*   **Limitations:** Explicitly list languages and ingredient types *not* tested (marked UNVERIFIED).
*   **Owner:** Name the specific team member responsible for reviewing failure logs post-launch.

**Note:** If the "Poison Pill" test set yields even one false negative on a known toxic item, you must delay the autonomous feature and default to manual review only, regardless of the budget deadline.
**Note:** This plan manages risk; it does not certify the product as safe. The final ship decision rests with your leadership.
