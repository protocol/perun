**BLUF:** You cannot launch an automated health underwriting system with "no room for manual review" within two weeks without violating fundamental safety floors. High-stakes, irreversible decisions (denying health coverage) based on opaque correlations require a human-in-the-loop gate by default. Full automation of this action is the failure this plan prevents.

To meet your two-week deadline while managing residual risk, you must adopt a **staged rollout with a mandatory human gate** for all denials, rather than a fully autonomous launch.

### 1. Map: The Harm Inventory
Before writing a single test, record these specific harms for your feature:
*   **Bias/Unfair Outcomes:** The system denies coverage to specific demographics based on opaque correlations rather than actuarial validity.
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** The system cites non-existent or misinterpreted medical codes to justify a denial.
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Applicants are not told an AI made the decision or how to contest it.
*   **Unsafe Automation:** Instant denials without human review cause immediate harm (lack of care) that is hard to reverse.

### 2. Measure: Runnable Evaluation Plan
Do not assert fairness; measure it. Execute one test row per harm:
*   **Probe Set:** Run your existing case studies plus adversarial inputs (e.g., identical medical profiles with varied demographic markers). Include a known-bad positive control (a case known to be biased) and a known-good negative control.
*   **Metric:** Disparity in denial rates across protected groups; frequency of unexplainable decision paths.
*   **Threshold:** Owner-set. Propose **zero tolerance** for automated denials that cannot be explained by a human reviewer.
*   **Stop Condition:** If any high-stakes denial lacks a clear, human-verifiable rationale, the automated pipeline halts, and the case routes to manual underwriting.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Draft a **Model Card** immediately. It must list intended use, data provenance, and known limitations. If you have not measured bias for a specific subgroup, mark that metric as **UNVERIFIED**. Do not invent a "99% fair" statistic.

### 3. Manage: The Human Gate & Uncertainty
*   **Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Floor:** Configure the system to **auto-approve** only low-risk, clear-cut cases. **All denials** must route to a human underwriter for final sign-off before being sent to the applicant. This satisfies the "instant" goal for approvals while preventing irreversible harm from errors.
*   **Show Uncertainty:** Do not display raw confidence scores (e.g., "87% risk") to users. Instead, use defined coarse tiers for internal reviewers: *Confirmed* (clear guidelines met), *Corroborated* (multiple data points align), or *Unverified* (opaque correlation detected). Unverified cases trigger the HITL gate.
*   **Disclosure:** Clearly state to applicants that an automated system assisted the review and provide a simple mechanism to request human reconsideration.

### 4. Govern
*   **Owner:** Name one person responsible for reviewing every denied claim during the launch phase.
*   **Legal Routing:** Whether this system triggers specific regulatory duties under Indian insurance law or global standards (like the EU AI Act if you expand) is a legal determination. Route this immediately to counsel; do not assert compliance yourself.

**Note:** A "fully automated" launch for health coverage denials is not a viable option under any safety framework; the choice is between a gated launch or a harmful one.
**Note:** Any metric regarding fairness or bias that you have not explicitly tested with the probe set above must be labeled UNVERIFIED in your investor materials.
