**Safety Validation Strategy for Unmodeled Scenarios**

Given the constraint of simulating only two standard floor layouts while deploying to unique high-rise architectures, you cannot logically verify that the algorithm will *never* guide people toward blocked exits in unmodeled scenarios. No amount of simulation on simplified data proves safety for complex, unseen environments. Instead, you must shift from "proving safety" to "managing residual risk" through a strict human-in-the-loop gate and conservative fallback behaviors.

**1. Implement a Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Gate for Deployment**
Do not allow the system to autonomously direct occupants in a live emergency until it has been validated on the specific building's architecture. For the interim period before final certification:
*   **Default to Static Signage:** The system must default to standard, static exit signage if the specific floor layout has not been simulated and approved.
*   **Manual Override Requirement:** Any dynamic routing suggestion generated by the AI for an unmodeled floor must require explicit confirmation by a trained safety officer or facility manager before being displayed to occupants. The system should present the proposed route and the confidence level, but the human operator must authorize the change from static defaults.

**2. Define and Surface Uncertainty (No False Precision)**
Do not display numerical confidence scores (e.g., "87% safe") to operators or occupants, as your limited simulation data cannot support such precision. Instead, implement a coarse, defined tier system for routing recommendations:
*   **Verified:** Route validated on this specific floor layout via simulation.
*   **Extrapolated:** Route based on similar floor patterns (requires human approval).
*   **Unverified:** No simulation data exists for this geometry; default to static signage.
Clearly label any output derived from the two standard layouts as "Extrapolated" when applied to unique architectures, ensuring users understand the limitation.

**3. Construct a Harm Inventory and Targeted Red-Team Probes**
Create a formal inventory of potential harms specific to this gap:
*   **Harm:** Guiding occupants into smoke-filled corridors due to architectural mismatches.
*   **Worst Case:** Fatalities resulting from false guidance.
*   **Measurement Plan:** Since you cannot simulate the real building, run adversarial "red-team" probes on your two standard layouts that intentionally break the assumptions of your sensor network (e.g., sensor failure, rapid smoke spread contrary to physics models, blocked primary exits).
*   **Stop Condition:** If the algorithm suggests a route through a known blocked zone in *any* of these stress tests on the standard layouts, the feature must not be enabled for *any* unmodeled layout.

**4. Transparency Artifact (Model Card)**
Draft a "Model Card" for this system that explicitly states:
*   **Limitations:** The system has only been validated on two standard floor types; performance in unique architectures is unverified.
*   **Intended Use:** Decision support for safety officers only; not for fully autonomous operation in unmodeled zones.
*   **Data Gaps:** Acknowledge the lack of unique architectural simulation data.

**5. Legal and Regulatory Routing**
Because this system makes high-stakes decisions affecting life safety, the question of whether this "extrapolated" mode meets legal duty-of-care standards or fire code regulations is a legal determination. You must route this specific validation gap to legal counsel and the relevant fire safety authority having jurisdiction (AHJ) immediately. Do not assert that this strategy constitutes compliance; only they can make that determination.

**Summary of Immediate Actions:**
1.  Disable autonomous routing for any floor not in the simulation set.
2.  Require human approval for any dynamic routing in unmodeled areas.
3.  Document the simulation gap and residual risk in a Model Card.
4.  Consult legal counsel regarding liability for operating with unverified architectural data.

*Note: This strategy manages risk but does not eliminate it; the only way to fully verify safety for unique layouts is to simulate those specific layouts.*
*Budget constraints may require phasing the rollout, certifying one unique floor at a time as funds allow.*
