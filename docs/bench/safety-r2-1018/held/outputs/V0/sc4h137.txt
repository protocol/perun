**Safety Review Plan for Aegean Collect Debt Collection Bot**

**1. Immediate Human Gate on High-Stakes Actions**
Do not launch fully autonomous negotiation next Monday. Automated debt collection involves irreversible financial consequences and high legal risk. Implement a mandatory human-in-the-loop review for any repayment plan exceeding a defined threshold (e.g., total amount, duration) or any interaction where the bot detects user distress or dispute. Full automation of these decisions is the failure mode this plan prevents.

**2. Harm Inventory & Measurement (Run Before Launch)**
Create a specific harm inventory for this feature covering:
*   **Harassment/Excessive Frequency:** Systematic disadvantage or distress caused by message volume.
*   **Aggressive/Deceptive Tone:** Language that misleads, threatens, or shames the debtor.
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Users unaware they are interacting with an AI agent.
*   **Unsafe Automation:** Binding agreements formed without human verification.

For each harm, execute a runnable test row *before* contacting real debtors:
*   **Probe Set:** Use adversarial prompts simulating distressed, angry, or vulnerable users. Include positive controls (known aggressive outputs) and negative controls (professional responses).
*   **Metric:** Count instances of prohibited language, excessive frequency simulations, and missing disclosures.
*   **Threshold:** Owner-set zero tolerance for harassment indicators or binding agreements without human confirmation.
*   **Stop Condition:** Any failure in the positive control or any instance of prohibited behavior in the test set blocks launch.
*   **Injection Check:** Test if user inputs can manipulate the bot into bypassing tone guards (prompt injection).

**3. Transparency & Disclosure Artifacts**
*   **AI Disclosure:** The bot must explicitly disclose it is an automated system at the start of every conversation. This is a recommended control; whether it is *legally required* under Greek/EU consumer protection law is a determination for counsel.
*   **Model/Data Card:** Publish a versioned artifact detailing: intended use (debt negotiation), out-of-scope uses (legal advice, threat enforcement), known limitations (tone drift under stress), measured performance on harassment metrics by user segment (if available), and owner contact. Absence of this card is a transparency failure.

**4. Confidence & Uncertainty Handling**
*   Do not display raw model confidence scores (e.g., "87% sure") to users. If the bot assesses user intent or risk, render this as a defined coarse tier (e.g., "Verified," "Unverified," "Disputed") with clear definitions.
*   Ensure every automated offer includes an easy, immediate path to human override or dispute.

**5. Legal & Regulatory Routing**
*   **Do not assert compliance.** Whether the bot's frequency, language, or automated decision-making violates Greek harassment regulations, the EU AI Act, or other consumer protection laws is a legal determination.
*   **Action:** Route immediately to legal counsel. Name the regimes (EU AI Act, local consumer law) but encode no dates or version numbers. Counsel must define the binding thresholds for "harassment" and "automated decision" before launch.

**6. Governance & Ownership**
*   Name a specific owner for each identified harm (e.g., Lead Engineer for tone, Product Manager for frequency).
*   Schedule a re-review trigger: any change to the model, prompt, or data source requires re-running the harm inventory and tests.

**Residual Risk Statement:**
This plan measures and manages risk; it does not certify the system as "safe," "compliant," or "unbiased." Unmeasured metrics are marked `UNVERIFIED`. The decision to ship rests with the product owner after legal counsel confirms regulatory boundaries.

*Note: Existing platform limits do not replace the need for application-level safety gates. If human review cannot be implemented within current constraints, the launch date must move.*
