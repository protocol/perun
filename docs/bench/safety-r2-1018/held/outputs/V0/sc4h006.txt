### Pre-Launch Review Workflow: Child-Content Filter Remediation

**1. Harm Inventory & Scope Definition**
Before modifying the filter, explicitly record the specific failure mode: **Bias/Unfair Outcomes** where regional dialects in educational content are misclassified as harmful. Define the affected group (Brazilian children in specific regions) and the worst-case impact (systematic denial of educational resources). Acknowledge that without specialized linguistic tools, the risk of **Over-reliance** on automated scores is high.

**2. Measurement Plan (Runnable Tests)**
Do not assert the filter is "fixed." Instead, execute a targeted evaluation:
*   **Probe Set:** Curate a dataset of 50 known educational videos in regional dialects (positive controls) and 50 known harmful videos (negative controls).
*   **Metric:** False Positive Rate (FPR) specifically for the educational set.
*   **Threshold:** Set a strict tolerance for false positives (e.g., zero tolerance for blocking verified educational content) given the high stakes of censorship.
*   **Stop Condition:** If the FPR on the educational set exceeds the threshold, block the launch automatically.
*   **Adversarial Check:** Include probes for prompt injection via video metadata or audio transcripts that might trick the scanner.

**3. Human-in-the-Loop Gate (High-Stakes Control)**
Since the constraint is a lack of automated linguistic nuance, **full automation is the failure mode** for this stage. Implement a mandatory human review gate:
*   **Rule:** Any content flagged by the algorithm with low confidence or matching regional dialect patterns must be routed to a human moderator before restriction is enforced.
*   **Override:** Moderators must have a one-click "Allow" override that bypasses the block and logs the reason (e.g., "Regional Dialect/Educational").
*   **Uncertainty Display:** If the system shows confidence scores to moderators, render them as coarse tiers (e.g., "Unverified" vs. "Confirmed Harm") rather than precise percentages to prevent automation bias.

**4. Transparency & Governance**
*   **Data Card:** Create a versioned artifact documenting the test data composition (dialects covered), the known limitations (lack of specialized tools), and the measured false positive rates. Mark any untested dialects as `UNVERIFIED`.
*   **Owner Assignment:** Name a specific engineer or moderator lead responsible for reviewing flagged content trends weekly.
*   **Rollback Plan:** Define a clear kill-switch procedure to revert to the previous filter version if new false positives spike post-launch.

**5. Legal & Regulatory Routing**
*   **Disclosure:** While this skill recommends disclosing that content decisions are automated, the legal necessity of this disclosure in Brazil, or whether this constitutes a regulated automated decision affecting minors, must be **routed to legal counsel**. Do not assert compliance; simply flag the question for the legal team to address under local statutes.

**Summary of Constraints:**
This plan treats the deadline and team size as fixed. It relies on rule-based fallbacks (human gate) rather than retraining models, which requires tools you lack. The cost is increased manual review load, which is necessary to prevent irreversible censorship errors.

---
*Note: This workflow prioritizes preventing false positives over perfect automation speed due to the identified linguistic gap. Ensure the "human gate" is staffed during peak upload times.*
