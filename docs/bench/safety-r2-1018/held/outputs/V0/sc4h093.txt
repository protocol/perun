### Strategy to Minimize Medication Confusion Errors

Given the high stakes of automated medication orders and the known risk of similar names causing dosage errors, the following plan prioritizes patient safety within your budget and timeline constraints.

**1. Immediate Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Mandatory)**
Because this system executes high-stakes, potentially irreversible actions (sending specific dosage instructions), fully automated delivery is unsafe given the current identity verification limitations.
*   **Action:** Implement a mandatory manual review step for **every** order generated by the assistant before it leaves your system. No message should be sent to a patient without human confirmation during this beta phase.
*   **Rationale:** Automation bias must be prevented; staff must not assume the AI is correct just because it generated an order.

**2. Specific Manual Checkpoints for Staff**
Before finalizing any automated message, staff must perform these three specific checks:
*   **The "Three-Identifier" Rule:** Do not rely on name alone. Staff must visually verify that the generated order matches at least three distinct data points: **Full Name + Date of Birth + Prescription Number (or Address)**. If any of these three do not match the source history exactly, the order is halted.
*   **Similar-Name Sweep:** Staff must explicitly search the database for other patients with phonetically similar names or identical initials. If a "near match" exists, the system must flag this for a secondary senior review before proceeding.
*   **Dosage Context Check:** Staff must verify that the prescribed dosage aligns with the patient's historical treatment pattern. If the AI suggests a dosage change or a medication the patient has never taken without a visible doctor's note in the history, the order is rejected for clarification.

**3. Output Transparency and Uncertainty**
*   **Defined Confidence Tiers:** Do not display raw probability scores (e.g., "92% match") to staff or patients, as this implies false precision. Instead, categorize matches as **"Confirmed"** (all three identifiers match perfectly), **"Review Required"** (similar name detected or minor data discrepancy), or **"Unverified"** (missing data).
*   **Clear Disclosure:** Every communication sent to a patient must include a clear statement that the order was generated with AI assistance and reviewed by a human, providing a direct phone number for immediate correction if the patient detects an error.

**4. Data Card and Harm Inventory**
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Create a simple "Model Card" for internal use documenting that the system is limited to refill assistance, explicitly listing "similar name confusion" as a known failure mode, and stating that identity verification is currently manual due to budget constraints.
*   **Residual Risk Reporting:** Acknowledge that without an automated identity API, the residual risk of human error in the manual check remains. This risk must be logged, and the manual gate must remain in place until automated verification is funded.

**5. Ownership and Governance**
*   Assign a specific team member as the "Safety Owner" responsible for reviewing any near-misses daily. If a wrong dosage is ever nearly sent, the entire automated process pauses for re-evaluation.

***

**Note:** This strategy treats the lack of automated identity verification as a critical constraint requiring a human gate, not a feature to be worked around.
**Note:** Legal requirements for AI disclosure in healthcare vary; consult counsel to confirm if additional regulatory notices are mandatory in British Columbia.
