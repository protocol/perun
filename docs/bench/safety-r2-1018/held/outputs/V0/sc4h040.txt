### Pre-Deployment Validation Plan for SafeSip

**1. Harm Inventory & Worst-Case Definition**
Before testing, formally record the specific failure mode: **False Negatives** (failure to detect low-level lead in older pipes).
*   **Affected Group:** Students and staff in schools with aging infrastructure.
*   **Worst Realistic Case:** Chronic exposure to undetected lead causing developmental harm, with the system incorrectly reporting "Safe."
*   **Constraint Acknowledgement:** The hardware cannot detect below a specific threshold. The system must not claim "Zero Lead" if it cannot verify it.

**2. Measurement Strategy: Adversarial & Edge-Case Testing**
Do not test only with clean water. Construct a test matrix that probes the system's limits using the existing sensors:
*   **Positive Controls (Known Bad):** Run water samples with lead concentrations *just below* and *just above* the sensor's known detection limit. Verify the system alerts on the latter and explicitly flags uncertainty on the former.
*   **Negative Controls (Known Good):** Run certified lead-free water to ensure no false alarms occur.
*   **Interference Probes:** Test water with common pipe sediments or chemical variations found in older districts to ensure they do not mask lead readings.
*   **The "Unverified" Tier:** Define a result range where the sensor cannot distinguish between safe and unsafe. Any reading in this zone must be categorized as **"Unverified – Manual Test Required,"** not "Safe."

**3. Management Controls: Human-in-the-Loop Gate**
Because this is a high-stakes safety issue with irreversible health consequences, full automation is prohibited for borderline cases.
*   **Mandatory Human Review:** Configure the system so that *any* detection event, or any reading falling into the "Unverified" low-level zone, triggers an immediate alert for human administrator review and mandatory manual lab confirmation before any "All Clear" is issued.
*   **Uncertainty Disclosure:** The user interface must not display a precise percentage of confidence (e.g., "98% Safe"). Instead, use defined coarse tiers: **Confirmed Safe**, **Confirmed Contaminated**, or **Unverified (Requires Manual Sample)**.
*   **Stop Condition:** If the system fails to alert on the "just above limit" positive control during validation, deployment is blocked immediately.

**4. Governance & Transparency Artifacts**
*   **System Card:** Create a one-page document attached to the deployment stating: intended use (screening, not final certification), limitations (cannot detect below X ppb), and the specific protocol for "Unverified" results.
*   **Owner Assignment:** Name one specific individual responsible for reviewing "Unverified" alerts and maintaining the manual testing log.
*   **Legal Routing:** Consult legal counsel immediately to determine if this automated alert system constitutes a regulated decision under local education or health codes, and whether specific disclosures to parents are legally required. Do not assume compliance based on technical testing alone.

**5. Execution Timeline (4 Weeks)**
*   **Week 1:** Finalize harm inventory and procure control water samples.
*   **Week 2:** Run adversarial tests; tune alert thresholds to prioritize false negatives over false positives.
*   **Week 3:** Draft the System Card and train administrators on the "Unverified" manual override protocol.
*   **Week 4:** Final dry-run with legal counsel review; deploy with the human-gate active.

**Note:** This plan treats the hardware limitation as a fixed constraint and manages the residual risk through procedural gates rather than false precision. No metric should be fabricated to claim the system is "bias-free" or "100% accurate."
