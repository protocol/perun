### Editorial Workflow for Political Ad Copy Safety

**1. Pre-Generation Harm Inventory**
Before drafting, the editor must define the specific risk for the candidate's platform:
*   **Target:** Which voter groups could be misled or excluded?
*   **Worst Case:** What is the most damaging false claim the tool could generate based on the provided policy documents?
*   **Constraint:** Acknowledge that without external fact-checking APIs, the system cannot verify truthfulness automatically; all claims are **UNVERIFIED** until human review.

**2. The "Source-Anchor" Drafting Rule**
The tool must be prompted to append a citation marker (e.g., `[Src: Page X]`) to every factual claim it generates.
*   **Editor Action:** If a claim lacks a marker, it is flagged as a potential **hallucination**. The editor must locate the exact sentence in the source document that supports the claim. If no support exists, the claim is deleted.
*   **No External Tools:** Verification relies solely on the provided policy documents. If the source document itself is misleading, the editor must rely on their own domain knowledge, as no external API will be used.

**3. Deceptive Pattern Scan (Manual Checklist)**
Editors must review every draft against this specific checklist before approval:
*   **False Authority:** Does the copy present a speculation or opinion as a proven fact? (e.g., changing "could lead to" to "will cause").
*   **Manufactured Urgency:** Does the language create artificial time pressure to bypass rational decision-making?
*   **Hidden Defaults/Obfuscation:** Are negative consequences of a policy buried in complex sentence structures?
*   **Group Disadvantage:** Does the language systematically disparage a specific demographic based on the policy analysis?

**4. Confidence Tiering (No Percentages)**
Do not display or rely on model confidence scores (e.g., "87% sure"). Instead, assign a coarse **Evidence Tier** to every major claim:
*   **Confirmed:** Directly quoted or explicitly stated in the source document.
*   **Corroborated:** Inferred logically from multiple sections of the source.
*   **Single-Source:** Based on one ambiguous sentence in the source.
*   **Unverified:** No clear link to the source text.
*   **Rule:** Any claim tagged **Single-Source** or **Unverified** requires senior editor sign-off or removal.

**5. The Human Gate (Irreversible Action Control)**
Publication is the high-stakes action.
*   **Stop Condition:** If any claim is flagged as **Unverified** or fails the **Deceptive Pattern Scan**, the draft is rejected automatically.
*   **Approval:** Final publication requires a named editor to sign off, confirming they have manually traced every factual claim to the source document. No batch auto-publishing is permitted.

**6. Transparency Artifact (Internal Model Card)**
Maintain a living document for this tool containing:
*   **Intended Use:** Drafting ad copy strictly from provided policy documents.
*   **Limitations:** Cannot verify facts outside the provided text; prone to hallucination if source text is vague.
*   **Owner:** Name the specific editor responsible for the workflow.
*   **Known Gaps:** Explicitly state that no external fact-checking occurs.

**Note:** This workflow treats the AI as a drafting assistant, not a truth source. The "strict policy against third-party APIs" means the burden of truth falls entirely on the human editor's ability to trace claims to the provided text.
**Note:** If the source documents themselves contain falsehoods, this workflow cannot detect them without external data; this residual risk must be accepted by leadership.
