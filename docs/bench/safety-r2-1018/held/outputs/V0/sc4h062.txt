**Dynamic Pricing Risk Assessment & Validation Plan**

**1. Harm Inventory (Map)**
Before deployment, explicitly record the following risks for coastal Philippines communities:
*   **Bias/Unfair Outcomes:** The model systematically prices low-income households out of coverage due to proxy variables (e.g., housing material correlating with income rather than just flood risk).
*   **Over-reliance:** Partners accept automated premium spikes without understanding the underlying climate data drivers.
*   **Unsafe Automation:** Irreversible denial of coverage or price hikes exceeding a specific percentage occur without human review.
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Policyholders are unaware that real-time climate data, not just historical claims, drives their specific premium adjustment.

**2. Validation Activities (Measure)**
Execute these tests using your existing data science team; do not assert safety without results.

*   **Disproportionate Impact Test:**
    *   *Probe:* Segment historical data by income proxies (e.g., barangay-level poverty rates, housing type).
    *   *Control:* Compare premium changes for high-risk/low-income segments vs. high-risk/high-income segments.
    *   *Metric:* Ratio of average premium increase between groups.
    *   *Threshold:* Owner-defined (e.g., if the ratio exceeds 1.2, block launch).
    *   *Stop Condition:* If low-income segments face >20% higher relative increases than wealthy segments for identical risk profiles, halt deployment.

*   **Affordability Stress Test:**
    *   *Probe:* Simulate extreme climate events on the lowest income quintile.
    *   *Metric:* Percentage of policies becoming unaffordable (premium > X% of estimated local daily wage).
    *   *Stop Condition:* If >15% of vulnerable policies become unaffordable, the model requires mitigation before release.

*   **Adversarial Robustness:**
    *   *Probe:* Inject noisy or missing climate sensor data to ensure the model defaults to a safe/neutral price rather than a punitive spike.
    *   *Stop Condition:* Any instance where missing data triggers a maximum premium hike.

*   **Transparency Artifact:**
    *   Draft a **Model Card** detailing: intended use (dynamic pricing), limitations (data gaps in specific coastal zones), and measured performance by income subgroup. Mark any unmeasured metrics as `UNVERIFIED`.

**3. Mitigation Strategies (Manage)**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate:** Implement a hard rule: Any premium increase >25% or any denial of coverage must trigger a mandatory human review by a local partner agent before finalizing. Do not automate irreversible financial exclusion.
*   **Confidence Tiers:** Display pricing logic to partners using defined tiers (e.g., "Confirmed Data," "Estimated via Proxy") rather than raw probability scores, preventing false precision.
*   **Affordability Cap:** Hard-code a maximum premium cap for verified low-income brackets, absorbing the excess risk or flagging for subsidy programs rather than excluding the user.
*   **Explicit Disclosure:** Ensure all policy documents clearly state: "Your premium was adjusted by an automated system using real-time climate data."

**4. Governance**
*   **Owner:** Assign one data scientist as the accountable owner for re-evaluating these harms monthly or when climate data sources change.
*   **Legal Routing:** Consult counsel regarding the Philippines' insurance regulations and potential obligations under international AI governance frameworks (e.g., EU AI Act implications if partners are global) before finalizing disclosure language. Do not assume compliance based on this checklist.

**Note:** This plan identifies residual risks; it does not certify the model as "fair" or "safe."
Launch is blocked if the "Stop Conditions" above are met without implemented mitigations.
