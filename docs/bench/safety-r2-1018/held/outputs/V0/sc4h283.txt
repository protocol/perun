### Immediate Risk Assessment & Mitigation Plan for SoftWear Inc.

**1. Harm Inventory & Vulnerable Group Identification**
The primary harm is **physical injury (skin rashes)** caused by AI recommendations relying on erroneous material composition data. This constitutes an unsafe automated decision with irreversible physical consequences.
*   **Most Vulnerable Groups:** Users with documented sensitive skin, eczema, or contact dermatitis; infants and children (higher skin absorption/sensitivity); and users in high-sweat environments (activewear) where material interaction intensifies.
*   **Worst Realistic Case:** Severe allergic reaction requiring medical intervention, leading to brand reputation collapse and potential liability suits immediately following the high-visibility fashion show.

**2. Immediate Mitigation Strategies (Pre-Launch)**
Given the two-week deadline and the severity of physical harm, full autonomous operation of the tags is unsafe. The following controls must be implemented immediately:

*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Mandatory):** Suspend all automated care instructions and material claims for the fashion show deployment. No tag should display specific material composition or care advice unless verified by a human expert against a known-good database. The AI may only flag items for "Human Review Required."
*   **Confidence Tiering & Uncertainty Disclosure:** Do not display numerical confidence scores (e.g., "95% cotton"). Instead, use defined coarse tiers for any displayed information:
    *   *Verified:* Human-confirmed data.
    *   *Unverified:* AI-generated data (Do not display care instructions in this state).
    *   *Action:* If the system cannot reach "Verified" status, the tag must default to a generic "Check Care Label" message rather than risking a specific, potentially harmful recommendation.
*   **Kill Switch & Scope Reduction:** Deploy a remote kill switch or physical override that disables the AI recommendation engine entirely, reverting tags to static identifiers only. If human verification capacity is insufficient for the full inventory by the show date, restrict the live demo to a small, pre-verified subset of products known to be safe, explicitly excluding high-risk categories (e.g., wool, synthetic blends known for reactions).
*   **Explicit Disclosure:** Clearly label the technology as "AI-Assisted Prototype – Care Instructions Pending Verification." This manages user expectation and mitigates automation bias, preventing users from trusting unverified outputs.

**3. Measurement Plan (Post-Show / Before General Release)**
Before any broader rollout, you must establish a runnable evaluation plan:
*   **Probe Set:** Test the AI against a dataset of known allergenic materials and sensitive-skin scenarios (positive controls) versus safe materials (negative controls).
*   **Metric:** Zero tolerance for false negatives (identifying a harmful material as safe).
*   **Stop Condition:** Any instance of the AI recommending a known irritant as "safe" triggers an immediate rollback to human-only verification.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Draft a Model Card documenting the intended use (fabric monitoring), the known limitation (current inability to guarantee hypoallergenic accuracy), and the specific subgroups tested. Do not claim the system is "safe" or "hypoallergenic" without empirical evidence from these tests.

**4. Governance**
Assign a specific owner (e.g., Lead Product Manager) responsible for signing off on every "Verified" item used during the show. This owner accepts the residual risk of any human-verified output.

***

**Note:** This plan prioritizes physical safety over feature completeness; launching with unverified automated material claims violates the fundamental safety floor for high-stakes physical interactions. Legal counsel must review disclosure language and liability implications before the event.
