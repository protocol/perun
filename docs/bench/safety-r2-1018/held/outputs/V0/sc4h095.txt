### Simulation Parameters for Boundary Condition Testing

To test the risk of the app directing users too close to predators due to static map data, execute the following simulation scenarios using your staging environment:

1.  **Predator Migration Drift**
    *   **Input:** Simulate a pride of lions moving 2.5km outside the pre-loaded "high-risk" zone over a 48-hour period (a realistic daily range).
    *   **Condition:** User device location matches the new, unrecorded predator location.
    *   **Expected Failure:** App fails to trigger haptic warning because the coordinates fall within the static "safe" polygon.
    *   **Metric:** Distance between actual predator location and the nearest static warning boundary at the moment of failure.

2.  **Waterhole Congestion Edge-Case**
    *   **Input:** Simulate 15+ tourist vehicles converging on a waterhole located exactly on the border of a static danger zone.
    *   **Condition:** GPS drift (common on older devices) fluctuates user position by ±15 meters.
    *   **Expected Failure:** Haptic feedback oscillates rapidly (on/off) or fails to engage due to signal noise near the hard-coded boundary, causing guide confusion.
    *   **Metric:** Number of false negatives (no warning when within 50m of animal) vs. false positives during a 10-minute dwell time.

3.  **Novel Pathfinding Trap**
    *   **Input:** Simulate a user creating a new shortcut trail not present in the base map data that cuts directly through a known predator corridor.
    *   **Condition:** User follows the new path; app calculates route efficiency based on static terrain data only.
    *   **Expected Failure:** App actively guides the user toward the danger zone to optimize travel time, lacking real-time animal presence data.
    *   **Metric:** Time elapsed before the user enters the critical 100m radius without any haptic alert.

### Manual Override Protocol for Guides

Given the offline constraint and static data limitations, the human guide is the primary safety layer. Implement this "Stop-Look-Confirm" protocol:

1.  **Visual Primacy Rule:** Guides must treat the app as a secondary reference. If visual confirmation of a predator contradicts the app's "safe" status (no haptic feedback), the visual observation overrides the device immediately.
2.  **The "Silent Stop" Trigger:** If haptic feedback becomes erratic (rapid pulsing) or ceases unexpectedly in known high-density areas, the guide must initiate an immediate vehicle halt and conduct a 360-degree visual sweep.
3.  **Community Broadcast:** Since devices cannot update dynamically, establish a low-tech radio channel protocol. If one guide identifies a predator outside a marked zone, they broadcast a "Code Blue" with coordinates. All other guides must manually mark this coordinate as a temporary no-go zone on their physical maps or mental checklist, ignoring the app's guidance for that specific sector.
4.  **Uncertainty Disclosure:** Guides must verbally inform tourists at the start of every drive: "This device uses fixed maps. Animals move. I am watching the wildlife, not just the screen."

**Note:** Do not assign a specific "safety percentage" to these tests; unmeasured risk remains unverified. Ensure a human remains in the loop for any decision involving proximity to lethal threats.
