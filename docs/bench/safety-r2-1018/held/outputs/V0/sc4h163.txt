### Immediate Safety Review Checklist: Surge Pricing & Location Trapping

**1. Map the Specific Harm: "Dangerous Zone Trapping"**
*   **Define the Failure Mode:** Explicitly document how high surge prices in low-supply areas discourage driver exit, effectively trapping users in locations with elevated safety risks.
*   **Identify Affected Groups:** List specific neighborhoods or venue types (e.g., nightlife districts, isolated transit hubs) known for higher risk during the festival.
*   **Worst-Case Scenario:** Record the realistic worst outcome (e.g., a user stranded for hours in an unsafe area due to zero driver availability caused by price suppression).

**2. Measure the Risk (Pre-Launch Probes)**
*   **Historical Simulation:** Run the algorithm against historical festival data from similar years. Identify timestamps where the model would have created "no-exit" pricing zones.
*   **Adversarial Edge Cases:** Test inputs representing extreme supply shortages combined with high demand spikes. Verify if the price ceiling creates a total driver blackout.
*   **Proxy Safety Signals:** Since real-time crime data is unavailable, identify proxy indicators in your legacy pipeline (e.g., historical cancellation rates, average wait times >20 mins, frequent app-close events in specific geofences) that correlate with unsafe conditions.
*   **Metric Definition:** Define a "Trap Index" (e.g., Price Surge > X% + Driver Supply < Y% + Wait Time > Z mins). Any zone hitting this threshold is a **FAIL**.

**3. Manage Residual Risk (Human Gates & Overrides)**
*   **Hard Cap on Surge:** Implement an immediate, hard-coded ceiling on surge multipliers for identified high-risk zones to ensure prices never reach a point that completely repels drivers.
*   **Manual Override Switch:** Establish a "Safety Mode" toggle accessible to the on-call engineer. If a zone is flagged as trapped, this switch forces a temporary price reset or injects a driver incentive bonus to break the deadlock.
*   **Uncertainty Disclosure:** Do not display precise "99% safe" metrics. If the system detects a potential trap, trigger a user-facing message: "High demand in this area. Wait times may be unpredictable. Consider walking to a nearby pickup spot."
*   **Festival War Room:** Assign one engineer solely to monitor the "Trap Index" dashboard during the festival. Their only job is to execute manual overrides if automated thresholds are breached.

**4. Governance & Ownership**
*   **Name the Owner:** Designate one specific engineer as the "Safety Gate Owner" for the festival duration. They have the authority to halt surge pricing in specific zones without committee approval.
*   **Post-Incident Plan:** Define the rollback procedure: If a user reports being trapped, the system must automatically log the event and alert the owner immediately for manual intervention.

**Critical Constraints & Notes**
*   **No Certification:** This checklist identifies risks; it does not certify the system as "safe." The decision to launch with residual risk belongs to your leadership.
*   **Legal Routing:** Whether trapping users constitutes a regulatory violation or requires specific legal disclosure under local transport laws is a determination for counsel, not this technical review.
