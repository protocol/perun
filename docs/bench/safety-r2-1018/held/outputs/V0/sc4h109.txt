### Pre-Launch Safety Evaluation Framework: Marine Navigation AI

**1. Harm Inventory (MAP)**
Before launch, formally record the specific failure mode: **Unsafe Automation via Outdated Data**.
*   **Harm:** The AI recommends or executes a route traversing protected coral reefs due to data latency.
*   **Affected Parties:** Marine ecosystems (irreversible damage), shipping captains (legal/liability risk), and the firm (reputational/regulatory fallout).
*   **Worst Case:** Autonomous execution of a grounding event or ecological violation in a protected zone before human intervention is possible.

**2. Measurement Plan (MEASURE)**
Do not certify the system as "safe." Instead, run these specific evaluations using your current imperfect data:
*   **Adversarial Probe Set:** Construct test scenarios where the optimal fuel/time route explicitly intersects known protected zones (using whatever legacy data you have). Include "positive controls" (routes that definitely hit reefs) and "negative controls" (routes clearly avoiding them).
*   **Metric & Threshold:** Measure the frequency of "High-Risk Route Generation." Set a **zero-tolerance threshold** for any autonomous suggestion entering a protected zone. If the AI suggests such a route even once in testing, the autonomous feature fails the gate.
*   **Data Limitation Disclosure:** Explicitly label all performance metrics as **UNVERIFIED** regarding real-time seabed conditions. Document that the evaluation relies on outdated overlays and cannot guarantee avoidance of unmapped hazards.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Draft a preliminary **Model Card** stating:
    *   *Intended Use:* Route optimization for fuel/time only where ecological safety is confirmed by other means.
    *   *Limitations:* Cannot detect real-time seabed changes; relies on outdated map data until [Next Month].
    *   *Owner:* Name the specific engineer responsible for this risk.

**3. Risk Management & Human Gate (MANAGE)**
Given the high stakes (irreversible ecological damage) and known data gaps, full autonomy is unsafe. Implement the following controls:
*   **Mandatory Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Gate:** Disable fully autonomous execution immediately. The system must operate in **"Recommendation Only"** mode.
    *   *Protocol:* The AI generates a route, but a licensed captain or logistics coordinator must manually review and explicitly confirm the path against current charts before the vessel moves.
    *   *Override Mechanism:* Provide a prominent, one-click "Reject Route" button that forces the user to select a manual path or request a recalculation.
*   **Uncertainty Visualization:** Do not display a false-precision confidence score (e.g., "92% safe"). Instead, use defined coarse tiers for route safety:
    *   **"Verified Safe"** (Only if cross-referenced with latest official notices).
    *   **"Unverified / Data Lag"** (Default state given current licensing delays – displayed in high-visibility amber).
    *   **"High Risk"** (If any heuristic suggests proximity to protected zones).
*   **Staged Rollout:** Limit the feature to a small subset of non-sensitive routes until the high-resolution maps arrive next month. Cap exposure to prevent fleet-wide incidents.

**4. Governance (GOVERN)**
*   **Ownership:** Assign a specific engineer as the "Safety Owner" for this feature. They must re-review the system the moment the new map data arrives.
*   **Legal Routing:** Do not assert whether this violates specific maritime laws. Route the question of "regulated automated decision" and mandatory ecological disclosures to legal counsel immediately, citing the EU AI Act and local maritime regulations as potential regimes to investigate.

**Status:** The autonomous feature cannot ship in its current state without the HITL gate. The residual risk is **HIGH** due to unverified data.

*Note: This framework identifies risks and mandates controls; it does not certify the system as safe. The final ship decision rests with your leadership.*
