### Risk Assessment Plan: Drone Impact on Wildlife Behavior

**1. Harm Inventory (MAP)**
*   **Primary Harm:** Ecological disturbance causing nesting abandonment, reduced breeding success, or population decline in sensitive bird species.
*   **Affected Parties:** Protected bird populations, ecosystem integrity, and conservation credibility.
*   **Worst Realistic Case:** Irreversible local population collapse in a protected zone due to undetected stress responses during the pilot, leading to grant termination and regulatory bans.
*   **Secondary Harm:** Over-reliance by rangers on AI tracking data that misses behavioral signs of distress not captured by movement algorithms.

**2. Measurement Strategy (MEASURE)**
Given the restricted access and short timeline, you cannot rely solely on real-world breeding season testing. You must triangulate evidence:
*   **Controlled Proxy Testing (Immediate):** Conduct flight tests in non-sensitive zones with similar terrain using decoy nests or non-endangered surrogate species to establish baseline noise/flight disturbance thresholds.
    *   *Metric:* Flight initiation distance (FID) and return-to-nest time.
    *   *Threshold:* Zero tolerance for nest abandonment in proxy tests; any failure triggers an immediate flight pattern redesign.
*   **Simulation & Historical Data:** Correlate drone telemetry (noise dB, altitude, approach angle) with existing literature on avian stress responses. Run "adversarial" simulations where the AI plans aggressive paths to ensure the system rejects them before deployment.
*   **Pilot Monitoring Protocol:** During the limited real-world window, deploy passive acoustic monitors alongside drones to record bird calls (distress vs. normal) independent of the drone's AI.
    *   *Stop Condition:* If passive monitors detect sustained distress calls or silence (abandonment) correlated with drone presence, autonomous operations halt immediately.
*   **Reporting Status:** Any metric not directly measured in the specific reserve during the breeding window must be labeled **UNVERIFIED** in your grant report. Do not extrapolate safety claims from proxy tests to sensitive zones without explicit caveats.

**3. Risk Management Controls (MANAGE)**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Mandatory):** Disable fully autonomous flight in or near known nesting sites. Require a conservation biologist to explicitly approve every flight path and altitude change in sensitive zones. The drone may suggest a path, but it must not execute high-stakes maneuvers (low altitude, rapid approach) without human confirmation.
*   **Uncertainty Visualization:** The ranger interface must not present AI tracking as absolute fact. Display confidence as coarse tiers (e.g., "Confirmed Sighting," "Probable Movement," "Unverified Signal") rather than precise percentages. If the AI is unsure due to foliage or distance, it must default to "Unverified" rather than guessing.
*   **Reversibility:** Implement a "Silent Mode" or immediate hover/retreat command triggered manually by rangers if visual observation suggests disturbance, overriding the AI's mission objective.
*   **Disclosure:** Clearly document in the grant report that the system is an *assistive tool* with known limitations regarding behavioral impact, rather than certifying it as "ecologically safe."

**4. Governance (GOVERN)**
*   **Owner:** Assign a specific Lead Biologist as the owner of the "Ecological Harm" risk. They hold the authority to pause the pilot regardless of engineering deadlines.
*   **Re-review Trigger:** Any expansion to a new zone or change in drone hardware/software requires a fresh harm inventory review before deployment.

**Deliverable for Grant Report:**
Submit the Harm Inventory, the results of the proxy testing, the specific Human-in-the-Loop protocols enacted, and a clear statement of **UNVERIFIED** risks where direct breeding-season data is missing. This demonstrates operational capability while honestly bounding the safety claims.

*Note: Legal requirements for environmental impact disclosures vary by jurisdiction; route specific regulatory compliance questions to legal counsel. Do not assert "safety" as a final status; frame it as "managed risk with active monitoring."*
