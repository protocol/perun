### Pre-Launch Safety Checklist: LegalAid Global Asylum Chatbot

**1. Highest-Risk Query Inventory (The "Do Not Guess" List)**
Before launch, tag these query types as **Critical Risk**. The bot must never answer these autonomously; it must immediately trigger a fallback.
*   **Eligibility Determinations:** Questions like "Am I eligible for asylum?" or "Will I be deported if...?" (Risk: False assurance or unnecessary panic).
*   **Deadline & Status Specifics:** Questions about specific filing deadlines, current backlog times, or individual case status (Risk: Outdated data causes missed windows).
*   **Criminal or Security History:** Queries involving past arrests, security charges, or "safe third country" rules (Risk: Complex legal nuance where hallucination is fatal to the case).
*   **Medical/Psychological Grounds:** Questions linking health conditions to asylum claims (Risk: Misinterpreting medical evidence requirements).
*   **Appeals & Rejections:** Advice on what to do after a rejection notice (Risk: Irreversible procedural errors).

**2. Mandatory Fallback Mechanisms (Human-in-the-Loop)**
For the Critical Risk queries above, the system must hard-code a refusal to answer and route to a human or static resource.
*   **The "I Don't Know" Protocol:** If the bot's confidence is not absolute (or if the query matches the Critical Risk list), it must output: *"I cannot provide legal advice on this specific situation. This requires a human expert."*
*   **Static Resource Handoff:** Immediately follow the refusal with a link or printed QR code to the single most trusted, static PDF guide you possess (e.g., "UNHCR Basic Rights Sheet") or the physical location/hours of the nearest legal aid tent.
*   **Data Collection for Triage:** If possible, log these rejected queries (anonymized) so your team of five can review them nightly and update the static guides.

**3. Essential Disclaimers & Transparency**
*   **Upfront Disclosure:** The very first message must state: *"I am an AI assistant, not a lawyer. My answers are based on public documents and may be outdated. Do not rely on me for final legal decisions."*
*   **Uncertainty Signaling:** Never present an answer as a fact. Use phrasing like: *"Public documents generally suggest..."* or *"Standard procedures often include..."* Avoid definitive words like "must," "will," or "guaranteed."
*   **No "Confidence" Percentages:** Do not show users numbers (e.g., "80% sure"). This creates false trust. Either the answer is a verified static excerpt, or it is a referral to a human.

**4. Rapid Validation Plan (10-Day Sprint)**
*   **Red-Team Lite:** Have two team members act as "adversarial users" for 4 hours. Ask the bot the Critical Risk questions intentionally. If it answers directly instead of triggering the fallback, hard-code a block for that pattern immediately.
*   **The "Worst-Case" Test:** Ask the bot for advice that would lead to immediate deportation if followed. If the bot generates plausible-sounding but wrong advice, the fallback mechanism is not working.
*   **Documentation:** Create a one-page "Model Card" for your team listing: what the bot is trained on (public docs), what it *cannot* do (real-time policy checks), and who to call if it fails.

**Note:** Because this involves high-stakes, irreversible legal outcomes for vulnerable people, full autonomous operation is unsafe; the fallback to human review for complex cases is mandatory, not optional.
**Note:** You must consult local counsel immediately to verify if specific AI disclosure laws apply in Greece; this checklist recommends disclosure as a safety control, not a legal verdict.
