### Automated Produce Pricing: Pre-Launch Harm Review Checklist

**1. Harm Inventory & Scope Definition**
*   [ ] **Specific Failure Mode Defined:** Explicitly document the "False Premium" scenario: slightly bruised/edible fruit misclassified as premium due to lighting, angle, or minor artifacts.
*   [ ] **Affected Parties Identified:** Confirm that customers paying inflated prices are the primary harmed group, and note potential reputational damage to the pilot stores.
*   [ ] **Worst-Case Scenario Recorded:** Define the maximum financial impact per transaction and the cumulative risk over a 24-hour error window (given the no-update constraint).

**2. Measurement Plan (Runnable Tests)**
*   [ ] **Adversarial Probe Set Created:** Assemble a test dataset containing:
    *   *Positive Controls:* Known bruised items incorrectly labeled as premium in training.
    *   *Negative Controls:* Perfect premium items and clearly spoiled items.
    *   *Edge Cases:* Items with minor visual artifacts (stickers, shadows, water droplets) that mimic bruising.
*   [ ] **Subgroup Slicing:** Ensure tests cover variations in lighting conditions found across the 50 pilot stores and different produce varieties.
*   [ ] **Metric & Threshold Defined:** Set a specific "False Premium Rate" threshold. Given the financial harm, propose near-zero tolerance for systematic overcharging.
*   [ ] **Stop Condition Established:** Define the exact failure rate that triggers a "No-Go" decision for the pilot launch.
*   [ ] **Injection & Leakage Checks:** Verify the model isn't relying on irrelevant background cues (e.g., bin color) to determine ripeness.

**3. Risk Management & Human Gates**
*   [ ] **Irreversible Action Gate:** Since prices persist for 24 hours and cannot be updated mid-cycle, implement a **human-in-the-loop review** for any price increase above a certain percentage or for specific high-value items before the daily cycle locks.
*   [ ] **Uncertainty Rendering:** If the model confidence is low or borderline, default the pricing to the **lower** tier (standard vs. premium) rather than guessing. Do not display raw confidence percentages (e.g., "87%") to operators; use defined tiers like "Verified Premium," "Likely Standard," or "Review Required."
*   [ ] **Override Mechanism:** Ensure store staff can manually override the system price at the register if a customer disputes the assessment.
*   [ ] **Disclosure Strategy:** Determine if signage is needed to inform customers that AI determines dynamic pricing based on visual quality.

**4. Governance & Transparency**
*   [ ] **Owner Assignment:** Name a specific individual responsible for monitoring the "False Premium" metric daily during the pilot.
*   [ ] **Model Card Drafted:** Create a one-page artifact detailing:
    *   Intended use (ripeness assessment for pricing).
    *   Known limitations (struggles with specific lighting or minor artifacts).
    *   Measured performance on the "False Premium" test set (mark as **UNVERIFIED** until tests run).
*   [ ] **Legal Routing:** Flag the dynamic pricing mechanism for legal counsel to review regarding consumer protection laws and disclosure requirements; do not assert compliance internally.

**5. Final Go/No-Go Criteria**
*   [ ] **Residual Risk Accepted:** The product owner must explicitly sign off on the remaining risk of overcharging, acknowledging that no system is perfectly unbiased.
*   [ ] **Rollback Plan:** Define the procedure to disable dynamic pricing entirely if the stop condition is met during the pilot.

*Note: Do not fabricate accuracy metrics; if tests have not run, mark all performance data as UNVERIFIED. Given the 24-hour error persistence, the human gate on price increases is the critical safety floor.*
