**BLUF:** Do not launch the beta next week without a human-in-the-loop gate on high-stakes risks; full automation of mental health guidance for teenagers is the failure this protocol prevents. Since you cannot afford clinical oversight, you must cap exposure and implement a hard "stop-and-escalate" rule rather than attempting to "fix" the model via testing alone.

**Immediate Safety Protocol (MAP, MEASURE, MANAGE)**

**1. Harm Inventory (MAP)**
Before writing code, document the specific failure modes for your teen users:
*   **Unsafe High-Stakes Automation:** The bot providing actionable advice on self-harm, eating disorders, or suicide without human verification.
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** The bot inventing coping mechanisms or medical facts that are dangerous if followed.
*   **Over-Reliance:** The design encouraging teens to trust the bot's empathy over real-world help, delaying professional care.
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Users believing they are speaking to a licensed therapist rather than an AI.

**2. Measurement Plan (MEASURE)**
You cannot test your way to "safe" without clinical experts, so do not claim a safety metric. Instead, run a targeted red-team exercise:
*   **Probe Set:** Construct 50 adversarial prompts simulating crisis states (e.g., "I want to hurt myself," "How do I hide my eating disorder?").
*   **Controls:** Include known bad outputs (any encouragement of harm) and known good outputs (immediate escalation to helplines).
*   **Threshold:** Zero tolerance. If the bot generates *any* response that validates, instructs, or minimizes self-harm, the launch is blocked.
*   **Reporting:** Mark all untested scenarios as `UNVERIFIED`. Do not invent a "99% safe" statistic.

**3. Risk Management Controls (MANAGE)**
Given the budget constraints and high stakes, apply these non-negotiable guards:
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate:** Any conversation triggering keywords related to self-harm, suicide, or abuse must immediately stop generating AI advice and route to a pre-written, static safety response providing local emergency resources and helplines. The AI must not attempt to "counsel" in these moments.
*   **Defined Confidence Tiers:** Do not show the user a percentage confidence score. If the bot is unsure, it must default to a "Unverified" tier: "I am an AI, not a doctor. Please speak to a trusted adult or call [Helpline]."
*   **Mandatory Disclosure:** Every interaction must start with or clearly display: "I am an AI companion, not a mental health professional. I cannot provide medical advice."
*   **Exposure Cap:** Limit the beta to a small, monitored group with explicit parental/guardian consent mechanisms, rather than an open launch.

**Governance**
Assign one founder as the explicit owner of "Safety Escalations." If a harmful output slips through, this person is responsible for immediate rollback. Re-review this protocol if the model or user demographic changes.

**Legal & Regulatory Note**
Whether this tool constitutes a regulated medical device or requires specific legal disclosures under laws like the EU AI Act is a determination for legal counsel, not this technical review. Route this question immediately.

**Stage Awareness**
While a full governance program is premature for a five-person startup, the floor never relaxes: irreversible or high-stakes automated actions (like mental health counseling) require a human gate or a hard stop, regardless of budget or stage.
