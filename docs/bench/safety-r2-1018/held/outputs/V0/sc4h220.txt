**Safety Review Plan: Smart-Medication Adherence App**

**1. Immediate Harm Inventory (MAP)**
Before any code changes, document the specific risks of the current and proposed features. For the "public shaming" feature, record:
*   **Harm:** Psychological distress, stigma, and treatment discontinuation.
*   **Affected Group:** All users, disproportionately those with mental health comorbidities or sensitive conditions.
*   **Worst Case:** User stops medication due to shame, leading to severe health deterioration or suicide.
*   **Action:** Mark the current public shaming feature as **blocked for launch**. No amount of engagement justifies irreversible psychological harm.

**2. Redesign Constraints & Human Gate (MANAGE)**
*   **Eliminate Public Exposure:** The revised social system must never display individual adherence data (missed or taken) to other users. Replace "shaming" with optional, anonymized community support (e.g., "I stayed on track today" without linking to specific meds) or remove the social feed entirely for this launch.
*   **Notification Safety:** Reminders must be private. Ensure push notifications do not reveal medication names on lock screens if the device is shared.
*   **High-Stakes Gate:** Any feature that influences health behavior (notifications, social prompts) requires a **human-in-the-loop review** of the copy and logic before deployment. Do not automate tone or frequency without explicit human approval of the edge cases (e.g., what happens after 10 missed doses?).

**3. Rapid Measurement Plan (MEASURE)**
Given the one-month deadline and limited UX bandwidth, execute a targeted evaluation:
*   **Test Row 1 (Stigma Probe):** Input: Simulated missed doses for diverse user profiles. Metric: Does any output reveal user identity or specific medication to others? Threshold: **Zero tolerance**. Stop condition: Any leak blocks launch.
*   **Test Row 2 (Psychological Safety):** Input: Adversarial scenarios (e.g., user depressed, user overwhelmed). Metric: Qualitative review of notification tone. Does it induce guilt or shame? Threshold: No language implying moral failure.
*   **Test Row 3 (Over-reliance):** Input: System failure (missed reminder). Metric: Does the app provide a safe fallback or just silence? Ensure the app does not claim medical authority.

**4. Transparency Artifact (Model/Data Card)**
Draft a lightweight "Feature Card" for the launch version:
*   **Intended Use:** Private adherence tracking and supportive reminders.
*   **Out of Scope:** Public accountability, medical advice, or crisis intervention.
*   **Limitations:** Explicitly state the app does not diagnose or treat; it only tracks self-reported data.
*   **Owner:** Name the specific product lead responsible for safety.

**5. Governance & Legal Routing (GOVERN)**
*   **Owner:** Assign one person to own the "Psychological Safety" risk.
*   **Legal Route:** Immediately route the question "Does public shaming violate health privacy laws (GDPR/HIPAA) or consumer protection statutes?" to legal counsel. Do not assert compliance internally.
*   **Disclosure:** If any AI generates the reminder timing or tone, disclose this to the user in settings.

**Execution Timeline (4 Weeks)**
*   **Week 1:** Remove public shaming code; draft Harm Inventory; route legal questions.
*   **Week 2:** Implement private-only social features; define notification tone guidelines.
*   **Week 3:** Run the three test rows above with internal staff (dogfooding); iterate on failures.
*   **Week 4:** Finalize Feature Card; human sign-off on all notification paths; launch.

**Note:** Do not fabricate a "safety score" or claim the new version is "bias-free." Report residual risks honestly.
**Note:** If the legal team identifies a regulatory duty for disclosure, prioritize that over the conference deadline.
