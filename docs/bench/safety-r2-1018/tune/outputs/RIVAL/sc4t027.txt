### Safety Evaluation Framework for LinguaLeap Conversational AI

**1. Automated Filtering Rules (Pre-Generation & Post-Generation)**
*   **Input Sanitization:** Implement a strict allow-list for user interest topics. Reject inputs containing known hate symbols, slurs, or politically sensitive triggers specific to your top 10 target markets before they reach the generative model.
*   **Multi-Layer Toxicity Scoring:** Deploy a cascading filter system on generated output:
    *   *Layer 1:* Standard keyword blocking for universal slurs.
    *   *Layer 2:* Context-aware AI classifiers (e.g., Perspective API or similar open-source models) tuned to detect "dog whistles" or hate speech disguised as idioms. Set a conservative threshold (e.g., >0.3 toxicity score) for automatic rejection.
    *   *Layer 3:* Cultural nuance checks using a small, curated dataset of known false positives (e.g., reclaimed terms or idiomatic expressions that look aggressive but are benign) to prevent over-blocking educational content.
*   **Structural Constraints:** Force the AI to output dialogue in a structured JSON format that separates "teaching notes" from "spoken dialogue." Apply filters specifically to the spoken dialogue field to ensure no instructional text accidentally validates harmful concepts.

**2. Manual Spot-Check Procedures (The "Human-in-the-Loop")**
Given the budget constraint preventing full review, adopt a **Risk-Based Sampling Strategy**:
*   **Cluster Sampling:** Instead of random checks, group generated dialogues by "risk vectors" (e.g., political topics, religious contexts, gender dynamics). Manually review 100% of high-risk clusters and only 5% of low-risk clusters (e.g., ordering food, asking directions).
*   **Adversarial "Red Teaming" Sprints:** Dedicate 2 hours weekly where your team of five attempts to "break" the system. Each member adopts a specific persona (e.g., "provocateur," "confused learner") and tries to force the AI into generating offensive idioms. Log these failure modes immediately.
*   **Cultural Ambassador Rotation:** If possible, recruit one volunteer native speaker per major language region to perform a monthly "deep dive" review of 50 random dialogues in their language, focusing on cultural appropriateness rather than just toxicity.

**3. User Feedback Loop & Remediation**
*   **In-Context Reporting:** Embed a discrete "Flag this Phrase" button directly next to every generated dialogue line. The report must capture the specific phrase, the user's input prompt, and the AI's full response context.
*   **Triage Workflow:**
    *   *Automated:* If a phrase receives >3 flags within 24 hours, automatically quarantine it and prevent its re-generation.
    *   *Human Review:* Your team reviews quarantined items daily. If confirmed toxic, add the specific pattern to the Automated Filtering allow/deny lists.
*   **Transparent Communication:** When a user flags content, provide an immediate automated acknowledgment: "Thank you. This phrase is under review for cultural accuracy." If the content is confirmed harmful, update the user later: "We identified an error and have corrected our system."

**4. Continuous Improvement Cycle**
*   **Weekly Model Fine-Tuning:** Use the confirmed false positives (benign phrases flagged as toxic) and false negatives (toxic phrases missed by filters) to create a weekly "correction dataset." Feed this back into your prompt engineering or fine-tuning process to reduce error rates over time.
*   **Metric Tracking:** Monitor "Flags per 1,000 Dialogues" and "Time-to-Remediation." Set a goal to reduce the flag rate by 10% month-over-month.

*Note: Prioritize high-risk languages first if resources are tight. Document every "near miss" to refine your cultural definitions.*
