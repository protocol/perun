### Pre-Launch Safety Verification Plan: Smart-Shower Pressure Fluctuations

**1. Test Environment & Instrumentation Setup**
*   **Hydraulic Rig:** Construct a closed-loop test bench mimicking residential plumbing (standard 40–60 PSI). Install high-frequency pressure transducers (sampling ≥100Hz) at the showerhead inlet to capture micro-fluctuations.
*   **Thermal Logging:** Place fast-response thermocouples directly in the water stream at the point of user contact. Log temperature every 50ms to detect spikes invisible to standard sensors.
*   **Actuation Simulation:** Use a programmable valve to simulate specific "edge case" pressure events: sudden drops (simulating a toilet flush), rapid oscillations (washing machine cycle), and total supply loss.

**2. Defined Edge Case Scenarios**
Execute the following specific pressure profiles against the firmware:
*   **The "Sudden Drop":** Instant pressure decrease of 30% lasting 2 seconds (simulating a concurrent toilet flush).
*   **The "Oscillation":** Rapid pressure cycling (±15%) over 5 seconds (simulating multiple household appliances activating).
*   **The "Brownout":** Pressure drop to near-zero for 0.5 seconds, then immediate restoration.
*   **The "Slow Bleed":** A gradual pressure decline over 10 seconds, testing if the system fails to react until it is too late.

**3. Performance Metrics & Pass/Fail Criteria**
Given the vulnerability of the target demographic, the criteria are strict:
*   **Reaction Latency:** Time from pressure anomaly detection to valve actuation must be **< 200ms**. Any latency >500ms is a critical failure.
*   **Temperature Ceiling:** During any pressure drop, water temperature must not exceed **43°C (109°F)** for more than 1 second.
*   **Fail-Safe Action:** Upon detecting a drop >20%, the system must immediately either cut flow or inject cold water to neutralize the hot supply ratio.
*   **Recovery Stability:** Once pressure normalizes, the system must not overshoot the target temperature by more than ±1°C.

**4. Execution Protocol**
*   **Baseline Calibration:** Run 10 cycles with stable pressure to establish the "normal" thermal baseline.
*   **Stress Testing:** Run each edge case scenario 50 times consecutively to check for firmware state errors or memory leaks that might degrade response time over repeated events.
*   **Worst-Case Input:** Perform tests with the water heater set to its maximum output (e.g., 60°C) to ensure the fail-safe works even when the potential scalding risk is highest.
*   **Power Interruption:** Simulate a power loss during a pressure spike to verify mechanical or battery-backed fail-safes engage instantly.

**5. Documentation & Go/No-Go Decision**
*   Record all latency and temperature data. Plot temperature vs. time for every failed cycle.
*   **Go Condition:** 100% of tests meet the Reaction Latency and Temperature Ceiling criteria.
*   **No-Go Condition:** Any single test results in water >45°C for >2 seconds, or reaction latency >500ms.

**Note:** Given the firmware size constraints, prioritize optimizing the interrupt handler for the pressure sensor over non-essential features.
If hardware latency is the bottleneck, consider a purely mechanical thermal bypass valve as a redundant physical layer.
