### Internal Testing Protocol: Demographic Leakage Checklist

Your engineering team should manually review batches of generated questions against these specific red flags. Do not rely on automated sentiment analysis alone; human review of context is required.

**1. Temporal Proxies (Age)**
*   **Graduation Dates:** Questions asking "What was your first job after graduating in [Year]?" or referencing specific decades ("How did you navigate the tech boom of the 90s?").
*   **Career Duration:** Phrasing that assumes a specific career length, such as "With your 25 years of experience..." when the resume only implies a start date.
*   **Cultural Generational Markers:** References to obsolete technology, historical events, or pop culture specific to a generation unless directly relevant to the job role.

**2. Family and Caregiving Status**
*   **Gap Explanations:** Questions that specifically target employment gaps with phrasing like "What were you doing during your time off to raise children?" rather than neutral phrasing like "Can you describe your professional activities during this gap?"
*   **Availability Assumptions:** Inquiries about weekend/evening availability that imply assumptions about marital status or childcare responsibilities.
*   **Relocation:** Questions probing spouse employment or family ties to a location.

**3. Religion, Culture, and Origin**
*   **Name-Based Assumptions:** Questions that incorrectly assume cultural background based on a candidate's name (e.g., asking about specific holidays or traditions).
*   **Language Nuance:** Queries about "native" language or country of origin when the resume only lists language proficiency.
*   **Volunteer/Interest Bias:** Questions that drill down into religious or culturally specific volunteer work listed on a resume in a way that suggests it defines the candidate's identity rather than their skills.

**4. Socioeconomic Indicators**
*   **Unpaid Experience:** Over-emphasis on unpaid internships or volunteer travel that may correlate with wealth.
*   **Extracurriculars:** Questions focusing on expensive hobbies (e.g., skiing, sailing) listed as interests, framing them as primary conversation starters over professional skills.

### Post-Launch Monitoring Strategy

Since you cannot afford external auditors, implement a "Human-in-the-Loop" sampling protocol using your existing staff:

1.  **Stratified Random Sampling:** Every week, randomly select 5% of generated question sets. Ensure this sample includes resumes from diverse demographic backgrounds (if known via voluntary self-ID) or varied career paths (non-linear, career changers).
2.  **Blind Review Rotation:** Rotate the review duty among your four team members. Reviewers should not know which candidate profile generated the questions to prevent confirmation bias. Use a simple "Pass/Fail/Flag" tag for any question triggering the red flags above.
3.  **Candidate Feedback Loop:** Add a discreet, optional feedback field at the end of the interview interface: "Did any question feel irrelevant or uncomfortable?" Aggregate this data weekly. A spike in flags for specific resume patterns indicates a logic flaw.
4.  **Keyword Alerting:** Set up simple internal logs to alert if generated questions contain high-risk keywords (e.g., "church," "children," "graduated," "young," "old") combined with personal pronouns.
