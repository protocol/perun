### Pre-Launch Evaluation Plan: Low-Light Stationary Obstacle Detection

**1. Controlled Environment Testing (Proving Grounds)**
*   **Scenario Matrix:** Conduct tests at dusk, full night, and pre-dawn using unlit stationary emergency vehicle surrogates (police cruisers, ambulances) with varying light configurations (lights off, parking lights only, flashing emergency lights).
*   **Variable Introduction:** Introduce environmental degradations specific to the known sensor constraint: heavy rain, fog, and road spray to reduce contrast further. Test on both dry and wet asphalt to analyze reflection interference.
*   **Approach Vectors:** Execute approaches at varying speeds (55–75 mph) and lanes (adjacent vs. direct approach) to determine the maximum detection range and the "point of no return" where braking is insufficient.
*   **Sensor Stress Testing:** Deliberately introduce glare from oncoming traffic and curved road segments to evaluate if the fusion software falsely filters out low-contrast stationary objects as noise.

**2. Data-Driven Simulation**
*   **Edge Case Replay:** Utilize recorded sensor data from previous night drives to inject digital surrogates of emergency vehicles into the perception stack. This allows for thousands of iterations of the specific "low-contrast stationary" failure mode without physical risk.
*   **Contrast Threshold Mapping:** Systematically lower the digital contrast of test objects in simulation to identify the exact luminance threshold where the current software fails to classify an object as an obstacle versus background noise.

**3. Operational Limitations & Driver Alerts**
Given the confirmed constraint regarding low-contrast objects in poor lighting, the following safeguards must be enforced immediately:

*   **Geofenced Speed Cap:** The system must automatically limit maximum speed to 45 mph when ambient light sensors detect night-time conditions combined with active precipitation or when GPS indicates absence of overhead highway lighting.
*   **Mandatory Hands-On-Wheel Policy:** Disable "hands-off" tolerance timers during night operation. The system must require continuous torque input on the steering wheel to confirm driver engagement.
*   **Enhanced Visual/Auditory Warnings:** Implement a persistent dashboard alert stating: *"Night Vision Limited: Monitor Road for Stationary Objects."* If the system detects reduced visibility (via camera noise levels), it must issue an audible chime every 30 seconds until driver torque is detected.
*   **Emergency Vehicle Specific Warning:** If the fusion stack detects *any* low-confidence stationary object ahead, the system should default to an early, aggressive alert ("Object Ahead – Prepare to Brake") rather than waiting for high-confidence classification, shifting the burden of final verification to the driver sooner.
*   **Feature Degradation:** If the sensor fusion confidence score drops below a safe threshold due to lighting/contrast issues, the system must disengage automatic steering and revert to Lane Departure Warning only, requiring the driver to manually maintain lane position.

**Note:** This plan assumes the surrogate vehicles accurately mimic the reflectivity and thermal signature of real emergency units.
**Note:** All road testing involving disabled safety features must be conducted with a professional safety driver ready to take immediate control.
