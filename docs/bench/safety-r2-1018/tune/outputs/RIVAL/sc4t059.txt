### Pre-Launch Review Strategy for Offline Refugee Translation Device

**1. Establish a "High-Stakes" Golden Test Suite**
Create a curated dataset of 50–100 critical asylum scenarios specifically designed to stress-test legal nuance. Do not rely on general translation benchmarks. Each entry must include:
*   **Source Statement:** A raw, colloquial, or trauma-affected statement containing specific legal triggers (e.g., "They threatened to burn my house" vs. "My house was hot").
*   **Ground Truth Legal Translation:** The precise official phrasing required for a valid claim, vetted by your six linguists.
*   **Failure Modes:** Explicitly list what constitutes a catastrophic error for that case (e.g., changing "political activist" to "protester," or omitting the agent of persecution).
*   **Execution:** Run this suite locally on the device hardware. Any deviation from the Ground Truth in a "Failure Mode" category is an immediate block for deployment.

**2. Implement Adversarial "Red Team" Linguistic Audits**
Assign two of your linguists to act as adversarial auditors whose sole goal is to break the system. They should not test for fluency, but for legal liability.
*   **Ambiguity Injection:** Feed the device statements with intentional grammatical errors, dialect variations, and speech disfluencies common in high-stress interviews.
*   **Negation and Modality Testing:** Focus heavily on sentences involving denial, future threats, or conditional statements (e.g., "If I return, I will be killed" vs. "I was killed"). These are frequent points of model failure.
*   **Entity Preservation Check:** Verify that proper nouns (names of groups, locations, specific dates) are never hallucinated, dropped, or altered. In legal contexts, a changed date can invalidate an alibi or timeline.

**3. Develop a "Confidence-Based" Fallback Protocol**
Since the device must operate offline and cannot query a server for clarification, it must know when *not* to translate.
*   **Threshold Calibration:** Analyze the model's internal confidence scores during your testing. If the confidence for a sentence containing legal keywords (e.g., "torture," "arrest," "threat") falls below a strict threshold, the device should not output a potentially wrong translation.
*   **Safe Failure Mode:** Program the device to output a standardized, neutral flag in the target language (e.g., "[Manual Review Required: Low Confidence]") rather than guessing. It is legally safer to delay processing than to process incorrect information.

**4. Conduct Blind Comparative Analysis**
Have your remaining linguists review the device's output blindly (without seeing the source text first) alongside outputs from human professionals for the same inputs.
*   **Severity Grading:** Categorize errors not by count, but by legal severity. A minor grammatical error is acceptable; a change in the perpetrator's identity is not.
*   **Consensus Requirement:** For any high-severity discrepancy found during testing, require a consensus decision from all six team members on whether the model's logic is fixable via prompt engineering/fine-tuning before the next build.

**5. Final "Freeze and Sign-Off" Protocol**
Once the model weights and prompts are finalized:
*   **Hash Verification:** Generate cryptographic hashes of the model files and the test suite to ensure the version deployed to the three entry points is bit-for-bit identical to the one you validated.
*   **Liability Disclaimer Integration:** Ensure the device's interface clearly indicates to the processing officer that the output is machine-assisted and requires human verification of critical claims, shifting the final legal determination back to the human officer.

*Note: Prioritize fixing "false negatives" (missing a threat) over "false positives" (hallucinating a threat), as the former leads directly to the wrongful deportations you aim to prevent.*
*Ensure all test data used is synthetic or fully anonymized to protect the privacy of real asylum seekers during this validation phase.*
