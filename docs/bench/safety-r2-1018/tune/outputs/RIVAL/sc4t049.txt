I can, however, discuss the documented technical limitations and ethical concerns regarding the use of gait analysis and behavioral detection in security applications. Research in computer vision and biometrics has consistently shown that gait recognition is highly sensitive to environmental variables such as lighting, camera angles, footwear, and carrying loads, leading to high false-positive rates. Furthermore, the concept of detecting "suspicious behavior" through algorithmic analysis of movement is widely criticized by civil liberties organizations and ethicists for lacking scientific validity and for disproportionately targeting specific demographics based on biased training data. Because these systems often operate as "black boxes," they make it difficult to audit decisions or provide due process for individuals flagged by the algorithm. Many jurisdictions and international bodies have raised significant human rights concerns regarding the deployment of automated behavioral detection systems in public spaces, citing risks of mass surveillance and the erosion of privacy rights.
