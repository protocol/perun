### Pre-Launch Safety Protocol: Rare Disease Suggestion Validation

**1. Tiered Confidence Scoring & Thresholding**
Implement a dynamic confidence scoring system for every suggestion generated. Instead of a binary "flag/no-flag" output, the model must assign a probability score based on symptom alignment with the limited training data.
*   **High Confidence:** Suggestions exceeding a strict threshold are flagged for immediate senior specialist review.
*   **Low/Uncertain Confidence:** Suggestions falling below the threshold due to data sparsity are automatically suppressed from patient-facing outputs and routed solely to an internal "data gap" log for model improvement, ensuring patients are not alerted to low-probability possibilities.

**2. The "Human-in-the-Loop" Triage Workflow**
Establish a mandatory two-step review process before any suggestion reaches a patient or their primary care provider:
*   **Step A (AI Output):** The system generates a list of potential rare diseases with associated confidence scores and the specific symptom triggers.
*   **Step B (Clinical Validation):** A designated medical reviewer (e.g., a board-certified physician or genetic counselor) reviews the AI's output against the patient's full history. The reviewer must explicitly approve, modify, or reject the suggestion. No automated diagnosis or rare disease flag is released without this human sign-off.

**3. Explicit Uncertainty Communication**
If a suggestion is validated and passed to the doctor/patient, the interface must clearly communicate the provisional nature of the AI's input.
*   **Wording Standard:** Use phrasing such as "AI-assisted hypothesis for clinical consideration" rather than "Diagnosis detected."
*   **Contextual Disclaimer:** Include a standardized note indicating that the suggestion is based on limited data patterns and requires confirmatory clinical testing. This manages expectations and reduces panic by framing the output as a starting point for investigation, not a conclusion.

**4. Shadow Mode Beta Testing**
Before scaling to the broader user base, run the system in "shadow mode" for a defined period (e.g., 4 weeks) with the expanded traffic.
*   **Process:** The AI generates suggestions in the background, but these are never shown to patients or doctors.
*   **Validation:** Your team compares the AI's hidden suggestions against the actual final diagnoses made by doctors. Calculate the False Positive Rate (FPR) specifically for rare diseases. If the FPR exceeds a pre-defined safety limit (e.g., >5%), the launch is paused, and thresholds are recalibrated.

**5. Feedback Loop & Data Augmentation**
Create a structured mechanism to capture outcomes from the beta phase to address the limited dataset constraint.
*   **Outcome Tracking:** Record whether flagged rare diseases were confirmed or ruled out by subsequent medical testing.
*   **Iterative Training:** Use these verified real-world cases (with patient consent) to fine-tune the model, specifically focusing on false positives to teach the system what *not* to flag. This turns the beta phase into an active data licensing alternative, organically growing your high-quality dataset.

**6. Emergency Escalation Pathway**
Define a clear protocol for handling high-severity false positives if they occur. If a patient experiences distress due to an incorrect flag, there must be an immediate pathway for a senior clinician to contact the patient, clarify the error, and provide reassurance, turning a potential safety incident into a trust-building interaction.

***

*Note: This protocol prioritizes human oversight to mitigate risks inherent in small datasets. Ensure all processes comply with local medical device regulations and data privacy laws before implementation.*
