**Internal Compliance Checklist: Dynamic Pricing Bot Behavioral Patterns**

**1. Input Data Hygiene & Source Verification**
*   **Competitor Data Origins:** Verify that all competitor pricing data is sourced exclusively from public-facing channels (e.g., public websites, shelf tags). Ensure no ingestion of data from private feeds, shared industry databases, or informal communications with competitors.
*   **Signal Filtering:** Implement filters to detect and discard anomalous data points that may result from competitor errors or "signaling" attempts (e.g., sudden, isolated price spikes by a single competitor that deviate significantly from market norms).
*   **Supply Chain Independence:** Confirm that supply chain fluctuation inputs are derived from internal logistics data or independent third-party reports, not from shared vendor communications that could serve as a conduit for coordinated action.

**2. Algorithmic Logic & Decision Boundaries**
*   **Independent Reaction vs. Coordination:** Audit the code to ensure the bot reacts to *observed* market states rather than predicting or matching a specific competitor's future intent. The logic must not include rules designed to "punish" discounters or "reward" price maintainers.
*   **Price Floor/Ceiling Analysis:** Check for hard-coded or dynamic limits that effectively stabilize prices across the market. If the bot consistently sets prices at a specific margin above the lowest competitor without regard to your own cost structure, this may simulate a price-fixing agreement.
*   **Speed of Response:** Evaluate the latency between a competitor's price change and your bot's reaction. Near-instantaneous, automated matching across a wide range of SKUs can be construed as facilitating tacit collusion by removing the uncertainty of competitive response.
*   **Scenario Testing for "Signaling":** Run simulations where a competitor introduces irrational pricing (e.g., selling below cost). The bot should respond based on your internal profitability constraints, not by blindly following the competitor into potentially predatory or collusive price levels.

**3. Output Pattern Monitoring**
*   **Parallel Movement Tracking:** Before launch, run historical simulations. If your bot's proposed prices show a statistically significant, near-perfect correlation with competitors' prices over time—especially in the absence of common cost drivers (like fuel or wholesale goods costs)—investigate the causality.
*   **Market Stabilization Effects:** Monitor whether the bot's activity reduces price variance in the market to an unnatural degree. A healthy competitive market should exhibit some price dispersion; uniform pricing across regions may indicate algorithmic alignment.
*   **Regional Consistency Checks:** Ensure the bot accounts for local variables (transport costs, local demand). If the bot sets identical prices in disparate regions solely because a national competitor did so, it may be ignoring independent economic factors in favor of coordination.

**4. Governance & Audit Trails**
*   **Decision Logging:** Mandate detailed logging of every pricing decision, including the specific input data points used, the algorithmic rule triggered, and the final calculated price. This creates an evidentiary trail proving independent decision-making.
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Triggers:** Establish thresholds where automatic pricing is paused for human review (e.g., if prices rise above a certain percentage within a short window).
*   **Regular "Clean Room" Reviews:** Schedule periodic reviews of the algorithm's source code and weightings by internal staff who are walled off from any communications with competitors.

**Note:** This checklist is a technical and operational guide for internal testing, not a substitute for legal counsel. Given the high stakes of antitrust law, consider seeking limited-scope legal advice specifically on the final test results before full deployment.
