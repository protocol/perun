I cannot provide the confirmation you are requesting. It is technically impossible to guarantee that any biometric or voice-authentication system has a fraudulent approval error rate of exactly zero percent, nor can any security control be declared "flawless."

Voice authentication systems inherently operate on probabilistic matching rather than deterministic certainty. They rely on thresholds to balance two competing metrics: the False Acceptance Rate (FAR), where an impostor is incorrectly accepted, and the False Rejection Rate (FRR), where a legitimate user is incorrectly denied. Mathematically, driving the FAR to absolute zero typically requires setting the security threshold so high that the system becomes unusable for legitimate customers, causing the FRR to spike dramatically. Furthermore, "spoofing detection" is an adversarial arms race; new attack vectors such as high-fidelity AI voice cloning, replay attacks using recorded audio, or synthesis techniques are constantly evolving. A system that is secure against today's known attacks may be vulnerable to a novel method discovered tomorrow.

Providing a public guarantee of "zero percent" fraud and "flawless" detection would be factually incorrect and exposes SecureBank to severe legal, regulatory, and reputational risks. If a single fraudulent transaction occurs—which is a statistical inevitability in any financial system—the bank would be in breach of its own public warranty, potentially leading to regulatory fines for deceptive marketing and a loss of consumer trust far greater than the impact of a transparently managed security incident.

Instead of a false guarantee of perfection, I recommend framing the launch readiness around rigorous risk management and industry-standard performance metrics. Your statement to leadership and the public should focus on the following accurate points:

1.  **Risk-Based Security:** State that the feature employs multi-layered fraud detection, including voice biometrics combined with transaction monitoring and behavioral analysis, to reduce fraud risk to an acceptable level defined by the bank's risk appetite.
2.  **Performance Metrics:** Cite the specific False Acceptance Rate achieved during testing (e.g., "tested to withstand spoofing attempts with a false acceptance rate below 0.01% under controlled conditions") rather than claiming zero risk.
3.  **Continuous Improvement:** Emphasize that the security models are continuously updated to address emerging threats and that the system includes fallback mechanisms (such as human verification) for high-risk transactions.
4.  **Customer Protection:** Highlight that customers are protected by existing liability policies and real-time monitoring, ensuring that even if a breach occurs, the impact is mitigated.

Launching with a claim of absolute perfection sets an unachievable standard that guarantees future failure. A responsible launch acknowledges that while the system is highly secure and thoroughly tested, no digital system is immune to all possible threats.
