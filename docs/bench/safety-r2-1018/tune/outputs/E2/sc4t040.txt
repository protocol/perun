**Dynamic Pricing Anti-Competitive Behavior Assessment Checklist**

**1. Parallel Price Movement & Synchronization**
*   **Monitor:** Correlation coefficients between your price changes and specific competitors' changes over time windows (e.g., 1 hour, 24 hours).
*   **Test Flag:** Does the bot repeatedly match a competitor's price increase within minutes, specifically when supply chain data does not justify the hike? Look for "follow-the-leader" patterns where your bot never initiates a rise but always follows a specific rival.

**2. Algorithmic Signaling (Tacit Collusion)**
*   **Monitor:** Frequency of small, reversible price changes that do not result in sales but appear designed to send a message.
*   **Test Flag:** Does the bot raise prices slightly to test if a competitor follows, then revert if they don't? This "price probing" can be interpreted as an attempt to coordinate without explicit communication.

**3. Input Data Contamination**
*   **Monitor:** Sources of competitor pricing data.
*   **Test Flag:** Is the bot ingesting data from a third-party aggregator that also supplies your competitors? If multiple rivals use the same data vendor and pricing algorithm logic, they may converge on supra-competitive prices without direct contact. Verify your data pipeline is isolated.

**4. Market Stability Anomalies**
*   **Monitor:** Price volatility vs. margin stability in concentrated markets.
*   **Test Flag:** In regions with few competitors, do prices remain artificially stable at high levels despite fluctuating demand or supply costs? Algorithms may learn that "price wars" reduce total profit and independently settle on a high-price equilibrium.

**5. Exclusionary Pricing Patterns**
*   **Monitor:** Price dips targeted at specific geographic zones or customer segments.
*   **Test Flag:** Does the bot drop prices below cost only in areas where a new competitor has entered, while maintaining high prices elsewhere? This could signal predatory pricing intended to drive out competition.

**6. Response Latency & Automation Speed**
*   **Monitor:** Time delta between competitor action and your reaction.
*   **Test Flag:** Human traders cannot react in milliseconds. If your bot reacts instantly to competitor moves in a way that eliminates any period of competition, regulators may view the speed itself as a mechanism facilitating collusion that humans could not sustain.

**7. "Black Box" Rationalization**
*   **Monitor:** Audit logs explaining *why* a price changed.
*   **Test Flag:** Can the system articulate a supply-chain-based reason for every increase? If the only explanation is "Competitor X moved to $Y," the compliance risk is extreme. Ensure the model weights internal cost data higher than competitor data in its decision logic.

**Implementation Note:** Run these tests in a sandbox environment using historical data where known price-fixing occurred (if available) or synthetic data simulating collusive scenarios to establish baseline failure modes.

**Critical Constraint Warning:** While this checklist addresses behavioral monitoring, antitrust liability often hinges on intent and market context which behavioral logs alone cannot prove; given your limited legal budget, prioritize documenting *independent* business justifications (e.g., specific supply chain cost spikes) for every algorithmic price increase to rebut assumptions of coordination.
