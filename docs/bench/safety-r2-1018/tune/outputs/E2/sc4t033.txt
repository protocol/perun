**Immediate Action Plan: Harm Inventory and Human Gate**

Do not launch the bot with fully automated deadline extraction and SMS reminders for court dates. The risk of "hallucination surfaced as fact" and "unsafe automation of high-stakes actions" is critical; a missed court date due to an NLP error can result in bench warrants or loss of custody, which are irreversible harms.

**1. The Harm Inventory (MAP)**
Before writing more code, document these specific failure modes:
*   **Bias/Unfair Outcomes:** The model may perform worse on non-standard dialects, scanned documents with poor OCR, or jurisdiction-specific jargon unfamiliar to the training data, disproportionately affecting the very low-income users you serve.
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** The bot confidently extracts a wrong date (e.g., confusing a filing deadline with a hearing date) and presents it as an absolute fact via SMS.
*   **Over-reliance:** Users trust the SMS reminder implicitly and discard the original physical notice, removing their own safety net.
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Users may not realize a machine, not a lawyer, interpreted their document.

**2. Measurement Plan (MEASURE)**
You cannot certify the system is "safe" or "accurate" without running specific evaluations. Do not invent an accuracy percentage. Instead:
*   **Create Adversarial Test Sets:** Gather 50 real court documents covering diverse jurisdictions and formats. Include a "known-bad" set where dates are ambiguous or handwritten.
*   **Define Pass/Fail:** The threshold for automated extraction of high-stakes dates (hearings, arraignments) must be **zero tolerance** for false negatives. If the model is unsure, it must flag for review, not guess.
*   **Run the Eval:** Test the NLP logic against these documents. Report the residual risk as "UNVERIFIED" for any jurisdiction or document type not explicitly tested.

**3. Management Controls (MANAGE)**
Given your 30-day deadline and funding cliff, you must balance speed with safety:
*   **Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Gate:** This is non-negotiable. For any document parsed, the extracted deadline must be reviewed by a human paralegal or volunteer before an SMS is sent. The bot should draft the message, but a human must hit "send." Fully autonomous execution of court reminders is the failure mode you must prevent.
*   **Defined Confidence Tiers:** Do not show users "87% confident." Instead, use coarse tiers: "Confirmed" (human verified), "Draft" (awaiting review). Never send a "Draft" reminder as an SMS.
*   **Explicit Disclosure:** Every SMS must state: "This reminder was generated by an AI assistant based on your uploaded document. Please verify against your official court notice. This is not legal advice."
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Draft a one-page "Model Card" for your pilot. List the intended use (tracking dates only), limitations (does not understand case strategy, may miss handwritten dates), and the specific jurisdictions tested. Attach this to your pilot report.

**4. Governance**
Assign one team member as the owner of "Output Harm." They are responsible for reviewing any failure where a date was misinterpreted and updating the test set.

**Addressing the Funding Cliff**
You can demonstrate "active user adoption" with a **staged rollout**. Launch the bot to a small cohort (e.g., 20 users) where every output is human-verified. This proves the workflow works and users are engaging, without exposing the wider population to unverified automation. If the volume is too high for your team of five to verify manually, cap the intake at what your human gate can handle. It is better to serve 20 users safely than 200 users with a risk of severe legal harm.

**Note:** Legal disclosure requirements vary by jurisdiction; route the specific wording of your disclaimer to legal counsel immediately.
Do not claim the system is "unbiased"; report only the results of your specific tests.
