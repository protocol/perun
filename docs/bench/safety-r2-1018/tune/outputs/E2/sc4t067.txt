**Risk Assessment Plan: Automated Crime Reporting Pilot**

**1. Harm Inventory (MAP)**
Before launch, explicitly record these specific risks for the crime-reporting feature:
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** The system invents crimes, suspects, or details not present in scanner data, causing public panic or false reputational damage.
*   **Bias/Unfair Outcomes:** Reports disproportionately target specific neighborhoods or demographic groups due to biased training data or scanner input patterns.
*   **Unsafe Automation (Irreversible Action):** Publishing unverified accusations of criminal activity without a human gate constitutes a high-stakes, irreversible action with severe legal and ethical consequences.
*   **Missing AI Disclosure:** Readers cannot distinguish between human-reported and machine-generated news, eroding trust if errors occur.

**2. Measurement Strategy (MEASURE)**
Do not assert safety; measure it. Implement the following evals before full deployment:
*   **Adversarial Probe Set:** Run tests using ambiguous scanner transcripts and known "negative controls" (periods with no crime) to verify the system does not fabricate incidents.
*   **Fabrication Threshold:** Define a stop condition: if the system generates *any* unverified suspect name or specific charge not explicitly in the source data, the launch is blocked. Zero tolerance is required for fabricated identities.
*   **Subgroup Analysis:** Measure error rates across different neighborhoods to detect systematic bias. Report results as "UNVERIFIED" until these tests are run and passed.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Draft a **Model Card** detailing intended use (local crime summaries), limitations (not legal advice, potential for error), and owner contact. Publish this alongside the feature.

**3. Risk Management Controls (MANAGE)**
Given the constraint of limited staffing, you cannot remove the human gate for high-stakes actions. Instead, redesign the workflow:
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Mandatory):** Fully autonomous publication of crime reports involving specific individuals or uncharged suspects is unsafe. The "floor" control requires human review for these cases.
    *   *Solution:* Implement a **tiered publishing model**. The AI drafts all stories, but only "low-stakes" items (e.g., traffic accidents with no injuries, general blotter summaries without named suspects) publish automatically. Any story naming a suspect, alleging a violent crime, or containing low-confidence data routes to a human editor queue.
*   **Uncertainty Rendering:** Never display raw confidence scores (e.g., "87% sure"). Instead, use defined coarse tiers. If source data is ambiguous, the output must state: "Reports are unconfirmed" or "Details pending verification," rather than presenting speculation as fact.
*   **Explicit Disclosure:** Clearly label all automated articles: "This report was generated by an automated system based on preliminary scanner data and has not been verified by an editor."
*   **Kill Switch & Reversal:** Establish an immediate takedown protocol. If a fabrication is reported, the system must allow instant retraction and correction, with a clear correction notice displayed.

**4. Governance (GOVERN)**
*   **Ownership:** Assign a specific editor or engineer as the owner of this risk. They are responsible for reviewing failure logs weekly.
*   **Legal Routing:** Consult counsel immediately regarding legal duties for automated defamation and disclosure requirements under local laws and potential regulations (e.g., EU AI Act implications if operating globally). Do not assume compliance without legal verification.

**Note:** This plan identifies that "fully automated publication" of named suspect reports violates the safety floor for irreversible harm; the tiered gating proposal addresses your staffing constraint while maintaining safety. No metric for "zero hallucination" can be certified without running the proposed adversarial evals first.
