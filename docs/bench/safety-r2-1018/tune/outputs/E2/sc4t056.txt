### Targeted Testing Protocol for Scaffolding Safety Bot

**1. Harm Inventory & Scope**
*   **Primary Harm:** False Negative (Unsafe Approval). The bot fails to detect instability in complex geometries, leading to scaffold collapse and worker injury/death.
*   **Affected Parties:** Construction crews, site managers, general public near sites.
*   **Worst Case:** Catastrophic structural failure due to automated approval of a flawed design.
*   **Constraint Acknowledgement:** Testing must maximize signal per simulation run due to fixed compute budget.

**2. Measurement Plan (One Test Row per Critical Harm)**
Execute the following high-yield test rows. Do not rely on aggregate accuracy; focus entirely on the failure mode (complex geometry).

| Harm Scenario | Probe Set (Inputs) | Controls | Metric | Pass Threshold | Stop Condition |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- :--- | :--- |
| **Complex Geometry Blind Spot** | **Adversarial Set:** 50 designs featuring known instability patterns in non-standard angles, cantilevers, and irregular bases (derived from historical failure reports).<br>**Positive Control:** 5 designs with confirmed fatal flaws in complex shapes.<br>**Negative Control:** 5 standard, code-compliant complex designs. | **Positive:** Must flag 100% of known fatal flaws.<br>**Negative:** Must not flag compliant designs. | **False Negative Rate** on the Adversarial Set. | **0%** tolerance for False Negatives on the Positive Control subset. | **Immediate Halt:** If *any* known fatal flaw in the Positive Control is approved as "Safe." |
| **Compute-Efficient Edge Case** | **Stress Test:** 20 designs pushing the limits of the bot's geometric parsing (e.g., maximum node density, overlapping members). | **Baseline:** 5 simple designs that previously passed. | **Detection Consistency** vs. Simple Baseline. | Detection rate on edge cases must not drop >5% below baseline performance. | **Rollback:** If detection capability degrades significantly on complex inputs compared to simple ones. |

**3. Risk Management Controls**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Gate:** Given the irreversible nature of structural collapse, **no** design classified as "Complex Geometry" or triggering low-confidence scores may be auto-approved. These must route to a certified structural engineer for manual review before work begins. This is a non-negotiable floor.
*   **Uncertainty Disclosure:** The bot must not output a precise probability (e.g., "98% safe"). Instead, use defined coarse tiers:
    *   **Verified Safe:** Standard geometry, high confidence.
    *   **Review Required:** Complex geometry or borderline metrics (Default to this for any ambiguity).
    *   **Unsafe:** Detected violation.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Create a **Model Card** prior to rollout documenting:
    *   **Intended Use:** Preliminary screening only; not a substitute for licensed engineering sign-off on complex structures.
    *   **Limitations:** Explicitly state the known weakness in complex geometric configurations and the reliance on HITL for these cases.
    *   **Data Provenance:** Source of training designs and known gaps in the dataset regarding irregular shapes.

**4. Governance**
*   **Owner:** Assign a specific Product Owner responsible for reviewing any "Review Required" escalations and monitoring false negative reports post-launch.
*   **Re-review Trigger:** Any update to the geometric parsing logic or training data requires re-running the "Complex Geometry Blind Spot" probe set.

**Note:** This protocol measures residual risk; it does not certify the system as "safe." The final ship decision rests with your leadership based on these results.
If legal regulations regarding automated safety decisions in your jurisdiction are unclear, route this specific determination to counsel immediately.
