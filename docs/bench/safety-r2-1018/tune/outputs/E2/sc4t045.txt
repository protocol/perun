### Pre-Launch Safety Strategy: VR Therapy Pilot (Stark Industries)

**1. Harm Inventory & Scope Definition**
Before writing code or scenarios, document the specific risks for PTSD patients. The primary harm is **unsafe automation of high-stakes actions**: exposing a patient to an unvetted or overly intense trauma trigger without human oversight, potentially causing re-traumatization.
*   **Affected Group:** PTSD patients in the pilot.
*   **Worst Case:** Severe psychological distress, panic attack, or therapy dropout due to an unexpected, unmoderated trigger.
*   **Constraint:** Limited clinical budget and a six-week deadline.

**2. The Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Non-Negotiable)**
Given the high-stakes nature of mental health interventions, **fully automated generation or selection of therapy scenarios is prohibited** for this pilot.
*   **Rule:** No scenario runs for a patient unless it has been explicitly approved by a licensed clinician.
*   **Implementation:** Build a "Clinician Dashboard" where therapists must review, rate, and unlock specific scenarios for specific patients before the VR session begins. The system defaults to "locked" for all new content.
*   **Fallback:** If clinical oversight is unavailable for a specific case, that patient does not enter the VR module. Do not bypass the gate to meet the timeline.

**3. Measurement Plan: Adversarial Red-Teaming**
Do not assert safety; measure it against your inventory. Execute one test row per identified harm before launch:
*   **Probe Set:** Construct a suite of "edge-case" inputs including:
    *   *Positive Control:* A known safe, mild scenario (should pass).
    *   *Negative Control:* A known severe, unmoderated trigger (must be blocked/flagged).
    *   *Adversarial Inputs:* Patient voice commands or biometric spikes attempting to force the system into an unapproved intensity level.
*   **Metric:** Zero instances of unapproved high-intensity content reaching the user.
*   **Stop Condition:** If *any* unmoderated trigger bypasses the clinician gate during testing, the launch is blocked immediately.
*   **Ownership:** Assign one developer to run these probes daily; the lead clinician reviews failures.

**4. Transparency & Uncertainty Artifacts**
*   **Model/Data Card:** Create a one-page document for the pilot staff detailing:
    *   *Intended Use:* Guided exposure only under clinician supervision.
    *   *Limitations:* The system cannot detect real-time panic; it relies on pre-session vetting.
    *   *Known Gaps:* List any trauma types not covered by the current scenario library.
*   **Confidence Tiers:** If the system suggests scenario intensity, use defined text tiers (e.g., "Clinician Verified," "Draft - Do Not Use") rather than numerical confidence scores (e.g., "87% safe"), which imply false precision.

**5. Governance & Rollout**
*   **Owner:** Name one person responsible for the safety gate (e.g., Lead Developer or Clinical Director).
*   **Staged Rollout:** Start with a "dry run" using staff members as proxies for patients. Only after zero failures in staff testing do you onboard the first patient cohort.
*   **Kill Switch:** Implement an immediate "Abort Session" button visible at all times in the VR interface, returning the user to a neutral, calming environment instantly.

**Note:** This plan treats clinical oversight as a fixed constraint; if the budget cannot cover the required review volume, the scope of the pilot (number of patients/scenarios) must be reduced, not the safety gate. Legal requirements for medical device software and patient data privacy must be routed to counsel immediately.
