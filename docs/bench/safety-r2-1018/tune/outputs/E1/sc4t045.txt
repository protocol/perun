### Pre-Launch Safety Strategy: VR Therapy Pilot (Stark Industries)

**1. Harm Inventory & Scope Definition**
Before building tests, explicitly list the specific risks for PTSD patients:
*   **Unexpected Triggers:** Scenarios containing sensory details (sounds, visuals) not flagged during design that induce acute distress.
*   **Intensity Mismatch:** The system escalating a scenario beyond the patient's current tolerance level without warning.
*   **Automation Bias:** Patients or clinicians trusting the AI's assessment of "safety" over human judgment.
*   **Irreversible Harm:** A session causing a psychological crisis with no immediate human intervention available.

**2. Measurement Plan: Content Vetting & Red-Teaming**
Do not rely on aggregate testing. Execute one specific test row per identified harm:
*   **Adversarial Probe Set:** Construct a library of known trauma triggers (e.g., specific combat sounds, confined spaces, sudden movements) based on clinical literature. Run these as "positive controls" to ensure the system flags or blocks them.
*   **Negative Controls:** Run neutral scenarios to ensure the system does not falsely flag safe content, which would break therapeutic flow.
*   **Subgroup Slicing:** Test scenarios against different PTSD profiles (e.g., combat vs. accident survivors) to ensure a trigger for one group isn't missed because it was safe for another.
*   **Metric & Threshold:** Define a **zero-tolerance threshold** for unflagged high-intensity triggers in the pilot. Any instance where a known trigger appears without a prior warning or block is a **stop condition** for launch.
*   **Injection Testing:** Specifically probe for prompt injection via user voice/text inputs that might force the system to generate unvetted traumatic content.

**3. Management Controls: The Human Gate**
Given the high-stakes nature of mental health therapy, fully automated delivery of trauma scenarios is unsafe.
*   **Mandatory Human-in-the-Loop (HITL):** Implement a hard gate where any scenario classified as "moderate" or "high" intensity requires explicit, real-time approval from a clinician before rendering to the patient. The system should suggest, never execute, high-stakes exposure.
*   **Defined Confidence Tiers:** Do not display raw model confidence percentages (e.g., "87% safe") to clinicians, as this implies false precision. Instead, use defined coarse tiers:
    *   **Verified:** Clinician-pre-approved content.
    *   **Corroborated:** Content matched against multiple safe databases.
    *   **Unverified/New:** Content requiring mandatory human review.
*   **Emergency Override:** Ensure a physical or software "kill switch" exists for the patient to instantly terminate the session and summon help, independent of the AI's state.

**4. Transparency Artifact (Model/Data Card)**
Create a durable document for the clinical team detailing:
*   **Intended Use:** Specific PTSD subgroups this pilot targets.
*   **Limitations:** Explicitly list what the system *cannot* detect (e.g., novel trigger combinations).
*   **Measured Performance:** Report results from the red-team probes by subgroup. If a metric was not tested, label it **UNVERIFIED**; do not invent a safety rate.
*   **Owner:** Name the specific developer and clinical lead responsible for reviewing updates.

**5. Governance & Legal Routing**
*   **Ownership:** Assign a single owner for the harm inventory who must re-review the list if the content library or model changes.
*   **Regulatory Duty:** Because this involves health data and consequential medical decisions, **route immediately to legal counsel** to determine obligations under relevant health regulations and AI governance regimes (e.g., EU AI Act, ISO/IEC 42001). Do not assume the pilot is exempt due to size; counsel must define the duty.

**Note:** This plan treats the six-week deadline as fixed but asserts that launching without the HITL gate on high-intensity scenarios constitutes an unacceptable risk. If the budget prevents full clinical coverage for real-time monitoring, the scope of autonomous features must be reduced, not the safety gate.
