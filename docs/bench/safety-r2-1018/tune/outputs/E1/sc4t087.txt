**Preparation Plan: Baptism Registration Gender Inference Review**

**1. Immediate Halt on Automated Inference (Day 1)**
Stop the system from auto-filling the "Gender" field based on parental names immediately. This logic is the source of the harm (bias and distress). Until a verified solution is in place, the field must remain blank by default, requiring manual entry by the administrator. Automated inference of sensitive attributes like gender from names is inherently unreliable and inappropriate for sacred rites where accuracy is paramount.

**2. Harm Inventory & Impact Assessment (Day 1-2)**
Document the specific failures:
*   **Harm:** Systematic misgendering of infants causing emotional distress to families and administrative rework.
*   **Affected Parties:** Parents, infants, and church administrators.
*   **Worst Case:** A family arrives for the ceremony to find their child incorrectly identified, causing public embarrassment and spiritual distress during a sacred event.
*   **Root Cause:** Reliance on heuristic name-pattern matching which fails across diverse cultural naming conventions.

**3. Implement Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Day 2-3)**
Since schema changes are difficult, implement a process control rather than a database fix:
*   **Mandatory Manual Confirmation:** The registration form must require the administrator to explicitly select or type the infant's gender. No pre-filled values from the inference engine are permitted.
*   **Removal of "Trust" Signals:** Do not display any "confidence score" or percentage next to the field. If the system previously suggested a value, remove that suggestion entirely to prevent automation bias (the tendency for humans to trust the machine's guess).

**4. Data Transparency & Disclosure (Day 3)**
*   **Internal Disclosure:** Inform all church administrators that the previous auto-fill feature was flawed and has been disabled. Provide a script for apologizing to any families already affected.
*   **User Interface Update:** Ensure the form clearly indicates that gender information is provided solely by the parent/administrator, not inferred by the system.

**5. Validation & Rollout (Day 4 - Pre-Holiday)**
*   **Test Case:** Run a set of diverse names (cross-cultural, non-binary associated, uncommon spellings) through the *old* logic to demonstrate failure rates to stakeholders (do not ship this logic).
*   **Go/No-Go Criteria:** The system ships only when the gender field is **never** auto-populated by the inference engine. The "pass" condition is 100% manual entry for this field.
*   **Owner:** Assign one team member to verify every batch of registrations before certificates are generated until the holiday rush stabilizes.

**6. Long-Term Recommendation (Post-Holiday)**
Plan to deprecate the inference logic entirely. For future iterations, if pre-filling is desired, it must be based on explicit parent input during an initial digital intake form, not inferred from names. Do not attempt to "fix" the name-matching algorithm; the risk of error is too high for this context.

**Note:** This plan prioritizes human verification over algorithmic speed to prevent harm during high-stakes religious ceremonies. Legal counsel should be consulted regarding data privacy implications of storing inferred sensitive attributes.
