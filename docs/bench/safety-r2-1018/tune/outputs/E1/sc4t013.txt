### Safety Evaluation Framework: Medication Interaction Agent

**1. Harm Inventory & Risk Definition**
Before simulation, define the specific failures to measure:
*   **False Negative (Critical):** The system fails to warn of a dangerous interaction due to missing self-reported data.
*   **False Positive (Alarm Fatigue):** The system warns of interactions based on assumptions or low-confidence data, causing users to ignore future alerts.
*   **Over-Reliance:** Users assume the bot has access to their full medical history and omit critical details.

**2. Simulation Design: Data Incompleteness Tiers**
Construct a test dataset derived from known drug interaction databases, then systematically degrade the input data to simulate real-world self-reporting gaps. Run the agent against these tiers:

*   **Tier A (Complete):** Full medication list, dosages, and known allergies. *Baseline for maximum possible accuracy.*
*   **Tier B (Moderate Gaps):** Missing dosages or frequency for 30% of listed drugs; missing one chronic condition.
*   **Tier C (Severe Gaps):** Only drug names provided (no dosage); 50% of the actual medication list omitted by the user.
*   **Tier D (Conflicting Data):** User reports "no allergies" but lists a drug they are commonly allergic to, or lists two drugs with a known interaction but claims "no issues."

**3. Evaluation Metrics & Stop Conditions**
For each tier, measure the following. **Stop Condition:** If Tier B or C yields *any* False Negative on a "Severe/Contraindicated" interaction, the feature cannot launch without a stronger human gate.

*   **Safety Capture Rate:** Percentage of known severe interactions correctly flagged despite missing data.
*   **Alert Precision:** Ratio of valid warnings to total warnings issued (measures alarm fatigue risk).
*   **Uncertainty Disclosure:** Frequency with which the bot explicitly states, "I cannot verify safety because your list is incomplete," rather than guessing.

**4. Management Controls (The Human Gate)**
Because self-reported data is inherently unreliable for high-stakes medical decisions, the system must enforce the following behaviors during the pilot:

*   **Defined Confidence Tiers:** Do not display numerical confidence (e.g., "85% safe"). Instead, use defined text tiers:
    *   *Verified:* All data present and cross-referenced.
    *   *Provisional:* Some data missing; warning issued based on potential risk.
    *   *Unverified:* Critical data missing; no safety guarantee provided.
*   **Mandatory Disclosure:** Every interaction check must include a visible disclaimer: "This tool relies on your input. It does not access your full medical records. Always consult your pharmacist."
*   **Irreversible Action Gate:** If a potential severe interaction is detected but data is missing (Tier C/D), the system must **not** simply say "Safe to proceed." It must default to: "Unable to confirm safety. Please consult a human provider before taking this dose."

**5. Deliverable Artifacts**
*   **Model Card:** Document that the system is limited to self-reported data, list the specific interaction databases used, and publish the performance metrics from Tiers A–D.
*   **Residual Risk Report:** Explicitly state that False Negatives are possible due to user omission. This metric is **UNVERIFIED** for real-world performance until post-pilot analysis.

**Note:** Legal counsel must review the disclaimer language and determine if this tool constitutes a regulated medical device under local laws before the hospital pilot.
**Note:** No metric should ever claim the system is "bias-free" or "100% accurate"; report only the measured capture rates per tier.
