**Pre-Launch Safety Plan: Bias Mitigation for Northwind Education Beta**

**1. Immediate Harm Inventory (Day 1)**
Stop guessing; name the specific risks. Create a one-page document listing:
*   **Harm:** Grammar corrections reinforcing regional stereotypes (e.g., correcting dialects to "standard" forms in a way that implies inferiority).
*   **Affected Users:** Learners from the specific regions flagged; users relying on cultural context notes.
*   **Worst Case:** Users feel alienated or mocked, leading to public backlash and loss of trust before full launch.
*   **Status:** This inventory is now your baseline. An empty list is not a pass; it is an unfilled requirement.

**2. Internal "Red-Team" Evaluation (Days 2–4)**
You cannot audit everything, so target the highest-risk inputs. With two engineers, execute a focused probe set:
*   **Construct Adversarial Inputs:** Write 50–100 test prompts specifically designed to trigger the suspected biases. Include sentences using dialects, slang, or cultural references from the regions of concern.
*   **Define Controls:**
    *   *Positive Control:* Inputs known to produce stereotypical outputs (based on your observations).
    *   *Negative Control:* Neutral inputs that should not trigger cultural corrections.
*   **Run the Sweep:** Execute these inputs against your current beta build. Do not look for "zero bias" (which is unprovable); look for the frequency and severity of the specific stereotypes you identified.
*   **Metric:** Count the number of outputs containing flagged stereotypes.
*   **Threshold:** Set a strict stop condition. Example: "If >5% of targeted probes produce stereotypical corrections, the feature requires a human gate or rollback for those regions."

**3. Manage Residual Risk with Human Gates (Day 5)**
Since you cannot retrain the model or afford external audits before the deadline, you must constrain the output:
*   **Uncertainty & Override:** If the system's confidence in a "cultural correction" is low, or if the input matches a high-risk pattern, suppress the automatic correction. Instead, display a neutral note: "Suggestions may vary by region," or simply provide the grammar fix without the cultural commentary.
*   **High-Stakes Gate:** Treat "cultural context" as a high-stakes output. If your automated tests flag a region as problematic, disable automated cultural suggestions for that region entirely until a human can review them. It is safer to show no cultural note than a harmful one.
*   **Disclosure:** Add a visible disclaimer in the beta: "Cultural insights are AI-generated and may not reflect all regional nuances. Please report inappropriate suggestions." This manages user expectation and turns users into sensors for remaining errors.

**4. Transparency Artifact (Day 6)**
Draft a lightweight "Model Card" for internal and beta-user visibility. It must state:
*   **Intended Use:** Grammar correction and general cultural context for language learning.
*   **Known Limitations:** Explicitly list that the system has known issues with specific regional dialects and may reinforce stereotypes.
*   **Measurement Status:** Mark the bias metric as **UNVERIFIED** for the general population, noting that only targeted internal probes were run. Do not invent a "99% fair" statistic.

**5. Ownership and Review**
Assign one engineer as the owner of this harm inventory. Schedule a re-review immediately after the beta term starts, using real user feedback to update the probe set.

**Notes**
*   This plan meets your deadline by focusing on targeted probing and output gating rather than full model retraining.
*   If legal questions arise regarding discriminatory outcomes in specific jurisdictions, route those immediately to counsel; this plan addresses product safety, not legal compliance.
