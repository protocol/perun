# Pre-Launch Wildlife Impact Assessment Plan

**1. Simulation Scenarios: Flight Parameters vs. Behavioral Response**
Before field deployment, run controlled simulations mapping drone variables to species-specific stress thresholds.
*   **Altitude Gradients:** Test flight paths at 50m, 100m, and 150m above canopy/ground level. Measure "flush distance" (distance at which animals flee) and "alert posture" duration for target species (e.g., nesting birds, large mammals).
*   **Speed & Trajectory:** Compare linear high-speed passes against hovering or slow-orbit patterns. Aggressive path optimization must be constrained by a "minimum disturbance corridor" that avoids known breeding dens, even if it reduces battery efficiency.
*   **Control Groups:** Include ground-based observer data to distinguish drone-induced stress from natural environmental triggers.

**2. Acoustic Signature Mitigation Guidelines**
Minimize the "predator mimicry" effect of drone noise through hardware and operational constraints.
*   **Frequency Tuning:** Prioritize propeller designs that shift noise frequencies outside the most sensitive hearing ranges of local endangered species (e.g., shifting away from ultrasonic bat ranges or low-frequency mammal rumbles).
*   **Operational Curtains:** Establish strict "quiet windows" during critical biological events (mating calls, feeding times, nocturnal activity). During these windows, autonomous patrols must pause or switch to passive, high-altitude gliding modes.
*   **Approach Vectors:** Program flight paths to approach animals from behind or above their blind spots where possible, avoiding direct head-on trajectories that trigger fight-or-flight responses.

**3. Post-Deployment Monitoring Strategy**
Implement a feedback loop to validate simulation data against real-world behavior.
*   **Baseline Comparison:** Utilize existing camera trap and GPS collar data to establish pre-deployment movement baselines. Compare post-deployment range usage, dwelling time, and migration paths against this baseline.
*   **Physiological Proxies:** Where feasible, use non-invasive methods (e.g., fecal glucocorticoid metabolite analysis) to measure stress hormone levels in areas with high drone traffic versus control zones.
*   **Adaptive Triggers:** Define clear "stop conditions." If a specific species shows a >15% reduction in habitat usage or a sustained increase in stress markers, the autonomous system must automatically geofence that area and alert human operators for review.

**4. Human-in-the-Loop Governance**
*   **Irreversible Action Gate:** While the system optimizes paths autonomously, any decision to enter a newly identified sensitive zone (e.g., a discovered nest) requires human confirmation. Full autonomy is suspended for high-stakes proximity events.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Publish a "Conservation Impact Card" detailing the intended use, tested altitude limits, known failure modes (e.g., "may disturb ground-nesting birds below 80m"), and the owner responsible for monitoring biological data.

**Notes**
*   All metrics regarding "safe" noise levels or "zero impact" must be treated as **UNVERIFIED** until empirical data from the monitoring strategy confirms them; do not assert safety claims prior to data collection.
*   Legal requirements regarding wildlife disturbance permits and protected species regulations must be routed to legal counsel; this plan addresses operational risk, not regulatory compliance.
