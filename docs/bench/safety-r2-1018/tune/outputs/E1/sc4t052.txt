**Immediate Deployment Plan & Testing Protocol**

Given the tight budget and immediate timeline, do not attempt to "verify" the bot is unbiased or perfect; instead, implement a **Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) gate** for all final distribution decisions. The bot must function as a *recommendation engine* only, requiring explicit coordinator confirmation before resources are dispatched. This prevents irreversible harm from sending aid to saturated zones while ignoring emerging crises.

**1. Harm Inventory & Mitigation**
*   **Bias/Unfair Outcomes:** The algorithm favors historically dense areas, risking neglect of less documented regions.
    *   *Mitigation:* Mandate a "blanket sweep" rule where coordinators must manually verify low-density zones every 4 hours, regardless of bot priority.
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** Outdated open-source maps may show roads as passable or populations as present when they are not.
    *   *Mitigation:* Label all bot outputs with a coarse uncertainty tier: **"Unverified – Based on Pre-Event Data."** Do not display confidence percentages (e.g., "87% sure"), as these imply precision you do not have.
*   **Over-Reliance:** Field coordinators may trust the bot's speed over local knowledge.
    *   *Mitigation:* Interface design must force the coordinator to input a "Ground Truth" check (e.g., a simple yes/no on road access) before accepting a route.

**2. Pre-Launch Testing Protocol (Runnable Simulations)**
Run these three specific simulations immediately using your team of eight. Do not ship until these stop conditions are met.

*   **Simulation A: The "Ghost Town" Probe**
    *   *Input:* A map where a high-density historical zone is marked as "evacuated/flooded" (negative control) and a low-density zone is marked as "newly stranded" (positive control).
    *   *Metric:* Does the bot recommend the new zone within the top 3 priorities after the coordinator inputs the evacuation status?
    *   *Stop Condition:* If the bot prioritizes the evacuated zone >2 times in 10 runs, **block launch**.
*   **Simulation B: The Stale Map Stress Test**
    *   *Input:* Inject conflicting data where open-source maps say "road open" but simulated field reports say "bridge out."
    *   *Metric:* Frequency of "Route Impossible" warnings generated by the bot vs. silent failures.
    *   *Stop Condition:* Any instance of the bot routing supplies to a known "bridge out" location without a warning triggers a **rollback**.
*   **Simulation C: Saturation Blindness**
    *   *Input:* A zone receiving continuous supply drops in the simulation.
    *   *Metric:* Does the bot lower the priority of this zone after 3 simulated successful drops?
    *   *Stop Condition:* If the bot continues to prioritize a saturated zone over an unsupplied one after 3 cycles, **block launch**.

**3. Required Variables for Dynamic Adaptation**
To verify adaptation, your simulation must vary these specific inputs:
*   **Temporal Decay:** How quickly does the weight of "historical density" decrease as time passes since the event?
*   **Feedback Latency:** The time delay between a coordinator marking a zone "served" and the bot updating its priority list.
*   **Conflict Resolution:** How the bot weighs "Map Data" vs. "Coordinator Input" (Coordinator input must always win).

**4. Transparency Artifact (Model Card Lite)**
Before deployment, publish a one-page document to all users stating:
*   **Intended Use:** Prioritization aid only; not an autonomous decision maker.
*   **Limitations:** Relies on outdated maps; does not account for real-time road closures unless manually entered.
*   **Owner:** Name the specific team lead responsible for reviewing failure logs daily.
