**Risk Assessment Plan: Automated Crime Reporting Pilot**

**1. Harm Inventory & Worst-Case Scenarios**
Before deployment, explicitly document these specific risks for the pilot:
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** The system invents crimes, suspects, or details not present in scanner data. *Worst Case:* Innocent individuals are publicly accused of non-existent crimes; community panic over fake incidents.
*   **Bias/Unfair Outcomes:** The model disproportionately flags specific neighborhoods or demographics based on biased training data or scanner language. *Worst Case:* Systematic reputational damage to specific communities; erosion of public trust.
*   **Unsafe High-Stakes Automation:** Publishing unverified accusations without human review. *Worst Case:* Irreversible reputational harm to named individuals before correction is possible.
*   **Missing AI Disclosure:** Readers cannot distinguish machine-generated reports from investigative journalism. *Worst Case:* Users attribute machine errors to editorial negligence, damaging the brand's credibility.

**2. Measurement Strategy (Do Not Assert Safety)**
Do not claim the system is "accurate" or "unbiased" without evidence. Instead, establish a runnable evaluation plan:
*   **Adversarial Testing:** Create a test set including "known-good" (verified records) and "known-bad" (inputs designed to trigger hallucinations or injection via scanner noise) cases.
*   **Metric Definition:** Define a pass threshold for "zero unverified accusations of specific individuals." Any failure here is a stop condition.
*   **Residual Risk Reporting:** If a metric cannot be measured yet (e.g., long-term bias), label it **UNVERIFIED**. Do not invent a confidence percentage (e.g., "99% accurate").

**3. Risk Management Controls**
Given the constraint of limited staffing, you cannot review *every* story, but you must gate the highest risks:
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate (The Floor):** Fully autonomous publication of stories naming specific individuals or alleging violent crimes is prohibited. These must trigger a mandatory human review queue. This is non-negotiable for irreversible reputational harm.
*   **Confidence Tiers, Not Percentages:** Do not display raw model confidence scores (e.g., "87% sure") to users. Instead, use defined coarse tiers internally to route content:
    *   *Confirmed:* Matches multiple public records (Auto-publish allowed for low-stakes stats only).
    *   *Single-Source:* Scanner data only (Requires human review if names/addresses are included).
    *   *Unverified:* Low confidence or conflicting data (Hold for investigation).
*   **Explicit Disclosure:** Clearly label all automated articles as "Generated by AI from public records" to manage user expectations and prevent over-reliance.
*   **Kill Switch & Reversibility:** Implement an immediate takedown mechanism for flagged stories and a clear correction protocol visible to readers.

**4. Governance & Transparency**
*   **Ownership:** Assign a specific editor or engineer as the owner for each harm category.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Publish a "Model Card" for this feature detailing its intended use (local crime summaries), limitations (prone to hallucination on names), and data sources. Absence of this card is a transparency failure.
*   **Legal Routing:** Consult counsel regarding legal duties for AI disclosure and automated decisions affecting reputation; do not assume compliance based on this plan alone.

**Note:** This plan identifies risks and controls; it does not certify the system as safe. The decision to ship rests with MetroMedia leadership based on the residual risk.
**Note:** Re-evaluate this inventory immediately if the data sources or model version changes.
