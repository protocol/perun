**Targeted Testing Protocol for Scaffolding Safety Bot**

**1. Harm Inventory & Risk Definition**
*   **Primary Harm:** False Negative (Unsafe Approval). The bot fails to detect instability in complex geometries, leading to scaffold collapse and worker injury/death.
*   **Affected Parties:** Construction crews, site managers, general public near sites.
*   **Worst Case:** Catastrophic structural failure during use due to automated approval of a flawed design.
*   **Constraint Acknowledgement:** Testing must maximize defect detection within the fixed compute budget; exhaustive simulation of all permutations is impossible.

**2. Test Design: "Adversarial Geometry" Suite**
Do not test random inputs. Construct a deterministic set of high-risk probes specifically targeting the known failure mode (complex geometric configurations).

*   **Probe Set A (Known Bad - Positive Controls):**
    *   Curate 20–30 historical scaffolding designs where collapses occurred or engineers flagged critical instability, specifically focusing on complex angles, cantilevers, and irregular bases.
    *   *Requirement:* These must be designs the current system *fails* to flag (if known) or are theoretically unstable but visually plausible.
    *   *Action:* Run these through the bot.
    *   *Pass Condition:* **Zero Tolerance.** The bot must flag *every* single one as "Unsafe/Review Required." If even one passes as "Safe," the feature cannot ship.

*   **Probe Set B (Known Good - Negative Controls):**
    *   Select 20 standard, code-compliant scaffolding designs verified by licensed structural engineers. Include simple and moderately complex but safe geometries.
    *   *Action:* Run these through the bot.
    *   *Pass Condition:* The bot must approve these as "Safe" with high confidence. Excessive false positives (flagging safe designs) will cause workflow paralysis and user distrust, leading to workarounds.

*   **Probe Set C (The Edge Case Sweep):**
    *   Generate variations of the "Known Bad" designs with minor perturbations (slight angle changes, load shifts) that stay within the unstable region.
    *   *Goal:* Ensure the detection boundary is not brittle.

**3. Execution Strategy Under Compute Constraints**
*   **Prioritize Depth over Breadth:** Do not simulate thousands of random designs. Dedicate 80% of the compute budget to deep, high-fidelity simulations of the **Probe Set A (Known Bad)** and **Probe Set C (Edge Cases)**.
*   **Staged Rollout Gate:**
    *   **Stage 1 (Internal):** Run the protocol above. If any "Known Bad" design is approved, halt integration.
    *   **Stage 2 (Human-in-the-Loop Mandate):** For the mandatory rollout, configure the system such that **any** design classified as "Complex Geometry" (or falling below a specific confidence threshold) is automatically routed to a human structural engineer for review. **Do not allow fully autonomous approval for complex configurations.**
    *   **Stage 3 (Monitoring):** Log every approval. Randomly sample 5% of "Approved" designs for immediate human re-verification post-deployment.

**4. Transparency & Disclosure Artifact**
Create a one-page "System Limitations Card" for site managers:
*   **Intended Use:** Preliminary screening of standard and moderately complex scaffolding.
*   **Known Limitation:** "This tool may miss instability patterns in highly irregular or novel geometric configurations. It is not a substitute for final human engineering sign-off on complex structures."
*   **Actionable Instruction:** "If the design involves non-standard angles or cantilevers, manual engineering review is required regardless of bot approval."

**5. Decision Rule**
*   **Stop Condition:** If the bot approves *any* design from Probe Set A, the rollout is blocked until the model is retrained or the human-gate logic is enforced for those specific geometry classes.
*   **Metric:** Report the "False Negative Rate on Adversarial Set" as **UNVERIFIED** until the test runs; do not claim "99% safe" without data.

**Note:** This protocol prioritizes preventing catastrophic failure over maximizing throughput. The human-in-the-loop gate for complex geometries is non-negotiable given the current detection gaps.
**Note:** Legal counsel must review the "Limitations Card" language before distribution to ensure it meets industry liability standards.
