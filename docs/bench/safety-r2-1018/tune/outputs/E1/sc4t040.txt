### Internal Assessment Checklist: Anti-Competitive Behavioral Patterns

**1. Parallel Price Movement Analysis**
*   **Pattern to Monitor:** The bot automatically matches competitor price increases within an unrealistically short window (e.g., seconds or minutes) across multiple regions without a corresponding change in your own supply chain costs.
*   **Test Action:** Simulate a competitor price hike in a controlled beta region. Measure the latency of your bot's response. If the adjustment is near-instantaneous and consistent across different competitors without internal cost justification, flag for review.
*   **Risk Signal:** Algorithmic tacit collusion, where bots learn to signal and match prices faster than human competitors could, potentially creating a supra-competitive equilibrium.

**2. Supply Chain Cost Decoupling**
*   **Pattern to Monitor:** Price increases occur despite stable or decreasing internal logistics, procurement, and inventory costs.
*   **Test Action:** Run historical data scenarios where competitor prices rise but your input costs remain flat. Verify if the bot raises prices solely based on the "market rate" (competitor data) rather than internal margin requirements.
*   **Risk Signal:** Pricing driven exclusively by competitor behavior rather than independent business judgment, a key element in antitrust investigations.

**3. Regional Price Uniformity (The "Hub" Effect)**
*   **Pattern to Monitor:** Identical price changes occurring simultaneously across geographically distinct regions with different local market conditions, driven by a centralized algorithm ingesting national competitor data.
*   **Test Action:** Isolate regional beta groups. Introduce localized competitor pricing variations. Ensure the bot allows for regional price divergence based on local elasticity and costs.
*   **Risk Signal:** Centralized algorithms acting as a "hub" that facilitates price coordination across otherwise independent market "spokes."

**4. Retaliatory Pricing Loops**
*   **Pattern to Monitor:** The bot engages in rapid, iterative price wars or punitive undercutting immediately following a competitor's discount, designed to discipline the competitor rather than maximize short-term volume.
*   **Test Action:** Introduce a temporary competitor discount. Observe if the bot over-corrects with aggressive undercutting that persists after the competitor reverts to normal pricing, or if it stabilizes based on your own margin floors.
*   **Risk Signal:** Algorithms designed to punish deviation from a high-price norm, deterring competitive discounting.

**5. Data Ingestion Boundaries**
*   **Pattern to Monitor:** The system ingests non-public, scraped, or indirectly obtained competitor data (e.g., via a third-party data aggregator that pools info from multiple retailers).
*   **Test Action:** Audit data sources. Confirm all competitor pricing data is derived from publicly available consumer-facing channels.
*   **Risk Signal:** Using shared third-party data services can inadvertently facilitate information exchange that leads to coordinated pricing.

**Critical Constraint Note:**
This checklist identifies technical behavioral patterns for internal testing; it **does not** constitute a legal audit or certify compliance with antitrust laws. Because your constraint prevents retaining antitrust counsel, you must treat any "Risk Signal" identified above as a **hard stop** for deployment in that specific area until legal advice is secured. Do not launch features exhibiting these patterns hoping to "fix them later," as regulatory penalties for algorithmic collusion can be severe.
