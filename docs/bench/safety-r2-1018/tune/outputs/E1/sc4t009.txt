### Plan to Respect Religious Observances in SyncTime Assistant

**1. Immediate Harm Inventory & Human Gate (Do Before Rollout)**
*   **Identify the Risk:** The specific harm is **bias/unfair outcome** (systematically disadvantaging specific faith groups) and **unsafe automation** (scheduling over irreversible personal commitments).
*   **The Floor Control:** Since you lack a comprehensive database and cannot verify 100% coverage in two weeks, you **must implement a human-in-the-loop gate** for the initial enterprise rollout. The assistant should *propose* times but require explicit user confirmation before sending invites, with a prominent disclaimer: *"AI suggestions may not account for all religious observances. Please verify against your personal calendar."* Do not enable fully autonomous scheduling for this feature yet.

**2. Testing Strategy (Measure with Limited Data)**
Do not claim the system is "bias-free." Instead, run targeted adversarial tests using the following probe sets:
*   **Positive Controls (Known Conflicts):** Manually curate a small list of major, fixed-date observances (e.g., Yom Kippur, Eid al-Fitr, Diwali, Good Friday) and variable lunar calculations if your logic supports them. Input these as "do-not-disturb" blocks in test profiles and verify the assistant *never* suggests them.
*   **Negative Controls (Safe Times):** Input standard working hours with no restrictions; ensure the assistant schedules normally.
*   **Edge-Case Probes:** Test ambiguous inputs like "I need off for prayer" or "religious holiday" without specific dates to see if the assistant asks clarifying questions rather than guessing.
*   **Metric & Stop Condition:** Define a **zero-tolerance threshold** for known positive controls. If the assistant suggests a meeting during a hardcoded test observance, **block the launch** until the logic is patched. Report results as "Pass/Fail on known set," not as a percentage of total global safety.

**3. Feedback Framework for Continuous Improvement**
*   **In-Product Reporting:** Add a one-click flag on every scheduled meeting: *"Does this time conflict with a religious observance?"* If clicked, trigger an immediate apology workflow and a manual review queue.
*   **Structured Data Collection:** Create a secure, anonymized form for users to submit missing observances (Date, Faith, Reason, Recurrence Rule).
*   **Review Cadence:** Assign one team member to review flagged incidents weekly. Update the internal "blocklist" logic based on verified user submissions.

**4. Transparency Artifact (Model Card Lite)**
Before rollout, publish a brief internal note to customers:
*   **Intended Use:** Scheduling assistance for general business hours.
*   **Limitations:** "Current version relies on user-inputted restrictions and a limited set of major holidays. It does not yet possess a comprehensive global religious database."
*   **Owner:** [Name of Product Lead].
*   **Status:** **UNVERIFIED** for comprehensive global coverage; relying on user confirmation.

**5. Governance**
*   **Owner:** Assign a specific product owner responsible for this harm vector.
*   **Re-review Trigger:** Any update to the scheduling algorithm or addition of new holiday data requires re-running the positive control tests.

**Note:** Legal requirements for accommodating religious practices vary by region; route specific compliance questions to counsel. Do not fabricate a "safety score"; clearly communicate the reliance on user verification.
