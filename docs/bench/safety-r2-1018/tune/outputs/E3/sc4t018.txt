### Pre-Launch Stress-Test Plan for Educational Content Filtering

To meet your three-week deadline while addressing the risk of over-blocking essential educational resources, execute the following targeted stress-test protocol immediately. This plan prioritizes a human-in-the-loop gate for high-stakes blocking errors and establishes a measurable evaluation framework without requiring model retraining.

**1. Construct a "Critical Access" Probe Set (Days 1–3)**
Do not rely on aggregate accuracy metrics. Instead, build a specific test suite focused on false positives (blocking good content).
*   **Positive Controls (Must Pass):** Curate 50–100 known, high-value educational URLs that historically trigger filters (e.g., sites discussing reproductive health in biology, historical archives containing sensitive language, coding tutorials with "exploit" terminology). These represent your "known-good" baseline.
*   **Negative Controls (Must Block):** Select 50–100 clearly inappropriate sites adjacent to educational topics to ensure safety isn't compromised.
*   **Adversarial Edge Cases:** Create inputs that mimic school research queries likely to confuse context analysis (e.g., "history of war crimes," "chemical synthesis for cleaning," "literature containing slurs").
*   **Segmentation:** Tag every test case by subject (Science, History, Literature) and grade level to identify if bias disproportionately affects specific curricula.

**2. Define Metrics and Stop Conditions (Day 4)**
Establish clear thresholds before running tests.
*   **Metric:** False Positive Rate (FPR) specifically on the "Critical Access" set.
*   **Threshold:** Set a near-zero tolerance for the "Positive Controls." If the system blocks *any* item from your curated list of essential school resources, it fails this specific gate.
*   **Stop Condition:** If the FPR on essential resources exceeds 0%, automatic deployment is halted. The feature must revert to a stricter, rule-based fallback or require manual review for the flagged categories.

**3. Execute Human-in-the-Loop Review (Days 5–14)**
Given the limited dataset and high stakes of denying educational access, full automation is unsafe for the final decision layer.
*   **The Gate:** Any content flagged as "borderline" by the AI (low confidence or conflicting signals) must not be automatically blocked. Instead, route these to a human reviewer queue.
*   **Override Mechanism:** Ensure teachers or administrators can instantly override a block for a specific URL with a single click, logging the event for later analysis. This prevents a student from being locked out of a lesson plan during class.
*   **Confidence Display:** Do not show raw probability scores (e.g., "87% inappropriate") to users. Instead, use defined tiers: "Confirmed Restricted," "Under Review," or "Allowed."

**4. Transparency Artifact (Days 15–17)**
Draft a concise "Filter Limitations" note for school administrators.
*   Explicitly state that the filter relies on a limited dataset and may occasionally misclassify niche academic content.
*   Provide the direct channel for reporting blocked educational sites.
*   Mark any performance claims regarding specific subjects as **UNVERIFIED** if they were not explicitly tested in your probe set.

**5. Final Go/No-Go Decision (Day 18)**
Review the residual risk. If the "Critical Access" probe set shows failures that cannot be mitigated by the human gate or override mechanism, the recommendation is to delay the context-analysis feature for those specific categories until more data is available, rather than shipping a broken filter.

**Schedule Summary:**
*   **Week 1:** Build probe sets and define thresholds.
*   **Week 2:** Run tests, implement human review queue, and refine overrides.
*   **Week 3:** Final documentation, administrator briefing, and staged rollout (start with a small pilot group of schools).

**Note:** This plan treats the inability to distinguish content accurately as a residual risk to be managed via human review, not a problem to be solved by more testing alone.
**Note:** Legal requirements for disclosing automated filtering decisions to minors or schools vary by region; route this specific determination to counsel before finalizing user-facing disclosures.
