### Safety Evaluation Plan: FitPulse Chest Pain Advisor

**1. Harm Inventory & Worst-Case Scenarios**
Before testing, explicitly record the primary risk: **False Negative Diagnosis**.
*   **Harm:** The system advises "muscle fatigue" when the user is experiencing a cardiac event (e.g., angina, heart attack).
*   **Affected Group:** Highest risk for demographics missing from your training data (women, older adults, non-athletes, individuals with pre-existing conditions).
*   **Worst Realistic Case:** A user ignores severe chest pain based on device advice, delays seeking emergency care, and suffers permanent injury or death.
*   **Secondary Harm:** **Over-reliance**. Users may trust the device's "all clear" over their own intuition or standard medical advice.

**2. Measurement Strategy: Adversarial & Edge-Case Testing**
Since you cannot measure accuracy across missing demographics due to data gaps, you must measure **failure modes** and **conservatism**. Do not claim an accuracy rate for groups not in your dataset; mark these metrics as `UNVERIFIED`.

*   **Test Design:** Construct a "Red Team" probe set containing known cardiac event patterns that mimic muscle fatigue (e.g., atypical heart rate variability, specific pain radiation descriptions).
    *   *Positive Controls:* Inputs representing clear cardiac events (from public medical datasets). The system **must** flag these as serious.
    *   *Negative Controls:* Inputs representing pure muscle strain.
    *   *Edge Cases:* Inputs from demographic profiles absent in your training data (synthetic or borrowed from literature) showing atypical cardiac symptoms.
*   **Pass Threshold:** Zero tolerance for false negatives on cardiac event probes. If the model classifies a known cardiac pattern as "muscle fatigue," the test fails.
*   **Stop Condition:** Immediate halt to beta expansion if *any* probe representing a cardiac event is dismissed as non-critical.

**3. Risk Management & Guardrails**
Given the high-stakes nature of medical advice and the identified data gaps, full automation is unsafe. Implement the following floors:

*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate (The "Uncertainty" Gate):** The device must never autonomously declare "Muscle Fatigue" with high confidence if the input signals fall outside the distribution of your training data (e.g., user demographics not represented). In these cases, the output must default to: *"We cannot verify the cause of this pain. Please seek immediate medical attention."*
*   **Defined Confidence Tiers:** Do not display numerical probabilities (e.g., "90% likely muscle strain"). Use coarse, defined tiers:
    *   *Consistent with Strain:* Only shown when signals perfectly match known safe patterns in your specific dataset.
    *   *Unverified / Seek Care:* The default for any ambiguity, demographic mismatch, or atypical signal.
*   **Mandatory Disclosure:** Every output must include a clear disclaimer: *"This advice is generated by an AI trained on limited data (primarily young males). It is not a medical diagnosis. If you have chest pain, always consult a doctor."*

**4. Governance & Transparency Artifacts**
*   **Model Card:** Publish a versioned document detailing the "Intended Use" (fitness context only, not emergency diagnosis) and "Limitations" (explicitly listing the missing demographics).
*   **Owner Assignment:** Name one team member responsible for reviewing any failure reports from the beta. Re-evaluate the model before adding any new demographic data.

**Note:** Because this feature provides high-stakes health guidance, you must route the question of legal disclosure requirements and regulatory classification (e.g., SaMD) to legal counsel immediately; do not rely on internal assumptions.
**Note:** Do not expand access beyond the current beta until the "Zero False Negative" threshold is met on your adversarial test set.
