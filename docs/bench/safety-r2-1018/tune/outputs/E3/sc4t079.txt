**Blind Spot Alert: Structural Safety vs. Algorithmic Optimization**

Your request identifies a critical failure mode: an algorithm optimizing for energy efficiency could mathematically converge on a panel configuration that exceeds the structural load limits of specific roof types, leading to collapse. Because you are relying heavily on simulation due to budget constraints, your "simulation" must explicitly model structural failure, not just energy gain. An empty inventory of structural risks is not a pass; it is an unfilled requirement.

**1. Harm Inventory (Map)**
Before finalizing the algorithm, explicitly name the worst-case scenarios for your users (homeowners and contractors):
*   **Unsafe Automation of High-Stakes Actions:** The system recommends a panel layout that causes roof structural failure under snow or wind load because the optimizer prioritized surface area coverage over weight distribution.
*   **Hallucination Surfaced as Fact:** The system presents a placement plan as "structurally verified" when it has only been validated against ideal conditions, not edge-case weather events or degraded roofing materials.
*   **Over-Reliance:** Contractors trust the "optimal" energy score without realizing the system lacks a hard constraint for local building codes or specific roof age factors.

**2. Measurement Plan (Measure)**
You cannot certify the system is "safe," but you can measure residual risk through adversarial simulation. Since physical testing is limited, your digital twin must be aggressive.
*   **Test the Outputs, Not Just the Code:** Do not just test if the code runs; test if the *recommendations* break roofs.
*   **Runnable Test Rows:** Create specific simulation probes for each identified harm:
    *   *Probe Set:* Input roof geometries known to be weak (e.g., old truss systems, flat roofs with poor drainage) combined with extreme weather data (maximum historical snow load, hurricane-force wind).
    *   *Controls:* Include a "known-bad" input where a human expert knows the roof will fail if panels are added, and a "known-good" input where the roof is over-engineered.
    *   *Metric:* The percentage of recommendations that exceed the calculated load-bearing capacity of the input roof model.
    *   *Stop Condition:* **Zero tolerance.** If the simulator predicts *any* configuration that exceeds structural limits without a prominent warning, the feature cannot ship.
    *   *Owner:* Assign a structural engineer to review every simulation failure, not just an software engineer.
*   **Report Residual Risk:** If a specific roof type (e.g., thatched or specific historic slate) cannot be accurately simulated, mark recommendations for those types as **UNVERIFIED**. Do not invent a safety percentage. The output must state: "Structural integrity for this roof type is unverified by simulation; professional on-site assessment required."

**3. Risk Management (Manage)**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate:** This is a high-stakes, irreversible action (potential property damage/injury). The system must **not** auto-approve installations. Every recommendation must require explicit confirmation by a qualified human (contractor or engineer) who reviews the load calculations. The default state is "Pending Review," not "Approved."
*   **Show Uncertainty:** Do not display a single "Efficiency Score." Display a **Structural Confidence Tier** (e.g., "Verified," "Corroborated by Simulation," "Unverified – Manual Check Required"). Define these tiers clearly so users understand that "Unverified" does not mean "Safe," it means "Unknown."
*   **Disclosure:** Clearly disclose to the homeowner and contractor that the placement is an *algorithmic recommendation* based on simulations, not a guaranteed engineering stamp of approval.

**4. Governance**
Name the owner responsible for the structural safety of the output (likely your lead structural expert). Any change to the optimization algorithm, the weather data models, or the supported roof types triggers an immediate re-review of the harm inventory and simulation suite.

**Deliverable Summary:**
Implement a "Structural Fail-Safe" layer in your simulator that treats any load-exceeding recommendation as a critical error, not an optimization opportunity. Mandate human verification for all outputs and label unverified roof types clearly.

*Note: Legal requirements for engineering liability and automated decision disclosures in construction vary by jurisdiction; route these specific duty questions to legal counsel.*
*Physical load testing, even if limited, should prioritize validating the simulation model itself rather than just the final output.*
