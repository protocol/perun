**Pre-Launch Safety Plan for Northwind Education Beta**

**1. Harm Inventory (Immediate Mapping)**
Before touching code, document the specific risk: the grammar engine may reinforce regional stereotypes. Define the affected groups (users from or learning about specific regions) and the worst-case impact (alienating users, perpetuating harmful cultural narratives, reputational damage). An empty inventory is not a pass; write down exactly which regions and stereotype types are suspected based on your team's observations.

**2. Internal Measurement Strategy (Two-Engineer Workflow)**
Do not attempt to prove the system is "unbiased"; instead, measure specific failure modes using your existing resources.
*   **Build Targeted Eval Sets:** Create a small, high-impact dataset of 50–100 prompts specifically designed to trigger the observed stereotypes. Include "positive controls" (inputs known to produce biased outputs based on your observations) and "negative controls" (neutral inputs that should not trigger bias).
*   **Run Adversarial Probes:** Have your two engineers manually run these prompts through the beta engine. Record every instance where the output reinforces a stereotype.
*   **Define Stop Conditions:** Set a strict threshold. For example: "If more than 5% of the targeted probe set produces stereotypical content, the feature affecting those regions must be disabled or gated." Do not invent a "safe" percentage; if the metric is unmeasured for a specific region, mark it as **UNVERIFIED**.

**3. Risk Management & Mitigation**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop for High-Stakes Context:** If the app provides cultural context (which carries higher risk of stereotyping than simple grammar), consider disabling fully automated cultural explanations for the beta. Instead, flag these outputs for human review or display them with a clear uncertainty indicator (e.g., "Cultural context suggestions may vary; verify with local sources").
*   **Surface Uncertainty:** Do not present corrections as absolute facts if the confidence is low. Use coarse tiers like "Confirmed" vs. "Suggested" rather than precise percentages (e.g., avoid "87% accurate"), which imply a calibration you have not verified.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Draft a lightweight "Model Card" for the beta. Explicitly state:
    *   **Intended Use:** Grammar correction and cultural hints for language learners.
    *   **Known Limitations:** "The model may reinforce regional stereotypes; outputs regarding cultural context are experimental."
    *   **Measured Performance:** List the results of your internal probe test (e.g., "Tested against 50 stereotypical triggers; 3 failures detected in Region X").
    *   **Owner:** Name the specific engineer responsible for this feature.

**4. Governance & Launch Decision**
Assign one engineer as the owner of this risk. If the internal testing reveals systematic bias that cannot be fixed before the school term, the plan must include a fallback: disable the "cultural context" feature entirely for the affected regions while keeping grammar correction active. The decision to ship with known, unmitigated bias rests with your leadership, not the engineering team.

**Note:** This plan identifies and measures risk but does not certify the system as safe or unbiased. If legal questions arise regarding disclosure requirements for automated decisions, route those specifically to legal counsel.
