**Equity Impact Testing Plan for Route Cancellation Engine**

Given the hard deadline of next Friday and limited data granularity, we cannot certify the system as "unbiased" or guarantee no demographic harm. Instead, we will execute a targeted risk assessment to identify disproportionate impacts on vulnerable populations and implement a mandatory human gate for high-stakes cancellations.

**1. Harm Inventory & Worst-Case Definition**
*   **Harm:** Systematic exclusion of specific neighborhoods from night transit due to broad incident tagging.
*   **Affected Groups:** Night-shift workers, low-income commuters, and residents in neighborhoods with coarse crime data tags.
*   **Worst-Case Scenario:** A cluster of minor incidents in a specific area triggers a blanket cancellation of the only late-night route for that zone, stranding vulnerable workers with no alternative transport, while similar incident clusters in other areas do not trigger cancellation due to data noise.

**2. Runnable Evaluation Plan (Simulated)**
We will construct a synthetic test suite using adversarial prompts and edge-case inputs to probe the cancellation logic. Each test row includes a probe set, a metric, and a stop condition.

*   **Test Row A: The "Broad Tag" Stress Test**
    *   **Probe Set:** Inject identical incident reports (e.g., "minor disturbance") into different neighborhood tags (high-income vs. low-income proxies) using the available broad granularity. Include a known-bad positive control (severe safety threat) and a known-good negative control (no incidents).
    *   **Metric:** Cancellation rate disparity between neighborhood tags for identical incident severity.
    *   **Threshold:** Owner-set tolerance (propose zero tolerance for disparate cancellation rates on identical inputs).
    *   **Stop Condition:** If cancellation rates vary by >5% across demographic proxies for identical inputs, block launch for logic review.

*   **Test Row B: The "Vulnerable Cluster" Probe**
    *   **Probe Set:** Simulate a sequence of low-severity incidents concentrated in a single neighborhood serving a known night-shift hub.
    *   **Metric:** Frequency of full route suspension vs. partial delay or rerouting.
    *   **Threshold:** Any full suspension triggered solely by low-severity, high-volume noise without human review.
    *   **Stop Condition:** Automatic rollback of the autonomous cancellation feature if full suspensions occur without human confirmation.

*   **Test Row C: Data Leakage & Injection Check**
    *   **Probe Set:** Input malformed incident reports or data from adjacent zones to test if the engine incorrectly attributes risk to the wrong neighborhood tag.
    *   **Metric:** Rate of incorrect neighborhood attribution leading to cancellation.
    *   **Stop Condition:** Any confirmed misattribution causing a route cancel triggers a manual review gate.

**3. Risk Management Controls**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate:** Because canceling a route is a high-stakes, hard-to-reverse action that strands users, **fully autonomous cancellation is disabled by default.** All cancellation decisions generated by the engine must pass through a human operator or require explicit, real-time confirmation before execution. This is non-negotiable regardless of the deadline.
*   **Uncertainty Reporting:** The system must surface its decision confidence as a defined coarse tier (e.g., "Confirmed Threat," "Unverified Report") rather than a precise percentage. Operators must see the data limitation (broad neighborhood tags) explicitly flagged.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** A Model Card must be drafted before launch, documenting the intended use, the limitation of broad neighborhood tags, the results of the above simulations, and the owner of the cancellation logic. Any metric not measured in this sprint must be marked "UNVERIFIED."

**4. Residual Risk Statement**
Due to the lack of granular historical crime data, we cannot verify if the broad tags themselves encode historical biases. The residual risk of disparate impact remains **UNVERIFIED**. The proposed testing plan isolates logic errors, but not data-origin bias. The human-in-the-loop gate is the primary control mitigating this unknown.
