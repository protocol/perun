# Pre-Launch Impact Assessment Plan: Wildlife Conservation Drone System

## 1. Harm Inventory & Simulation Scenarios
Before deployment, we must define the specific behavioral disruptions risked by autonomous patrols. The primary harms are **habitat displacement** (animals fleeing breeding grounds due to noise/movement) and **chronic stress** (altered feeding or mating patterns).

**Simulation Scenarios:**
*   **Altitude Sweep:** Simulate flight paths at 50m, 100m, and 150m above ground level (AGL) against known species sensitivity profiles.
    *   *Metric:* Estimated decibel exposure at ground level vs. species-specific disturbance thresholds.
    *   *Stop Condition:* Any scenario where predicted noise exceeds the "flight initiation distance" trigger for endangered species in the zone.
*   **Speed & Trajectory:** Model aggressive path optimization (sharp turns, rapid acceleration) vs. smooth, gliding patterns.
    *   *Probe Set:* Use historical animal movement data as a negative control (no drone) and simulated high-speed drone passes as a positive control (known disturbance).
    *   *Threshold:* Zero tolerance for predicted stampede events or abandonment of active nests.

## 2. Acoustic Minimization Guidelines
To manage the risk of acoustic disruption, the following operational constraints are proposed as immediate controls:
*   **Dynamic Altitude Flooring:** The autonomy engine must enforce a hard minimum altitude (e.g., >120m AGL) over mapped breeding zones, overriding path optimization if battery constraints threaten return-to-base safety.
*   **Speed Capping:** Limit maximum velocity in sensitive sectors to reduce propeller pitch noise, even if it increases patrol time.
*   **Flight Path Smoothing:** Disable aggressive waypoint cutting in favor of continuous curvature paths to minimize sudden acoustic spikes.
*   **Temporal Restrictions:** Prohibit autonomous patrols during known peak breeding or nursing hours unless a human operator explicitly overrides the gate.

## 3. Monitoring Strategy & Human-in-the-Loop Gate
Because the action of patrolling is potentially irreversible (displaced animals may not return), a **human-in-the-loop gate** is required for the initial phase.

*   **Pre-Deployment Validation:** Conduct physical test flights with bio-acoustic sensors and camera traps in non-critical areas to validate simulation models. Do not rely solely on algorithmic predictions.
*   **Post-Deployment Tracking:**
    *   Deploy passive acoustic monitors and motion-sensor cameras in patrol zones to track animal presence pre- and post-flight.
    *   **Rollback Trigger:** If monitoring detects a >10% reduction in species presence or signs of distress behaviors within 48 hours of deployment, the autonomous system must automatically suspend operations in that sector pending human review.
*   **Uncertainty Disclosure:** Operational dashboards must display confidence in "safe passage" as a coarse tier (e.g., "Verified Safe," "Unverified – Human Review Required") rather than a precise percentage, preventing operators from over-trusting the algorithm's risk assessment.

## 4. Transparency Artifact (Draft Model Card)
A durable record must accompany this feature:
*   **Intended Use:** Poaching detection in protected areas; **Out of Scope:** Dense nesting zones during peak season without human override.
*   **Limitations:** Acoustic models are based on limited battery-constrained flight profiles; real-world wind conditions may alter noise propagation.
*   **Measured Performance:** To be populated only after physical validation flights; currently **UNVERIFIED**.
*   **Owner:** [Name/Role of Lead Ecologist or System Owner].

**Note:** Legal requirements regarding automated environmental impact disclosures vary by jurisdiction; route specific regulatory duties to counsel. This plan measures residual risk but does not certify the system as "harm-free."
