**Pre-Launch Safety Workflow for Northwind Education**

**1. Harm Inventory & Scope Definition**
Immediately document the specific risks: (a) **Historical Hallucination** (fabricated events presented as fact), (b) **Cultural Bias** (offensive language or stereotyping), and (c) **Over-Reliance** (teachers accepting generated text without verification). Define the "worst case" for each: a student learning false history or a community being offended by school-sanctioned material. This inventory is your baseline; an empty list is not a pass.

**2. Targeted Output Evaluation (The "Measure" Phase)**
Do not attempt to manually check every page. Instead, build a runnable test suite focused on your highest risks:
*   **Probe Sets:** Create 20–30 specific "adversarial" prompts targeting known sensitive historical topics and cultural groups where you previously found errors. Include "known-bad" inputs (prompts likely to trigger bias) and "known-good" controls (standard, safe prompts).
*   **Metric & Threshold:** Define a pass/fail criterion. For high-stakes errors (offensive language, major historical falsehoods), set a **zero-tolerance threshold**. If any probe in the sensitive set triggers a failure, the model version fails the gate.
*   **Execution:** Run these probes against the generator. Report results as **UNVERIFIED** for any area not explicitly tested. Do not invent an accuracy percentage (e.g., "99% accurate") without this data.

**3. Human-in-the-Loop Gate for High-Stakes Content**
Since you cannot manually review every page, you must gate the *distribution*, not just the generation:
*   **Irreversible Action Control:** Treat the final "Publish to District" action as irreversible. Implement a mandatory human review step for any content flagged by your automated probes or containing keywords related to sensitive cultural/historical topics.
*   **Uncertainty Display:** Modify the output interface to show a **defined confidence tier** (e.g., "Verified," "Needs Review," "Unverified") rather than a raw probability score. Clearly label the material as "AI-Generated Draft" to prevent automation bias, ensuring teachers know they must validate facts before classroom use.

**4. Transparency Artifact (Model Card)**
Create a one-page **Model Card** to accompany the release. This document must state:
*   **Intended Use:** Drafting supplementary history materials for middle schoolers.
*   **Limitations:** Explicitly list known gaps (e.g., "May hallucinate specific dates," "Requires human verification for cultural narratives").
*   **Measured Performance:** Summarize the results of your probe tests. If a metric wasn't measured, mark it **UNVERIFIED**.
*   **Owner:** Name the specific team member responsible for monitoring feedback post-launch.

**5. Staged Rollout & Kill Switch**
Given the three-week deadline and small team:
*   **Pilot First:** Release the tool to only *one* partner district or a single pilot group of teachers initially. Cap the exposure.
*   **Feedback Loop:** Establish a direct channel for teachers to flag errors.
*   **Rollback Plan:** Define the specific condition that triggers an immediate halt to distribution (e.g., "Report of offensive language in live material").

**6. Governance & Legal Routing**
Assign one team member as the owner of this safety workflow. Regarding the requirement for AI disclosure or whether this constitutes a regulated automated decision: **Route this specific legal determination to counsel**. Do not assert whether you are legally required to disclose AI usage; instead, recommend disclosure as a safety control while legal verifies the duty.

**Notes**
*   This plan prioritizes gating the highest risks (bias/hallucination) over exhaustive review, fitting your resource constraints.
*   Never certify the system as "safe" or "unbiased"; only report the residual risk remaining after these controls.
