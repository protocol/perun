**Risk Assessment Plan: Automated Local Crime Reporting**

**1. Harm Inventory & Worst-Case Scenarios**
Before deployment, explicitly document these specific risks for the crime-reporting feature:
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** The system invents crimes, suspects, or victims. *Worst Case:* An innocent resident is publicly accused of a felony they did not commit, leading to reputational destruction and potential legal liability.
*   **Bias/Unfair Outcomes:** The model disproportionately flags specific neighborhoods or demographics based on skewed scanner data. *Worst Case:* Systemic stigmatization of a community, eroding public trust.
*   **Over-Reliance:** Readers assume every published story is vetted truth. *Worst Case:* Panic spreads based on a fabricated active shooter or disaster report.
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Users cannot distinguish between human-reported and machine-generated news. *Worst Case:* Loss of brand credibility when errors are inevitably discovered.

**2. Measurement Strategy (Runnable Tests)**
Do not assert safety; measure it. Establish a test suite with one row per harm:
*   **Probe Set:** Create adversarial inputs using garbled scanner audio transcripts, conflicting public records, and known "negative controls" (dates/locations with zero crime). Include "positive controls" where the system must correctly identify a real, verified event.
*   **Metrics & Thresholds:** Define a "Fabrication Rate" (claims made without source evidence). Set the threshold for any claim involving a named individual to **zero tolerance**. If the system generates a name without a matching public record ID, the test fails.
*   **Stop Condition:** Any instance of naming an innocent person as a suspect in a test run triggers an immediate launch block.
*   **Residual Risk Reporting:** If you cannot measure the hallucination rate on named entities, label it **UNVERIFIED**. Do not publish a "99% accurate" claim without this data.

**3. Risk Management Controls**
Given the constraint of limited staffing, you cannot remove the human gate for high-stakes outputs; you must narrow the scope of automation.
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Non-Negotiable):** Fully autonomous publication of stories naming specific individuals or alleging violent crimes is unsafe. Implement a rule: **If a generated draft contains a proper name (suspect/victim) or alleges a violent felony, it must route to a human queue for confirmation before publishing.** Only "blotter-style" summaries (e.g., "Police responded to a noise complaint at [Address]") with no named individuals may publish automatically.
*   **Uncertainty Tiers:** Do not display model confidence percentages (e.g., "87% sure"). Instead, use defined coarse tiers in the editorial dashboard: *Confirmed* (multiple sources match), *Single-Source* (scanner only), or *Unverified*. Stories marked *Unverified* cannot publish.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Publish a "Model Card" on your site explaining that these reports are automated, listing known limitations (e.g., "may contain errors in names until verified"), and providing a prominent "Report an Error" button.
*   **Disclosure:** Clearly label automated articles with "Generated by AI" near the headline. Whether this is legally required is a determination for your legal counsel; however, as a control against deception, it is mandatory here.

**4. Governance**
Assign a specific owner (Editor-in-Chief or Lead Engineer) responsible for reviewing the harm inventory and test results weekly. If the data sources or model version changes, re-run the evaluation suite immediately.

**Note:** This plan treats the naming of individuals as a high-stakes action requiring human review; bypassing this for speed creates unacceptable liability. Legal counsel must review disclosure requirements for your specific jurisdiction.
