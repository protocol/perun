**Immediate Halt and Human Gate**
Do not schedule customer appointments or deploy the automated measurement feature for live installation until a human-in-the-loop gate is established. Because the current failure mode involves glass shattering and potential injury, this is a high-stakes, irreversible action. The default control is mandatory human verification of all measurements before any glass is cut or installed. Full autonomous execution of this action is the failure this plan prevents; no software simulation can replace this physical safety floor given the known miscalculation rate.

**Harm Inventory and Worst-Case Scenarios**
- **Physical Injury and Property Damage:** The algorithm miscalculates dimensions, causing glass to shatter during fitting. Worst case: Technician suffers lacerations or eye injury; homeowner property is damaged.
- **Over-reliance / Automation Bias:** Technicians trust the AR overlay despite known instability, skipping manual tape verification. Worst case: Systemic injury across the team due to blind trust in the tool.
- **Unsafe Automation:** The system authorizes glass cutting based on unverified digital data. Worst case: Irreversible material loss and safety incidents before a human can intervene.

**Simulation and Stress-Test Plan (Software Only)**
Since hardware upgrades are impossible this quarter, validation must occur entirely in a simulated environment before any field use. Execute the following test rows against the calculation engine:

1.  **Probe Set: Geometric Edge Cases**
    *   **Inputs:** Non-rectangular shapes (arches, circles), warped frames, and extreme aspect ratios generated in software. Include "known-bad" positive controls (dimensions guaranteed to fail physical fit) and "known-good" negative controls (perfect standard squares).
    *   **Metric:** Percentage of simulations where the calculated area deviates >2% from ground truth.
    *   **Threshold:** Zero tolerance for deviations that would cause physical interference (glass too large).
    *   **Stop Condition:** If any positive control passes (i.e., the system approves a dimension that should fail), block deployment immediately.

2.  **Probe Set: Environmental Noise and Lighting**
    *   **Inputs:** Simulated AR video feeds with varying lighting (glare, low light, shadows) and visual obstructions (dirty glass, reflections).
    *   **Metric:** Stability of dimension output across 1,000 iterations per lighting condition.
    *   **Threshold:** Output variance must remain within safety margins for cutting.
    *   **Stop Condition:** If variance exceeds the physical tolerance of the glass material, the feature remains disabled.

3.  **Probe Set: Injection and Sensor Drift**
    *   **Inputs:** Corrupted sensor data streams and adversarial patterns designed to confuse edge detection.
    *   **Metric:** System ability to detect invalid input and refuse to calculate rather than outputting a wrong number.
    *   **Threshold:** 100% refusal rate on corrupted inputs.
    *   **Stop Condition:** Any instance of the system outputting a confident but false measurement on corrupted data.

**Residual Risk and Transparency**
Report all test results as **UNVERIFIED** for real-world safety until physical pilot testing with scrap material confirms simulation accuracy. Do not fabricate a "safe" percentage or claim the system is unbiased. Create a **Model Card** documenting these limitations, the specific lighting conditions where the model fails, and the explicit instruction that the tool is for estimation only, not final cutting authorization.

**Confidence and Disclosure**
Surface uncertainty to the technician using defined coarse tiers (e.g., "Unverified – Manual Check Required") rather than precise percentages. If the simulation confidence is low, the interface must hard-block the "Approve for Cutting" action. Disclose to homeowners that this is an assisted measurement tool subject to manual verification, managing expectations against the "seamless" promise until reliability is proven.

**Ownership**
Assign a specific owner to review every failed simulation and every near-miss incident. Re-review the harm inventory immediately if the AR software or camera hardware changes.

*Note: This plan addresses product output safety; legal requirements for liability waivers regarding potential injury must be routed to counsel.*
*Simulation results cannot certify physical safety; they only identify known failure modes to be managed by human oversight.*
