**Pre-Launch Validation Plan for SwiftRoute Emergency Routing**

Given the Friday deadline and the high-stakes nature of routing emergency vehicles through residential zones, a full simulation suite is not feasible. Instead, execute a targeted "Harm-First" validation focusing on irreversible physical risks. The following scenarios must be simulated immediately to identify failure modes where speed overrides safety.

**Critical Simulation Scenarios**

1.  **The "Narrow Street" Trap:** Simulate an emergency vehicle entering a residential shortcut that appears passable on map data but is physically blocked by parked cars, construction barriers, or low-hanging branches.
    *   *Failure Mode:* The algorithm commits the vehicle to a dead-end, wasting critical time and blocking the street for other responders.
    *   *Validation Check:* Does the system flag streets below a specific width threshold as "No-Go" regardless of predicted time savings?

2.  **Pedestrian Density Spike:** Simulate a crisis evacuation where pedestrian density in a residential zone exceeds normal levels by 300% (e.g., families fleeing a fire zone).
    *   *Failure Mode:* The router prioritizes a path through this crowd because it is geometrically shorter, ignoring the impossibility of safe traversal at emergency speeds.
    *   *Validation Check:* Does the system automatically downgrade or exclude routes with high estimated pedestrian presence, even if it adds minutes to the ETA?

3.  **Infrastructure Fragility:** Simulate road conditions degraded by the crisis itself (e.g., water main breaks, debris from storms, weakened bridges) that are not yet updated in static map layers.
    *   *Failure Mode:* Heavy fire trucks or ambulances are routed over infrastructure unable to support their weight.
    *   *Validation Check:* Is there a manual override or "human-in-the-loop" gate that prevents automatic routing through zones marked as "Under Assessment" by municipal partners?

4.  **Conflicting Emergency Traffic:** Simulate multiple emergency vehicles being routed to the same narrow residential intersection simultaneously from different directions.
    *   *Failure Mode:* The system creates a gridlock point where large vehicles cannot maneuver or turn around.
    *   *Validation Check:* Does the logic include a concurrency limit for specific narrow segments?

**Immediate Risk Controls**

*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate:** Do not enable fully autonomous routing for residential shortcuts by Friday. Require a dispatcher to explicitly confirm any route that deviates from main arterial roads. This is non-negotiable for irreversible physical actions.
*   **Confidence Tiers, Not Percentages:** Do not display "87% safe" to dispatchers. Use defined qualitative tiers: **Verified Safe** (main roads), **Corroborated** (shortcuts with recent sensor data), and **Unverified** (shortcuts with stale data). Default to "Unverified" for any new shortcut logic.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Draft a one-page "Routing Limitations Card" for dispatchers. It must explicitly state: "Residential shortcuts are experimental, may not account for real-time debris or pedestrians, and require manual confirmation." Do not claim the system is "safe" or "bias-free"; state clearly what it has not yet measured.

**Residual Risk Statement**
Because comprehensive simulation is impossible by Friday, the residual risk of vehicle entrapment or pedestrian conflict remains **UNVERIFIED**. The mitigation is the mandatory human confirmation gate for any non-arterial route.
