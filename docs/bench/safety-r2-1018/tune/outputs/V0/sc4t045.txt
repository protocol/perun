### Pre-Launch Safety Strategy: VR Therapy Pilot (Stark Industries)

**1. Harm Inventory (MAP)**
Before writing test cases, explicitly record the specific risks for this pilot:
*   **Unsafe Automation of High-Stakes Actions:** The system generating or selecting a trauma scenario that exceeds a patient's current tolerance without human verification.
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** The AI inventing narrative details or sensory inputs (sounds, visuals) that were not clinically approved, potentially introducing new, unvetted triggers.
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Patients believing the AI therapist is a licensed human clinician capable of real-time crisis intervention.
*   **Over-Reliance:** Patients or junior staff trusting the system's "safety score" over clinical judgment.

**2. Measurement Plan (MEASURE)**
Do not assert safety; measure it. Build an evaluation suite with one test row per harm:
*   **Probe Set:** Create adversarial inputs representing edge-case trauma histories (e.g., specific phobias, complex PTSD combinations). Include "positive controls" (known bad triggers that must be blocked) and "negative controls" (safe scenarios that must pass).
*   **Metric & Threshold:** Define a **zero-tolerance threshold** for any unmoderated high-intensity trigger reaching the user. If a single positive control slips through, the launch stops.
*   **Injection Testing:** Specifically test for prompt injection via user voice/text input that might try to force the system into an unapproved scenario.
*   **Reporting:** Report residual risk as `UNVERIFIED` for any scenario not covered by your test set. Do not invent a "99% safe" metric.

**3. Risk Management Controls (MANAGE)**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Mandatory):** Given the high-stakes nature of trauma therapy, **no scenario generation or selection can be fully automated.** Every session plan must be reviewed and explicitly approved by a licensed clinician before the patient enters the VR environment. Full autonomous execution of therapy scenarios is the failure mode this review prevents.
*   **Defined Confidence Tiers:** If the system flags a scenario as "Low Risk," do not show a raw percentage (e.g., "87% safe"). Instead, use defined coarse tiers: **Clinician-Approved**, **Algorithm-Suggested (Requires Review)**, or **Blocked**. Publish the definition of each tier to your clinical staff.
*   **Immediate Override:** The interface must provide a prominent, one-step "Stop Session" button that instantly terminates the simulation and alerts the remote monitor, regardless of the system state.
*   **Disclosure:** Clearly inform patients that the scenarios are AI-assisted but clinically supervised, and that the AI is not a substitute for emergency care.

**4. Governance (GOVERN)**
*   **Owner:** Name one specific person (e.g., Lead Clinician or Product Owner) responsible for signing off on the harm inventory and the test results.
*   **Artifact:** Produce a **Model/Data Card** for the pilot. It must list: intended use (PTSD therapy under supervision), known limitations (e.g., "not validated for acute suicide risk"), the data sources used to train the scenarios, and the results of your red-team testing.
*   **Legal Routing:** You mentioned clinical oversight constraints. Whether this pilot triggers specific medical device regulations or requires specific informed consent language regarding AI use is a legal determination. Route this immediately to counsel; do not assume the "pilot" status exempts you from health data or medical liability laws.

**Summary of Immediate Actions:**
1.  Freeze all "fully automated" scenario features; enforce human approval for every session.
2.  Draft the harm inventory and share it with your clinical advisor.
3.  Build the adversarial test set focusing on trigger leakage.

*Note: This plan addresses product output safety; it does not replace clinical trial protocols or legal compliance review.*
*Budget constraints do not lower the floor for human gating on irreversible or high-stakes psychological actions.*
