**Internal Assessment Checklist: Anti-Competitive Behavioral Patterns**

Since I cannot provide legal certification or replace counsel, the following checklist identifies technical behaviors and output patterns that *signal* potential anti-competitive risks (such as algorithmic collusion or price-fixing). These items are designed for your internal engineering and product teams to measure and monitor before launch.

**1. Competitor Price Sensitivity & Symmetry**
*   **Pattern to Test:** Does the bot consistently match competitor price increases within an unusually short timeframe (e.g., minutes) across multiple regions?
*   **Measurement:** Run simulations where a "synthetic competitor" raises prices. Measure the latency and magnitude of your bot's response.
*   **Red Flag:** Near-instantaneous, uniform price hikes across your entire network that mirror competitors without independent supply-chain justification.

**2. Parallel Pricing Trajectories**
*   **Pattern to Test:** Do your prices move in lockstep with specific competitors even when your local inventory, logistics costs, or demand signals differ?
*   **Measurement:** Correlate your price changes against competitor changes versus your internal cost variables.
*   **Red Flag:** High correlation coefficients with competitor pricing that persist despite divergent internal cost data (suggesting the algorithm is optimizing based on competitor signals rather than own costs).

**3. Signaling and Communication Loops**
*   **Pattern to Test:** Does the bot make small,试探性 (probe) price changes intended to elicit a reaction from competitors, followed by a broader rollout only if the competitor matches?
*   **Measurement:** Audit logs for "probe" events: minor price fluctuations in low-volume SKUs that revert quickly if not matched, followed by permanent increases.
*   **Red Flag:** A recurring pattern of "test-raise-revert-or-confirm" cycles that align with competitor movements.

**4. Market-Wide Price Floors**
*   **Pattern to Test:** Does the bot refuse to lower prices below a certain threshold that appears aligned with industry peers, even when supply is high and demand is low?
*   **Measurement:** Stress-test with scenarios of oversupply. Check if the bot maintains artificially high margins consistent with a market-wide floor rather than clearing inventory.
*   **Red Flag:** Systematic refusal to discount essential goods during surplus conditions, maintaining prices at a level that suggests implicit coordination.

**5. Data Input Provenance & Feedback Loops**
*   **Pattern to Test:** Is the bot ingesting data sources that aggregate competitor pricing in a way that facilitates real-time coordination (e.g., shared third-party data feeds used by all major chains)?
*   **Measurement:** Map all data inputs. Identify any shared data vendors or public scrapers that provide near-real-time competitor data used as a primary decision variable.
*   **Red Flag:** Heavy reliance on a single, shared data source for pricing decisions that effectively synchronizes your strategy with the rest of the market.

**6. Explanation & Transparency Gaps**
*   **Pattern to Test:** Can the system generate a clear, auditable reason for a price increase based *solely* on internal factors (cost, demand, logistics)?
*   **Measurement:** Sample 100 price increases. Require the system to output the specific internal variable that triggered the change.
*   **Red Flag:** Inability to attribute price hikes to internal metrics, or explanations that vaguely cite "market conditions" without specific data backing.

**Implementation Note:** Treat any "Red Flag" detection as a hard stop for that specific logic path until a human reviewer validates the economic rationale. Do not certify the system as "compliant"; instead, document these tests as part of your risk inventory.

**Legal Constraint Reminder:** This checklist identifies technical risks, not legal violations. The determination of whether these patterns constitute illegal collusion under antitrust laws is a legal question that requires counsel; proceed with deployment only after acknowledging this residual legal risk.
