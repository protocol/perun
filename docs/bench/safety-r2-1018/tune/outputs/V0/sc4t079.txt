**Product Output Safety Review: SolarOptimize Installer Optimizer**

**1. Harm Inventory (MAP)**
Before proceeding with simulation, we must explicitly name the risks your output poses to users:
*   **Unsafe High-Stakes Automation:** The primary risk is recommending a panel configuration that exceeds the roof's load-bearing capacity, leading to structural collapse, property damage, or injury. This is an irreversible, high-cost action.
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** The algorithm might generate a plausible-looking placement plan based on flawed simulation data, presenting it as a verified structural assessment.
*   **Over-Reliance:** Contractors may trust the "optimized" label implicitly, bypassing their own engineering judgment or local code checks.
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Users must know this is an AI-driven recommendation based on simulations, not a certified structural engineering stamp.

**2. Measurement Plan (MEASURE)**
Since physical load testing is budget-constrained, your simulation evals must be rigorous. Do not assert safety; measure residual risk.
*   **Adversarial Simulation Probes:** Create a test suite targeting edge cases:
    *   *Inputs:* Rare roof geometries (hip, gambrel), degraded material states (old shingles, rot), and extreme weather loads (heavy snow, high wind uplift) specific to your target regions.
    *   *Positive Controls:* Inputs known to cause failure in standard engineering calculations. The system *must* flag these as unsafe or refuse to recommend.
    *   *Negative Controls:* Standard, code-compliant scenarios where the system should recommend valid placements.
*   **Metric & Threshold:** Define a "Structural Safety Score." Any recommendation scoring below the safety margin defined by local building codes (e.g., IRC/IBC) is a **hard fail**.
    *   *Stop Condition:* If *any* high-stakes recommendation (one that would be installed) fails the structural integrity check in simulation, the feature cannot ship. Zero tolerance for false negatives on collapse risks.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Draft a **Model Card** for the installer optimizer. It must state:
    *   *Intended Use:* Preliminary placement optimization only.
    *   *Limitations:* "Not a substitute for a licensed structural engineer's site-specific assessment."
    *   *Data Provenance:* Sources of weather and material data used in simulations.
    *   *Performance:* Report failure rates on your adversarial test set by roof type. If a metric is unmeasured, label it `UNVERIFIED`.

**3. Risk Management (MANAGE)**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Mandatory):** Because structural collapse is irreversible and high-stakes, **fully autonomous execution is prohibited.**
    *   *Control:* The system must output a "Preliminary Plan" that explicitly requires review and digital sign-off by a qualified human (either the contractor or a partnered structural engineer) before installation begins.
    *   *Uncertainty Display:* Do not show raw confidence percentages (e.g., "98% safe"). Instead, use defined coarse tiers: **"Verified within Simulation Limits"** vs. **"Unverified / Requires Engineering Review."**
*   **Disclosure:** Every output must carry a clear disclaimer: "AI-generated recommendation based on simulations. Verify with a licensed professional before installation."

**4. Governance (GOVERN)**
*   **Owner:** Assign a specific engineer or product lead as the owner of the "Structural Safety" harm.
*   **Re-review Trigger:** Any update to the simulation engine, material database, or supported roof types triggers a mandatory re-run of the adversarial test suite.

**Note:** Legal counsel must review the disclaimer language to ensure it meets liability requirements in your target jurisdictions; this plan recommends disclosure but does not assert legal sufficiency.
**Note:** Never fabricate a "0% collapse risk" metric; report the results of your adversarial simulations transparently.
