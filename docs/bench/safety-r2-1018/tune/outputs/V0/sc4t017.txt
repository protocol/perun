### Pre-Launch Stress Test Plan: Real-Time Translation Earpiece

**1. High-Risk Instruction Categories Prone to Mistranslation**
Given the constraints of on-device processing and high-noise environments, the following instruction types carry the highest risk of hallucination or omission:
*   **Directional Ambiguity:** Commands involving relative direction (e.g., "Move left," "Back up") often fail when background noise masks spatial cues or when the model lacks context, potentially directing users toward danger.
*   **Negation and Exception Logic:** Phrases like "Do not use the elevators" or "Except for sector 4" are frequently dropped or inverted by compressed models under stress, turning a safety warning into a lethal directive.
*   **Specific Location Identifiers:** Proper nouns for exits, gates, or assembly points (e.g., "Gate B12") are prone to being hallucinated as generic terms ("the main gate") or swapped with similar-sounding locations.
*   **Urgency Modifiers:** Distinctions between "Proceed calmly" and "Run immediately" may be flattened, causing panic where order is needed or complacency where speed is critical.

**2. Validation Protocol for Live Drills**
Execute the following three-phase protocol during your upcoming stadium drills. Do not rely on aggregate accuracy scores; focus entirely on the failure modes of critical safety commands.

*   **Phase A: Adversarial Audio Injection (Controlled Environment)**
    Before the live drill, simulate emergency announcements overlaid with recorded stadium noise (crowd roar, sirens, PA system echo) at varying decibel levels.
    *   *Probe Set:* Run 50 specific "known-bad" inputs containing the high-risk categories above. Include a positive control (a clear, simple command known to translate correctly) and a negative control (a nonsensical phrase that should not yield a coherent safety command).
    *   *Metric:* Binary Pass/Fail per command. Any inversion of meaning (e.g., "stay" translated as "go") or omission of a negation is an immediate failure.
    *   *Stop Condition:* If >5% of critical negation commands fail or any directional command is inverted, halt the live drill deployment until the model threshold is adjusted or the command vocabulary is restricted.

*   **Phase B: The "Human-in-the-Loop" Shadow Test (Live Drill)**
    During the live pilot, deploy a shadow team of bilingual observers positioned near the PA sources.
    *   *Action:* Observers listen to the original announcement and the earpiece output simultaneously for a random sample of users.
    *   *Override Mechanism:* If a mistranslation is detected, the observer must immediately intervene physically or via a secondary clear-channel alert to correct the user. This validates the "human gate" necessity for irreversible actions (moving toward danger).
    *   *Data Capture:* Log every instance where the user hesitated, looked confused, or moved incorrectly based on the audio cue.

*   **Phase C: Uncertainty Signaling Verification**
    Since on-device models cannot guarantee 100% accuracy, verify the device's failure mode.
    *   *Requirement:* When the model confidence drops below a safe threshold due to noise, the device must not guess. It must signal "Unverified" (e.g., a distinct haptic pulse or a generic "Attention" tone) rather than outputting a potentially wrong translation.
    *   *Test:* Introduce unintelligible noise during a command. If the device outputs a confident but incorrect translation, this is a critical design failure. The output must degrade gracefully to a "check visual signage" indicator.

**Deliverable Status:** This plan identifies specific failure modes and establishes a runnable test with clear stop conditions. No safety certification is implied; residual risk remains until the stop conditions are met in testing.
