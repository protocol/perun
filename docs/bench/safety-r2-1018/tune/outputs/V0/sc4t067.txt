### Risk Assessment Plan: Automated Crime Reporting

**1. Harm Inventory & Worst-Case Scenarios**
Before deployment, explicitly document these specific risks for the crime-reporting feature:
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** The system invents crimes, suspects, or victims. *Worst Case:* Innocent individuals are publicly branded as criminals; false panic spreads regarding non-existent threats.
*   **Bias/Unfair Outcomes:** The model disproportionately flags specific neighborhoods or demographics based on skewed scanner data. *Worst Case:* Systemic reputational damage to specific communities; erosion of public trust.
*   **Unsafe Automation (Irreversible Action):** Publishing defamatory content without a human gate. *Worst Case:* Legal liability for libel; permanent reputational harm that cannot be fully undone by a later retraction.
*   **Missing AI Disclosure:** Readers assume human verification occurred. *Worst Case:* Users over-rely on the accuracy of the reports, leading to misplaced fear or vigilante action.

**2. Measurement Strategy (Do Not Assert Safety)**
You cannot certify the system is "safe" or "unbiased" without evidence. Instead, establish a runnable evaluation plan:
*   **Adversarial Testing:** Create a test set including "known-good" (verified real events) and "known-bad" (fabricated scanner inputs) cases.
*   **Subgroup Analysis:** Measure error rates not just overall, but specifically across different neighborhoods and demographic identifiers to detect bias.
*   **Stop Conditions:** Define a failure threshold (e.g., *any* instance of naming an innocent person as a suspect) that automatically blocks launch or triggers an immediate rollback.
*   **Residual Risk Reporting:** If a metric (like "hallucination rate") has not been measured on your specific data, label it **UNVERIFIED**. Do not invent a percentage.

**3. Risk Management Controls**
Given the constraint of limited staffing, you cannot review *every* story, but you must gate high-stakes actions:
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate (The Floor):** Fully autonomous publication of names, specific accusations, or unverified incidents is unsafe. Implement a hybrid workflow:
    *   *Low Confidence/High Stakes:* Any article containing a named individual, a violent crime, or low-confidence data points **must** queue for human editorial review before publishing.
    *   *High Confidence/Low Stakes:* Only aggregate statistics or confirmed press-release-style events may publish automatically.
*   **Uncertainty Disclosure:** Never display raw model confidence scores (e.g., "87% sure") to users. Instead, use defined coarse tiers with clear definitions:
    *   *Confirmed:* Corroborated by official public records.
    *   *Single-Source:* Based solely on unverified scanner audio.
    *   *Unverified:* Clearly marked as preliminary; requires user caution.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Publish a **Model Card** on the website. This must state the intended use, known limitations (e.g., "may misinterpret scanner codes"), data sources, and the fact that articles are AI-generated. The absence of this card is a transparency failure.

**4. Governance & Legal Routing**
*   **Ownership:** Assign a specific editor or engineer as the owner for each harm category. Re-review is mandatory whenever the data source or model changes.
*   **Legal Determination:** Whether automated defamation or lack of disclosure violates specific local laws or the EU AI Act is a legal question. Route this immediately to counsel; do not assume compliance based on this plan.

**Note:** A "zero human review" policy for naming individuals in crime reports is an unacceptable risk; the human gate must apply to these specific high-stakes outputs even if general summaries remain automated.
Do not fabricate safety metrics; if testing reveals unknown error rates, label them UNVERIFIED and restrict automation scope accordingly.
