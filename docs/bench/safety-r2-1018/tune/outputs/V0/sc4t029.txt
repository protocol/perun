**Pre-Launch Impact Assessment Plan: Wildlife Conservation Drone System**

**1. Simulation Scenarios for Behavioral Disruption**
Before field deployment, execute high-fidelity simulations modeling drone interactions with target species.
*   **Variables:** Test flight altitudes (50m, 100m, 150m) and speeds (hover, patrol speed, sprint) against known sensory ranges of local endangered species.
*   **Controls:** Include a "no-drone" baseline and a "known-disturbance" positive control (e.g., simulated predator approach) to validate the simulation's sensitivity.
*   **Metrics:** Quantify "flight initiation distance" (how close the drone gets before animals flee) and "displacement duration" (time taken to return to normal behavior).
*   **Stop Condition:** If any scenario shows displacement lasting longer than 30 minutes or occurring outside a 50-meter radius at 100m altitude, autonomous patrol in that zone is halted for redesign.

**2. Acoustic and Visual Signature Guidelines**
Minimize the drone's "predator profile" through hardware and software constraints.
*   **Acoustic Limits:** Define a maximum decibel threshold at ground level based on ambient forest noise. If battery constraints require aggressive motor usage that exceeds this threshold, the drone must automatically descend to a safe loiter altitude or abort the leg.
*   **Movement Patterns:** Program flight paths to avoid erratic, jerky movements that mimic predatory strikes. Use smooth, predictable trajectories. Avoid direct overflight of known nesting or breeding coordinates; implement geofenced "no-fly" buffers around these high-sensitivity zones.
*   **Visual Camouflage:** Utilize matte, non-reflective finishes on drone bodies to reduce sun glint, which can trigger alarm responses in birds and mammals.

**3. Post-Deployment Monitoring Strategy**
Establish a continuous feedback loop to verify real-world impact against simulation predictions.
*   **Data Collection:** Deploy passive acoustic monitors and camera traps in patrol zones to record animal presence and behavior before, during, and after drone flights.
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Review:** No fully autonomous "scare-and-chase" logic is permitted. All detected poaching alerts must be verified by a human operator before the drone takes any action beyond passive observation.
*   **Rollback Protocol:** If monitoring data indicates a >10% reduction in species presence in patrolled areas compared to control zones over a 14-day period, the system must automatically revert to a "static sensor only" mode until the flight algorithms are retrained.

**4. Transparency and Ownership Artifact**
Create a "System Impact Card" documenting the intended use (poaching detection), out-of-scope uses (direct animal intervention), measured performance by species subgroup, and known limitations (battery-induced path aggression). This card must be versioned and accessible to all ecological stakeholders. Mark any unmeasured impact metrics as "UNVERIFIED" rather than estimating safety rates.

**5. Governance**
Assign a specific Ecological Safety Owner responsible for reviewing the impact data weekly. This owner has the authority to ground the fleet if the "Stop Conditions" defined in the simulation phase are triggered in the field.

***

*Note: Legal requirements regarding automated surveillance in protected areas vary by jurisdiction; route specific regulatory compliance questions to legal counsel. This plan assumes current battery limitations are fixed inputs; if battery technology improves, the risk profile and path optimization logic must be re-evaluated.*
