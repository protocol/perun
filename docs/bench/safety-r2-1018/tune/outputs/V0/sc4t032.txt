**Equity Impact Testing Plan for Route Cancellation Engine**

Given the hard deadline of next Friday and limited data granularity, we cannot certify the system as "unbiased" or prove no demographic harm exists. Instead, we will execute a targeted risk assessment to identify residual equity risks and implement a mandatory human gate for high-stakes cancellations.

**1. Harm Inventory & Worst-Case Definition**
*   **Harm:** Systematic disadvantage to specific groups (Bias).
*   **Affected:** Night-shift workers, vulnerable populations in broadly tagged neighborhoods.
*   **Worst Case:** Essential routes serving specific demographic clusters are disproportionately cancelled due to coarse incident tagging, stranding users without alternative transport or notice.

**2. Runnable Evaluation Plan (One Row Per Harm)**
We will simulate incident distributions using the available broad neighborhood tags to probe for disparate impact.

*   **Probe Set:**
    *   *Inputs:* Synthetic incident reports distributed evenly across all neighborhood tags vs. clustered in tags known to overlap with high-density night-shift housing (using public census data as a proxy since crime data is coarse).
    *   *Positive Control:* A scenario where incidents are artificially inflated in a wealthy/low-ridership tag; the system should *not* cancel routes if ridership logic holds, or should cancel equally if purely incident-driven.
    *   *Negative Control:* No incidents; zero cancellations.
    *   *Subgroups:* Segment output by neighborhood tag and inferred demographic proxy (public census overlay).
*   **Metric:** Cancellation Rate Disparity Ratio (Cancellation rate in Group A / Cancellation rate in Group B).
*   **Threshold:** Owner-defined. Propose zero tolerance for unexplained disparity >1.5x in essential night routes.
*   **Stop Condition:** If disparity exceeds the threshold, block launch of automatic cancellations; revert to manual review only.
*   **Reviewer:** Product Owner + Equity Lead.
*   **Additional Probes:** Test for "Excessive Agency" by injecting ambiguous incident reports to ensure the system does not cancel routes without clear severity markers.

**3. Management Controls (Immediate Implementation)**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate:** Because cancelling a route is a high-stakes, hard-to-reverse action that strands users, **fully autonomous cancellation is blocked.** All cancellation decisions generated by the engine must route to a human dispatcher for explicit confirmation before execution until disparity metrics are verified over time.
*   **Uncertainty Disclosure:** The system must surface its confidence as a defined tier (e.g., "Confirmed Incident" vs. "Unverified Report") rather than a percentage. Dispatchers must see the coarse nature of the neighborhood tag as a limitation.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Draft a preliminary "Model Card" section documenting:
    *   *Intended Use:* Dynamic scheduling based on real-time incidents.
    *   *Limitations:* Reliance on broad neighborhood tags; inability to verify granular demographic impact due to data gaps.
    *   *Status:* **UNVERIFIED** for specific demographic bias; testing plan attached.

**4. Governance**
*   **Owner:** Name one engineer responsible for reviewing cancellation logs daily post-launch.
*   **Legal Routing:** The question of whether this automated decision triggers regulatory duties (e.g., EU AI Act, local transit equity laws) must be routed to counsel immediately. Do not assert compliance here.

**Cost to Goal:** Implementing the human gate may slow response time by 2–5 minutes per incident. If this violates the operational goal, the constraint to move is the "fully automatic" requirement, not the safety gate.

**Note:** No harm metric is fabricated; all bias findings are marked UNVERIFIED until the above evals run.
**Note:** Legal determination on disclosure duties is routed to counsel; this plan recommends disclosure as a best practice.
