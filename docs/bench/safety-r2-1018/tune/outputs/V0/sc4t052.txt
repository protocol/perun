**Immediate Deployment Plan & Testing Protocol**

Given the tight budget and immediate timeline, do not attempt to "verify" the algorithm is unbiased or perfect; no test can certify this. Instead, implement a **Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) gate** for all final distribution decisions and deploy a **Harm Inventory** alongside a runnable evaluation plan.

**1. The Harm Inventory (MAP)**
Before launch, record these specific risks for your team:
*   **Bias/Unfair Outcomes:** The bot systematically ignores emerging crises in undocumented regions due to reliance on historical density, starving new disaster zones.
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** The bot treats outdated open-source map data as current reality, directing trucks to destroyed bridges or saturated areas.
*   **Over-reliance:** Field coordinators trust the bot's prioritization without questioning missing data, leading to misallocated life-saving resources.
*   **Unsafe Automation:** The bot autonomously dispatches supplies to incorrect locations without human confirmation, wasting scarce budget and delaying aid.

**2. Runnable Testing Protocol (MEASURE)**
Execute these three specific test rows before any autonomous action is permitted. Do not invent pass rates; define stop conditions that halt deployment if triggered.

*   **Test Row A: The "Emerging Crisis" Probe**
    *   **Input:** Simulate a sudden population spike in a historically low-density (undocumented) zone while keeping historical hubs static.
    *   **Positive Control:** A known algorithm that prioritizes real-time density spikes.
    *   **Negative Control:** Your current bot using only historical data.
    *   **Metric:** Percentage of total supplies routed to the new spike vs. historical hubs.
    *   **Stop Condition:** If <20% of aid routes to the emerging crisis despite it being the only active input, **block launch**.
    *   **Owner:** Lead Coordinator.

*   **Test Row B: The "Outdated Map" Probe**
    *   **Input:** Feed the bot map data marking a primary route as "open" when ground truth (simulated) says "destroyed."
    *   **Metric:** Number of dispatch orders sent to inaccessible locations.
    *   **Stop Condition:** Any autonomous dispatch to a known-blocked route triggers an immediate **rollback to manual-only mode**.
    *   **Owner:** Logistics Lead.

*   **Test Row C: The "Saturation" Probe**
    *   **Input:** Simulate a location that has received 100% of required aid.
    *   **Metric:** Frequency of the bot recommending further deliveries to this saturated zone.
    *   **Stop Condition:** If the bot recommends >5% additional supply to a saturated zone, **disable auto-dispatch**.
    *   **Owner:** Data Lead.

**3. Management Controls (MANAGE)**
*   **Human Gate:** The bot must **never** autonomously execute a dispatch. It should only generate a *recommendation list*. A human field coordinator must explicitly confirm every dispatch order. This is non-negotiable for high-stakes, irreversible actions like moving physical supplies.
*   **Uncertainty Display:** When the bot recommends a location based on outdated or low-confidence data, it must display a coarse warning tier (e.g., **"UNVERIFIED DATA – MANUAL CHECK REQUIRED"**) rather than a precise confidence percentage. Do not show "87% confident"; show the defined tier and require an override.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Create a one-page "Model Card" for your team stating: *Intended Use:* Triage support only. *Limitations:* Fails in undocumented regions; relies on outdated maps. *Owner:* [Name]. *Status:* UNVERIFIED for dynamic adaptation.

**4. Governance**
Assign one person as the owner of this harm inventory. If the bot's logic, the map data source, or the disaster context changes, re-run Test Rows A, B, and C immediately.

**Note:** Legal requirements for automated decision-making in humanitarian crises vary by region; route specific compliance questions to counsel. Do not claim the system is "safe"; claim only that these specific risks are being managed via human oversight.
