**Steelman claim** – Launch the newly‑ready tablet built on the acquired mobile OS, price it competitively with the market‑leading tablet, ship with a modest app catalog, and back the launch with a heavy push of developer outreach and advertising.

**Skeptic attacks**  
1. **App ecosystem** – A “small” catalog cannot sustain consumer demand when the leader’s store offers thousands of apps. Without a rapid influx of apps, usage time and purchase intent will collapse.  
2. **OS market share** – The OS “has almost no market share.” Even aggressive advertising cannot create a network effect fast enough to overcome the entrenched leader.  
3. **Leadership transition** – Ongoing executive turnover will likely impair execution of the outreach and marketing plan, increasing the chance of missed milestones.  
4. **Consumer perception** – Shoppers will view the tablet as unproven; early‑adopter skepticism will translate into poor launch‑week sales, making it hard to recoup the heavy ad spend.

**Better‑way alternatives**  
- **Delay launch** until the app catalog reaches a critical mass (e.g., 1 000 high‑quality apps) or a partnership with a major app developer is secured.  
- **Target a niche segment** (education or enterprise) where a smaller app set is acceptable and price competition is less intense.  
- **Leverage the existing OS** on smartphones to cross‑promote the tablet, reducing the need for a separate advertising blitz.

**Kill‑criteria** (pre‑mortem)  
- If the app count remains < 500 three months post‑launch, suspend further marketing spend.  
- If cumulative advertising cost exceeds 30 % of projected first‑year revenue without a ≥ 10 % market‑share gain, abort the program.  
- If leadership turnover continues past the launch window, trigger a pause.

**Remaining risk** – Even with outreach, building a viable app ecosystem in a market dominated by an entrenched leader is a two‑way door; failure will be costly and hard to reverse.

Given the high probability that the tablet will launch with an insufficient app catalog, low OS adoption, and uncertain leadership, the proposal should not be approved in its current form. A revised plan that secures a robust app ecosystem or targets a niche market first is required.

DECISION: NO-GO
