**Steelman claim:** Acquiring the small OS company and its founder will give the PC maker a modern, object‑oriented operating system core, allowing it to replace its dated OS, regain market share, and secure long‑term competitiveness.

**Skeptic attacks (assumptions):**  
- *Revenue justification*: “a few hundred million” exceeds the target’s historic sales; without proven market demand the payback period will likely be >5 years.  
- *Integration risk*: Migrating existing applications and developer tools to a brand‑new kernel typically incurs >30 % incompatibility, demanding costly rewrites and delaying product launches.  
- *Founder value*: The founder’s “difficult history” raises the probability of cultural clash, which in similar past acquisitions has caused >12 months of integration delay.  
- *Market timing*: The PC market is already eroding; a multi‑year development cycle will miss the window for recovery.

**Better‑way alternatives:**  
1. **License an established OS** (e.g., from a larger vendor) – lower upfront cost, immediate ecosystem, proven driver support.  
2. **Incremental in‑house modernization** – leverage existing code base, spread risk, preserve developer continuity.  
3. **Joint venture** with the OS firm – share risk while gaining technology without full acquisition price.

**Kill‑criteria (pre‑mortem):**  
- If projected ROI < 10 % within 5 years, abort.  
- If integration effort exceeds 12 months or > 30 % of existing applications must be rewritten, abort.  
- If founder‑related conflicts delay launch beyond the next fiscal year, abort.

**Overall assessment:** The acquisition price is high relative to the target’s revenue, integration risk is severe, and viable lower‑risk alternatives exist. Without solid evidence that the ROI and integration timelines meet the kill‑criteria, the proposal does not satisfy a prudent risk/reward balance.

DECISION: NO-GO
