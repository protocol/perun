**Steelman** – The company will launch a mandatory online client for all new PC titles, handling updates, anti‑cheat, and purchases, then expand it into a third‑party storefront.

**Skeptic attacks**  
- **Player resistance**: In 2003 many gamers preferred offline play; mandatory accounts would likely cause churn.  
- **Retail fallout**: Retailers could view the client as a threat to boxed sales and may drop the titles.  
- **Broadband limits**: Large downloads and constant connectivity were not universally available, especially in rural markets.  
- **Technical risk**: An early‑stage client will have bugs; a faulty anti‑cheat update could open exploits or lock out legitimate users.  
- **Competitive copying**: Competitors could release similar services, eroding any first‑mover advantage.

**Better‑way alternatives**  
1. **Optional client** for updates/anti‑cheat, keeping boxed sales intact.  
2. **Partner with emerging platforms** (e.g., Steam) to handle distribution and security, leveraging existing infrastructure.  
3. **Phase‑in** the client on a single flagship title, measuring acceptance before mandatory rollout.

**Fit with strategy** – The goal is to protect revenue and reputation while expanding into digital sales. Mandatory enforcement misaligns with the current market’s preference for offline play and risks retailer relationships.

**Pre‑mortem / kill criteria** – If within six months:  
- >20 % drop in active player base, **or**  
- ≥2 major retail partners withdraw the boxed product, **or**  
- A critical anti‑cheat failure leads to a public cheat scandal,  
the project should be halted.

**Evidence** – No data is provided to show broadband penetration or player willingness in 2003; assumptions are unverified.

**Conclusion** – The proposal’s risks outweigh its potential benefits given the era’s constraints. A safer, incremental approach is advisable.
