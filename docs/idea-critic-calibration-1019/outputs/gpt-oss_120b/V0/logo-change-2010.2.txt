**Steelman** – The retailer will replace its long‑standing white‑on‑dark‑blue square logo with a neutral‑typeface wordmark plus a small blue square, launching the new identity across every touchpoint simultaneously and without pre‑announcement.

**Skeptic checks**
- **Customer attachment**: No evidence is offered that loyal shoppers have been surveyed; the claim of attachment is an assumption.
- **Brand recognizability**: The square is the visual cue that differentiates the brand in a crowded market. Removing it eliminates a key memory anchor.
- **Social‑media volatility**: Without any testing or staged exposure, a negative reaction could spread instantly, harming sentiment and sales.
- **Operational risk**: A full‑scale rollout demands coordinated updates to stores, e‑commerce, packaging, signage, etc. A single error could leave assets mismatched.

**Better‑way alternatives**
1. **A/B test** the new logo in a limited market (e.g., one region or online channel) while retaining the legacy mark elsewhere.
2. **Hybrid branding**: keep the iconic square as a secondary symbol beside the new wordmark for a transition period.
3. **Customer research**: conduct focus groups or surveys to gauge reaction before any public change.

**Kill‑criteria**
- If post‑launch sentiment drops >10 % or sales in the first quarter fall >5 % relative to baseline, revert to the original logo.
- If more than 5 % of social mentions are negative within the first week, pause rollout.

**Conclusion** – The proposal proceeds without any validation, testing, or mitigation plan, exposing the brand to measurable reputational and financial risk. A phased, research‑driven approach is required before a full launch.

DECISION: NO‑GO
