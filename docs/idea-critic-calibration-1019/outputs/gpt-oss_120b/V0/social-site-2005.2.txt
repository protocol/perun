**Steelman claim:** Buying the fast‑growing social network for $580 M, keeping it separate, and selling ads across the conglomerate’s media will instantly give the group a youthful social‑media foothold and unlock cross‑channel advertising revenue.

**Attack:**  
1. **Price vs. revenue** – Paying $580 M for a site with “little revenue” implies a valuation many times earnings; without clear cash‑flow forecasts the payback period likely exceeds 5 years.  
2. **Brand‑safety risk** – User‑generated pages are a known source of offensive or illegal content; a single scandal could tarnish the conglomerate’s whole portfolio. Mitigation would require costly moderation infrastructure.  
3. **Technology debt** – “Built quickly and unevenly” suggests fragile architecture, high integration cost, and future scalability problems.  
4. **Competitive pressure** – A college‑focused rival is already gaining traction; the target’s growth may stall once the rival scales, limiting long‑term audience value.  
5. **Monetization difficulty** – Young users are price‑sensitive and historically generate low CPMs; advertising alone may never cover the purchase price.

**Better ways:**  
- **Build an in‑house platform** – Leverage existing content and data assets; lower capital outlay, full control over tech and moderation.  
- **Strategic partnership** – Co‑market with an existing social network, sharing ad inventory without acquisition risk.  
- **Acquire a smaller, profitable niche community** – Provides a proven revenue base and manageable tech stack.

**Fit with strategy:** The conglomerate’s north‑star of expanding digital ad revenue is valid, but the proposed acquisition mis‑aligns on cost, risk, and timeline.

**Pre‑mortem & kill‑criteria:** If ROI < 5 % after three years or a brand‑safety incident triggers a PR crisis, the investment should be divested. Both thresholds are likely to be hit given current data.

**Conclusion:** The strategic upside does not outweigh the financial, technical, and reputational risks at the proposed price.

DECISION: NO-GO
