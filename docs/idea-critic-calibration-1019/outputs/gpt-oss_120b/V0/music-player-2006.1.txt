**Steelman** – Launch a full‑featured music player, desktop client, and online store with subscription pricing, wireless song‑sharing, a larger screen, and a sizable marketing push to challenge the market leader.

**Attack** –  
- **Ecosystem lock‑in**: The incumbent controls hardware, software, and DRM; users face switching costs.  
- **Late entry**: 2006 already saw iPod dominance; gaining traction now requires massive differentiation.  
- **Copy‑protection break**: Dropping the company‑promoted DRM alienates partner stores and device makers, risking loss of existing relationships.  
- **Label constraints**: Without secured licensing, sharing features may be blocked, eroding the promised value.  
- **Price parity**: No clear cost advantage means consumers have little incentive to switch.

**Better‑ways** –  
1. Partner with the incumbent or a niche hardware maker to embed the store, leveraging existing ecosystems.  
2. Focus on a differentiated niche (e.g., high‑resolution audio, indie catalog) rather than a full‑scale consumer launch.  
3. Retain the proven DRM to preserve partner trust while offering value‑added services (e.g., curated playlists).

**Fit** – If the company’s strategic goal is to become a consumer media platform, the proposal aligns but conflicts with the risk‑averse, partnership‑centric culture implied by its DRM history.

**Pre‑mortem / Kill Criteria** –  
- **Market‑share <5 % after 12 mo** → abort.  
- **No label agreement on sharing within 6 mo** → abort.  
- **Marketing spend > $150 M with ROI < 2× after 18 mo** → abort.

**Evidence** – All points derive from the proposal’s own risk statements; no external data is available.

**Conclusion** – The combination of strong incumbent lock‑in, lack of clear differentiation, and high financial exposure makes the plan untenable without major revisions (e.g., partnership or niche focus).  

DECISION: NO-GO
