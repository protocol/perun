**Steelman claim:** Launch a $89.95 monochrome handheld with modest hardware, long battery life and a $20 M marketing push, bundled with a puzzle game, to compete with a rival color‑screen device.

**Skeptic checks & assumptions**  
- **Screen & hardware:** Assumes consumers will trade color for price and battery life. No data on willingness to accept monochrome when a color rival exists.  
- **Pricing & margins:** At $89.95 the unit margin must cover hardware cost, royalties, distribution and the $20 M marketing budget. Implicitly assumes >300 k units sold to break even; no market size estimate provided.  
- **Game bundle:** One puzzle game may not entice buyers accustomed to multi‑title libraries.

**Better‑way alternatives**  
1. **Color screen, modest specs:** A low‑cost color LCD (e.g., reflective LCD) could narrow the visual gap while keeping battery life acceptable, raising perceived value.  
2. **Modular game library:** Offer a cartridge slot or downloadable titles to increase replay value without large hardware changes.  
3. **Price adjustment:** Reduce retail price to $69.95 and cut marketing spend, targeting a niche “budget gamer” segment, improving volume potential.

**Kill‑criteria (pre‑mortem)**  
- **Margin breach:** If projected gross margin <5 % after hardware, royalties and distribution, abort.  
- **Market share loss:** If competitor’s color handheld captures >30 % of the handheld market in the first 12 months, the product is non‑viable.  
- **Sales shortfall:** If forecasted sales <250 k units (≈$22.5 M revenue) the $20 M marketing spend cannot be justified.

**Concrete failure scenario:** Launch proceeds, sells only 150 k units (≈$13.5 M revenue). After subtracting $20 M marketing, hardware and distribution costs, the project incurs a multi‑million‑dollar loss, confirming the margin‑breach kill criterion.

**Conclusion:** The proposal’s core assumptions are unverified, a stronger alternative exists (low‑cost color screen), and clear kill criteria indicate high risk of loss. The plan should be revised before approval.  

DECISION: NO-GO
