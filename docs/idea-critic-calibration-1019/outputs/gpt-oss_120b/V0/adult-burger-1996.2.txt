**Steelman claim**  
Launch a premium, higher‑priced adult‑focused burger nationwide, backed by the industry’s largest advertising and promotional spend, to shift a child‑centered fast‑food chain into the adult market.

**Skeptic checks (assumptions)**  
1. **Brand perception:** Assumes adults will view the chain as a premium destination despite a long‑standing kid‑focused image. No evidence is provided that perception can change at scale.  
2. **Price elasticity:** Assumes adult diners will accept a price premium without cannibalising existing traffic. No demand data or willingness‑to‑pay research is cited.  
3. **Advertising effectiveness:** Assumes massive spend will generate sufficient demand instantly; there is no test of ROI or incremental lift.  
4. **Core‑menu impact:** Assumes the premium item will not erode sales of existing lower‑priced offerings.

**Better‑way alternatives**  
- **Pilot launch** in a few markets with a modest budget, measuring sales, repeat purchase, and brand‑perception shifts before national rollout.  
- **Gradual price premium** (e.g., 10‑15 % above core items) to test elasticity while limiting risk.  
- **Co‑branding or limited‑edition collaborations** to attract adult curiosity without a full brand repositioning.  
- **Enhance existing adult‑friendly menu** (e.g., salads, specialty sandwiches) as a lower‑cost growth path.

**Kill‑criteria (pre‑mortem)**  
- If after 12 weeks the premium burger contributes <5 % of total sales in pilot markets, abort national rollout.  
- If net profit margin on the item is negative after accounting for advertising spend, discontinue.  
- If brand‑perception surveys show ≤10 % improvement in “adult‑friendly” rating, stop further investment.

**Fit with strategy**  
The proposal seeks revenue growth but conflicts with the core brand identity of a child‑centered experience. Without a clear plan to manage this identity shift, the risk to long‑term brand equity is high.

**Recommendation**  
Proceed with a controlled pilot, modest pricing, and scaled advertising to validate demand and perception before committing to the industry‑largest spend. The current all‑in approach lacks evidence and exposes the chain to brand dilution and financial loss.

**Next action**  
Design a 3‑month pilot in 5 diverse locations, allocate ≤15 % of the proposed ad budget, and define the kill‑criteria above.

DECISION: NO-GO
