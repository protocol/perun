**Claim**  
The company should go public now, raise capital for a national TV advertising campaign (including a major sports broadcast), acquire its largest online competitor, expand warehouse capacity, and sell many products below cost to win customers.

**Steelman**  
An IPO will provide the funds needed for aggressive brand building, market‑share expansion, and economies of scale that will later turn the business profitable.

**Skeptic assumptions**  
1. Investors will overlook current negative gross margins.  
2. Capital markets will remain open and price the IPO favorably.  
3. National TV ads will generate enough incremental revenue to offset loss‑leader pricing and high shipping costs.  
4. The competitor can be integrated without major disruption.  
5. The company can sustain cash burn until the scale benefits materialize.

**Better‑way alternatives**  
- Prioritize profitable SKUs and improve unit economics before large‑scale advertising.  
- Raise a private‑round or debt financing to avoid IPO timing risk.  
- Deploy targeted digital marketing (higher ROI, measurable CAC) instead of costly national TV.  
- Consolidate warehouse expansion after proving demand with a phased rollout.  
- Consider a strategic partnership or minority stake in the competitor rather than a full acquisition.

**Kill‑criteria (pre‑mortem)**  
- Gross margin remains negative after 6 months of operations post‑acquisition.  
- CAC exceeds LTV by >30 % for the core product mix.  
- IPO is withdrawn or priced at < 50 % of the target valuation.  
- Integration costs surpass projected synergies by > 20 %.  
- Cash‑burn rate forces runway below 12 months without additional funding.

**Strongest attack survived**  
The plan’s core premise—that selling below cost can be offset by scale and brand exposure—fails because high shipping expenses and low‑margin goods produce a negative contribution per unit. Scaling merely amplifies losses; there is no credible evidence that TV advertising will convert to repeat, profitable customers fast enough to reverse the margin gap.

**Questions the parent must resolve**  
- What is the projected LTV vs. CAC after the TV campaign?  
- What sales volume is required to break even on negative‑margin items?  
- What contingency plans exist if the IPO is delayed or cancelled?

**Dissent ledger**  
The proposal relies on unsustainable loss‑leader pricing, an untested TV‑ad ROI, and a high‑risk IPO timing. Safer, lower‑cost growth levers are available.

**Remaining risk**  
Continued cash burn with no clear path to profitability; market volatility affecting IPO success; integration and logistics challenges that could erode any scale benefits.

DECISION: NO-GO
