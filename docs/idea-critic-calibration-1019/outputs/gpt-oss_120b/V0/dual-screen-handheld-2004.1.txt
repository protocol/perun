The proposal targets a clear market gap: a lower‑priced handheld that differentiates through dual screens and touch input rather than raw graphics. By retaining the existing device, revenue from the current install base is preserved while the new platform can attract casual and non‑gamer audiences, expanding the total addressable market. Historical analogues show that innovative form factors (e.g., dual‑screen, touch) can create new game genres and drive third‑party support once a critical mass of titles appears.

Key risks are mitigated by concrete actions: 

* Developer confusion is reduced by providing a unified SDK and early “touch‑first” design guidelines.  
* Higher graphics capability of the rival is offset by pricing (≈20 % cheaper) and unique gameplay experiences unavailable on the competitor.  
* Touch‑screen viability is tested through a pilot program with three flagship partners before full launch.  
* Additional hardware cost is capped by leveraging existing screen‑panel suppliers and sharing components with the older model.  

A one‑way‑door kill criterion is set: if third‑party commitment falls below 10 % of projected titles within six months, the project will be halted. With these safeguards, the upside outweighs the manageable risks.

DECISION: GO
