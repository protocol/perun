**Steelman claim**: Launch a premium touchscreen phone (no physical keyboard) that integrates the company’s leading digital music player, full web browser, and email. Sell it exclusively through a single carrier that allows full control over hardware/software and pays a share of monthly service revenue, in exchange for several years of exclusivity.

**Skeptic attacks**  
- **Phone‑industry inexperience**: No track record of handset design, supply‑chain, or carrier negotiations; risk of costly missteps.  
- **Premium price without keyboard**: In 2007 the market is dominated by keyboard‑centric smartphones; a high‑price, keyboard‑less device may face low adoption.  
- **Exclusivity limits reach**: One carrier caps the addressable market; if that carrier’s subscriber base is modest, sales volume may be insufficient to amortize R&D.  
- **No third‑party apps**: Lack of an app ecosystem reduces device attractiveness versus competitors with emerging platforms.

**Better‑way alternatives**  
1. **Co‑development with an experienced OEM** – leverage existing handset expertise, reduce time‑to‑market, share risk.  
2. **Multi‑carrier launch** – broaden initial market, avoid lock‑in to a single carrier’s subscriber base.  
3. **Use an existing mobile OS (e.g., Symbian, Windows Mobile)** – instantly gain an app ecosystem and developer support.

**Kill‑criteria (must be satisfied to proceed)**  
- Projected unit sales ≥ 5 % of the carrier’s total handset volume in the first 12 months.  
- Development + launch cost recouped within 24 months at the proposed revenue‑share terms.  
- Carrier commits to a minimum marketing spend equivalent to 2 % of its total handset marketing budget.

**Required next action**: Obtain the carrier’s subscriber count, projected handset market share, detailed cost‑and‑revenue model, and a risk‑mitigation plan for hardware/software development.

Given the high uncertainty and unmet kill‑criteria, the proposal cannot be approved as‑is.

DECISION: NO-GO
