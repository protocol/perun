**Steelman** – Launch a low‑priced, sporty two‑door coupe/convertible built on the existing compact’s mechanicals, with a long hood, short deck, many options and a massive advertising push, to capture post‑war baby‑boom buyers seeking style.

**Key Assumptions Tested**  
1. **Volume demand for sporty small cars** – No proven sales data; early 1960s U.S. market still favors larger family cars.  
2. **Brand perception will stay premium despite shared cheap parts** – Past cases (e.g., “economy‑based sport” models) showed a “cheap‑look” backlash that hurt core model sales.  
3. **Heavy ad spend will create demand** – Advertising can’t create demand for a product the market hasn’t validated; large spend risks cash‑flow before revenue.

**Better‑Way Alternatives**  
- **Incremental sport trim** on the existing compact (minor styling tweaks, limited options) – lower cost, uses same parts, tests market response.  
- **Limited‑run “special edition”** (few thousand units) to gauge buyer interest before full launch.  
- **Partnership with an established sporty brand** for co‑branding, sharing development risk.

**Kill‑Criteria / Pre‑Mortem**  
- **One‑way door:** Advertising budget > $30 M before any pre‑orders → abort if pre‑launch reservations < 5 % of target volume.  
- **Metric trigger:** First‑year sales < 50 % of projected 150,000 units → discontinue model and re‑allocate tooling.  
- **Brand impact:** Net‑promoter score for the core compact drops > 5 points within six months → halt further sporty variants.

**Residual Risk** – Even with a limited‑run test, the core risk remains the unproven market size and potential dilution of the brand’s value proposition.

**Recommendation** – Do not proceed with the full‑scale launch as described. Instead, start with a low‑volume special edition or a sport‑trim package to collect real demand signals before committing to massive advertising spend and full production tooling.

DECISION: NO-GO
