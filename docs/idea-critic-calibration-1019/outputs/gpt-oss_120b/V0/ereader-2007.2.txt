**Steelman claim** – The company will design, manufacture, and sell an e‑reader with a paper‑like e‑ink display and free cellular connectivity, allowing customers to browse and download books directly from the store at a low per‑title price.

**Skeptic attacks**  
1. **Hardware inexperience** – No prior consumer‑hardware design means development cost, supply‑chain risk, and time‑to‑market delays. A first‑generation device will likely have quality and reliability issues that damage brand reputation.  
2. **Margin squeeze** – Offering “single low price per title” forces e‑book prices down, threatening margins on both hardware (which must be sold at cost or loss) and content (publishers may refuse or demand higher royalties).  
3. **Market history** – Earlier dedicated e‑readers (e.g., early Kindle, Sony Reader) sold modestly; consumer adoption was driven by ecosystem (apps, multi‑device sync), not hardware novelty.  
4. **Cannibalisation** – Cheap e‑books could accelerate the decline of print sales, undermining existing profitable lines and upsetting publisher relationships.

**Better‑way alternatives**  
- **Partner** with an established e‑reader maker (e.g., Amazon, Sony) to embed the store app, leveraging existing hardware and distribution.  
- **Release a mobile app** for iOS/Android tablets and phones, eliminating hardware risk and reaching a larger installed base instantly.  
- **License the e‑ink display technology** to a third‑party device, sharing development costs.

**Kill‑criteria** (pre‑mortem)  
- Prototype cost > $150 per unit before volume discounts.  
- Publisher pushback leading to > 30 % reduction in new‑title agreements.  
- First‑year unit sales < 50,000 (break‑even threshold).  
- Hardware return rate > 10 % in launch quarter.

**Dissent ledger** – The proposal ignores lower‑risk, higher‑return paths (partnering or app‑first) and lacks concrete financial thresholds. The outlined kill‑criteria are realistic guardrails that the plan does not meet.

**Decision** – Given the high execution risk, margin pressure, and better alternatives, the proposal should not be approved as written.

DECISION: NO-GO
