**Steelman:** Launching a premium, higher‑priced burger with an unprecedented advertising push will reposition the chain as an adult‑focused venue, attracting new, higher‑spending customers and expanding market share beyond its child‑centric base.

**Skeptic:**  
- **Brand mismatch:** The chain’s core identity is tied to family‑friendly, low‑price perception; a premium offering may be dismissed as inauthentic.  
- **Price elasticity:** Without evidence of adult willingness to pay a premium at a fast‑food outlet, the higher price risks cannibalizing existing traffic.  
- **Ad spend inefficiency:** Deploying the industry’s largest budget before confirming demand likely yields low ROI; past fast‑food premium attempts (e.g., “premium” lines in the 1990s) showed modest lift at high cost.  

**Better‑Way:**  
1. **Pilot program:** Test the burger in 5–10 geographically diverse locations with a modest, locally‑scaled media plan.  
2. **Incremental pricing:** Position the burger 10‑15 % above core items rather than a large premium, reducing price‑sensitivity risk.  
3. **Leverage existing brand assets:** Use co‑branding with adult‑oriented partners (e.g., craft beer, sport events) instead of a blanket ad blitz.  

**Kill‑Criteria:**  
- If average weekly units sold < 10 % of the projected baseline after 12 weeks, halt rollout.  
- If incremental advertising cost per acquired adult customer exceeds $5, stop further spend.  
- Cap total spend at $2 M for the pilot; any overrun triggers immediate review.

**Dissent Ledger:** The proposal’s full‑scale spend and premium pricing ignore brand‑fit and demand validation, exposing the chain to significant financial loss and brand dilution.

**Remaining Risk:** Even with a pilot, adult adoption may be slow, and the brand could be perceived as “trying too hard,” harming the core family segment.

Given the high uncertainty and costly exposure, the proposal should not be approved as‑is.  

DECISION: NO-GO
