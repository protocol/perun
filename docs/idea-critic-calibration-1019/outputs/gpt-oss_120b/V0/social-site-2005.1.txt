**Steelman** – Buying the social network gives the conglomerate an instant foothold with a youthful, engaged audience and a platform for cross‑selling advertising across its existing media properties.

**Attack** –  
1. **Valuation vs revenue** – $580 M for a site with “little revenue” implies an extreme multiple; without clear cash‑flow forecasts the purchase is financially speculative.  
2. **Competitive pressure** – A college‑focused rival is already gaining traction; the market may fragment further, limiting growth potential.  
3. **Brand‑safety risk** – User‑generated pages are a known vector for inappropriate content, threatening the conglomerate’s reputation and advertiser confidence.  
4. **Monetisation of a young audience** – Historically, users under 25 are hard to convert to high‑value ad spend; the projected ROI is uncertain.  
5. **Technical debt** – Rapid, uneven development suggests future stability and scalability problems, increasing integration costs.

**Alternatives** –  
- **Build in‑house**: develop a proprietary platform with controlled tech stack and brand‑safe content policies; lower upfront cost, full ownership of IP.  
- **Strategic partnership**: co‑market with the target or similar networks, sharing ad revenue while avoiding a large cash outlay.  
- **Acquire a smaller, profitable niche**: focus on a platform with proven monetisation and healthier margins.

**Fit** – The proposal does not clearly advance the conglomerate’s short‑term profitability goals; the high‑cost, high‑risk acquisition mis‑aligns with a prudent growth strategy.

**Premortem** – If the site fails to generate >$100 M annual revenue within two years, or a brand‑safety incident forces major advertisers to withdraw, the $580 M outlay becomes a sunk loss with reputational damage.

**Kill‑criteria** – 1) Revenue < $100 M after 24 months; 2) Any public brand‑safety breach leading to ≥5 % advertiser churn; 3) Unforeseen integration cost > $150 M.

Given the disproportionate price, significant brand‑safety exposure, uncertain monetisation, and viable lower‑cost alternatives, the acquisition does not meet a defensible risk‑adjusted return threshold.

**Decision:** the proposal should not be approved in its current form.

DECISION: NO-GO
