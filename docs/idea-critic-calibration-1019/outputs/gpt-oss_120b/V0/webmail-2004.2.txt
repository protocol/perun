**Steelman claim** – Launch a free web‑mail service offering each user 1 GB of storage, fast full‑text search and a responsive web UI, monetised by text ads automatically matched to message content, rolled out invitation‑only.

**Attack points**

1. **Storage & server cost** – 1 GB per user at today’s hardware prices (even in 2004) implies terabytes of disk for modest user bases. Without a clear break‑even model, the cash burn could outpace any ad revenue.  
2. **Privacy objection** – Scanning user mail to serve contextual ads raises legal and reputational risk; any breach could trigger regulatory action or mass user churn.  
3. **Market incumbency** – Established free providers already enjoy massive user bases and network effects; gaining traction with a “new” service will require heavy marketing spend, further inflating cost.  
4. **Zero launch revenue** – Relying solely on ad impressions before any user base is built creates a cash‑flow gap that may force premature monetisation or cut‑backs.

**Better‑ways / alternatives**

- Offer a modest 100 MB free tier (already common) and sell premium storage upgrades.  
- Use opt‑in ads or a paid‑premium, ad‑free tier to avoid privacy‑scan controversy.  
- Partner with an existing mail platform to piggy‑back on its infrastructure, reducing capex.

**Fit with strategy** – The proposal promises a differentiator (large storage) but conflicts with cost‑containment and privacy‑first principles that the executive team has adopted for new consumer products.

**Pre‑mortem** – If storage costs exceed $0.10 per GB per month, the service will lose > $5 M in the first year with 5 M users, triggering a shutdown. Privacy complaints could force a complete redesign within three months.

**Kill criteria** – Abort if projected monthly storage cost > $0.08/GB per user *or* if a privacy audit flags non‑compliance with data‑protection regulations.

Given the high‑cost, privacy, and competitive risks without a clear near‑term revenue path, the proposal should not be approved in its current form.

DECISION: NO-GO
