**Claim**  
Go public, use IPO proceeds for a national TV advertising blitz (including a major sports broadcast), acquire the largest online competitor, expand warehouse capacity, and sell many products below cost to win customers.

**Steelman**  
The company will raise capital via an IPO, fund a high‑visibility TV campaign to accelerate brand awareness, eliminate a key rival through acquisition, and increase fulfillment speed with more warehouses; aggressive pricing will capture market share quickly, leading to long‑term dominance.

**Assumptions**  
- Capital markets remain receptive and the IPO price is favorable.  
- TV advertising will generate enough incremental revenue to offset the loss‑leader pricing.  
- The competitor’s assets and customer base can be integrated without major disruption.  
- Cash burn from acquisition, warehousing, and advertising can be covered by IPO proceeds plus existing cash.

**Better‑Ways**  
1. Delay the IPO; first achieve sustainable positive gross margins on core SKUs.  
2. Replace costly TV spots with targeted digital marketing that has a lower CAC.  
3. Pursue a strategic partnership or minority stake in the competitor rather than a full acquisition.  
4. Focus on high‑margin, low‑shipping‑cost products before expanding warehousing.

**Kill‑Criteria**  
- Gross margin stays negative for > 5 % of total revenue after 12 months.  
- Cash runway falls below 6 months post‑IPO.  
- IPO fails to raise ≥ $150 M (targeted amount).  
- Acquisition cost exceeds 40 % of cash on hand.  

**Strongest Attack Survived**  
Selling many items below cost creates a structural loss that cannot be compensated by advertising or scale in the short term; the resulting cash burn would likely exceed the capital raised, leading to insolvency before the brand gains traction.

**Questions Parent Must Resolve**  
- What is the projected net cash flow after accounting for negative‑margin sales, acquisition financing, and advertising spend?  
- How will the company achieve a positive gross margin within 12 months?

**Dissent Ledger**  
The core pricing strategy is unsustainable; the combined financial risk of IPO, acquisition, and aggressive advertising outweighs the uncertain upside of market share gains.

**Remaining Risk**  
Even if the IPO succeeds, the company may still run out of cash due to continued negative margins and high operating expenses.

DECISION: NO-GO
