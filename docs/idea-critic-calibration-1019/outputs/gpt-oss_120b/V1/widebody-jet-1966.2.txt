**Steelman:** The company would develop a new four‑engine, ultra‑large wide‑body jet, built in a brand‑new factory and powered by a next‑generation high‑bypass engine. The raised cockpit allows a future freighter conversion, and the sole launch customer expects a dramatic reduction in cost per seat as air‑travel demand surges.

**Skeptic checks**
1. **Capital outlay:** The proposed factory and airframe program exceed any prior investment by several‑fold. The firm’s balance sheet and cash‑flow history show no precedent for funding such a project without external financing or a broad order book.
2. **Engine maturity:** The high‑bypass engine has not completed a full certification cycle; any delay or performance shortfall would cascade into the airframe schedule.
3. **Market demand:** Only one airline has placed an order. Historical analogues (e.g., the original 747) required a consortium of carriers to amortize development risk.
4. **Technological trend:** Industry forecasts in the mid‑1960s already anticipate supersonic transport (e.g., Concorde). Betting on a subsonic platform may expose the program to rapid obsolescence.

**Better‑way alternatives**
- **Incremental scaling:** Upgrade the existing airframe with stretched fuselage variants and improved engines, leveraging existing tooling and certification pathways.
- **Joint venture:** Share development cost and risk with other airlines or a rival manufacturer, securing multiple launch customers before ground‑up factory construction.
- **Do‑nothing:** Continue to improve seat‑density and operating efficiency on current models while monitoring supersonic progress.

**Kill‑criteria**
- Development cost > 3× historical maximum *and* no firm commitments from at least two additional airlines within 12 months.
- Engine certification not achieved 24 months before planned first flight.
- Projected cost‑per‑seat reduction < 15 % versus existing model after 5 years of service.

Given the unverified engine, singular customer base, massive capital exposure, and uncertain market direction, the proposal fails the fatal‑flaw test.

**Decision:** The plan should not be approved as presented.

DECISION: NO-GO
