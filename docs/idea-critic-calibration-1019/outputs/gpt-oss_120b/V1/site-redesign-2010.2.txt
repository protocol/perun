**Steelman**  
Rewrite the site from the ground up, launch a brand‑new front page that re‑weights story surfacing to favor publisher‑generated content, and migrate every existing user to this version in a single cut‑over.

**Attack**  
1. **Community trust** – The core value is democratic voting; changing the algorithm risks alienating the loyal base that fuels traffic.  
2. **Engineering risk** – A full rewrite entails unknown bugs, performance regressions, and schedule overruns; no incremental safety net exists.  
3. **Rollback impossibility** – Moving all users at once removes any ability to revert if critical issues appear, exposing the product to a single‑point failure.  
4. **Competitive pressure** – A rival community is gaining users; a mis‑step could accelerate their migration.

**Better‑Way Alternatives**  
- **Incremental redesign**: Refactor the front‑end and introduce a publisher‑content layer behind the existing voting engine, rolling out to a small user segment first.  
- **A/B test surfacing**: Deploy the new algorithm to 10 % of traffic, monitor engagement, and keep the original for the rest.  
- **Hybrid model**: Keep voting as the primary ranking signal while surfacing publisher content as a supplemental “recommended” lane, preserving community ethos.

**Fit with Strategy**  
Growth through publisher partnerships aligns with revenue goals, but the north‑star of a vibrant, community‑driven news feed is jeopardized. The proposal advances revenue at the expense of the platform’s unique value proposition.

**Pre‑mortem**  
If the launch breaks voting, a large fraction of active users could churn within days, handing the rival site a surge of power users and damaging brand reputation. Recovery would require a costly rebuild.

**Verdict**  
Given the high‑risk engineering scope, irreversible launch, and conflict with core community values, the proposal should not be approved in its current form.

DECISION: NO-GO
