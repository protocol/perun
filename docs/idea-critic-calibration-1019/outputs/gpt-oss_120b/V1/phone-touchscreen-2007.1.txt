The proposal targets a rapidly shifting market: consumers are moving from keypad phones to touch‑screen devices that integrate media, web, and email. The company already dominates two core components—PC hardware and digital music—so it can leverage brand strength and existing content ecosystems. Partnering with a single carrier that grants hardware‑software control and shares service revenue mitigates the “no‑phone‑experience” risk by providing distribution, billing, and network expertise while preserving product vision.  

Key risks and mitigations:  

* **Lack of phone‑industry expertise** – offset by carrier partnership and hiring of seasoned telecom engineers.  
* **Premium price without a physical keyboard** – early adopters value design and integration; pricing can be tiered after the launch.  
* **Exclusivity limiting reach** – the carrier’s market share (e.g., a major U.S. operator) supplies sufficient volume for a launch‑phase test; later expansion to other carriers is possible.  
* **No third‑party apps** – the core experience (music, web, email) is strong; an SDK can be released shortly after launch to seed an app ecosystem.  

The venture is not a one‑way door: the hardware can be discontinued or re‑engineered without sunk‑cost irrecoverability beyond R&D, and the carrier agreement can be renegotiated. Expected upside—capturing the emerging touch‑screen segment and creating a new revenue stream from service fees—outweighs the controllable risks if the company proceeds with a focused, carrier‑backed pilot.

DECISION: GO
