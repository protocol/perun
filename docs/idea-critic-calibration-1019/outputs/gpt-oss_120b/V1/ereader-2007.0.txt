**Steelman claim** – The company should design, manufacture, and sell a proprietary e‑reader with a paper‑like display and free cellular connectivity, allowing customers to browse and download books directly from its store at a low per‑title price.

**Attack & alternatives**  
1. **Do nothing / continue selling e‑books via existing platforms** – zero hardware risk, leverages existing customer base, preserves margins.  
2. **Partner or white‑label an existing e‑reader** – uses proven hardware, reduces R&D cost, lets the company focus on content and pricing.  
3. **Launch a mobile app for smartphones/tablets** – taps the already‑massive device market, requires only software work, avoids hardware cannibalisation.

All three alternatives meet the core goal (selling e‑books) at far lower cost and risk.

**Fit with strategy** – The proposal stretches the firm beyond its core competency (bookselling) into consumer hardware, a domain where it has no track record. The strategic benefit is unclear versus the strategic cost of diverting resources.

**Premortem scenarios**  
- **Design / production delays**: lack of hardware expertise leads to missed market windows.  
- **Cost overruns**: free cellular and paper‑like screen drive unit cost high, forcing a low selling price that erodes margins.  
- **Publisher push‑back**: low per‑title pricing may trigger demands for higher royalties or refusal to supply titles.  
- **Cannibalisation**: customers shift from higher‑margin print to low‑margin e‑books, reducing overall profitability.  
- **Market rejection**: prior e‑readers performed poorly; a new device may face the same consumer indifference.

These are one‑way‑door risks that can be partially mitigated only after significant sunk investment.

**Evidence gaps & kill‑criteria** – The proposal provides no quantitative targets (e.g., minimum units sold, margin thresholds, publisher agreement metrics). Without measurable “stop” criteria, there is no way to abort the project before losses become irreversible.

**Conclusion** – Given the high hardware risk, lack of clear financial justification, strong alternative paths that achieve the same objective with far less exposure, and the absence of concrete kill‑criteria, the plan should not be approved in its current form.

DECISION: NO-GO
