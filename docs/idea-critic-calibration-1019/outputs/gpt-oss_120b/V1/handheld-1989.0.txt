**Steelman claim**  
Launch a $89.95 monochrome‑screen handheld with a modest processor, long battery life, a bundled puzzle game and a $20 M marketing push.

**Skeptic checks**  
- **Screen value**: 1989 consumers already expect color on handhelds; a monochrome screen is a clear competitive disadvantage.  
- **Hardware**: “Modest processor” risks unplayable third‑party titles and poor performance perception.  
- **Content**: Prior devices sold only one game; a single puzzle title is unlikely to drive repeat purchases or justify the price.  
- **Margin**: $89.95 retail leaves little room after manufacturing, distribution and $20 M marketing; without volume, profit is negative.

**Better‑way alternatives**  
1. Use a low‑cost color LCD (≈ $5 extra per unit) and raise price to $99.95, preserving margin while matching rivals.  
2. Bundle a small library (3–4 games) to increase perceived value without large cost increase.  
3. Delay launch 6 months to benefit from falling LCD prices and prototype a dual‑mode (color/monochrome) option.

**Kill‑criteria (go‑no‑go triggers)**  
- First‑year unit sales < 100 000.  
- Gross margin < 5 % after accounting for marketing spend.  
- Competitor releases a new color handheld within 6 months, eroding market share.

**Pre‑mortem**  
If the device sells poorly, the sunk tooling cost and marketing outlay will damage the company’s cash flow, and the brand may be associated with a “budget‑only” handheld, harming future premium products.

**Conclusion**  
Given the strong competitive disadvantage of a monochrome screen, insufficient content offering, and thin margins, the proposal should not be approved in its current form. It requires a redesign toward color display and a richer game bundle before proceeding.

DECISION: NO-GO
