**Steelman claim** – Re‑engineer the site from the ground up, launch a brand‑new front page that reshapes story surfacing to prioritize publisher‑curated content, and switch every user to the new version in a single, simultaneous rollout.

**Attack** –  
1. **Community core value loss** – The existing voting mechanic is the social glue; removing or diluting it risks alienating the loyal base that fuels traffic.  
2. **Engineering risk** – A full rewrite carries high defect density, unknown performance characteristics, and likely schedule overruns.  
3. **No rollback** – An all‑users cut‑over provides no safety net; any critical failure forces a painful migration back to legacy code, if that even exists.  
4. **Competitive pressure** – A rival community is gaining momentum; a disruptive launch could accelerate user migration away.

**Better‑way alternatives** –  
- **Incremental redesign**: Refactor the front page behind a feature flag, A/B test with 5‑10 % of traffic, preserve voting for the majority, and iterate based on real‑world metrics.  
- **Hybrid launch**: Deploy the new engine in parallel, allow users to opt‑in, and retain the legacy version for fallback.  
- **Publisher partnership pilot**: Introduce publisher‑boosted slots as an optional module rather than a wholesale surfacing change.

**Fit with strategy** – Growing traffic aligns with the north‑star, but preserving community trust is a non‑negotiable constraint. The proposed all‑or‑nothing rewrite violates that constraint.

**Pre‑mortem / kill criteria** – If after one week the active user count drops ≥5 % or sentiment surveys show ≥20 % negative feedback, abort the rollout and revert to the legacy UI. Also abort if engineering delays exceed three months.

**Conclusion** – The risks outweigh the potential upside; a phased, reversible approach is required to protect the core community and manage engineering uncertainty.

DECISION: NO-GO
