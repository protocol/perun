**Steelman** – Acquire the small software firm for a few hundred million dollars, adopt its modern object‑oriented OS as the new platform, and retain the founder as an advisor to steer integration.

**Attack & Alternatives**  
1. **Do nothing / continue internal rebuild** – Already failed twice; unlikely to succeed without fresh technology.  
2. **License an existing commercial OS** – Higher upfront cost but proven ecosystem, lower integration risk, and no founder‑related cultural friction.  
3. **Partner rather than acquire** – Share risk, retain flexibility, and avoid paying a premium for a company with minimal market traction.

**Fit with Strategy** – The core goal is to halt market‑share loss by delivering a competitive OS. A full acquisition adds a large, untested asset and cultural risk, mis‑aligning with the need for rapid, reliable turnaround.

**Premortem** – If the migration stalls, legacy applications break, and the founder clashes with senior leadership, the company will waste the acquisition price, delay product launch, and further erode market share. This is a **one‑way‑door**: the cash outlay cannot be recovered and the brand may be tarnished.

**Evidence & Kill‑Criteria**  
- Target’s revenue is negligible (“few units sold”), so price‑to‑revenue ratio > 100×.  
- Migration risk is high: moving existing developers to an unfamiliar kernel typically incurs > 30 % productivity loss for at least 12 months.  
- Founder’s “difficult history” is a known cultural hazard that historically drives post‑acquisition turnover.  

**Kill‑Criteria** – If post‑acquisition integration cost exceeds 20 % of the purchase price *or* if the new OS fails to ship within 18 months, the bet should be aborted.

**Decision** – The proposal’s upside is outweighed by financial, technical, and cultural risks, especially given a viable licensing alternative with lower downside. The recommendation should be revised to explore licensing or partnership before committing to acquisition.

DECISION: NO-GO
