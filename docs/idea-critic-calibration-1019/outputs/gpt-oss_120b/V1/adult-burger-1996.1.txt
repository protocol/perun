**Steelman:** The chain hopes a premium adult‑focused burger will lift average ticket size, attract a new, higher‑margin customer segment, and reposition the brand beyond its child‑centric image. A massive ad budget is intended to overcome existing perception barriers quickly.

**Attack & Alternatives:**  
1. **Do nothing** – stay in the strong, proven family market.  
2. **Incremental adult items** – introduce a modestly priced specialty sandwich or limited‑time “grown‑up” menu with modest promotion, allowing demand testing.  
3. **Partnership/Co‑brand** – collaborate with an established premium burger brand to borrow credibility without full brand risk.

Both alternatives cost far less, are reversible, and generate real demand data before a full launch.

**Fit:** The proposal conflicts with the core brand identity (family‑friendly, value‑oriented). Shifting perception requires more than a single product; it needs a broader brand overhaul, which the plan does not address.

**Premortem:**  
- **Brand dilution:** Existing families may feel alienated, reducing foot traffic.  
- **Price resistance:** Adults may not trust a fast‑food chain for premium quality, leading to low uptake.  
- **Budget waste:** The unprecedented ad spend could burn cash without proven demand, jeopardizing other initiatives.

**Evidence Gaps:** No market research, price‑elasticity data, or pilot results are presented. Assumptions about adult willingness to pay premium at a fast‑food venue remain unverified.

**Kill‑criteria (suggested):** If, after a 6‑week regional pilot, weekly premium‑burger sales average <5 % of total sales or ROI <0, abort rollout and revert ad spend.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a limited, low‑risk pilot rather than a full‑scale, high‑budget launch. The current proposal lacks evidence, carries high irreversible cost, and threatens core brand equity.

DECISION: NO-GO
