The proposal targets a clear market gap: a rival’s premium handheld threatens our share, yet price‑sensitive consumers and casual gamers remain underserved. By introducing a dual‑screen device—one touch‑enabled—at a lower price point, the company can differentiate on interaction rather than raw graphics, preserving the core‑player base with the existing model still on sale.

**Strengths**
* **Audience expansion** – Touch and dual screens open new game genres (puzzle, educational, casual) that attract non‑core users.
* **Price advantage** – Under‑cutting the rival reduces the barrier for price‑sensitive buyers and leverages existing manufacturing capacity.
* **Brand continuity** – Keeping the current handheld on shelves avoids alienating loyal customers and provides a fallback for titles that do not need the new hardware.

**Risks & Mitigations**
1. **Developer confusion** – Unusual hardware can be eased with a unified SDK, clear documentation, and early‑access dev kits. A dedicated developer portal will reduce integration friction.
2. **Weaker graphics** – Position the device as “experience‑focused” rather than “graphics‑focused.” Emphasize unique gameplay enabled by touch and dual screens.
3. **Unproven touch market** – Pilot a small set of touch‑centric titles (e.g., puzzle, drawing) to demonstrate viability and generate buzz before a full launch.
4. **Added cost of a second screen** – Bulk‑order LCD panels and negotiate volume discounts; offset cost through the lower price tier and by retaining sales of the older, cheaper model.
5. **Gimmick perception** – Pair the hardware launch with a strong software lineup that showcases genuine use cases for dual screens, avoiding “novelty‑only” marketing.

**Strategic Fit**
The move aligns with a broader diversification strategy, hedges against the rival’s graphics lead, and creates a platform that can later evolve (e.g., downloadable content, third‑party media). The risk profile is manageable with the outlined mitigations, and the potential upside—capturing a larger, more varied user base—justifies proceeding.

**Conclusion**
Approve the launch, while allocating resources to developer support, a curated launch‑title slate, and cost‑control on the second display.

DECISION: GO
