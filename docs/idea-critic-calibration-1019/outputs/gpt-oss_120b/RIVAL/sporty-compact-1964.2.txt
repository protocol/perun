**Executive Summary**  
The proposal leverages an existing reliable compact platform to create a sporty two‑door coupe/convertible aimed at post‑war baby‑boomers seeking style on a budget. It promises a low base price, extensive options, and a massive launch campaign.

**Opportunities**  
1. **Market Gap** – Young buyers are entering the market and historically gravitate toward affordable, stylish vehicles.  
2. **Cost Efficiency** – Using the compact car’s mechanicals reduces R&D and tooling expense.  
3. **Brand Refresh** – A sporty image could rejuvenate the brand and attract a new demographic.

**Key Risks**  
1. **Unproven Volume Demand** – No evidence that a sporty small‑car segment can sustain high sales; a mis‑read market could leave excess inventory.  
2. **Perceived Cheapness** – Sharing parts with a low‑priced economy model may erode the premium look required for a “sporty” image, undermining the styling goal.  
3. **Heavy Up‑Front Advertising** – A large launch spend before any sales traction creates a cash‑flow risk; if demand falters, the campaign cost becomes sunk.

**Mitigation Gaps**  
- The proposal does not include a phased rollout, market test, or pilot production to validate demand.  
- No strategy is outlined to differentiate the new model visually or qualitatively from the base car beyond body styling.  
- Advertising spend is not tied to performance milestones, leaving no control mechanism.

**Recommendation**  
Approve the concept only after a **validation phase**: conduct limited‑run market testing in key regions, develop a distinct visual package that masks shared mechanicals, and tie advertising spend to early sales targets. Without these safeguards, the financial exposure is high and the brand could be damaged by a perceived cheap, under‑selling product.

**Decision**  
Given the current lack of demand validation, potential brand dilution, and unbounded launch cost, the proposal should not proceed in its present form.

DECISION: NO-GO
