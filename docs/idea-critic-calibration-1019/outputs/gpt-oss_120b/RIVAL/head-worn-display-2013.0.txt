The proposal targets a niche “early‑adopter” market at a premium price, yet it attempts to evolve into a mass‑consumer product without solving three critical blockers:

1. **Privacy** – A always‑on camera on a head‑worn device is a direct privacy threat to by‑standers and users alike. Regulations (e.g., GDPR, CCPA) and public sentiment demand explicit, revocable recording controls, on‑device indicators, and data‑minimization. No technical or policy safeguards are described.

2. **Value proposition** – The device’s core capabilities (camera, voice, screen) lack a compelling everyday use case. Without a clear “why wear this now?” scenario—such as hands‑free navigation, contextual AR assistance, or enterprise workflow support—most consumers will see it as a novelty that does not justify $1,500.

3. **Social acceptance & ergonomics** – Wearing a conspicuous camera in public will provoke discomfort and possible legal pushback. The design must address visual subtlety, optional cover‑modes, and social‑norm guidelines. Battery life is also cited as “short” but no target or mitigation (e.g., power‑efficient hardware, hot‑swap modules) is provided.

**Risks vs. Rewards** – At $1,500 the price ceiling limits adoption to enthusiasts with disposable income. Early‑adopter revenue may cover R&D, but scaling to “everyday consumer” will require a price drop of at least 40‑50 % and a demonstrable utility that integrates into daily routines (e.g., hands‑free video calls, real‑time translation, on‑the‑fly documentation).

**Recommendations before proceeding**  
- Conduct a privacy‑by‑design audit and embed visible, user‑controlled recording shutters.  
- Define three concrete, high‑impact use cases and prototype them with target users.  
- Engineer a battery that supports ≥8 hours of typical mixed use, or provide easy hot‑swap packs.  
- Re‑price the early‑adopter tier to ≤$800 or bundle with subscription services that offset cost.  
- Run a limited “privacy‑shield” pilot with consented participants to gather social‑acceptance data.

Without addressing these gaps, the launch risks regulatory backlash, poor market reception, and brand damage. The executive team should pause and iterate on the proposal.

DECISION: NO-GO
