The proposal targets a new market segment—young post‑war baby‑boom buyers—by turning a reliable, inexpensive compact car into a sporty two‑door coupe/convertible. The core idea leverages existing mechanical components, which should keep production costs low and enable a low base price. A long options list offers customization, and an aggressive advertising push aims to create demand quickly.

**Potential upside**
- Uses existing platform → lower tooling and parts‑supply costs.
- Low base price plus many options can attract price‑sensitive buyers while allowing higher‑margin accessories.
- A strong launch campaign could generate buzz and capture early adopters before competitors respond.

**Key risks**
1. **Unproven demand** – No evidence that a high‑volume, sporty compact will sell at scale; the market may prefer established sportier models or remain loyal to the plain compact.
2. **Brand perception** – Sharing parts with a cheap economy car could make the new coupe look inexpensive, undermining the “sporty” positioning and reducing willingness to pay a premium.
3. **Financial exposure** – A very large launch advertising budget commits significant capital before any sales data exist, increasing break‑even pressure.

**Mitigations to consider**
- Conduct limited‑region pilot production to test demand before full‑scale launch.
- Introduce distinct styling cues (e.g., unique front fascia, interior trim) that differentiate the coupe from the plain compact, reducing cheap‑car perception.
- Phase advertising spend: start with targeted, lower‑cost media and scale up only after early sales indicators meet targets.

Given the current lack of market validation and the financial risk of a large upfront ad spend, proceeding without further demand testing and a clearer differentiation plan could jeopardize profitability.

**Recommendation:** Hold the full launch until a pilot program validates demand and the styling/marketing plan is refined to protect the brand’s sportier image.

DECISION: NO-GO
