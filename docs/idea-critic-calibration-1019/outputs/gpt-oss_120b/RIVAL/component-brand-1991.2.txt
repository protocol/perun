The proposal tries to shift a traditionally B2B product into the consumer spotlight. While raising brand awareness could eventually let end‑users request the chip by name, the plan carries three major, unmitigated risks:

1. **Unprecedented consumer advertising** – In 1991 no component was marketed directly to shoppers. The market’s ability to translate a logo into purchase intent is unproven, making ROI highly uncertain.  
2. **Large budget share** – Paying a portion of computer makers’ ad spend consumes a significant slice of the chip maker’s marketing budget without a clear measurement framework. This reduces flexibility for other critical initiatives.  
3. **Potential partner backlash** – Computer makers may view the co‑funded logo requirement as interference in their messaging, risking strained relationships or reduced cooperation.

Without concrete mechanisms to test consumer response, caps on spend, or guarantees of partner alignment, the initiative is likely to expend resources for an ambiguous payoff. A more prudent approach would be to run a limited pilot, gather data on brand recall, and negotiate clear co‑marketing terms before scaling.

Given the current scope and risk profile, the proposal should not be approved as‑is.

DECISION: NO-GO
