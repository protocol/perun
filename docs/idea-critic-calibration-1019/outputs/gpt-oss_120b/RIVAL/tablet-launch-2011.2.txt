The plan proposes a flagship‑priced tablet running an OS that currently has almost no market share, a tiny app catalog, and a brand perceived as unproven. Even with heavy developer outreach and advertising, the product will face three entrenched barriers:

2. **Market Dominance** – The leader’s dominance creates a high barrier to entry; price‑parity alone does not overcome brand loyalty or network effects. Competing on price risks a race to the bottom without a differentiated value proposition.

3. **Organizational Instability** – Leadership transition adds execution risk. Shoppers’ perception of the product as “unproven” will amplify concerns about long‑term support and software updates.

Heavy advertising can raise awareness but cannot substitute for a viable app ecosystem or clear differentiation. The proposal lacks a concrete plan to close the app‑catalog gap before launch, and it does not address how to mitigate the leadership transition’s impact on product rollout.

Given these constraints and the high probability of poor market reception, the prudent course is to pause the launch, strengthen the OS ecosystem, and define a differentiated positioning (e.g., niche use‑cases, unique hardware features, or enterprise focus) before committing to a market‑leader‑priced tablet.
