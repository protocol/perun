**Executive Summary**  
The discount‑store chain (DSC) and the older department‑store/appliance chain (ODC) are both in decline, with aging store footprints and under‑invested assets. The proposal calls for a 2005 acquisition, corporate integration, selective store conversion, and brand‑sale into DSC locations. While the deal could generate modest scale economies, the upside is outweighed by several structural and execution risks.

**Key Risks**  
1. **Parallel Revenue Decline** – Both businesses are losing market share; merging them does not reverse the underlying demand erosion.  
2. **Asset Deterioration** – Store sites are old, requiring capital upgrades to meet shopper expectations; the cost of refurbishment may exceed projected savings.  
3. **Cost‑Savings Uncertainty** – Consolidating corporate functions can cut overhead, but integration costs (systems, culture, labor contracts) are hard to quantify and may erode any benefit.  
4. **Customer Mismatch** – DSC targets value‑seeking shoppers, whereas ODC serves a more traditional, higher‑margin segment. Brand‑sale into discount stores risks diluting DSC’s value proposition and alienating core customers.  
5. **Competitive Pressure** – Big‑box rivals are expanding aggressively, leveraging superior logistics and omnichannel capabilities that the combined entity lacks.

**Strategic Alternatives**  
- **Organic Turnaround** – Focus on modernizing DSC stores, improving inventory, and enhancing price‑match policies before pursuing a large acquisition.  
- **Selective Asset Purchase** – Acquire only high‑performing ODC locations that complement DSC’s geography, avoiding full brand integration.  
- **Strategic Partnership** – Explore joint‑venture arrangements for shared sourcing or logistics to capture economies of scale without full merger risk.

**Recommendation**  
Given the compounded decline, uncertain synergies, and high integration cost, the proposed full acquisition is premature. The board should pause the deal, conduct a deep financial and operational due‑diligence study, and consider smaller, targeted asset purchases or partnership models.

**Conclusion**  
Approve a hold on the current proposal and require a revised plan that addresses the identified risks and presents a clearer, phased integration strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
