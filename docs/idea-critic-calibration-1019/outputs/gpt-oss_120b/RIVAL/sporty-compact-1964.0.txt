The proposal targets a clear gap: a youthful segment that values sportier looks but cannot afford premium pricing. Leveraging the existing compact‑car platform keeps tooling costs low and promises reliable mechanical performance, which is a strong foundation for any new model.

However, three material risks outweigh the upside at this stage:

1. **Unproven volume demand** – No market data show that a low‑priced sporty small car can sell in the volumes needed to amortize the large advertising outlay. A niche enthusiast market would not justify the scale of the launch spend.

2. **Brand perception** – Sharing parts with a known “cheap” economy model will likely transfer that cheap image to the new coupe/convertible, undermining the intended sporty appeal and eroding price premium potential.

3. **Heavy upfront spend** – A massive advertising campaign before any sales signal creates a cash‑flow mismatch. If demand falls short, the company could be left with high sunk costs and excess inventory.

Mitigation would require a phased rollout: a limited‑run pilot in a few regions, a modest advertising spend focused on test markets, and a distinct visual package that masks the shared mechanical underpinnings. Without such safeguards, the financial exposure is high and the brand risk significant.

Given the current information, the strategic upside does not yet outweigh the quantified risks.

DECISION: NO-GO
