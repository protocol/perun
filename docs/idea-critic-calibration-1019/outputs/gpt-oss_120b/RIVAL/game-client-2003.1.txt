**Executive Summary**  
The proposal aims to modernize post‑sale support by forcing all new titles through a proprietary online client that supplies updates, anti‑cheat verification, and digital sales, later expanding into a multi‑publisher storefront. It promises tighter control over patches and cheating, and a new revenue channel.

**Key Benefits**  
- Centralized update delivery eliminates the need for manual patch distribution.  
- Built‑in anti‑cheat can protect competitive integrity and reduce support tickets.  
- A storefront opens a recurring‑revenue stream and positions the company as a platform.

**Critical Risks (2003 context)**  
1. **Player Acceptance** – Mandatory online accounts and large downloads clash with the era’s strong preference for offline, boxed experiences. Early‑adopter backlash could damage brand loyalty.  
2. **Retail Partner Relations** – Retailers may view the client as a threat to physical sales and could drop boxed inventory, cutting a proven distribution channel.  
3. **Technical Maturity** – Rolling out a brand‑new client at scale introduces bugs that could block game launches, eroding trust.  
4. **Infrastructure Limits** – Home broadband penetration is uneven; many users still rely on dial‑up, making large downloads impractical.  
5. **Competitive Replication** – The concept is relatively low‑barrier; rivals could copy the model, eroding any first‑mover advantage.

**Mitigation Recommendations**  
- Pilot the client with a single high‑profile title, offering an offline “demo” mode for users without reliable internet.  
- Negotiate revenue‑share agreements with retailers to keep boxed sales viable.  
- Invest heavily in QA and staged roll‑outs to catch critical bugs before full release.  
- Provide optional lightweight patches for low‑bandwidth users.  

Given the substantial user‑experience and partner risks in the current market, proceeding without a controlled pilot and clear mitigation plans would be premature.

**Decision**: Hold the full rollout until the pilot validates acceptance and technical stability.

DECISION: NO-GO
