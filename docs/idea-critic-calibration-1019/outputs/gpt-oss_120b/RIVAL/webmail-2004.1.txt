The proposal promises a compelling user experience—gigabyte‑scale mailboxes, rapid full‑text search, and a snappy web UI—while keeping the service free through contextual text ads. However, the risks outweigh the upside given the 2004 context:

* **Storage cost:** In 2004 a gigabyte of disk still cost several dollars per GB. Scaling to even a modest user base would create unsustainable infrastructure expenses, especially for a free service with no initial revenue.
* **Privacy concerns:** Scanning every message to extract ad‑relevant keywords would likely violate user expectations and emerging privacy regulations, provoking backlash and possible legal exposure.
* **Market dynamics:** Established players (e.g., Hotmail, Yahoo! Mail, Gmail’s early beta) already dominate the free webmail space. Gaining market share would require aggressive differentiation, yet the proposed differentiation (larger storage, ads) is either costly or potentially off‑putting to users accustomed to ad‑free experiences.
* **Revenue timing:** Relying on text ads that depend on content analysis postpones any cash flow until sufficient volume and ad‑matching accuracy are achieved—both uncertain without a sizable user base.

A more viable path would start with a modest storage quota (tens of megabytes), avoid content‑based ad targeting, and focus on a compelling search UI to attract early adopters. Revenue could later be introduced via premium upgrades or non‑intrusive advertising once a critical mass is reached.

Given the high operational cost, privacy risk, and lack of immediate monetisation, the plan is not ready for launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
