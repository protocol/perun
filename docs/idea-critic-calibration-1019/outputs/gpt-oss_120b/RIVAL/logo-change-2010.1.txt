The proposal tries to modernize a mature retailer’s visual identity by swapping a long‑standing logo for a minimalist wordmark plus a small blue square. While a fresh look can attract new customers, the change carries several high‑impact risks that are not mitigated in the plan:

1. **Brand equity loss** – The existing square‑in‑dark‑blue is the primary visual cue for the brand. Decades of exposure mean loyal shoppers and the broader market instantly associate that shape with the retailer. Removing it without transition erodes that equity.

2. **Customer backlash** – Social media amplifies negative sentiment within hours. A silent, simultaneous rollout gives no time for the brand to frame the change, answer questions, or address concerns, increasing the likelihood of viral criticism.

3. **Lack of validation** – No customer testing or focus‑group data are presented. Without evidence that the new wordmark resonates, the retailer risks alienating its core audience and confusing new prospects.

4. **Operational risk** – Updating every channel at once amplifies the chance of inconsistent application (e.g., missed assets on legacy platforms), leading to a fragmented brand experience.

A more prudent approach would be a phased rollout: pilot the new logo in a limited market or digital channel, gather quantitative and qualitative feedback, and adjust the design if needed. Complement the launch with a clear communication plan that explains the rationale and celebrates brand heritage while highlighting the modern direction.

Given the absence of testing, communication, and a staged deployment, the current proposal exposes the brand to unnecessary reputational damage and operational friction. Approving it as written would be premature.

DECISION: NO-GO
