The airline’s request targets a dramatic reduction in cost per seat by scaling the aircraft to roughly 2.5 × the size of the current model. While the market outlook for air travel is strong, the proposal introduces several material risks that outweigh the potential upside at this stage:

1. **Capital intensity** – Building a new factory and tooling for a program larger than any the company has previously undertaken will require a massive upfront outlay. With only one confirmed customer, the return on this investment is highly uncertain.

2. **Engine immaturity** – The high‑bypass engine promised by the supplier has no flight history. Engine reliability and performance are critical for a four‑engine wide‑body; any delay or failure would cascade into the airframe program and erode the airline’s confidence.

3. **Market dynamics** – Industry forecasts in 1966 already anticipate supersonic airliners supplanting subsonic jets for premium routes. Betting on a larger subsonic platform could leave the product stranded as airlines shift toward faster, more efficient concepts.

4. **Customer concentration** – With a single airline as the launch customer, the program lacks a diversified order book. Any change in that airline’s strategic direction would jeopardize the entire venture.

5. **Conversion flexibility** – Designing a raised cockpit for future freighter conversion adds complexity without guaranteeing a secondary market. The conversion path will only be valuable if a robust cargo demand emerges later.

Given these factors, proceeding now would expose the company to excessive financial risk, supply‑chain uncertainty, and potential market obsolescence. A more prudent approach would be to:

* Conduct a detailed market study confirming long‑term demand for a larger subsonic aircraft.
* Secure commitments from additional airlines to diversify the order book.
* Validate the engine technology through a joint development program before committing to full‑scale production.
* Explore incremental capacity upgrades to the existing platform as a lower‑risk alternative.

Until these mitigations are in place, the proposal should not be approved for immediate execution.

DECISION: NO-GO
