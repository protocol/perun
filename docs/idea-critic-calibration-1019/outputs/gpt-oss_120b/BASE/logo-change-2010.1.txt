**Assessment**

The retailer’s desire to appear contemporary is understandable, yet the proposed logo change carries significant brand‑equity risk. The current logo – white capital letters inside a dark‑blue square – has been the visual anchor for decades, meaning it is embedded in customer memory, store signage, packaging, and digital assets. Removing it abruptly, without a transition or customer validation, threatens to erode that equity.

**Key concerns**

1. **Customer attachment** – Long‑time shoppers often see a logo as a guarantee of quality and consistency. An unannounced swap may be perceived as a loss of heritage, prompting negative sentiment.

2. **Speed of backlash** – Social media amplifies any perceived misstep instantly. A silent rollout leaves no opportunity to shape the narrative, increasing the likelihood of viral criticism.

3. **Brand recognizability** – The dark‑blue square is a distinctive visual cue. Replacing it with a neutral wordmark reduces visual distinctiveness, especially in crowded retail environments where quick identification matters.

4. **Lack of validation** – No evidence is presented that the new wordmark resonates with target audiences, nor that it performs better in recall or purchase intent studies. Without such data, the change is a gamble.

**Mitigation options**

- Conduct controlled testing (focus groups, A/B digital tests) to gauge reactions and refine the design.
- Develop a phased rollout: introduce the new element alongside the legacy logo for a defined period, allowing customers to acclimate.
- Create a communication plan that explains the rationale, celebrates the brand’s heritage, and highlights the benefits of the refresh.
- Prepare contingency assets (e.g., co‑branding materials) to revert quickly if negative feedback spikes.

**Conclusion**

Given the high stakes of brand perception and the absence of customer testing, proceeding with an immediate, unannounced, full‑scale launch is premature. The proposal should be revised to incorporate research, a gradual transition, and a proactive communication strategy before any final decision to replace the logo.

DECISION: NO-GO
