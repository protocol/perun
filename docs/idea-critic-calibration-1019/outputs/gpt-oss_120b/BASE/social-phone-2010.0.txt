The target market—teenagers who live on social networks—expects immediate access to a rich ecosystem of apps, frequent software updates, and low‑cost data. Existing rival smartphones already provide extensive app catalogs and affordable data bundles tailored to this segment. Launching a device without an application store will leave the product functionally incomplete at release, creating a perception of “unfinished” that is difficult to overcome, especially for a demographic that equates value with app variety.

Pricing the mandatory monthly data plan at the level of a full‑smartphone plan further reduces appeal. Teens and their families are price‑sensitive; a high‑cost plan will push them toward competitors offering cheaper, flexible options. Even if the hardware is attractive, the total cost of ownership will be a decisive barrier.

Choosing a brand‑new, in‑house operating system adds technical risk and delays. The company’s own mobile platform is already established; diverting resources to a separate OS fragments development effort and lengthens time to market, while also requiring a new developer community to be built from scratch. Competing against entrenched OS ecosystems will require substantial additional investment in SDKs, documentation, and developer incentives—none of which are addressed in the current plan.

The sunk two‑year development effort is a financial loss, not a justification to proceed. Continuing with a product that lacks core market expectations increases the likelihood of poor sales, inventory write‑offs, and damage to brand reputation among young consumers.

A more viable approach would be to:

1. Leverage the existing mobile platform to reduce development time and benefit from its app ecosystem.
2. Secure a partnership with multiple carriers to offer competitive, tiered data plans.
3. Ensure an app store is available at launch, even if limited, with a roadmap for rapid expansion.
4. Re‑evaluate pricing to align with teenage purchasing power.

Given the current misalignment with market expectations and the high risk of failure, the proposal should not be approved in its present form.

DECISION: NO-GO
