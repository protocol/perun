The proposal tackles a classic “invisible‑component” problem: the chip maker’s product is hidden from end‑users, so brand equity cannot be leveraged and price competition erodes margins. A consumer‑oriented brand, supported by a co‑funded advertising program, could create demand pull—buyers asking for the chip by name would give computer manufacturers a clear incentive to source it, potentially allowing the chip maker to command premium pricing and protect market share.

**Key strengths**

1. **Differentiation** – A recognizable logo and name would distinguish the chip from indistinguishable clones, turning a technical spec into a consumer‑facing benefit (performance, reliability, longevity).  
2. **Channel leverage** – By sharing advertising costs, the chip maker reduces the financial barrier for computer makers, encouraging them to display the logo and, in turn, to champion the chip in their own marketing.  
3. **Long‑term margin upside** – If the brand gains traction, the chip maker can move from a pure volume play to a value‑add positioning, supporting higher ASPs and protecting against commoditization.

**Critical concerns**

1. **Unprecedented consumer focus** – No comparable component has been marketed directly to end‑users; consumer awareness may be slow to build, and the ROI timeline is uncertain.  
2. **Budget allocation** – The program consumes a sizable slice of the current marketing spend, reducing resources for other strategic initiatives (e.g., OEM relationship development, R&D).  
3. **Partner friction** – Computer makers could view the co‑funded logo as intrusive, fearing loss of control over their own brand messaging. Resistance may manifest as reduced participation or even a shift to alternative suppliers that do not impose such requirements.  
4. **Measurement difficulty** – Isolating the impact of the logo on sales versus other variables (price, product cycles) will be challenging, making it hard to prove success to the board.

**Mitigation steps**

- **Pilot phase** – Launch the program with a limited group of cooperative computer makers (e.g., two major OEMs) to test consumer response and partner acceptance before a full rollout.  
- **Clear value proposition** – Provide partners with pre‑approved creative assets that integrate the logo without detracting from their own branding, emphasizing joint benefit (“Your brand + our trusted chip”).  
- **Performance metrics** – Define leading indicators (search volume for the chip name, ad recall, OEM sales lift) to track early impact and allow rapid course correction.  
- **Budget caps** – Set a maximum percentage of the total marketing budget for the co‑funded program, preserving flexibility for other priorities.

Given the strategic importance of preventing commoditization and the potential upside of a differentiated consumer brand, the proposal merits moving forward, but only after a controlled pilot that limits exposure and tests partner receptivity.

DECISION: GO
