**Assessment Overview**

The device combines a head‑worn display, camera, voice control and a small screen at a launch price of $1,500. Targeting early adopters first is sensible for a novel form factor, but several critical concerns arise from the facts presented.

**Market Viability**

* Early‑adopter segments in 2013 were small and price‑sensitive; $1,500 places the product above most comparable consumer electronics (e.g., tablets, smartphones).  
* No defined “everyday” use case is articulated. Without a clear utility—such as hands‑free navigation, real‑time translation, or enterprise workflow support—adoption beyond enthusiasts is unlikely.

**Privacy & Social Acceptance**

* A continuously active camera on a head‑worn device triggers immediate privacy objections. In 2013, public awareness of surveillance was rising, and legislation in many jurisdictions was beginning to address recording in public spaces.  
* Social stigma attached to visibly “recording” devices can deter usage in public, limiting the product to private or controlled environments—contradicting the “everyday consumer” positioning.

**Technical Constraints**

* Short battery life directly undermines the promised “everyday” experience; users will be forced to recharge frequently, reducing convenience.  
* Voice control reliability in noisy outdoor settings is uncertain, potentially diminishing core interaction pathways.

**Financial Exposure**

* Manufacturing a head‑worn display with integrated camera and battery at a $1,500 price point implies modest margins, especially if volume remains low during the invitation‑only phase.  
* Scaling to a mass market would require price reductions that may not be achievable without redesign, further eroding profitability.

**Risk Mitigation Needs**

* A clear, differentiated use case (e.g., professional field work, medical imaging assistance) should be validated before broader release.  
* Privacy safeguards (e.g., LED indicator, opt‑in recording, data encryption) must be built and communicated.  
* Battery capacity must be improved or usage scenarios limited to short bursts.  
* Pricing strategy should be revisited, possibly through tiered hardware or subscription models.

**Recommendation**

Given the high price, unresolved privacy concerns, limited everyday utility, and technical shortcomings, proceeding with the current plan would expose the company to market rejection and reputational risk. The proposal should be revised to address these gaps before any launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
