The company’s strengths—global PC brand, the world‑leading digital music player, deep expertise in hardware design, and a loyal music‑centric customer base—provide a compelling foundation for entering the handset market. A premium, all‑touchscreen device that bundles a full web browser, email and the company’s music player could differentiate it from the keyboard‑centric phones that dominate today and pre‑empt carrier‑added music players that threaten iPod sales.

Key risks are real but manageable. Lack of phone‑industry experience can be offset by hiring seasoned handset engineers and leveraging existing R&D for the music player and UI. The premium price is justified by the integrated ecosystem and the brand’s cachet; early adopters are willing to pay for a differentiated experience. Exclusivity with a single carrier limits reach, yet it secures the critical concession of hardware‑software control and a revenue‑share model that can fund ongoing development. The absence of third‑party apps is a short‑term limitation; a developer SDK can be released within months, and the company can seed the marketplace with its own services.

Mitigation steps—appoint an experienced mobile‑technology leadership team, sign a multi‑year carrier agreement with performance milestones, launch a developer program, and plan a phased rollout to additional carriers after the exclusivity period—reduce exposure while preserving the strategic upside.

Given the strategic imperative to protect music‑player revenue and the potential to create a new, high‑margin product line, the proposal’s benefits outweigh its risks if the outlined mitigations are executed.

DECISION: GO
