The company’s core brand is under pressure from a broader soft‑drink slowdown, creating a clear need for growth levers. The test‑market data show a “positive response” to a clear cola positioned as a “purer” alternative, indicating that the concept can attract attention and generate initial trial.

However, the risks outlined outweigh the early enthusiasm:

1. **Consumer association** – Cola flavor is historically linked to its dark hue. A clear product may create a cognitive mismatch, limiting repeat purchases once novelty fades.  
2. **Novelty decay** – The test markets likely captured early adopters; the risk that “novelty buyers may not repeat” suggests a steep drop‑off after the launch buzz.  
3. **Brand cannibalization** – Introducing a major brand extension could siphon sales from the flagship cola, eroding the very revenue the company needs to protect.  
4. **Test‑market inflation** – Small‑scale studies often overstate demand because of heightened media coverage, promotional intensity, and limited distribution alternatives.  
5. **Competitive response** – Rivals could quickly replicate a clear‑cola concept, eroding any first‑mover advantage and turning the launch into a costly arms race.

Given these uncertainties, a full‑scale national rollout with a large marketing spend is premature. A more prudent path would be a phased expansion to additional, demographically diverse markets while tightening metrics on repeat purchase and brand‑impact studies. Simultaneously, a modest, targeted promotional budget could validate the product’s staying power before committing the full national spend.

The current proposal does not mitigate the highlighted risks, nor does it provide a contingency plan for potential cannibalization or competitive copycats. Approving it as‑is would expose the company to significant financial and brand‑equity risk without sufficient evidence of sustainable demand.

**DECISION: NO-GO**
