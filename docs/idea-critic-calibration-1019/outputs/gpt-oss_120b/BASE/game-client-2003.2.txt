**Assessment**

The core challenge—delivering timely updates and preventing cheating—cannot be solved reliably through boxed distribution alone. A dedicated online client offers a single point for patch delivery, integrity checks, and a unified purchase platform, which could become a long‑term revenue stream if opened to third‑party titles.

**Key Risks**

1. **Player Acceptance** – Mandatory online accounts and large initial downloads may alienate a segment of the existing PC audience, especially in 2003 when many users still rely on dial‑up or limited‑bandwidth connections.  
2. **Retail Partner Reaction** – Retailers may view the shift as a threat to their boxed‑sale margins and could reduce shelf space or drop the publisher’s titles.  
3. **Technical Stability** – An early‑stage client is likely to contain bugs that could block game launches, eroding trust.  
4. **Infrastructure Gaps** – Home broadband penetration is not universal; users on slower connections could experience prohibitive download times.  
5. **Competitive Replication** – Competitors could launch similar services, diminishing first‑mover advantage.

**Potential Mitigations (if added)**  

- **Optional Offline Mode** for the first launch, with later mandatory online verification after a grace period.  
- **Incremental Roll‑out**: pilot the client with a single new title, gather telemetry, and refine before full deployment.  
- **Retail Incentives**: offer exclusive in‑store promotions or bundled physical‑digital bundles to maintain retailer goodwill.  
- **Download Optimization**: use compression, background patching, and allow users to schedule large downloads during off‑peak hours.  
- **Robust QA**: allocate dedicated testing resources to achieve a near‑zero‑crash release threshold.

**Conclusion**

While the strategic vision is compelling, the proposal as presented does not address the most immediate blockers—player friction, retailer relationships, and client stability. Approving it now would expose the company to significant revenue loss and brand damage before the benefits can be realized.

**Recommendation**

Delay approval until the outlined mitigations are incorporated and a pilot phase is defined.

DECISION: NO-GO
