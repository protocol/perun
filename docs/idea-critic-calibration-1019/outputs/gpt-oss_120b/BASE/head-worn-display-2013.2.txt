The proposal targets a niche early‑adopter segment at a premium price of $1,500 before attempting mass‑market adoption. While the hardware (head‑worn display, camera, voice control, small screen) is technically novel, the outlined risks outweigh the limited upside at this stage.

**Privacy** – A wearable camera is a known flashpoint. Without built‑in safeguards (e.g., automatic shutter indicators, data‑encryption, opt‑out modes) the product will attract regulatory scrutiny and consumer backlash, especially in jurisdictions tightening surveillance laws.

**Price** – $1,500 places the device well above typical consumer wearables (smart watches, earbuds) and even above many AR headsets that offer more mature ecosystems. Early adopters are price‑sensitive when the value proposition is unclear.

**Use‑Case Ambiguity** – No concrete everyday scenarios are identified. Without clear, repeatable tasks (e.g., hands‑free navigation, real‑time translation, professional workflow augmentation) the device will struggle to achieve sustained engagement beyond novelty.

**Social Acceptance** – Public wearables that record video are often stigmatized. The lack of a compelling social narrative or design that blends discreetly with everyday attire will limit adoption and could provoke negative publicity.

**Battery Life** – Short runtime directly undermines the “everyday” positioning. Users will be forced to recharge frequently, eroding the convenience promised by a head‑worn interface.

Given these factors, proceeding as‑is presents a high probability of poor market reception, brand risk, and potential legal challenges. The proposal should be revisited to:

1. Implement transparent privacy controls and obtain certifications.
2. Re‑evaluate pricing strategy or introduce a tiered model.
3. Define and prototype at least one high‑impact, repeatable use case.
4. Conduct user‑experience studies on form factor and social perception.
5. Upgrade power management to meet daily‑use expectations.

Until these mitigations are addressed, the current plan is premature.

DECISION: NO-GO
