The test‑market results show a clear, favorable response to the new “pure” cola, indicating that consumers are open to a differentiated offering within the soft‑drink category. This suggests a genuine market opportunity, especially as overall soft‑drink growth is slowing and a fresh product could revive interest.

However, the risks outlined are substantial and unaddressed in the current plan:

* **Flavor‑color association:** Consumers may reject a clear beverage that still tastes like traditional cola, undermining the “purity” promise.  
* **Novelty attrition:** Initial curiosity may not translate into repeat purchases, limiting long‑term volume.  
* **Brand cannibalization:** Pulling sales from the flagship cola could erode the core profit engine without delivering net growth.  
* **Test‑market bias:** Positive results in limited markets often overstate national demand, especially when supported by intensive local promotion.  
* **Competitive imitation:** Rivals could quickly launch similar “clear” variants, eroding any first‑mover advantage.

Given that the proposal lacks a mitigation strategy—such as targeted consumer education, phased rollout, safeguards against cannibalization, or contingency plans for competitor response—the executive team cannot responsibly commit the large marketing spend required for a nationwide launch at this stage.

A revised plan that pilots mitigation tactics, refines positioning, and validates repeat‑purchase intent is needed before full rollout.

DECISION: NO‑GO
