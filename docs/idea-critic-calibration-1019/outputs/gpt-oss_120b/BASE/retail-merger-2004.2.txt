The plan attempts to reverse two downward‑trending businesses by forcing a merger that hinges on cost‑saving and brand‑leveraging assumptions that are not demonstrated.

**Strategic fit:**  
- Both retailers are experiencing falling sales, indicating that market fundamentals (e.g., consumer shift to big‑box formats) are already adverse.  
- Their customer bases differ: the discount chain serves price‑sensitive shoppers, while the department‑appliance chain targets a higher‑spending segment. Aligning these groups under a single format will require a clear value proposition, which the proposal does not articulate.

**Operational risk:**  
- The acquired stores are described as “old and under‑invested.” Converting them to a new format will demand capital expenditures for refurbishment, technology upgrades, and inventory realignment—costs that are not quantified.  
- Merging corporate functions is presented as a source of savings, yet integration of two distressed organizations often yields hidden expenses (severance, systems migration, cultural clashes). The uncertainty around actual cost reductions makes the financial upside speculative.

**Competitive landscape:**  
- Big‑box rivals are expanding, capturing the very price‑sensitive and convenience‑driven shoppers the discount chain hopes to retain. Without a differentiated offering, the combined entity is likely to lose further share to these stronger competitors.

**Financial considerations:**  
- No revenue, margin, or cash‑flow forecasts are provided to assess whether the acquisition price can be justified or whether the combined entity can service any new debt.  
- Selling the older chain’s brands through discount stores may dilute brand equity and provide limited incremental revenue.

**Conclusion:** The proposal relies on unproven synergies, faces high integration risk, and does not address the core market shift toward larger, better‑funded competitors. Approving the deal as presented would expose the restructuring‑emerging discount chain to additional financial and operational strain.
