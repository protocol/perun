The proposal promises a fresh visual identity for a well‑known chilled orange‑juice brand, but it also removes the single most recognizable shelf cue that has driven sales for years. The existing orange‑with‑straw illustration is the primary trigger for shoppers, especially the brand’s core, older‑skewing loyalists who rely on visual shortcuts when selecting among many similar cartons.

Key concerns:

* **Recognition loss** – A complete nationwide swap eliminates the safety net of a gradual transition. Shoppers who do not instantly associate the new glass‑and‑cap motif with the familiar product may substitute a competitor, causing an immediate dip in volume.
* **Cost exposure** – Redesign, re‑tooling of packaging lines, and a large launch campaign represent a sizable upfront outlay. Without evidence that the new look will generate enough incremental sales or price‑premium to offset these costs, the financial risk is unclear.
* **Consumer inertia** – The buyer base is described as loyal and older, a segment that typically resists abrupt visual changes. A sudden, all‑at‑once rollout magnifies the chance of alienating this group.
* **Market testing omission** – The proposal does not include pilot testing in a limited geography or a phased rollout that would allow measurement of shelf‑impact and sales lift before committing to full‑scale implementation.

Potential upside—modernization, appeal to younger consumers, and differentiation—remains attractive, yet it cannot be weighed against the high‑risk factors without empirical data. A more prudent approach would be to conduct a controlled test, gather shopper‑recognition metrics, and refine the design based on real‑world feedback. This would protect existing market share while still exploring the brand‑refresh ambition.

Given the present information, the risks outweigh the unproven benefits, and the recommended course is to pause the full‑nation rollout until further validation.

DECISION: NO-GO
