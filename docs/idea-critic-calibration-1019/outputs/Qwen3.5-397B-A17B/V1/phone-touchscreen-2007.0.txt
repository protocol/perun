The claim is that a computer and music player maker can disrupt the mobile market by launching a premium, keyboard-less touchscreen phone with exclusive carrier control and revenue sharing, despite lacking phone industry experience.

Steelman: Leveraging existing mastery in miniaturization, touch interfaces, and digital media to create a superior user experience that justifies a premium price, while using exclusivity to force a carrier to subsidize the risk and cede design control.

Skeptic: The market is dominated by physical keyboard business phones; removing the keyboard is a fatal usability flaw for the core demographic. Premium pricing without third-party apps limits utility to a mere "music phone," insufficient to justify the cost. Carrier exclusivity severely caps market penetration, allowing competitors to dominate the broader landscape. Lack of cellular experience risks network reliability and battery life failures.

Better-way: License the software to multiple handset manufacturers (like Microsoft) to gain instant scale and avoid hardware risk. Partner with a carrier for a subsidized, lower-cost device with a physical keyboard to match current user expectations. Delay launch to develop an App Store ecosystem first, ensuring immediate third-party utility.

Kill-criteria: If the single carrier cannot guarantee nationwide network reliability matching current leaders, the brand reputation will suffer irreparable harm. If initial sales do not convert at least 15% of the carrier's high-end customers within six months, the premium strategy is invalid. If battery life fails to sustain a full day of mixed usage, the device is non-viable.

Pre-mortem: The device launches to critical acclaim for its screen but fails commercially because business users reject the lack of a keyboard and developers ignore the closed platform. The exclusive carrier fails to market it effectively outside their niche, and the high price point restricts it to early adopters only, never reaching mass market. The company burns cash on hardware losses without the volume to recoup R&D.

The proposal bets the company's reputation on unproven consumer behavior (touch-only) and an untested business model (carrier revenue share). The risks of alienating the core business market and failing to achieve scale due to exclusivity are too high without a proven app ecosystem or keyboard alternative.

DECISION: NO-GO
