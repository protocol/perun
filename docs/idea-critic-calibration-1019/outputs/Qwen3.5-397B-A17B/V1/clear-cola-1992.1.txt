The claim assumes clear color signals "purity" enough to drive national scale and that test market enthusiasm predicts repeat purchase. The steelman: a clear cola captures a specific health-conscious segment without eroding the flagship's core identity, creating a net-new revenue stream before competitors react.

However, the "better way" analysis reveals a critical flaw in the "do nothing" vs. "national launch" binary. The proposal ignores the high probability of cannibalization where the new product merely shifts existing volume rather than expanding the total user base, especially given the risk that consumers psychologically link cola flavor strictly to dark color. If the flavor profile does not distinctively overcome the visual dissonance, novelty buyers will churn immediately, leaving high marketing sunk costs.

The pre-mortem indicates failure arises from treating a niche novelty as a mainstream staple. Test markets often inflate demand for novel items that fail to sustain habit formation. Launching nationwide with a "large marketing campaign" maximizes exposure but also maximizes the cost of failure if the repeat rate collapses. A safer, reversible alternative exists: a staged regional rollout limited to the test markets' strongest demographics with a capped media budget. This validates the repeat purchase rate and checks for cannibalization against the flagship in a controlled environment before committing national infrastructure.

The evidence provided admits shoppers may link flavor to color and that rivals may copy quickly. If rivals copy, the first-mover advantage is negligible unless the brand locks in distribution immediately, which a national launch attempts but at extreme risk. The proposal lacks a specific kill criterion for cannibalization rates or a defined threshold for repeat purchase frequency required to sustain the brand. Without these guardrails, the decision relies on hope rather than verified unit economics.

Because the proposal demands a full national launch based on test market data known to overstate novelty demand, and fails to account for the high likelihood of flavor-color cognitive dissonance reducing retention, the risk outweighs the potential reward in its current form. The lack of a staged approach or specific metrics to monitor cannibalization makes this a one-way door with insufficient data.

DECISION: NO-GO
