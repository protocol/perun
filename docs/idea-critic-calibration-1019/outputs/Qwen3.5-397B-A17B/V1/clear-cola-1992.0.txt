The claim is that a clear cola, successful in test markets, should launch nationwide immediately with a major campaign. The steelman: this extension captures novelty demand and repositions the brand as "purer" before competitors act, leveraging existing distribution to offset slowing core growth.

However, the attack reveals fatal flaws in the current proposal. First, the fundamental product-market fit is unproven beyond novelty; cola flavor is psychologically linked to dark color, risking permanent brand confusion rather than expansion. Second, the proposal admits test markets often overstate demand for novelties, yet offers no correction factor or staged rollout to verify repeat purchase rates before committing to a "large marketing campaign." Third, cannibalization of the flagship is a near-certainty without a clear differentiation strategy beyond color, potentially shrinking total portfolio volume while increasing marketing spend. Finally, the risk of rival copies is high; a massive national launch telegraphs the strategy to competitors who can copy the formula faster than brand loyalty can form.

The "do nothing" or "limited regional pilot" alternative is superior. A full national launch is a one-way door with high sunk costs in marketing and inventory. The cost of delay is low; waiting six months to confirm repeat purchase rates in a broader but controlled region prevents a catastrophic national failure. The current plan lacks kill criteria: there is no defined metric for repeat purchase rate or cannibalization threshold that would trigger a stop. Without verifying that the "positive response" translates to sustained volume rather than one-time trials, the risk of eroding the core brand's equity outweighs the potential gain.

The proposal fails to address the core mechanism of cola consumption: visual expectation. Launching nationwide based on test market novelty is a gamble with the company's second-largest asset. The plan must be revised to include a phased rollout with strict repeat-purchase milestones before full national marketing spend is authorized.

DECISION: NO-GO
