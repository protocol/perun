The claim assumes a paid, mandatory service can succeed despite low broadband adoption and a market expectation of free online play. The steelman is that a unified, high-quality network with voice chat and friends lists creates a defensible moat and superior user experience that justifies the cost, turning the console into a premium ecosystem rather than just hardware.

However, the "better way" analysis reveals a fatal flaw in the current proposal's rigidity. Competitors offering free or publisher-managed services lower the barrier to entry, crucial when broadband penetration is minimal. A mandatory subscription on top of an already premium console price creates a double-gate that severely limits the install base required to attract third-party developers. The company lacks operational experience in running global hosting and support, introducing massive execution risk without a pilot phase.

The pre-mortem indicates failure stems from overestimating immediate consumer willingness to pay for infrastructure they do not yet fully utilize. The "do nothing" or "optional service" alternatives dominate because they allow the ecosystem to grow organically while the company builds operational maturity. A mandatory fee acts as a hard ceiling on adoption before the network effect can take hold.

While the vision of a unified identity is strong, the specific execution of a *mandatory* annual subscription in 2002, given the stated constraints of low broadband penetration and zero prior operational experience, presents a catastrophic adoption risk. The proposal fails to account for the friction of forcing a new behavior (broadband + subscription) on a mass market simultaneously. The kill criteria for success (mass adoption) are directly contradicted by the proposal's primary mechanism (mandatory paywall).

To proceed as proposed ignores the reality of the 2002 market landscape. The service should be optional or subsidized to drive adoption, not a mandatory gatekeeper. Without revising the mandatory nature of the subscription or the timing relative to broadband adoption, this plan is likely to stifle the console's growth.

DECISION: NO-GO
