**Steelman:** Transform an invisible component into a consumer pull-factor by subsidizing OEM ads featuring a new brand logo, forcing differentiation against clones and shifting bargaining power from manufacturers to the chip maker.

**Skeptic:** The premise that end-users care about internal components is unproven; historically, B2B ingredients do not drive B2C demand. If consumers ignore the logo, the company burns a massive budget share with zero sales lift. The claim that "buyers do not know which chip is in their computer" is the very problem to solve, but assuming they *will* care once told is a leap.

**Better-Way:** Alternatives include: (1) Do nothing and compete on price/performance specs with OEMs; (2) Technical certification programs for OEMs without consumer ads; (3) Direct rebates to consumers contingent on proof of purchase of specific models. However, these fail to address the core threat: clone makers eroding margins via part-number confusion. Only branding breaks the commodity trap.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** This aligns with a "North Star" of owning the value chain's mindshare. Failure scenario: OEMs refuse participation, fearing loss of their own brand equity. If OEMs boycott, the campaign dies instantly. However, the subsidy creates a financial incentive too strong to ignore; OEMs need marketing funds, and this provides them.

**Evidence & Calibration:** No data exists yet (unprecedented move), making this a high-risk bet. Yet, the risk of *inaction* (commoditization by clones) is existential. The "unprecedented" nature is the opportunity, not a flaw. The cost is high, but the potential to capture the entire market's mental availability justifies it.

**Kill Criteria:** If, after two quarters, consumer awareness of the logo remains below 20% despite full OEM participation, terminate the subsidy. The leading metric is "consumer mention rate at retail" tracked monthly.

**Verdict:** The proposal addresses a fatal strategic vulnerability (commoditization) with a bold, albeit expensive, mechanism that aligns OEM incentives (free ad money) with the chip maker's goal (differentiation). The risk of doing nothing outweighs the risk of failure.

DECISION: GO
