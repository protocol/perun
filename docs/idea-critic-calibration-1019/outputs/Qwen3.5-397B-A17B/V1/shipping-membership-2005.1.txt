The claim is that an annual membership eliminating per-order friction will increase lifetime value despite short-term margin erosion. The steelman: locking in loyalty via upfront revenue and behavioral change creates a defensible moat that outweighs immediate shipping overruns.

However, the proposal fails the evidence test. It relies on unproven economics with no pilot data, no elasticity modeling, and no cap on liability. The "heavy buyer" risk is not theoretical; it is the primary failure mode. If heavy users subscribe and double order frequency, the margin hit could be catastrophic before any long-term loyalty materializes. The proposal admits the economics are unproven yet asks for a full launch, not a test.

Alternatives exist: a capped pilot in one region, a higher price point to ensure positive unit economics from day one, or a tiered system requiring a minimum spend per order to qualify for free shipping. The current plan ignores Chesterton's Fence; the per-order fee exists to protect margins, and removing it without a verified replacement mechanism is reckless.

The pre-mortem is clear: six months in, shipping costs exceed membership revenue by 40%, the program is entrenched due to public承诺, and reversing it causes a PR disaster and churn. The cost of delay is low; the cost of being wrong is existential.

There is no kill criterion defined. There is no leading metric to watch. There is no "go-if" condition tied to a specific conversion rate or average order value threshold. The proposal asks for a binary "yes" on a high-stakes, one-way door based on hope rather than data.

Because the economics are explicitly admitted as unproven, the risk of margin collapse is high, and no safety valves (caps, pilots, or tripwires) are included, this cannot be approved as proposed. It requires a bounded experiment first.

DECISION: NO-GO
