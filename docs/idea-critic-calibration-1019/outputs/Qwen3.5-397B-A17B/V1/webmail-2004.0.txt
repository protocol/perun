**Steelman:** Offering 1,000x more storage than incumbents, coupled with superior search, creates a unique value proposition that justifies ad-supported monetization despite privacy concerns and high infrastructure costs.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary failure mode is unit economics: if storage costs exceed ad revenue per user, the service bleeds cash indefinitely. In 2004, storage prices are falling but not negligible; a gigabyte per user is a massive liability if engagement is low. Privacy scanning for ads risks regulatory backlash and user trust erosion, potentially limiting adoption to non-enterprise segments.

Alternatives include:
1.  **Do Nothing:** Avoids capital expenditure but cedes the "searchable memory" angle to competitors.
2.  **Tiered Model:** Offer limited free storage with paid upgrades. This reduces risk but sacrifices the viral "free gigabyte" marketing hook.
3.  **Enterprise First:** Target paying business users initially. This ensures revenue but slows mass adoption.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This fits a strategy of dominating information access via search. If we assume storage costs drop faster than ad rates rise, the model works. However, if privacy scandals erupt or ad fill-rates remain low, the cost per user becomes unsustainable. The "invitation-only" launch mitigates server load but delays revenue validation.

**Evidence & Calibration:**
The proposal assumes text ads can subsidize heavy storage usage immediately. Historical data on 2004 ad CPMs vs. storage costs is not provided, making this a high-risk assumption. The privacy objection is valid; scanning private correspondence was unprecedented at this scale.

**Verdict:**
The "gigabyte" differentiator is a powerful wedge against entrenched incumbents who offer mere megabytes. While the cost structure is risky, the potential to own the user's entire communication history creates a data moat that justifies the initial loss leader strategy. The invitation-only approach allows for controlled cost scaling. The privacy risk is real but manageable through transparency. The market is mature, but the product is fundamentally different (search-first vs. storage-first).
