The proposal commits to a head-on collision with a dominant incumbent while possessing a fraction of the critical asset: the app ecosystem. History demonstrates that in two-sided markets, hardware parity cannot compensate for a barren software catalog; shoppers reject unproven platforms regardless of advertising spend. The "small app catalog" is not a temporary hurdle but a structural death sentence in 2011, where network effects are the primary moat.

The steelman argument suggests that heavy developer outreach and advertising can jump-start the flywheel before cash runs out. However, this ignores the "chicken-and-egg" trap: developers will not build for a platform without users, and users will not buy a platform without apps. The proposed pricing strategy ("in line with the market leader") removes the only viable alternative lever: price disruption. Without a significant price advantage or a killer exclusive feature, there is no reason for a consumer to switch from the established ecosystem to an unproven one during a period of internal leadership transition.

The pre-mortem is clear: the device launches, sales lag due to lack of apps, developer interest evaporates despite outreach, and the company is forced to discount heavily, destroying margins without gaining share. The cost of delay is negligible compared to the certainty of burning capital on a losing position. The "do nothing" or "license the OS to others" alternatives dominate this direct hardware entry because they avoid bearing the full risk of platform adoption alone.

This plan fails the "fit" test against market reality: it attempts to solve a distribution problem (lack of apps) with a marketing solution (advertising). The leadership transition adds execution risk to an already flawed strategic premise. There is no kill criterion mentioned that would stop the bleed early, implying a commitment to a sunk-cost fallacy. The evidence of market dominance by the leader is overwhelming; the assumption that spending can overturn this quickly is unsupported.

DECISION: NO-GO
