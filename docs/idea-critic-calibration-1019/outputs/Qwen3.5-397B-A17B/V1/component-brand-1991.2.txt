**Steelman:** Transform an invisible component into a consumer demand driver by subsidizing OEM ads featuring a unified brand logo, forcing buyers to request the specific chip by name.

**Attack:** The premise that end-users care about internal components is unproven; historically, B2B ingredients fail in B2C markets. If consumers ignore the logo, the company burns a massive portion of its marketing budget subsidizing OEMs without generating pull, while simultaneously angering partners who resent creative constraints. The "unprecedented" nature of this move suggests a fundamental category error: computers are sold on system performance and price, not individual parts.

**Better Ways:** 
1. **Do Nothing:** Maintain current B2B relationships; invest budget in technical superiority to let specs sell themselves.
2. **Trade Marketing:** Offer direct rebates to OEMs for volume commitments rather than consumer ad subsidies, securing shelf space without consumer confusion.
3. **Co-Branding Lite:** Allow OEMs to optionally use a "Powered By" badge without financial strings or creative mandates, testing interest organically.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** Assuming the goal is pricing power and defense against clones, this strategy bets the entire brand equity on consumer education. If it fails (high probability given the "unprecedented" risk), the company loses cash, alienates its only distribution channel (OEMs), and has no fallback position. The cost of delay is low; clone makers are a threat, but a failed consumer campaign is an existential financial drain.

**Evidence & Calibration:** The proposal admits benefits "may not show in sales" and relies on changing entrenched consumer behavior with no prior success cases in this sector. The risk of partner resentment is concrete; if major OEMs refuse participation, the campaign collapses instantly. There is no pilot plan, no kill criterion for the ad spend, and no verification that consumers actually make purchasing decisions based on processor brands. The lack of a "safe-to-fail" experiment makes this a reckless all-in bet on an unverified psychological shift.

**Verdict:** The proposal lacks a mechanism to validate consumer interest before committing significant budget and partner goodwill. The potential for catastrophic failure (wasted budget + broken channel relationships) outweighs the theoretical upside without a phased test.

DECISION: NO-GO
