The claim is that building a massive, unproven wide-body jet with a new factory and engine will secure market dominance despite existential financial risk and emerging supersonic competition.

**Steelman:** Only a drastic capacity increase can meet projected demand growth and lower seat costs sufficiently to lock in the largest customer, creating a monopoly on high-volume routes before supersonic travel becomes viable.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Financial Ruin:** The development cost exceeds all prior company history. A single failure or delay bankrupts the firm. *Alternative:* Upgrade existing narrow-body efficiency; wait for supersonic maturity.
2.  **Technology Bet:** The high-bypass engine is unproven at this scale. Failure grounds the fleet. *Alternative:* Stick with proven low-bypass engines or delay until engine testing completes.
3.  **Market Obsolescence:** If supersonic jets replace subsonic ones quickly, this massive asset becomes stranded. *Alternative:* Invest in supersonic R&D instead.
4.  **Single Customer:** Relying on one airline order for such a massive undertaking is dangerous if their demand forecasts are wrong.

**Pre-mortem:** The project fails because the new factory drains cash flow before the first plane is delivered, and the unproven engine suffers catastrophic reliability issues, leading to mass cancellations. The "freighter conversion" feature adds complexity and cost without immediate revenue.

**Fit & Evidence:** The proposal assumes subsonic travel will dominate longer than critics predict and that the "raised cockpit" freighter conversion is a unique selling point that justifies the risk. However, no evidence confirms the engine supplier can deliver on time, nor that the supersonic threat is overstated. The "do nothing" option preserves capital but cedes the high-capacity market to competitors if the subsonic bet pays off.

**Kill Criteria:** This is a one-way door with irreversible financial exposure. The lack of multiple orders, unproven engine technology, and undefined path to profitability beyond a single customer makes the risk profile unacceptable without further validation. The potential for total company collapse outweighs the potential for market dominance.

DECISION: NO-GO
