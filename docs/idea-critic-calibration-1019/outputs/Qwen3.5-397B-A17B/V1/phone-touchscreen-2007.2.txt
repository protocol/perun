The claim is that a computer and music player maker should enter the phone market with a premium, keyboard-less touchscreen device, securing unprecedented carrier control and revenue sharing despite zero industry experience.

Steelman: The convergence of communication and media creates a unique opening where existing keyboard-centric business phones fail to deliver a superior internet and music experience, justifying a premium price if design control is absolute.

However, the proposal contains fatal flaws when attacked against the 2007 market reality. First, the "no keyboard" assumption ignores that the entire market, led by BlackBerry and Nokia, equates physical keys with productivity; removing them without a proven alternative input method is a bet on unverified user behavior, not a validated feature. Second, the dependency on a single carrier for exclusivity while demanding revenue sharing and total design control contradicts the power dynamic of 2007, where carriers dictated terms; no carrier would likely accept these terms for an unproven entrant without a massive subsidy risk. Third, launching without third-party applications relegates the device to a closed appliance in an ecosystem increasingly valuing extensibility.

The "better way" is to license the software to existing handset makers or launch a lower-risk device with a hybrid input method to validate the touchscreen interface before committing to a full hardware ecosystem. The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of a catastrophic market entry failure. The pre-mortem reveals high probability of carrier rejection or consumer rejection of the interface, leading to massive inventory write-downs and brand damage.

The evidence provided offers no validation that users want a glass slab over physical keys, nor that any carrier would agree to the requested revenue model for a first-time phone vendor. The risks are not merely "high"; they are foundational assumptions that contradict established market mechanics. Without a pilot, a phased rollout, or proof of the interface's viability, this is a gamble, not a strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
