The claim is that a radical visual overhaul of a legacy brand's packaging, executed via a "big bang" nationwide launch, will succeed despite removing the primary shelf recognition cue (the orange with a straw) for an older, loyal demographic.

Steelman: The new minimalist design modernizes the brand, and the massive advertising campaign will instantly retrain consumers, offsetting the loss of the historical visual anchor.

However, the attack reveals a fatal flaw in the execution strategy. The proposal explicitly identifies the old image as the "main shelf cue" and the buyer base as "older and loyal." Removing the primary recognition trigger for this specific demographic creates a high probability of immediate sales collapse before advertising can retrain behavior. Older demographics typically exhibit higher resistance to abstract design changes and rely heavily on established visual heuristics.

The "big bang" approach (all stores, all at once) eliminates the ability to measure this recognition failure in a controlled environment. If the hypothesis that "ads will overcome lost shelf cues" is false, the brand suffers catastrophic, unrecoverable revenue loss across the entire nation simultaneously. There is no off-ramp; the cost of reversal (reprinting and redistributing millions of cartons) would be prohibitive.

A safer alternative exists: a staged rollout in a test market to verify if the new design retains shelf visibility among the core demographic before committing to the nationwide switch. The current plan ignores the specific risk of "shopper failure to recognize" by removing the very tool (the old image) that prevents it, while betting the entire company on an unproven assumption about advertising efficacy against deep-seated habit.

The lack of a pilot phase for a change this fundamental, targeting a demographic known for resistance to change, constitutes a concrete failure scenario that the current kill criteria (none listed) do not address. The risk is not just "costly"; it is existential to the brand's market share.

DECISION: NO-GO
