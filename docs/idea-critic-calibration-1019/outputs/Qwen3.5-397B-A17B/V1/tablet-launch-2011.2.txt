**Steelman:** Leverage the acquired OS to enter the tablet market immediately, matching the leader's price while betting that heavy marketing and developer incentives can rapidly close the app gap before the leadership transition stalls momentum.

**Skeptic:** The proposal ignores the "app gap" gravity. In 2011, tablet buyers prioritize ecosystem over hardware specs. A small catalog at a premium price creates immediate consumer rejection. The "market leader" has a entrenched network effect; matching their price without matching their value proposition (apps) is a recipe for inventory bloat. The leadership transition adds execution risk to an already fragile go-to-market strategy.

**Better-Way:** The "do nothing" or "delay" option is stronger. Wait until the app catalog reaches a critical mass (e.g., top 500 tablet apps) before launching. Alternatively, undercut the market leader significantly on price to position the device as a budget media consumer rather than a productivity competitor, acknowledging the limited app suite. Launching now at parity pricing assumes consumers are blind to software ecosystems, which historical data contradicts.

**Kill-Criteria & Pre-mortem:** Assume failure: The device launches, sales are 10% of forecast, and developers ignore the outreach because install base is too small. The fatal flaw is the pricing strategy combined with the app deficit. If the top 50 essential tablet apps are not native or perfectly emulated by launch day, the value proposition collapses.

**Decision:** The proposal fails the "beat do nothing" test. Launching a premium-priced tablet with a fraction of the competitor's apps during a leadership transition is a high-risk, low-probability success scenario. The cost of delay (waiting for more apps) is lower than the cost of a brand-damaging flop. The pricing must be aggressive (lower) or the launch delayed until the ecosystem matures. As proposed, it is a capital destruction event.

DECISION: NO-GO
