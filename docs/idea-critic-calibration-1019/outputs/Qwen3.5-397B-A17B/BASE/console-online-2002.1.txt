The proposal introduces a paid, console-wide online service in 2002, requiring broadband and an annual subscription. This contrasts sharply with competitors offering free or publisher-managed online play. Significant risks are explicitly identified: broadband adoption is currently low, creating a small addressable market; consumers expect online features to be free, suggesting strong resistance to a mandatory fee; and the company lacks any prior experience running the necessary hosting and support operations.

Launching a service that requires hardware (broadband) few customers possess, charges for a feature rivals give away, and relies on operational capabilities the company does not own presents an unacceptable level of risk. The combination of limited market readiness, potential consumer backlash regarding pricing, and internal operational inexperience makes immediate execution dangerous. Proceeding now would likely result in low adoption rates, financial losses from building untested infrastructure, and damage to the console's reputation. The gaps in operational readiness and market alignment are too severe to ignore. Approval cannot be granted without first addressing the lack of internal infrastructure and validating whether the market is truly ready to pay for these specific services under current conditions. The current facts suggest the timing and model are flawed relative to the competitive landscape and consumer environment.

DECISION: NO-GO
