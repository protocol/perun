Continuing on the current path guarantees further erosion of relevance. While the target company has low sales volume, its value lies in its technology architecture, not its current revenue. Paying a premium for this strategic asset is justified when the alternative is business failure.

The risks are real but manageable. Migrating software and developers is difficult, yet it is less risky than persisting with a failing internal strategy that has already stumbled twice. Licensing a competitor's system would surrender long-term control and differentiation, reducing the maker to a commodity hardware assembler. Bringing the founder back as an advisor addresses the leadership gap without immediately reinstating full operational control, mitigating concerns about his difficult history while leveraging his unique vision. The "high price" relative to small revenue is a misapplication of standard valuation metrics; this is a rescue acquisition where the asset's strategic necessity outweighs its current financial output. Without this move, the company possesses no viable path to a modern platform. The status quo is not an option. The potential upside of regaining technological leadership far exceeds the financial cost and execution challenges. Immediate action is required to halt the decline and secure a future foundation.
