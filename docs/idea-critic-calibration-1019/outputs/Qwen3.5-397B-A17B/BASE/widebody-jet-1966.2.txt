The proposal presents a transformative opportunity but carries existential risks that cannot be ignored given the current constraints. The request involves scaling production to two and a half times current capacity, requiring capital outlays far exceeding any previous company commitment. This financial exposure is compounded by reliance on an unproven engine technology from an external supplier, introducing significant technical uncertainty.

Market assumptions appear fragile. The strategy hinges on a single customer's order, lacking a diversified portfolio to absorb potential failures. Furthermore, the industry expectation that supersonic aircraft will soon render subsonic jets obsolete poses a direct threat to the long-term viability of this specific design. Investing heavily in a technology potentially facing immediate obsolescence contradicts prudent strategic planning.

While the design feature allowing conversion to a freighter offers a clever mitigation for future utility, it does not offset the immediate dangers of over-leverage and technological dependency. The combination of unprecedented financial risk, unverified engineering, a nascent order book, and shifting competitive landscapes creates a scenario where failure could cripple the organization. Without additional orders to validate market demand or proven engine performance to guarantee operational success, proceeding immediately is reckless. The magnitude of the required investment demands a more secure foundation than currently exists. Approval under these specific conditions exposes the company to unacceptable levels of danger without sufficient safeguards or validated demand beyond one client.

DECISION: NO-GO
