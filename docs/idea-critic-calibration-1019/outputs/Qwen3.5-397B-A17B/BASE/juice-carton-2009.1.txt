The proposal presents an unacceptable level of risk to the brand's market position. The current packaging, featuring an orange with a straw, serves as the primary visual cue for shoppers on crowded shelves. Removing this established identifier threatens immediate recognition, particularly among the core demographic described as loyal and older. This group typically relies on familiar visual patterns rather than abstract design changes.

Replacing a proven, long-standing asset with a minimalist design introduces significant uncertainty. While the recipe and price remain unchanged, the visual disconnect could lead consumers to perceive the product as new or different, potentially causing them to switch to competitors during the transition. The risk of shoppers failing to locate or recognize the carton outweighs the potential benefits of a modernized aesthetic.

Furthermore, the proposal involves a costly nationwide rollout executed simultaneously across all stores. This "big bang" approach leaves no room for error or market testing. If the new design fails to resonate, the financial loss from printing millions of unused cartons and the subsequent cost of reverting or re-designing would be substantial. A large advertising campaign may mitigate some confusion, but it cannot guarantee that the deeply ingrained habit of looking for the orange-with-straw image will be overcome instantly.

Given the high cost of the redesign, the vulnerability of the older buyer base, and the critical importance of the current shelf cue, proceeding immediately is imprudent. The potential for a sharp decline in sales due to recognition failure is too great to ignore. The strategy lacks a phased approach or pilot testing to validate consumer response before committing to a full national launch. Without evidence that the new design maintains shelf visibility and consumer trust, the proposal cannot be approved in its current form.

DECISION: NO-GO
