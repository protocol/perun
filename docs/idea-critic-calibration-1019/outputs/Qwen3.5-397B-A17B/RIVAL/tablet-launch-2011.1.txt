This proposal carries extreme strategic risk and is not recommended for approval in its current form.

The core failure is a fundamental misalignment between product readiness and market expectations. Launching a tablet in 2011 with a "small app catalog" against a dominant market leader is a non-starter. In the tablet sector, hardware is merely a vessel for software; without a robust ecosystem, the device is functionally crippled regardless of advertising spend. Consumers do not buy tablets for specs; they buy them for the applications they run. A sparse catalog signals an incomplete product, not a challenger.

The pricing strategy is equally flawed. Pricing "in line with the market leader" removes the only viable advantage a new entrant possesses: cost. Without price parity or undercutting, there is zero incentive for a consumer to choose an unproven device with limited software over the established standard. This approach maximizes risk while minimizing potential adoption.

Furthermore, the context of leadership transition introduces dangerous instability. Executing a "heavy investment" in advertising during internal power shifts often leads to inconsistent messaging and wasted capital. The market perceives the product as "unproven," and this proposal does nothing to alleviate that concern; instead, it validates it by launching an inferior ecosystem at a premium price.

The reliance on "developer outreach" is a long-term play that cannot solve a day-one hardware launch problem. Developers follow users; they do not precede them. Expecting a thriving app environment to materialize quickly enough to save the initial hardware rollout is speculative wishful thinking, not a strategy.

To proceed would likely result in significant financial loss and brand damage, cementing the device as a failure before it gains traction. The gap between the current state (small catalog, no market share) and the requirements for success (massive ecosystem, user trust) is too wide to bridge with advertising alone.

DECISION: NO-GO
