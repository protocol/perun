This proposal attempts to launch a complex, intrusive hardware platform as a mass-market "everyday consumer product" following a brief early-adopter phase. The risks identified are not merely hurdles; they are fundamental existential threats to the product's viability in the proposed form.

First, the privacy objection is catastrophic for a search and advertising company. Selling a wearable camera that records constantly creates an immediate, visceral trust deficit. Without a pre-existing, ironclad solution for data handling, explicit consent mechanisms for recorded subjects, and a transparent privacy architecture, the public backlash will be swift and severe. An invitation-only program does not solve this; it merely delays the inevitable public scrutiny.

Second, the value proposition is critically weak. "No clear everyday use for most people" at a $1,500 price point is a fatal flaw. High early-adopter pricing can work for niche professional tools or status symbols with undeniable utility, but this device lacks both. The social acceptance barrier reinforces this; people will not pay a premium to wear something that makes them socially awkward or unwelcome in private spaces (gyms, locker rooms, casinos, restaurants).

Third, the technical limitations (short battery life) combined with the high price create a poor user experience that contradicts the "everyday" positioning. An everyday device must be reliable and unobtrusive; a device that dies mid-day and draws hostile attention fails both criteria.

The proposal treats these as risks to be managed during a rollout. In reality, they are proof that the product-market fit has not been established. Launching this as an everyday consumer product in 2013 is premature. The company should pivot to a strictly enterprise/industrial focus (logistics, manufacturing, field service) where the value proposition is clear, privacy is contractually managed, and the price is justifiable by productivity gains. Attempting to bypass this validation to chase a consumer dream will result in significant financial loss and reputational damage.

The gaps in utility, privacy trust, and social viability are too wide to bridge with a marketing launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
