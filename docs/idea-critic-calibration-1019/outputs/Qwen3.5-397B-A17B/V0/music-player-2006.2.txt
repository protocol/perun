Steelman: A well-funded entrant can displace a dominant music player by offering superior hardware, a seamless ecosystem, and a differentiated user experience, even as a late mover.

**Skeptic:** The proposal relies on unverified assumptions: that record labels will permit unrestricted wireless sharing despite previous copy-protection demands, and that consumers will abandon a loyal ecosystem solely for a larger screen. The "late to market" risk is critical; without a verified exclusive content deal or a drastic price advantage (explicitly absent), the value proposition is weak. The abandonment of the company's own promoted copy-protection standard risks alienating existing industry partners, creating a strategic vacuum before new alliances are formed.

**Better-Way:** The "do nothing" option avoids massive capital expenditure in a shrinking margin hardware market. A cheaper alternative is licensing the software to existing device makers rather than building new hardware. Another path is focusing purely on the store/subscription model compatible with the dominant player, capturing revenue without the hardware risk.

**Kill-Criteria:** If label contracts cannot be signed within 6 months guaranteeing sharing rights, the project must stop. If early adoption does not reach 5% of the target demographic in Q1, marketing spend must be capped. The pre-mortem indicates failure via regulatory backlash from labels or a price war initiated by the incumbent.

**Fit:** This conflicts with a strategy of partnership if it burns bridges with current device makers. It only fits a "disruptor" vision if the ecosystem lock-in can be broken, which is unproven.

**Decision:** The risks of label rejection and partner alienation are too high without pre-signed agreements. The lack of price advantage and lateness to market makes the "large marketing budget" a potential sinkhole with low ROI probability. The proposal attempts to fight a war on multiple fronts (hardware, store, standards, content) simultaneously without a verified beachhead.

DECISION: NO-GO
