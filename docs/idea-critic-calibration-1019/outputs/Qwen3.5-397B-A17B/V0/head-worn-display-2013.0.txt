**Steelman:** An invitation-only launch at $1,500 allows the company to validate demand, refine privacy norms, and iterate hardware with a tolerant early-adopter segment before a risky mass-market release.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "everyday consumer product" positioning test. A $1,500 price point excludes 99% of consumers, contradicting the mass-market narrative. More critically, the identified risks are not merely "raised"; they are existential and unmitigated in this plan. Privacy objections regarding a always-on camera create immediate regulatory and social hostility that an "invitation-only" program cannot contain once units leave the lab. Without a defined "killer app" or clear everyday utility beyond novelty, the device solves no urgent problem for the user, making the high price unjustifiable. Social acceptance is a network effect; launching a socially awkward device to a small group prevents the critical mass needed to normalize it. The short battery life renders an "everyday" carry impossible.

**Better Way:** Do not position as an everyday consumer product yet. Pivot to a strictly enterprise/industrial pilot (e.g., manufacturing, medicine) where the camera adds verified value, privacy is controlled, and the $1,500 cost is expensable. This bypasses social stigma and utility gaps while funding R&D.

**Kill Criteria:** If early adopter return rates exceed 15% due to "lack of utility" or "social discomfort" within 90 days, or if any major jurisdiction bans public use, the consumer launch must be aborted.

**Verdict:** The current plan ignores the fatal mismatch between the "everyday consumer" claim and the reality of high cost, low utility, and high social friction. Launching now invites brand damage and regulatory backlash without a path to product-market fit. The enterprise pivot is the only viable near-term path.

DECISION: NO-GO
