**Steelman:** Building a massively larger, wide-body jet with a convertible freighter design secures long-term market dominance and meets urgent customer demand for lower seat costs, despite huge upfront risks.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Financial Ruin:** The development and factory costs exceed all prior company history. A single failure bankrupts the firm. *Alternative:* Upgrade current models incrementally to preserve cash.
2.  **Technological Obsolescence:** If supersonic transport (SST) becomes the standard, this subsonic giant becomes worthless scrap. *Alternative:* Wait for SST viability before committing to subsonic expansion.
3.  **Market Fragility:** Relying on one airline for a product of this scale is dangerous; if they cancel, no other buyer exists. *Alternative:* Secure multiple launch customers before breaking ground.
4.  **Engine Risk:** The high-bypass engine is unproven; failure here grounds the entire fleet.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume the project failed. Cause: The company overextended financially on a single customer bet just as supersonic jets rendered large subsonic planes economically unviable, leaving them with a useless factory and no liquidity.

**Fit & Evidence:** The proposal addresses immediate cost-per-seat demands but ignores the strategic threat of supersonic travel. The "raised cockpit" feature is a smart hedge for freighter conversion, adding reversibility/value later. However, the financial exposure is absolute; there is no partial success state.

**Decision Logic:**
The risk profile is existential. Committing to a new factory, new engine, and new airframe based on a single order, while a disruptive technology (SST) looms, violates basic portfolio theory. The "do nothing" or "incremental upgrade" options preserve the company's ability to pivot to supersonic later. The potential reward (dominance) does not justify the probability of total insolvency if the supersonic shift occurs or the single customer falters. The proposal lacks multiple launch customers to dilute risk and offers no contingency if SST development accelerates.

While the freighter conversion is a strong tactical feature, it does not offset the strategic gamble of betting the entire company on one subsonic design in a transitioning market. The prudent path is to delay the full factory commitment until supersonic viability is clearer or more orders are secured.

DECISION: NO-GO
