**Steelman:** A dedicated, cellular-enabled e-reader removes friction for book buyers and solves publisher piracy fears via a closed ecosystem, creating a new high-margin revenue stream that complements rather than cannibalizes print.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary failure mode is hardware incompetence leading to a costly, unsellable device. However, partnering with an existing manufacturer mitigates execution risk while retaining control over the user experience and store integration. The "do nothing" alternative cedes the digital future to competitors who will eventually erode print margins anyway. Building a proprietary device with free cellular data creates a "walled garden" that directly addresses publisher piracy concerns, securing content inventory that generic devices cannot guarantee.

Earlier e-readers failed due to poor screens and complex PC-dependent workflows. This proposal specifically targets those failures with paper-like display technology and ubiquitous connectivity. While low prices squeeze per-unit margins, the strategy relies on volume and the lifetime value of a captured customer who purchases exclusively from this store. The risk of print cannibalization is acceptable; if customers switch to digital, they remain within the company's ecosystem rather than defecting to a rival.

**Pre-mortem & Kill Criteria:**
If the device launches and fails to secure major publisher buy-in due to pricing pressure, the inventory will be empty, rendering the hardware useless. Additionally, if the hardware cost exceeds the subsidy limit before reaching 100,000 active users within 18 months, cash burn will become unsustainable.

**Decision:**
The strategic necessity of owning the digital distribution channel outweighs the operational risk of entering hardware. The closed ecosystem is the only viable path to satisfy publisher security requirements in this era. The proposal must proceed immediately to secure first-mover advantage in the dedicated e-reader market, provided the hardware development is outsourced to mitigate lack of internal experience.

DECISION: GO
