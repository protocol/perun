The claim rests on converting a "dull" economy platform into a desirable sporty icon without incurring the cost of a clean-sheet design. The steelman is that style and marketing can decouple perception from mechanical origins, capturing a new demographic with high-margin options.

However, three critical failures threaten this. First, the "cheap" stigma may be ineradicable; if young buyers associate the shared chassis with the parent's economy image, the sporty styling will read as a costume, not a genuine performance machine. Second, the unproven demand for sporty small cars at volume is a massive gamble; if the segment is niche, the heavy launch spending becomes a sunk cost with no recovery path. Third, the long options list creates operational complexity; if supply chains cannot support high-variation production immediately, early deliveries will stall, killing momentum.

The "do nothing" alternative preserves capital but cedes the emerging youth market to competitors. A cheaper path would be a limited "trim package" on the existing coupe to test waters before committing to a full model launch and massive ad spend. Yet, the proposal's specific combination of low base price and high customization is the only way to achieve the necessary volume and margin mix simultaneously.

The pre-mortem reveals failure if the advertising fails to redefine the car's identity separate from its economy roots. This is a two-way door only if the tooling costs are truly shared; if unique stampings are required, the exit cost is high. The cost of delay is likely catastrophic, as the baby boom demographic wave is time-sensitive; waiting for perfect data means missing the cultural moment entirely.

The strongest attack—that the economy heritage will doom the brand image—survives only if the marketing campaign is sufficiently distinct and massive to overwrite existing perceptions. Given the demographic inevitability and the low marginal cost of using existing mechanicals, the risk of inaction outweighs the risk of brand dilution, provided the kill criteria are strict.

DECISION: GO
