**Steelman:** A massive ad blitz can overwrite a "kids-only" brand perception, forcing adult trial of a premium burger that generates new revenue streams.

**Attack:** The proposal relies on spending before validation. The brand equity is explicitly "child-centered" and "value." Adults do not currently associate the chain with quality or premium pricing. Launching the *largest* budget in industry history against an unproven product in a hostile perceptual environment creates catastrophic downside. If the burger fails to delight, the chain loses its value reputation without gaining premium status. The price point contradicts the core customer's expectation of affordability. There is no "two-way door" here; once the brand attempts to be premium and fails, returning to "fun value" is difficult. The pre-mortem is clear: the ads drive trial, the product/price mismatch kills retention, and the brand is left with neither kids nor adults.

**Better Way:** Test the burger in a limited market with moderate spend to validate adult willingness to pay the premium. If unit economics hold, *then* scale. Do not bet the company on a national launch without demand evidence.

**Kill Criteria:** The proposal lacks a stop condition. It commits maximum capital immediately. A sound plan requires a pilot phase with a specific same-store sales lift threshold (e.g., +5% adult traffic) before unlocking full ad spend. Without this, the risk is total capital waste.

**Verdict:** The proposal violates basic product-market fit discipline by scaling before validating. It ignores the specific brand constraint (kids/value) and assumes advertising can overcome a fundamental product/price mismatch. The "largest budget" clause amplifies the error rather than mitigating it. This is a high-cost, low-reversibility gamble on an unproven assumption.

DECISION: NO-GO
