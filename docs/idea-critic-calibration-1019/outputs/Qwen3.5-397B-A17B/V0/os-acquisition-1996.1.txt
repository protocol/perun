**Steelman:** Acquiring a modern, object-oriented OS core from a small vendor is the only viable path to rapidly replace a failed internal platform, halt market share erosion, and leverage the founder's unique vision as a strategic advisor, despite high upfront costs and integration risks.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary failure mode is cultural rejection of the founder and technical debt from migrating legacy software. However, internal attempts have already failed twice; "doing nothing" guarantees continued decline. Licensing a competitor's system surrenders long-term differentiation and control over the hardware-software integration, effectively turning the maker into a commodity assembler. The "high price" is relative; paying a premium for a working, modern core is cheaper than the opportunity cost of further market collapse.

The risk of the founder's "difficult history" is mitigated by limiting their role to "advisor" rather than immediate operational command, allowing the organization to acclimate to their influence while benefiting from their architectural insight. The low sales volume of the target company is irrelevant; the asset being purchased is the technology and the talent, not the current revenue stream.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This aligns with the north star of survival and regaining relevance. If this fails, it will be because the organization refused to adopt the new object-oriented paradigms, not because the acquisition was wrong. This is a one-way door; delaying increases the cost of eventual failure.

**Kill Criteria:**
Proceed only if the integration plan explicitly addresses the legacy migration path within 12 months. The leading metric is the successful beta release of the new OS to developers within 18 months. If the founder's advisory role creates immediate internal paralysis, the engagement must be restructured, but the acquisition stands.

**Verdict:**
The alternatives (status quo, licensing) are existential threats. The acquisition offers the only plausible route to a proprietary, modern future. The risks are manageable compared to the certainty of decline without action.

DECISION: GO
