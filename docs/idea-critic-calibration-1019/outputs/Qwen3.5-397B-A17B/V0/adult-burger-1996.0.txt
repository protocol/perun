**Steelman:** A massive ad spend can overwrite the "kids' value" brand perception, forcing adult trial of a premium burger that generates new revenue streams.

**Attack:** The proposal relies on the unproven assumption that advertising alone can shift deep-seated brand identity from "cheap fun for kids" to "premium dining for adults." History shows that when value-focused chains attempt upmarket pivots without operational proof (taste, service speed), heavy marketing often accelerates failure by highlighting the mismatch between promise and reality. The risk is not just wasted budget, but brand dilution: alienating the core child/family demographic while failing to convert skeptics who perceive the high price as unjustified for a "fast food" label.

**Better Way:** Before committing the "largest budget in industry history," the chain should run a limited regional test. This validates demand, refines the product, and measures actual brand elasticity at a fraction of the cost. If the product fails in a test market, the loss is contained; if it succeeds, the data justifies the spend.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** Assuming the goal is adult growth, this approach ignores the "door" type. This is a one-way door with irreversible financial loss if the brand pivot fails. A pre-mortem reveals that six months post-launch, the chain has burned cash on ads, confused loyal customers, and seen no repeat adult business because the core value proposition remains unchanged.

**Kill Criteria:** The proposal lacks specific metrics for success or stop-conditions. Without a capped initial spend or a clear "kill" metric (e.g., "if adult repeat visits <15% after 3 months, stop ads"), the project invites sunk-cost fallacy.

**Verdict:** The proposal demands an irreversible, massive expenditure based on unverified assumptions about brand malleability and product fit. It fails the "cheaper path" test by skipping validation. The risks of brand confusion and financial waste outweigh the potential upside in its current form.

DECISION: NO-GO
