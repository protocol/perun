**Steelman:** Leverage existing compact infrastructure to deliver affordable sporty styling to a emerging demographic, maximizing margin via options and mitigating development risk through parts sharing.

**Skeptic Attack:** The core failure mode is brand dilution; if the "sporty" image is destroyed by visible economy underpinnings, the target demographic will reject it as "cheap" rather than "value." Furthermore, unproven demand for sporty small cars makes the massive ad spend a potential sunk cost if the product-market fit is misread.

**Better Way:** Instead of a binary "launch big or don't," the safer path is a limited regional pilot to validate the "sporty compact" thesis before committing to a national ad blitz. However, this delays market capture and forfeits the first-mover advantage against competitors who may spot the same demographic shift.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** Assuming the goal is volume growth among young buyers, this fits. If we assume failure: the car launches, looks too much like the economy model, and the ad spend yields no conversion because the styling didn't mask the mechanical roots. The "long hood/short deck" design is the critical variable that must visually break the association with the cheap donor car.

**Evidence & Constraints:** No external data exists to verify demand. The decision rests entirely on the assumption that styling can overcome mechanical perception and that the options list will drive sufficient average revenue per unit to offset the low base price.

**Kill Criteria:** If initial pre-orders do not show a 20% attach rate on high-margin options within 60 days, or if consumer surveys indicate the "economy" perception persists despite the new body, the ad spend must be halted immediately.

**Verdict:** The risk of looking "cheap" is real but manageable through aggressive styling differentiation (long hood/short deck). The demographic tailwind is a rare, time-sensitive opportunity. Waiting for perfect data cedes the segment. The high fixed cost of the ad campaign is justified only if the styling successfully masks the economy roots. The proposal's specific design constraints address the primary skepticism.

DECISION: GO
