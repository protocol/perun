The claim is that acquiring the studio for $7.4 billion is the optimal path to secure future hits and revitalize the conglomerate's animation division. The steelman: only full ownership guarantees control over the studio's creative talent and IP, preventing a rival from capturing them, while installing proven leadership fixes the buyer's internal creative deficits.

However, the skeptic identifies fatal flaws in the current proposal. First, the valuation is extreme: $7.4 billion for a studio producing one film annually implies a multiplier that leaves zero margin for error; a single box-office disappointment could crater the stock price given the all-stock structure. Second, the "culture clash" risk is understated; handing control to external creatives often triggers exodus of key talent if integration is heavy-handed, destroying the very asset being purchased. Third, the founder becoming a major shareholder creates a governance conflict where one individual can hold the conglomerate hostage to personal creative whims.

The better-way analysis suggests alternatives: a profit-sharing joint venture without equity transfer, or a long-term distribution-only deal with first-look rights. These preserve the relationship at a fraction of the capital risk. The "do nothing" option risks losing the partner, but paying a 40-50% premium over market value to prevent that risk is financially reckless without ironclad retention contracts for the creative team.

The pre-mortem assumes the deal closes and the first joint film under new management flops. The result is a dual crisis: the stock tanks due to the write-down of goodwill, and the internal hand-drawn unit resents the new overlords, causing paralysis. The cost of delay is losing the partner to a rival, but the cost of overpaying is permanent capital destruction.

This proposal lacks specific kill criteria: there are no named retention agreements for the creative leaders, no cap on the purchase price tied to performance milestones, and no plan to mitigate the founder's new voting power. Without these safeguards, the risk profile is unacceptable.

DECISION: NO-GO
