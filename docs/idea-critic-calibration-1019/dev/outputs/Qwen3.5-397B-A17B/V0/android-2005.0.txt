The claim is that acquiring a six-person open-source mobile OS startup for $50M will drive mobile web usage and search ad revenue. Steelman: Owning the OS layer prevents search exclusion on mobile devices, creating a defensive moat for core ad business against future platform gatekeepers.

However, critical failures loom. First, **adoption friction**: Handset makers and carriers control distribution and have zero incentive to install a free, unproven OS that disrupts their existing revenue models or support costs. Without pre-installed volume, the OS remains irrelevant, generating no search traffic. Second, **capability mismatch**: A search-ad company lacks the hardware relationships, supply chain knowledge, and long-term capital patience required to nurture a platform ecosystem over many years. Third, **opportunity cost**: $50M is massive for 2005; deploying this capital into direct mobile search partnerships or acquiring established mobile content portals offers faster, verified returns with lower execution risk.

The "free software" argument ignores that "free" does not equal "distributed." The proposal assumes a vacuum where competitors will eagerly adopt a rival's strategic asset. History shows platform wars are won by vertical integration or massive subsidy, neither of which is guaranteed here. The risk of building an orphaned OS that consumes management focus while yielding zero market share is extreme. The payback period is too long and too dependent on external actors (carriers) who are currently hostile to open platforms.

While the strategic fear of being locked out of mobile search is valid, this specific acquisition is a high-cost, low-probability solution. It bets the farm on a hypothetical future where the world changes its distribution mechanics overnight. A "go-if" structure is impossible because the fundamental adoption barriers cannot be solved by money alone in this timeframe. The safer, higher-ROI path is to license search to existing dominant players until the market matures, rather than attempting to build the infrastructure itself from scratch with no relevant experience. The evidence suggests this venture will burn cash without achieving critical mass.

DECISION: NO-GO
