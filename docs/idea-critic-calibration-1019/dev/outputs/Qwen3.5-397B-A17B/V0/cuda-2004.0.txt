The claim is that investing heavily in general-purpose programmability for mass-market graphics chips (2004–2006), despite zero current revenue and high unit costs, will create a dominant new market.

Steelman: Embedding general-purpose compute features now creates an irreversible installed base, locking in future scientific and data markets before rivals can react, turning a commodity gaming player into a platform monopoly.

However, the attack reveals fatal flaws in the current proposal structure. First, the "build it and they will come" strategy ignores the chicken-and-egg problem: without a compiler and tools ready *today*, academic experiments won't translate to commercial demand, yet building the full toolchain before validating demand burns cash on every unit sold. Second, raising unit costs on *every* consumer chip to serve a niche that doesn't yet pay threatens the core gaming revenue stream; if the rival undercuts on price/performance for gamers while this firm subsidizes unused silicon, market share collapses before the new market matures. Third, the proposal lacks a "kill criterion" or phased gate; it commits to a multi-year, all-in spend without a pilot to verify if developers actually adopt the new architecture versus sticking to CPUs or specialized clusters.

The "do nothing" alternative (waiting for clear demand signals) preserves cash and focus. A "better way" is to partner with specific high-value academic labs to co-develop the toolchain on existing hardware or a limited specialty SKU, rather than bloating every mass-market unit. The cost of delay is low because the market is currently theoretical; the cost of failure (bankruptcy from lost gaming share) is existential.

Without a committed anchor customer, a verified toolchain prototype, or a capped initial rollout to test adoption, this is a speculative bet that jeopardizes the company's survival in its only proven market. The proposal fails to prove that the engineering distraction won't cause them to lose the core gaming race.

DECISION: NO-GO
