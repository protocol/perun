The claim is that acquiring a declining portal for $4.8 billion will create a scaled mobile media competitor to dominant ad platforms. The steelman posits that combining two legacy assets creates sufficient scale and data synergy to reverse declines and challenge duopoly leaders.

However, the "better way" analysis reveals a fatal flaw: the strategy relies on merging two deteriorating businesses to solve a growth problem. The target has falling traffic, shrinking ad revenue, and unstable management. The acquirer lacks any consumer media history. There is no evidence that $4.8 billion buys a turnaround rather than accelerating decay. The "do nothing" or "invest in organic mobile infrastructure" alternatives likely offer higher ROI with lower integration risk. Combining two weak positions rarely creates a strong one; it often compounds weaknesses.

The pre-mortem is clear: integration drags distract the carrier's core business, the target's revenue continues to slide post-acquisition due to market shifts not fixed by ownership change, and the combined entity fails to attract premium ad spend away from the dominant platforms. The cost of delay is low compared to the certainty of overpaying for a shrinking asset. The kill criteria are missing: there is no defined metric to stop the bleeding if the revenue decline accelerates post-merger.

The strongest attack is the lack of product-market fit evidence. The proposal assumes scale equals competitiveness, ignoring that the dominant platforms win on technology and user engagement, not just legacy inventory aggregation. Buying a "declining internet portal" contradicts the goal of building a future-proof mobile business. The $4.8 billion price tag for a failing asset suggests a "falling knife" scenario where the acquirer becomes the exit liquidity for the target's shareholders rather than building value.

Without a proven mechanism to reverse the target's decline or evidence of the carrier's ability to manage consumer media, this is a high-cost gamble on a broken model. The risks of revenue decline and integration failure are not just possibilities; they are the current trajectory of the target.

DECISION: NO-GO
