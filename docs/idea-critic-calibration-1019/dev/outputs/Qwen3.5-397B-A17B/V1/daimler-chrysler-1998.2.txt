**Steelman:** Combining German engineering prestige with US mass-market profitability creates a dominant global entity, balancing cyclical risks through geographic and segment diversification.

**Attack:** The "merger of equals" narrative masks a fundamental cultural collision. German hierarchical precision conflicts with US decentralized, volume-driven aggression. History shows such mergers often result in paralysis rather than synergy. The US partner's current profitability relies entirely on a light-truck boom; if this cycle turns (as cycles do), the German firm acquires a sinking anchor, not a growth engine. Platform sharing between premium and mass-market brands risks diluting the German brand's exclusivity, destroying the very value being purchased. The "equal" structure invites governance deadlock, preventing decisive action during the inevitable integration crises.

**Better Way:** Instead of a full merger, pursue a strategic alliance for specific platform sharing or a minority stake. This captures supply chain benefits without the catastrophic risk of cultural integration failure or brand dilution. Alternatively, wait for the US truck cycle to cool; acquiring during a boom maximizes purchase price and risk exposure.

**Pre-mortem:** In five years, the merged entity has lost billions. The German brand is perceived as "cheapened" due to shared components with low-margin US trucks. The US division drags down margins as truck sales normalize. Leadership is gridlocked by a fractured board representing conflicting national and labor interests. The integration costs exceeded all projections, and key talent fled both legacy cultures.

**Kill Criteria:** Proceed only if a binding governance mechanism breaks deadlocks within 30 days and a strict firewall prevents component sharing that touches visible premium brand elements. Without these, the risk of value destruction is absolute.

**Verdict:** The proposal ignores the high probability of cultural incompatibility and the timing risk of buying at a cyclical peak. The "merger of equals" structure is a recipe for governance failure. The potential for brand dilution is an unacceptable threat to the German firm's core asset. The risks vastly outweigh the theoretical synergies.

DECISION: NO-GO
