**Steelman:** Leveraging massive existing distribution (search, mail, video) to solve the cold-start problem via forced integration, while differentiating with superior privacy controls (circles) and real-time video (hangouts), can dislodge an incumbent complacent in ad-cluttered feeds.

**Attack:** The primary failure mode is not technical but behavioral: network effects create a high switching cost that "better features" rarely overcome. History shows this company has failed three times at social; a fourth attempt driven by executive bonus targets risks prioritizing vanity metrics over genuine engagement. Forced integration into core utilities (mail, search) treats users as a captive audience, likely generating resentment and churn in high-value products rather than organic social adoption. The "invite-only" beta contradicts the "forced integration" strategy, creating confusion about whether this is exclusive or mandatory.

**Better Way:** Instead of a company-wide "push" threatening core product integrity, run a contained experiment. Decouple executive bonuses from adoption numbers to prevent gaming. Focus strictly on the video hangout differentiator as a standalone utility within existing products, letting social graphs form around specific actions rather than forcing a generic profile layer everywhere. "Do nothing" regarding a full network replacement preserves core search dominance.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** Assuming the goal is ad-revenue diversification, this bet risks the core brand. If it fails (likely, given history), the annoyance of forced integration damages trust in search and mail. The cost of delay is low; the incumbent is not currently threatening the core search business.

**Evidence & Calibration:** The proposal admits three prior failures and relies on unproven assumptions that users want to merge their search identity with social sharing. No evidence suggests circles or hangouts are sufficient to break entrenched network effects. The bonus structure creates a perverse incentive to force usage, validating the risk of user annoyance.

**Verdict:** The plan repeats past mistakes with higher stakes (core product integration) and misaligned incentives (bonuses). It lacks a credible mechanism to overcome network effects beyond "force it."

DECISION: NO-GO
