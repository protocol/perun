**Steelman:** A premium, integrated device leverages a loyal, albeit small, user base to establish a foothold in a fragmented market, betting that superior usability and design will overcome high price and limited compatibility.

**Attack:** The proposal fails on three critical fronts. First, the addressable market is dangerously narrow; restricting usage to a niche computer platform ignores the vast majority of potential music listeners, capping growth before launch. Second, the $400 price point is unjustifiable against established, cheaper competitors when the core value proposition (legal downloads) lacks industry licensing support. Third, the one-year timeline for sourcing custom components and building a supply chain from scratch, without prior consumer electronics experience, invites catastrophic delays and quality failures.

**Better Way:** License the software to Windows immediately to expand the market, or delay launch until legal download infrastructure exists. Alternatively, partner with an existing hardware manufacturer to mitigate sourcing risks.

**Pre-mortem:** The project launches late due to supply chain ignorance, sells few units because of the high price and Mac-only restriction, and is discontinued within 18 months as a costly distraction. The company burns cash on inventory it cannot move.

**Fit:** This contradicts the goal of market expansion. It isolates the product rather than integrating it into the broader ecosystem where music consumers actually exist.

**Evidence:** Current market data shows low adoption of expensive, niche players. No legal download ecosystem exists to drive hardware necessity. The company has zero track record in mass-market consumer hardware sourcing.

**Kill Criteria:** If a supply chain partner cannot be secured within 60 days, or if music labels do not commit to a digital store framework within six months, the project must stop.

**Verdict:** The risks of market isolation, unproven supply chain execution, and premature timing outweigh the potential benefits. The proposal relies on assumptions about consumer behavior and industry readiness that are currently unsupported.

DECISION: NO-GO
