**Steelman:** Committing now secures a decade-long lead in hybrid technology, forcing competitors to play catch-up while defining the future standard for fuel efficiency before regulators mandate it.

**Attack:** The proposal fails on three verified fronts. First, with fuel cheap and no regulatory mandate, the market demand signal is nonexistent; customers will not pay a premium for unproven efficiency. Second, the nickel-metal-hydride battery and novel transmission lack durability data; a mass-market launch invites catastrophic warranty exposure that could bankrupt the division. Third, declaring a bold public schedule before solving these technical risks removes the ability to pivot quietly if failures occur.

**Better Way:** The "do nothing" stance is superior for now. A safer alternative is a limited fleet pilot (taxis/government) to gather real-world durability data without public scrutiny or mass warranty liability. This delays the public commitment until the technology is proven and the regulatory landscape clarifies.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume it is 1998. The cars are recalled due to battery degradation within 18 months. The novel transmission jams under cold weather stress. The company faces billions in losses, the brand is tainted as unreliable, and the stock plummets because the "bold schedule" forced a launch before the engineering was ready.

**Fit:** This does not move the north star of profitable volume sales; it gambles the company's liquidity on a speculative technology in an indifferent market. The cost of delay is negligible compared to the cost of a failed mass launch.

**Kill Criteria:** The proposal lacks a specific metric for battery cycle life validation or a capped budget for the development phase. Without a proven prototype surviving 100,000 miles, mass production is unjustifiable.

**Verdict:** The risks of financial ruin and brand destruction outweigh the speculative first-mover advantage. The lack of demand, unproven durability, and absence of regulatory pressure make this a fatal gamble.

DECISION: NO-GO
