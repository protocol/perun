**Steelman:** Secure dominant market share in online grocery by building an unassailable moat of automated infrastructure before competitors can react, betting that scale will eventually crush unit economics.

**Attack:** This strategy violates every known constraint of the 1999 landscape. The internet penetration rate is too low to support the customer density required for 30-minute slots in a dozen cities simultaneously. Grocery margins are too thin to absorb the depreciation of $1 billion in specialized automation before product-market fit is proven in a single zone. The "30-minute slot" promise creates a logistical nightmare that scales linearly with distance but exponentially with cost. There is zero evidence that customers will pay the premium necessary to cover these costs, nor that the technology can reliably execute this in 1999.

**Better Way:** The "do nothing" alternative regarding massive expansion is superior. The rational path is a single-city pilot to validate unit economics, delivery density, and customer retention. A "go-if" approach would tie further construction to achieving positive contribution margin in the first metro area. Expanding to a dozen cities before learning the basics of one turns unknown risks into certain catastrophes.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume it is 2001 and the company is bankrupt. Why? Because fixed costs (warehouses, trucks) skyrocketed while revenue remained flat due to low adoption and high churn. The capital ran out before the operational model worked. The one-way door of irreversible infrastructure spending locked the company into a failing model with no pivot option.

**Kill Criteria:** The proposal lacks any stop condition. It commits 100% of the capital based on hope, not data. A fatal flaw exists: committing to irreversible, high-fixed-cost infrastructure across multiple markets without a single data point on unit economics or demand density. This is not a calculated risk; it is a gamble with existential stakes.

**Verdict:** The plan fails the "fit" and "evidence" checks. It ignores the stage of the market (1999 infancy) and the north star of profitability. The risk is total loss of capital.

DECISION: NO-GO
