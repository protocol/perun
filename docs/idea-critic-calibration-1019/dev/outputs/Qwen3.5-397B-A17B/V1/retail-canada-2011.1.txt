**Steelman:** Rapidly acquiring 130–150 sites leverages existing lease opportunities to achieve immediate scale, deterring competitor retaliation and establishing market presence before local nuances can be fully studied.

**Attack:** The proposal conflates "speed" with "success." Opening nearly all stores within two years while simultaneously building an unproven cross-border supply chain and inventory system creates a single point of catastrophic failure. If the new inventory system glitches (highly probable in first deployment), 150 stores face stockouts simultaneously, destroying brand reputation instantly. The "larger, entrenched competitor" can easily outlast a cash-strain war of attrition. Learning regulation and bilingual labeling *after* signing leases invites costly retrofits or legal fines. The cost of delay is low; the cost of a botched launch is existential.

**Better Way:** A "beachhead" strategy. Open 5–10 pilot stores in diverse regions to stress-test the supply chain and inventory system. Validate the price perception against the entrenched competitor with real data, not assumptions. Only scale once the unit economics and logistics are proven. This reduces capital exposure and allows for iterative learning without risking the entire expansion budget.

**Fit:** This aggressive pace contradicts prudent capital allocation for a first-time international entrant. It ignores the "one-way door" nature of long-term leases and fixed infrastructure costs.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, the inventory system fails to handle local tax variations, causing widespread compliance issues. Simultaneously, supply chain bottlenecks leave shelves empty. The brand is perceived as unreliable rather than "discount." Cash reserves are depleted fixing systemic errors, forcing a retreat.

**Kill Criteria:** Do not proceed without a successful 6-month pilot of the inventory system in a domestic or limited cross-border context. Do not sign more than 10 leases until the supply chain moves 10,000 units without critical failure.

**Verdict:** The proposal lacks a staged approach, relying on unproven systems at maximum scale. The risk of total failure outweighs the benefit of speed.

DECISION: NO-GO
