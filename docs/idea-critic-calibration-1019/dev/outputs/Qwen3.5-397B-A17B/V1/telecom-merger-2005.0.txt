**Steelman:** Acquiring the target secures a sticky, high-margin enterprise segment (construction/business) via its unique push-to-talk service, creating immediate revenue synergy despite technological friction.

**Attack:** The core failure mode is not cost, but **technological deadlock**. In 2005, merging incompatible network architectures (e.g., CDMA vs. GSM/iDEN) requires maintaining parallel infrastructures indefinitely or forcing a painful, churn-inducing migration of the very customers being bought. The "limited data" capability of the target suggests the asset is a legacy peak, not a growth engine. The $36 billion valuation likely prices in synergies that the technical incompatibility makes impossible to realize without destroying the product's utility.

**Better Way:** Instead of a full merger, a commercial roaming or MVNO agreement could capture the revenue share without the $36 billion balance sheet risk and integration nightmare. Alternatively, acquire only the customer contracts and migrate them to the buyer's native Push-to-Talk solution over time, leaving the legacy network to die.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume the deal closes. Two years later, integration costs have doubled, the legacy network is draining capex, and business customers defect to competitors offering superior data-enabled devices because the merged entity cannot innovate on the stranded platform. The goodwill becomes an impairment charge.

**Fit & Evidence:** The proposal admits the technologies are "incompatible" and the target is "limited for data." In a rapidly converging voice/data market, buying a data-limited, incompatible network contradicts the industry trajectory. The risk of running dual networks erodes the margin advantage of the business base.

**Kill Criteria:** This fails the "strategic fit" test. You are buying a customer base attached to a dying, incompatible technology at a premium price ($36B). The integration risk threatens the core network stability of the buyer. The "do nothing" or "commercial partnership" options dominate this capital-intensive merger. The lack of a clear migration path for the incompatible tech is a fatal flaw.

DECISION: NO-GO
