**Steelman:** Launching a distinct mid-priced division leverages existing manufacturing to capture a growing market segment, preventing customer defection to competitors while maximizing dealership revenue through a dedicated network.

**Attack:** The proposal relies on static market data (growth trends) while ignoring dynamic macroeconomic indicators (recession risk). The mid-priced segment is the most discretionary; in a downturn, buyers trade down to low-priced reliability or up to premium status, hollowing out the middle. Furthermore, building a new dealer network requires massive fixed capital expenditure precisely when liquidity may be needed for survival. The "extensive research" likely reflects consumer desires in a boom, not behavior in a bust. The risk of internal brand overlap is secondary to the existential threat of launching a high-burn venture at the peak of a cycle.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Preserve capital, utilize existing brands to cover the gap with trim variations, and wait for economic clarity.
2.  **Badge Engineering:** Launch mid-priced models under existing dealer networks to test demand without infrastructure costs.
3.  **Delayed Launch:** Postpone the debut until recession signals clarify, sacrificing first-mover advantage for solvency.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume it is 1958 and the division is failing. Why? A recession hit in late 1957; consumers rejected the new brand as overpriced; the company is burdened with idle dealerships and unsold inventory while cash reserves are depleted. The massive ad spend cannot overcome the fundamental shift in consumer purchasing power.

**Fit:** This moves the north star of "market coverage" but jeopardizes the primary constraint of "financial stability." The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of failure. The one-way door is the dealer network construction; once built, those costs cannot be recovered.

**Verdict:** The proposal fails the timing test. Launching a capital-intensive, discretionary product line immediately before a probable recession, dependent on unproven dealer infrastructure, presents a fatal flaw. The "growth" cited is likely a lagging indicator. The safer path is to defer the network build and test the product via existing channels.

DECISION: NO-GO
