The claim is that a differentiated display and shopping feature can overcome a smaller app ecosystem, high price, and carrier exclusivity in a saturated two-platform market. The steelman: unique hardware utility creates a niche strong enough to sustain a walled-garden OS despite missing mainstream apps.

However, the failure hypothesis is severe. In a two-platform duopoly, the "app gap" is not a minor inconvenience but a primary purchase driver; missing mainstream apps renders the device non-viable for mass adoption, regardless of 3D capabilities. The "one-touch shopping" feature is a single-use case that cannot justify the daily utility loss of a fragmented app store. Furthermore, launching at flagship prices without subsidy, exclusively on one carrier, severely limits the total addressable market while demanding premium loyalty from a user base already entrenched in competing ecosystems.

The "do nothing" alternative (focusing on the successful e-reader and tablet) avoids the massive sunk cost of hardware R&D and ecosystem development for a product with low switching incentives. A cheaper, simpler path would be integrating the shopping camera feature into existing successful devices or partnering with existing OEMs rather than building a full phone.

The pre-mortem reveals the likely outcome: high initial curiosity driven by the 3D feature, followed by rapid churn and negative reviews due to the lack of essential apps, leading to inventory write-downs and brand dilution. The cost of delay is negligible compared to the risk of a high-profile failure. The evidence of market saturation and the dominance of two platforms suggests that differentiation via a single hardware gimmick is insufficient to break network effects.

This proposal fails the calibration test: the risk of the app gap is a fatal flaw that the proposed kill criteria (shopping feature) cannot mitigate. The value proposition does not align with the primary driver of smartphone adoption (app ecosystem).

DECISION: NO-GO
