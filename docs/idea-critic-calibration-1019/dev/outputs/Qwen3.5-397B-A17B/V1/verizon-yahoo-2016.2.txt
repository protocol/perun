The claim is that acquiring a declining portal for $4.8 billion will create a scaled mobile media competitor to dominant ad platforms. The steelman posits that combining two legacy assets creates sufficient data density and inventory to pivot effectively before revenue collapses completely.

However, the "better way" analysis reveals a fatal flaw: the strategy relies on merging two failing business models to solve a scale problem against entrenched winners. The target suffers from years of traffic decline, falling ad revenue, and management instability. The acquirer is a wireless carrier with zero history in consumer media, lacking the core competency to execute a complex cultural and technical integration. The "do nothing" or "build/buy emerging tech" alternatives likely offer superior risk-adjusted returns compared to paying a premium for a deteriorating asset.

The pre-mortem is clear: integration drags on, key talent flees due to culture clash, and the combined entity continues to lose market share to the dominant platforms while carrying massive debt. The cost of delay is low; waiting allows the price to drop further or reveals the asset as unsalvageable. There is no evidence provided that the carrier can reverse the target's structural decline. The $4.8 billion price tag assumes a turnaround capability that neither party possesses.

This fails the "fit" test: it attempts to solve a future mobile problem with past-era desktop assets. The risks (revenue decline, integration failure) are not merely "exposures" to be managed; they are the central trajectory of the target. No kill criteria or leading metrics are offered to validate the turnaround thesis because the thesis itself contradicts the verified trend of multi-year decay. A capped pilot is impossible at this acquisition scale; it is a binary, high-stakes bet on a broken model.

The strongest attack is that the proposal attempts to buy scale in a winner-take-all market using assets defined by their inability to compete. Without a proven mechanism to halt the decline or a track record in media, the integration will likely accelerate value destruction.

DECISION: NO-GO
