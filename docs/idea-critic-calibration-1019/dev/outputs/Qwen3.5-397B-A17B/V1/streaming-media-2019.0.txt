**Steelman:** Withdrawing content to launch a subsidized, proprietary streaming service is the only viable defense against long-term disintermediation by dominant digital distributors, accepting short-term financial pain to secure direct customer relationships and future pricing power.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary failure mode is underestimating the "content moat" required to sustain subscriptions without a deep, diverse library, leading to high churn once the initial launch hype fades. The proposal assumes tens of millions of subscribers will materialize quickly enough to justify the massive cash burn and lost licensing fees. However, the market leader's hundred-million-subscriber head start creates a network effect and content volume gap that a low-price strategy alone may not overcome.

*   **Do Nothing:** Continue earning billions in low-risk licensing revenue. This preserves cash flow but cedes the direct-to-consumer future to rivals, eventually reducing the conglomerate to a mere content supplier with diminishing leverage.
*   **Hybrid Approach:** License content selectively while launching a smaller, niche service. This reduces risk but fails to capture the mass-market scale necessary to compete with the leader.
*   **Proposed Path:** High risk, high reward. It requires flawless execution of an unproven technology platform and sustained investor patience during years of losses.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This aligns with the industry's shift toward direct-to-consumer models (North Star: owning the customer). If we assume the role of a late entrant, the pre-mortem reveals a likely failure where subscriber growth stalls at half the target due to content fatigue and technical instability, while the lost licensing revenue creates a cash crunch that forces a premature sale or merger. The "one-way door" nature of pulling content from rivals means this decision is largely irreversible; once licenses lapse, rebuilding those revenue streams is difficult.

**Evidence & Kill Criteria:**
The proposal lacks specific data on customer acquisition costs versus lifetime value in this saturated market. The "multi-year operating loss" is undefined; without a hard cap on total cash burn or a specific subscriber milestone per quarter, the project could drain capital indefinitely.

**Decision:**
The strategic imperative to own the distribution channel outweighs the short-term financial risks, provided the company has the balance sheet to endure the war of attrition. However, the proposal as stated lacks the necessary guardrails (specific burn limits, interim milestones) to prevent unlimited cash destruction. It is a "go" only if treated as a capped, time-boxed bet with strict kill triggers. Since the prompt asks to approve "as proposed" and the proposal lacks these critical financial controls, it is too open-ended to approve without revision.

DECISION: NO-GO
