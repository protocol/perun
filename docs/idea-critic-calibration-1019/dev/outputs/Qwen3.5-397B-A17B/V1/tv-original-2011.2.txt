**Steelman:** Leveraging proprietary viewing data to bypass the pilot system and commission a high-profile original series in one batch creates a unique market position, locks in top talent, and aligns release mechanics with binge-watching behavior, transforming the service from a distributor to a studio.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary failure mode is financial catastrophe: spending a massive portion of the content budget on an unproven format with no audience validation. Traditional de-risking via a pilot is absent. Alternatives include: (1) producing a low-budget pilot first; (2) licensing more content while costs are manageable; (3) commissioning a shorter, single-season order. However, these alternatives sacrifice the strategic shock value and talent acquisition possible only with a "straight-to-series" commitment. In 2011, the market undervalues data-driven greenlighting; being first offers a monopoly on the "binge" narrative.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This moves the North Star from "content aggregation" to "essential destination." If it fails, it is because the data misinterpreted niche interest as mass appeal, or because releasing all episodes kills water-cooler momentum. Yet, the risk of waiting (cost of delay) is higher: competitors will eventually use data similarly, and licensing costs will continue to erode margins. The "one-way door" nature is mitigated by the fact that even a flop generates immense brand awareness and subscriber acquisition data that licensing cannot provide.

**Evidence & Calibration:**
The claim relies on internal viewing data showing high completion rates for specific genres and directors. While external validation is zero, the internal signal is the only asymmetric advantage available. The lack of a pilot is a feature, not a bug, securing talent who refuse TV-style testing.

**Verdict:**
The proposal contains high risk but no fatal flaw. The risks (cost, audience uncertainty) are explicitly known and are the price of market creation. The kill criterion is clear: if the show does not drive a specific net-new subscriber threshold within 90 days, the original content strategy halts. The potential upside (defining a new industry standard) dominates the "do nothing" approach of slowly bleeding margin on licensing.

DECISION: GO
