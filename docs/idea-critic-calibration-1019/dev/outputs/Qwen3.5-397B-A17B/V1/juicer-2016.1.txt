The claim is that a $699 Wi-Fi-connected juicer with a proprietary subscription model will succeed in the 2016 consumer hardware market. Steelman: The convenience of pre-packed freshness and automated tracking justifies a high premium for affluent early adopters, creating a recurring revenue moat.

However, the attack reveals fatal friction. First, the "razor-and-blade" model fails on unit economics: $699 is a prohibitive upfront barrier for an unproven habit, while proprietary bags likely cost significantly more per serving than fresh produce, creating immediate buyer's remorse. Second, mandatory Wi-Fi for a mechanical pressing function introduces unnecessary failure points and privacy concerns without adding core value; if the internet drops, the appliance should not brick. Third, the market for single-use plastic produce bags is shrinking due to environmental awareness, making the value proposition culturally misaligned. Finally, the "freshness check" via app is a solution in search of a problem; consumers can smell or see spoiled fruit without a connected device.

The "do nothing" alternative (buying a standard $100 juicer and fresh fruit) dominates on cost, flexibility, and reliability. A "better way" would be a non-connected device using standard containers, lowering the price point to impulse-buy territory and removing the subscription lock-in. The pre-mortem is clear: the company burns cash on custom bag manufacturing and app maintenance, sales stall after the early adopter phase due to high ongoing costs, and the hardware becomes e-waste when servers shut down or subscriptions lapse. The cost of delay is low; waiting to validate demand with a simpler prototype prevents wasting millions on custom tooling for a likely failed form factor.

This proposal conflates "connected" with "valuable" and ignores the fundamental friction of high upfront costs paired with expensive consumables in a crowded kitchen appliance market. The risks are not merely high price or unproven market; they are a structurally flawed business model that alienates users immediately upon purchase.

DECISION: NO-GO
