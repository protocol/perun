The claim is that replacing the flagship formula with a sweeter alternative, validated by blind taste tests, will reverse market share losses. The steelman: sensory superiority drives purchase frequency, and removing the old formula forces migration to the superior product, maximizing efficiency.

However, the attack reveals a fatal flaw in the evidence methodology. The taste tests isolated liquid sensory input but excluded the brand context, which constitutes the majority of the product's value proposition for loyalists. By withholding the fact that the original would be removed, the tests measured preference for an *additional* option, not a *replacement*. This creates a false positive: consumers can prefer the taste of the rival while simultaneously valuing the heritage of the original enough to reject its removal.

The "better way" is a line extension: launch the sweeter formula as a new variant alongside the original, allowing the market to vote with wallets without triggering a loss-aversion backlash. The proposed plan ignores the "endowment effect," where ownership increases perceived value; removing the endowed asset (the original recipe) will be perceived as a loss, not an optimization.

The pre-mortem is clear: launching this as a total replacement will be interpreted not as an improvement, but as an erasure of cultural heritage. The emotional reaction will overshadow the sensory data. Competitors will exploit the void left by the withdrawn original, potentially reintroducing it themselves or capturing the alienated demographic entirely. The risk is not just failure to gain share, but the active destruction of the core customer base with no fallback mechanism.

The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of irreversible brand damage. A one-way door decision based on incomplete data (missing the "removal" variable in testing) violates basic decision hygiene. The lack of a pilot with full disclosure and a fallback option makes this unacceptable. The proposal fails to account for the non-rational drivers of brand loyalty which the blind tests explicitly filtered out.

DECISION: NO-GO
