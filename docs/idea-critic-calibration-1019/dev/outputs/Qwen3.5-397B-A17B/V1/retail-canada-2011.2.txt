**Steelman:** Rapidly acquiring 130–150 sites establishes immediate scale to deter the entrenched competitor, leveraging the retailer's loyal domestic base to quickly capture market share before prices normalize.

**Attack:** The proposal conflates "presence" with "viability." Opening nearly all stores within two years while simultaneously building an unproven cross-border supply chain and inventory system creates a single point of catastrophic failure. If the new logistics network falters—even briefly—empty shelves across 130+ locations will permanently destroy brand trust in the new market. The "loyal domestic following" offers zero insulation against local execution errors. Furthermore, entering a market with higher incumbent prices suggests a fragile margin structure; any delay in achieving operational efficiency turns every open store into a cash drain. The risk is not just "pace," but the impossibility of debugging a complex, bilingual, cross-border supply chain while under the pressure of mass rollout.

**Better Way:** A phased pilot (5–10 stores) would validate the supply chain, labeling compliance, and price perception with capped exposure. This allows the inventory system to stabilize before scaling. The current plan bets the entire international thesis on an untested system running at maximum load immediately.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** Assuming the goal is long-term profitability, not just footprint, this approach fails the "do no harm" test. In a pre-mortem: Six months in, the inventory system glitches during a peak season, causing stockouts in 80% of new stores. Local consumers, accustomed to the reliable incumbent, switch back permanently. The retailer is stuck with 130 leased sites it cannot fill, facing massive write-downs and reputational damage that bleeds back to the domestic brand.

**Kill Criteria:** The lack of a proven supply chain pilot is a fatal flaw. The proposal admits the systems are "not yet proven" yet mandates full deployment. There is no reversible off-ramp; signing 130 leases commits the capital before learning occurs. The cost of delay (waiting to validate the system) is negligible compared to the cost of a systemic collapse.

**Verdict:** The plan relies on flawless execution of unproven components at maximum scale. This is not a calculated risk; it is a gamble with undefined downside.

DECISION: NO-GO
