The claim is that a $699 Wi-Fi-connected juicer with a proprietary subscription model will succeed in the 2016 consumer hardware market. The steelman posits that convenience and freshness tracking justify a massive premium over traditional juicing, creating a recurring revenue moat.

However, the attack reveals fatal fractures. First, the unit economics are hostile: at $699, the device targets early adopters, yet the recurring cost of proprietary bags likely exceeds fresh produce, removing the value proposition after the novelty fades. Second, the "Wi-Fi connected" requirement for a juicer is a solution in search of a problem; checking freshness via an app adds friction compared to visual inspection and introduces unnecessary failure points (connectivity issues, server downtime) for a mechanical task. Third, the market for single-function, expensive countertop appliances is notoriously prone to rapid churn and "closet-ware" status. The dependency on a continuous supply chain for proprietary bags creates a single point of failure for the user experience that generic competitors do not face.

Alternatives include a cheaper, non-connected device using standard containers or a partnership with existing grocery delivery services without hardware lock-in. The "do nothing" option avoids the massive capital burn required to manufacture hardware, manage logistics for perishable goods, and subsidize customer acquisition in a skeptical market.

The pre-mortem is clear: the company runs out of cash trying to educate consumers on why a juicer needs internet and why bags cost 3x more than fruit. The cost of delay is low; entering later with a proven model is safer than pioneering this specific high-friction combination now. The evidence suggests the "smart" feature adds cost and risk without solving a core user pain point, while the subscription model fights against established, cheaper behaviors. This is a classic case of over-engineering a simple utility and betting on a behavioral shift that history suggests is unlikely to happen at this price point.

DECISION: NO-GO
