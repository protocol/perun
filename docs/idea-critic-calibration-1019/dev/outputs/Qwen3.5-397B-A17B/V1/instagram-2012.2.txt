**Steelman:** Acquire the dominant mobile photo threat immediately to neutralize competition, secure mobile relevance before IPO scrutiny, and integrate their culture rather than rebuilding a weak internal product.

**Attack:** The $1B price tag lacks revenue justification, creating massive financial risk if the hype cycle bursts. Integrating a "dozen employees" into a large corporate structure historically destroys the very culture that drove their success, potentially rendering the asset worthless. Regulatory bodies may block the deal as an anti-competitive monopoly grab during an IPO window.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Save $1B but face a entrenched mobile competitor post-IPO with weakened shareholder confidence.
2.  **Build Internally:** Attempt to fix the weak mobile product; high risk of failure given current traction gaps and time constraints.
3.  **Acquire:** High cost, but eliminates the primary threat instantly and captures the user base.

**Fit:** Aligns with the north star of maintaining social dominance during the mobile transition. The cost of delay (waiting for IPO or organic growth) likely exceeds the acquisition premium, as the competitor's network effects are accelerating.

**Pre-mortem:** The acquisition fails if the founders leave within six months due to cultural friction, or if regulators force a divestiture. The "no business model" risk is mitigated by the buyer's existing monetization engine, provided the user base remains engaged.

**Decision Logic:** This is a two-way door in strategy (can spin off or shut down later) but a one-way door in capital allocation. However, the strategic necessity of owning the mobile layer outweighs the financial overpayment. The "fatal flaw" of no revenue is irrelevant for a defensive acquisition of a network effect business; the metric to watch is user retention post-integration. The risk of *not* buying (losing the mobile war) is existential, whereas the risk of overpaying is financial.

**Kill Criteria:** If founder retention drops below 50% within 12 months, initiate independence protocols. Leading metric: Daily Active Users (DAU) growth rate on mobile platforms quarter-over-quarter.

DECISION: GO
