**Steelman:** Paying $19B eliminates an existential mobile threat and secures hundreds of millions of active users who have already rejected the buyer's current offerings, buying immediate relevance in a shifting market.

**Attack:** The price is 12x the last valuation with zero revenue, relying entirely on the assumption that messaging displaces social networking permanently. There is no monetization path, significant regulatory exposure regarding user data combination, and extreme key-person risk given the tiny 50-person team. If the founders leave or regulators block data integration, the asset becomes a costly, isolated silo.

**Alternatives:** 
1. *Do nothing:* Risk continued erosion of the core user base by this competitor.
2. *Build/Buy smaller:* Attempt to clone features or acquire smaller rivals; likely too slow given the target's network effects.
3. *Staged investment:* Impossible; the target seeks a full exit, and partial stakes do not solve the displacement threat.

**Fit:** Aligns with the survival imperative (North Star: maintain user engagement dominance) but violates standard financial discipline. This is a defensive "tax" paid to survive a platform shift, not a traditional ROI play.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if regulators force separation of data (nullifying the strategic value) or if the culture clash causes the founders to exit within 18 months, leaving the buyer with an overpriced app and no integration synergy.

**Decision Logic:** This is a one-way door with massive cost of delay; waiting even a year could cement the competitor's dominance irreversibly. While the financials are unjustifiable in isolation, the strategic cost of *not* acting (potential obsolescence) exceeds the $19B loss. The lack of a monetization plan is acceptable only if the acquisition successfully stops the bleeding of the core business. However, without a binding, verified retention agreement for the founders and a clear legal strategy for privacy integration *before* closing, the risk of paying $19B for a hollow shell is too high to approve *as proposed*. The proposal lacks the specific kill criteria and retention guarantees required for a bet of this magnitude.

DECISION: NO-GO
