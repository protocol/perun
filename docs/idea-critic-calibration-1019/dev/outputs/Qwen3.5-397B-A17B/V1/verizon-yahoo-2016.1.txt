The claim is that acquiring a declining portal for $4.8 billion will create a scaled mobile media competitor to dominant ad platforms. The steelman: combining two legacy assets creates critical mass in mobile data and content, leveraging the carrier's distribution to reverse the target's decline before it becomes worthless.

However, the "better way" analysis reveals a fatal flaw in the "do nothing" alternative comparison. The proposal assumes scale alone defeats network effects held by the two dominant platforms. History shows that in digital advertising, scale without unique data or superior technology leads to a "race to the bottom" on CPMs, not profitability. The target has falling traffic, falling revenue, and unstable management; the acquirer has zero consumer media experience. This is not a turnaround; it is catching a falling knife.

The pre-mortem is clear: integration drags down the carrier's core focus while the acquired assets continue to decay due to structural market shifts, not just management. The $4.8 billion price tag assumes the asset can be fixed, but the evidence (years of decline, repeated management failures) suggests the business model itself is obsolete. The "cheaper alternative" is to invest the $4.8 billion in the carrier's own network infrastructure or partner with an existing innovator, avoiding the massive integration risk.

The kill criterion is the lack of a proven mechanism to stop the revenue decline. The proposal offers no specific product synergy, only vague "scale." Without a concrete plan to arrest the decline immediately post-close, the integration risk becomes a certainty of value destruction. The assumption that a wireless carrier can successfully manage a consumer media turnaround contradicts the stated fact of their "no history in consumer media."

This fails the calibration test: the specific condition of "declining traffic + no acquirer expertise + high price" guarantees the bad outcome of capital destruction. The risks are not merely "high price" or "integration"; they are fundamental incompatibility and structural decay. No tripwire can save a strategy based on the hope that 1+1=3 when both numbers are negative.

DECISION: NO-GO
