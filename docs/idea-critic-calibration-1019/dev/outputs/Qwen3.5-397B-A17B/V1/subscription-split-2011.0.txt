The claim is that splitting the DVD and streaming services into separate brands, accounts, and billing systems will allow each to pursue optimal strategies despite a recent 60% price hike for the combined offering.

**Steelman:** Separating the declining DVD asset from the growth-oriented streaming business prevents brand drag, allows distinct management focus, and isolates the financial optics of the DVD decline from the streaming valuation.

**Attack:** The proposal ignores the immediate context: a massive 60% price increase occurred just two months prior. Executing a complex split (new names, separate logins, separate bills) immediately after a price hike compounds customer friction. The "loss of cross-service recommendations" is not a minor feature loss; it devalues the remaining DVD subscription by removing the modern streaming utility, effectively forcing a downgrade. The risk of "customer confusion" is understated; this looks like a bait-and-switch where customers pay more for a fragmented experience. The "do nothing" alternative (maintain one brand, absorb the DVD decline as a managed sunset) preserves customer goodwill during a sensitive pricing transition. The proposed split creates two points of failure (two logins, two bills) precisely when customer tolerance is lowest.

**Better Way:** Keep a single brand and account. Manage the DVD division internally as a "cash cow" in decline without forcing a chaotic customer-facing separation. Delay any structural split until the streaming base is dominant enough to absorb the churn, or until the DVD user base has naturally attrited significantly.

**Kill Criteria:** If the split causes a churn rate exceeding 5% in the first quarter post-launch or a >20% drop in DVD retention beyond the baseline decline, the strategy fails. The lack of a shared recommendation engine removes the primary synergy that justified the bundled existence.

This proposal forces a high-friction operational split immediately after a hostile pricing action, maximizing the likelihood of a customer revolt. The rationale of "different management" does not require separate customer-facing brands or logins, especially not now.

DECISION: NO-GO
