**Steelman:** Acquire the dominant mobile-native photo network immediately to neutralize the primary threat to the buyer's mobile strategy and secure the user base before the IPO creates a stronger, independent competitor.

**Attack:** The $1 billion valuation is unsupported by revenue or a business model, representing a massive financial risk for a company with a weak existing mobile product. Integrating a dozen-person culture into a large corporation often destroys the very innovation being purchased. Regulatory scrutiny could block the deal, wasting resources.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Allow the target to IPO, potentially becoming a well-funded, entrenched rival using the public markets to accelerate growth against the buyer's weak mobile offering.
2.  **Build/Buy Later:** Attempt to replicate features or acquire a smaller competitor; likely too slow given the target's current growth trajectory and network effects.
3.  **Acquire (Proposed):** High cost, but instantly removes the threat, acquires the team, and captures the mobile momentum.

**Fit:** The buyer's stated weakness in mobile and the target's status as the "leading mobile threat" suggests this acquisition directly addresses the strategic gap, despite the lack of current revenue. The value lies in the user network and defensive positioning, not current cash flow.

**Pre-mortem:** If this fails, it will be because the culture was crushed during integration, rendering the team useless, or regulators blocked the deal after significant distraction. The "one-way door" nature of such a large spend requires certainty, but the speed of the mobile market makes waiting riskier than overpaying.

**Decision:** The strategic necessity of securing the mobile foothold and eliminating the primary competitor outweighs the financial premium and integration risks. The cost of *not* acting (a strengthened, public competitor) is likely higher than the $1 billion price tag in the long term. The lack of revenue is secondary to the network effect and strategic defensive value.

DECISION: GO
