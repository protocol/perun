The proposal outlines a strategic pivot for a phone manufacturer that previously dismissed native applications in favor of web-based solutions. Despite the CEO's prior public stance, the plan introduces a controlled ecosystem where third-party developers can distribute native software through a single, company-operated storefront. The financial model allocates 70% of revenue to developers while retaining 30% for the platform owner. Crucially, the company maintains absolute gatekeeping authority by reviewing and approving every submission before publication.

Valid concerns regarding security, system stability, and potential friction with carrier partners are acknowledged. There is also the risk that low-quality applications could degrade the user experience that defined the device's initial success. However, the competitive landscape has shifted; rival platforms already possess thriving, openly distributed app ecosystems. Remaining dependent solely on web apps while competitors offer rich native experiences creates an existential threat to market relevance.

The proposed solution directly addresses the identified risks through centralized control. By mandating a review process for every application, the company mitigates security threats and preserves product polish, effectively balancing openness with quality assurance. The revenue-sharing model provides a clear incentive for developers to prioritize this platform over others. While carrier objections and review logistics present operational hurdles, these are manageable challenges compared to the cost of irrelevance. The shift from a closed philosophy to a curated open platform represents a necessary evolution to capture developer mindshare and sustain long-term growth. Delaying this transition to refine the approach further allows competitors to solidify their dominance, making immediate execution critical. The benefits of establishing a proprietary standard now outweigh the operational complexities of launching the store.

DECISION: GO
