The proposal seeks over $1 billion to launch a mobile-only, premium short-form video service in 2018. While the inclusion of major studios and A-list talent suggests strong content potential, the fundamental market assumptions present unacceptable risks. The core premise relies on consumers paying a subscription fee for short-form content when abundant free alternatives, including user-generated and ad-supported options, already dominate the mobile landscape.

The explicit mention of rising "subscription fatigue" directly contradicts the strategy of adding another paid service to an overcrowded market. Unlike music or long-form streaming, short-form video has historically struggled to sustain direct consumer subscription models because the perceived value per minute is lower, and attention spans are fragmented by free competitors. The high production budgets required for professional episodes under ten minutes further inflate the cost basis, demanding a massive subscriber count to achieve profitability—a feat unlikely given the stated lack of proven demand for premium short-form content.

Additionally, the primary technological differentiator—adaptive vertical and horizontal viewing—is a feature, not a moat. Existing platforms can and likely will replicate this functionality without the burden of billion-dollar content costs. The risks identified within the proposal itself, specifically regarding price sensitivity, unproven demand, and fierce competition for phone attention, are not merely hurdles but existential threats to the business model. Launching now, with such high capital exposure and no validated product-market fit for paid short-form video, exposes investors to significant financial loss. The abundance of free content creates a ceiling on what users are willing to pay, making the path to return on investment highly improbable under current conditions.

Given the saturation of free alternatives, the explicit market hesitation toward new subscriptions, and the absence of evidence that users will pay a premium for short episodes, proceeding with this investment at this time is imprudent. The proposal requires substantial validation of demand and a revised monetization strategy before any capital deployment.

DECISION: NO-GO
