The proposal relies heavily on quantitative data from blind taste tests, surveys, and focus groups, all indicating a consumer preference for a sweeter formula over both the current flagship and the main rival's product. While the data supports the notion that the new taste is superior in isolation, the strategy contains a critical blind spot regarding brand equity. The testing methodology failed to disclose to participants that the original formula would be permanently withdrawn. This omission is fatal to the validity of the results because it ignores the profound emotional attachment and heritage associated with the century-old brand.

Consumers do not purchase this product solely based on immediate sensory gratification; they buy it for nostalgia, tradition, and identity. By removing the original entirely without a fallback option, the company risks alienating its most loyal customer base. The risks identified in the proposal—emotional attachment, brand heritage, and potential backlash from bottlers and consumers—are not merely theoretical concerns but imminent threats that the current testing protocol did not adequately measure.

Furthermore, the market context shows a shift toward diet and non-cola beverages, suggesting that simply making the core product sweeter may not address the underlying diversification trends driving the loss of market share. Replacing the flagship formula entirely eliminates the company's ability to segment the market or offer choices, forcing a binary decision on consumers who may feel coerced.

Approving this plan as proposed ignores the distinction between liking a taste in a vacuum and accepting the removal of a cultural icon. The lack of a contingency plan or a parallel launch strategy exposes the organization to unnecessary volatility. The potential damage to brand reputation and retailer relationships outweighs the projected gains from improved taste test scores. The proposal must be rejected in its current form to prevent a catastrophic disconnect between the brand and its consumers.

DECISION: NO-GO
