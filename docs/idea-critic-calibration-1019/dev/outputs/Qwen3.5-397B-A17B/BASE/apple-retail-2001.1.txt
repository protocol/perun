The proposal suggests a high-risk pivot for a struggling PC maker with minimal market share and recent losses. The core strategy involves abandoning reliance on third-party retailers, where staff currently favor cheaper competitors, in favor of opening proprietary stores in expensive shopping malls. While management argues this is necessary to showcase products and a new digital-music strategy, the financial realities are stark.

The computer retail sector is characterized by notoriously low margins. Combining this with the high fixed costs of prime mall leases creates a dangerous cost structure, especially given the explicit mention of an ongoing recession. In such an economic climate, consumer discretionary spending typically contracts, making the projection of sufficient foot traffic to cover these heavy overheads highly speculative.

Furthermore, the company lacks any proven success in retail operations. Entering a capital-intensive channel without a track record while simultaneously managing inventory risk and potential conflict with existing resellers compounds the danger. The move requires significant upfront investment at a time when the firm is already recording losses. If the stores fail to generate immediate, substantial volume, the fixed lease obligations could accelerate financial distress rather than alleviate it.

The argument for better product demonstration is valid, but the proposed method exposes the firm to existential threats. The combination of low industry margins, high fixed costs, economic downturn, and operational inexperience suggests the likelihood of failure outweighs the potential benefits. The risks of channel conflict could also alienate current sales channels before the new stores prove viable. Given the absence of financial buffers and the severity of the identified risks, proceeding as proposed is imprudent. The company should explore lower-cost demonstration methods or partnerships rather than committing to expensive real estate leases immediately.

DECISION: NO-GO
