Committing to a bold public schedule by 1997 locks the corporation into a rigid timeline before the core technology is validated. This removes the flexibility needed to iterate on designs or delay launch if testing reveals flaws.

While being first to market offers potential long-term branding benefits, the combination of unproven technology, uncertain durability, high costs, and absent demand makes this specific proposal untenable at this time. The risks of cost overruns, reliability failures, and unsold inventory outweigh the speculative advantages of early entry. The corporation should not proceed with a mass-production commitment under these conditions. A phased approach involving limited pilot programs and further R&D to prove battery life would be prudent, but the current all-in strategy is reckless.
