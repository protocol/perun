### Changed
- The CI `perpr` legs are now balanced by recorded test time instead of by sorted position. `scripts/ci-tests-shard.sh` reads `scripts/ci-durations.tsv` (whole seconds per `scripts/ci-tests.d/*.sh` registration: the seconds between consecutive `== <registration>` lines of the three `perpr` job logs of public CI run 41148962559 (2026-10-31), rounded, at least 1), takes the serial registrations longest first (ties by name, C locale) and gives each to the least-loaded leg (ties to the lowest index). A registration with no row counts as the table's median, so adding a test never needs a table edit and two PRs never conflict on the table. `*.bg.sh` registrations keep the sorted round-robin, because they run beside the serial ones, so only serial seconds are balanced. The contract is unchanged: the `i/N` slices are disjoint and cover every registration for any N, a malformed `SHARD` exits 2, an empty slice exits 1, the listing is sorted and does not depend on the locale. On the committed table, which counts every test at least 1 s (so these are predictions from the table, not CI measurements), the old split gives legs of 283, 489 and 682 s of serial tests, and 544, 355 and 556 s once this change's own new registration moves every later test to the next leg; the new split gives 485, 485 and 485 s, so the slowest predicted leg drops from 682 to 485 s.
- Test: `scripts/test-perpr-shard-balance.sh` (registered as `scripts/ci-tests.d/perpr-shard-balance.sh`) checks the committed table's format, that the slices are disjoint and complete for N = 1 to 5, that the slowest of 3 legs is predicted within 1.1x of the mean (it fails on the old split), and, on a fixture, the exact longest-first assignment, the median default for a malformed or missing row, and the round-robin of background tests. `scripts/test-perpr-shard.sh` no longer asserts that the slices hold nearly equal numbers of registrations, which only the old split guaranteed. `CONTRIBUTING.md` describes the new split, says when to refresh the table (the slowest leg's `Per-PR tests` step more than 20% over the fastest on a PR run) and gives the awk command that rebuilds its rows from the three job logs; the test runs that command on synthetic logs.
