### Changed
- The CI `perpr` job now runs four legs instead of three (`shard: [1, 2, 3, 4]`, `SHARD=i/4`), and its legs are balanced by recorded test time instead of by sorted position. `scripts/ci-tests-shard.sh` reads `scripts/ci-durations.tsv` (whole seconds per `scripts/ci-tests.d/*.sh` registration: the seconds between consecutive `== <registration>` lines of the `perpr` job logs of public CI run 41148962559 (2026-10-31), rounded, at least 1), takes the serial registrations longest first (ties by name, C locale) and gives each to the least-loaded leg (ties to the lowest index). A registration with no row counts as the table's median, so adding a test never needs a table edit and two PRs never conflict on the table. `*.bg.sh` registrations keep the sorted round-robin, because they run beside the serial ones, so only serial seconds are balanced. The contract is unchanged: the `i/N` slices are disjoint and cover every registration for any N, a malformed `SHARD` exits 2, an empty slice exits 1, the listing is sorted and does not depend on the locale. On the committed table, which counts every test at least 1 s (so these are predictions from the table, not CI measurements), the old helper with three legs on the tree before this change gives legs of 283, 489 and 682 s of serial tests, and the new split over four legs gives 364, 364, 364 and 363 s.
- The `Per-PR tests` step also prints one `bg elapsed: <test> <seconds> s` line per background test (its wall time from the background start, `?` when it left no end time), because the `== <registration>` lines show only how long the step waited for it, not how long it ran.
- Test: `scripts/test-perpr-shard-balance.sh` (registered as `scripts/ci-tests.d/perpr-shard-balance.sh`) checks the committed table's format, that the slices are disjoint and complete for N = 1 to 5, that the slowest of the legs `ci.yml` runs (the N its `perpr` legs pass in `SHARD=i/N`, read from `ci.yml`) is predicted within 1.1x of the mean (it fails on the old split), and, on a fixture, the exact longest-first assignment, the median default for a malformed or missing row, and the round-robin of background tests. `scripts/test-perpr-shard.sh` no longer asserts that the slices hold nearly equal numbers of registrations, which only the old split guaranteed, and now checks that each leg logs its `bg elapsed:` line. `CONTRIBUTING.md` describes the new split, says when to refresh the table (the slowest leg's `Per-PR tests` step more than 20% over the fastest on a PR run) and gives the awk command that rebuilds its rows from the job logs of the legs; the test runs that command on synthetic logs.
